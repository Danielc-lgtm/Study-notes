## §1.1 Abstract Measure Theory — Exercises

The exercises of §1.1 drill the foundational $\sigma$-algebra and measure properties. Intersections of $\sigma$-algebras are $\sigma$-algebras (so "generated by" is well-defined); continuity from above requires *some* finiteness hypothesis (escape-to-infinity counterexamples on counting measure); and the first Borel-Cantelli lemma converts summability of $\mu(A_n)$ into a $\limsup$ being null. The unifying observation: countable additivity is strong enough to support a calculus of sets, but finiteness hypotheses are needed wherever a *limit* is taken.

- [[Ex - Unions and intersections of σ-algebras]] (⭐) — intersections of $\sigma$-algebras are $\sigma$-algebras, unions are not; justifies the "generated by" construction ([[Def - Algebra and σ-Algebra]], [[Def - Generated σ-Algebra]])
- [[Ex - Continuity from above requires finite measure]] (⭐) — the finiteness hypothesis is indispensable; escape-to-infinity counterexample on counting measure ([[Def - Measure and Measure Space]], [[Thm - Properties of Measures]])
- [[Ex - The first Borel-Cantelli lemma]] (⭐⭐) — summable measures kill the $\limsup$ set, via $\sigma$-subadditivity and a convergent tail ([[Def - Measure and Measure Space]], [[Thm - Properties of Measures]])
