# Problem Statement

Let $X=\mathbb{R}$ and let $\mathcal{A}$ be the algebra generated by the half-open intervals — finite disjoint unions of sets $(a,b]$ (with $\pm\infty$ allowed). Define a [[Def - Pre-Measure|pre-measure]] $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$ by
$$\widetilde\mu(A)=\begin{cases}0,&A=\emptyset,\\ \infty,&A\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}$$

**(a)** Check that $\widetilde\mu$ is a pre-measure on $\mathcal{A}$, and that $\sigma(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$.

**(b)** Show that $\widetilde\mu$ is **not** $\sigma$-finite.

**(c)** Exhibit two *distinct* measures $\mu_1,\mu_2$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ that both restrict to $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$. Conclude that the $\sigma$-finiteness hypothesis in the [[Thm - Uniqueness of the Hahn-Carathéodory Extension|uniqueness theorem]] cannot be dropped.

**Recall:**

The [[Thm - Hahn-Carathéodory Extension Theorem|extension theorem]] says a pre-measure on an algebra extends to a measure on the generated $\sigma$-algebra; the [[Thm - Uniqueness of the Hahn-Carathéodory Extension|companion]] says the extension is *unique* **if $\widetilde\mu$ is [[Def - σ-Finite Measure|σ-finite]]**.

---

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** demonstrating that a hypothesis is *necessary* by constructing a counterexample when it is removed.

**Assumption pattern:** the proof of uniqueness used $\sigma$-finiteness for exactly one purpose — to license the *subtraction* $\nu(A)=\nu(S)-\nu(S\setminus A)$ inside a finite-measure window. To break uniqueness, build a pre-measure with *no* finite-measure windows at all: every nonempty algebra set has measure $\infty$.

**Theorem routing:** the extension theorem still applies (existence needs no $\sigma$-finiteness), so extensions *exist*; the task is to find two of them.

**Key decision point:** finding two genuinely different measures on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ that agree on $\mathcal{A}$ — counting measure and "$\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\neq\emptyset}$" both work, and they disagree on a singleton.

---

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Verify the pre-measure axioms** on the algebra.
2. **Negate $\sigma$-finiteness** by showing no countable family of finite-measure algebra sets covers $X$.
3. **Exhibit two extensions** and separate them on a set in $\sigma(\mathcal{A})\setminus\mathcal{A}$.

---

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> For (a): every nonempty algebra set has $\widetilde\mu=\infty$. Countable additivity within $\mathcal{A}$: if a disjoint union of algebra sets is a nonempty algebra set, at least one piece is nonempty — what is the sum?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> For (b): a finite-measure set in $\mathcal{A}$ must be $\emptyset$. Can countably many copies of $\emptyset$ cover $\mathbb{R}$?

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For (c): try counting measure $\#$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, and the measure $\mu_\infty(A)=\infty$ for $A\neq\emptyset$. Do both agree with $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$? Do they agree on $\{0\}$?

---

# Solution

**Step 1 — (a) $\widetilde\mu$ is a pre-measure.** $\widetilde\mu(\emptyset)=0$. For countable additivity *within $\mathcal{A}$*: suppose $A=\bigsqcup_k A_k$ with all sets in $\mathcal{A}$. If $A=\emptyset$ then every $A_k=\emptyset$ and $0=0$. If $A\neq\emptyset$, some $A_{k_0}\neq\emptyset$, so $\sum_k\widetilde\mu(A_k)\ge\widetilde\mu(A_{k_0})=\infty=\widetilde\mu(A)$. Either way the identity holds. Since the half-open intervals generate $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, $\sigma(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 2 — (b) Not $\sigma$-finite.** $\sigma$-finiteness would require $\mathbb{R}=\bigcup_k S_k$ with $S_k\in\mathcal{A}$ and $\widetilde\mu(S_k)<\infty$. But $\widetilde\mu(S_k)<\infty$ forces $\widetilde\mu(S_k)=0$, i.e. $S_k=\emptyset$. A countable union of empty sets is $\emptyset\neq\mathbb{R}$. So no such exhaustion exists.

**Step 3 — (c) Two distinct extensions.** Let $\mu_1=\#$ be the counting measure on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mu_2=\mu_\infty$, where $\mu_\infty(A)=\infty$ for $A\neq\emptyset$, $\mu_\infty(\emptyset)=0$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Both are measures on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$: counting measure is standard; $\mu_\infty$ is a measure because $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable so any nonempty disjoint union has a nonempty piece, again giving $\infty=\infty$.
> Both restrict to $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$: a nonempty half-open interval is *infinite* (uncountably many points), so $\#$ assigns it $\infty=\widetilde\mu$; $\mu_\infty$ assigns every nonempty set $\infty=\widetilde\mu$; and both give $\emptyset\mapsto 0$.
> They differ: on the singleton $\{0\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, $\mu_1(\{0\})=\#\{0\}=1$ while $\mu_2(\{0\})=\mu_\infty(\{0\})=\infty$.

So two distinct measures on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ extend the same pre-measure. Uniqueness fails — precisely because, by (b), $\widetilde\mu$ is not $\sigma$-finite.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> (a) $\widetilde\mu(\emptyset)=0$; for a disjoint algebra-decomposition $A=\bigsqcup A_k$ inside $\mathcal{A}$, both sides are $0$ if $A=\emptyset$ and both $\infty$ if $A\neq\emptyset$ (some piece is nonempty). Half-open intervals generate $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$. (b) A finite-$\widetilde\mu$ algebra set is empty; a countable union of empties is not $\mathbb{R}$; so $\widetilde\mu$ is not $\sigma$-finite. (c) Counting measure $\#$ and $\mu_\infty$ are measures on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, agree with $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$ (every nonempty interval is uncountable), but $\#(\{0\})=1\neq\infty=\mu_\infty(\{0\})$. Hence the extension is non-unique; the $\sigma$-finite hypothesis is indispensable. $\blacksquare$

---

# Key Takeaways

**A hypothesis is proved necessary by amputating it and producing a counterexample — and the counterexample should attack exactly the step the hypothesis protected.** Here uniqueness was proved by a *subtraction inside a finite window*; so the counterexample is engineered to have *no finite windows whatsoever*. This is the general method for "is hypothesis $H$ needed?": locate the single line of the proof that uses $H$, then build an object where that line fails. A counterexample that breaks an unrelated step teaches nothing; one that breaks *the* step pinpoints the role of $H$.

**$\sigma$-finiteness is the precise dividing line between "a pre-measure determines its extension" and "it does not."** Existence of an extension never needs it; uniqueness always does. The mechanism is subtraction: comparing two extensions $\nu_1,\nu_2$ requires $\nu_i(A)=\nu_i(S)-\nu_i(S\setminus A)$, and $\infty-\infty$ is meaningless. Whenever you invoke "*the* measure extending..." — *the* Lebesgue measure, *the* product measure, *the* law with a given [[Def - Distribution Function|distribution function]] — silently you are using $\sigma$-finiteness; on a non-$\sigma$-finite algebra the definite article is unavailable.
