# Notation

$X$ is a set, $\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^X$ an [[Def - Algebra and σ-Algebra|algebra]], $\widetilde\mu:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty]$ a [[Def - Pre-Measure|pre-measure]]. $\mu^*$ is the [[Def - Outer Measure|outer measure]] built from $\widetilde\mu$ by the cover formula; $\Sigma$ is the [[Def - Carathéodory Measurable Sets|Carathéodory σ-algebra]] of $\mu^*$; $\mu=\mu^*|_\Sigma$.

---

# Motivation

This is the central existence theorem of measure theory — the reason Lebesgue measure, product measures, and the laws of stochastic processes all *exist*. The problem it solves: we can easily say what volume a *box* should have, and boxes form an algebra; we cannot directly say what volume an arbitrary *Borel* set should have. The theorem asserts that the easy datum on the algebra — a [[Def - Pre-Measure|pre-measure]] — uniquely determines (under $\sigma$-finiteness) a genuine measure on the whole generated $\sigma$-algebra. "Define it where it's easy, extend by a theorem" becomes legitimate.

The mechanism is the [[Thm - Carathéodory's σ-Algebra|Carathéodory machine]]: a pre-measure inflates to an outer measure $\mu^*$ on *all* subsets, and then deflates to a measure on the sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\Sigma$ where $\mu^*$ is additive. The two things still to check — and they are the content of *this* theorem on top of Carathéodory's — are that $\Sigma$ is large enough (it contains $\mathcal{A}$, hence $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$) and that the extension is faithful ($\mu$ agrees with $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$).

---

# Sources and Targets

**Sources.** The input is "a pre-measure on an algebra." The non-obvious source-recognitions: (i) the *elementary-volume function on boxes* is a pre-measure — but proving so needs a Heine–Borel compactness argument ([[Thm - Hahn-Carathéodory Extension Theorem#Lemma 4|Lemma below]]); (ii) the *finitely additive function on the algebra generated by rectangles*, with $\rho(A\times B)=\mu(A)\nu(B)$, is a pre-measure, yielding [[Def - Product σ-Algebra|product measures]]; (iii) a *consistent family of finite-dimensional distributions* defines a pre-measure on cylinder sets, yielding stochastic processes (Kolmogorov extension). In each case the work is verifying the *continuity property* — countable additivity within the algebra.

**Targets.** The output "a measure on $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$" combines with: (i) *$\sigma$-finiteness* of $\widetilde\mu$ to give **uniqueness** ([[Thm - Uniqueness of the Hahn-Carathéodory Extension|next theorem]]) — existence + uniqueness = "*the* Lebesgue measure," "*the* product measure"; (ii) the fact that $\Sigma$ is **complete** to give, for free, a complete measure (the [[Def - Null Set and Completion|Lebesgue completion]]) extending the one on $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$.

---

# Statement

Let $X$ be a set, $\mathcal{A}$ an algebra over $X$, and $\widetilde\mu:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty]$ a pre-measure. Build $\mu^*$ by the cover formula with $\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{A}$, let $\Sigma$ be its Carathéodory $\sigma$-algebra, and set $\mu=\mu^*|_\Sigma$. Then:

1. $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ is a [[Def - Measure and Measure Space|measure space]];
2. $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma$ — hence $\sigma(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\Sigma$;
3. $\mu^*(A)=\widetilde\mu(A)=\mu(A)$ for every $A\in\mathcal{A}$.

In words: every pre-measure on an algebra $\mathcal{A}$ **extends** to a (complete) measure on a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$, with no change of value on $\mathcal{A}$.

---

# Why Is It True

Part (1) is pure [[Thm - Carathéodory's σ-Algebra|Carathéodory]]: $\mu^*$ is an outer measure (it is built by the cover formula), so $\Sigma$ is a $\sigma$-algebra and $\mu^*|_\Sigma$ a measure. Nothing pre-measure-specific is used.

Part (2) — *why is the original algebra Carathéodory-measurable?* — is the heart. Take $A\in\mathcal{A}$ and any test set $B$. By [[Def - Carathéodory Measurable Sets|the criterion]] only "$\geq$" needs proof. Cover $B$ economically by algebra sets $K_j\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\sum\widetilde\mu(K_j)\le\mu^*(B)+\varepsilon$. Now each $K_j$ is *itself an algebra set*, so $K_j\cap A$ and $K_j\setminus A$ are again in $\mathcal{A}$, and crucially $\widetilde\mu(K_j)=\widetilde\mu(K_j\cap A)+\widetilde\mu(K_j\setminus A)$ — *finite additivity of the pre-measure, applied inside the cover*. The pieces $K_j\cap A$ cover $B\cap A$ and the pieces $K_j\setminus A$ cover $B\setminus A$, so $\mu^*(B\cap A)+\mu^*(B\setminus A)\le\sum\widetilde\mu(K_j\cap A)+\sum\widetilde\mu(K_j\setminus A)=\sum\widetilde\mu(K_j)\le\mu^*(B)+\varepsilon$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$. The slogan: *the cover that is cheap for $B$ splits, set by set, into a cheap cover of $B\cap A$ and a cheap cover of $B\setminus A$*, because each covering set is itself splittable by the algebra.

Part (3) — *faithfulness*. "$\le$" is trivial: cover $A$ by itself, cost $\widetilde\mu(A)$. "$\ge$": any countable algebra-cover $(K_j)$ of $A$ can be *disjointified inside $\mathcal{A}$* into $\widetilde K_j$, and then $A=\bigsqcup(\widetilde K_j\cap A)$ is a *countable disjoint union landing in $\mathcal{A}$* — exactly the situation where the pre-measure's countable-additivity-within-$\mathcal{A}$ (its defining property!) applies. So $\widetilde\mu(A)=\sum\widetilde\mu(\widetilde K_j\cap A)\le\sum\widetilde\mu(K_j)$; infimise. Faithfulness is where the "$\sigma$" in pre-measure is finally spent.

---

# What Makes This Hard

The conceptual hurdle is keeping straight which closure property is used where. Part (2) uses only *finite* additivity of $\widetilde\mu$ (splitting each $K_j$ by $A$); part (3) uses the full *countable*-additivity-within-$\mathcal{A}$ (the union $\bigsqcup\widetilde K_j\cap A$ lands back in $\mathcal{A}$). Conflating these — or thinking part (2) needs countable additivity — is the standard confusion. The other genuinely hard input is *external*: verifying that elementary volume is a pre-measure at all needs a compactness argument, and that is where the topology of $\mathbb{R}^n$ secretly enters an otherwise topology-free theorem.

---

# Rederivation Scaffold

**High-level strategy.** Inflate ($\widetilde\mu\to\mu^*$, cover formula), apply Carathéodory ($\to\Sigma,\mu$), then prove the two compatibility statements: $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma$ by splitting covers set-by-set, and faithfulness by disjointifying covers inside $\mathcal{A}$.

**Subgoal decomposition.**

1. **$(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ is a measure space.** Cite [[Thm - Carathéodory's σ-Algebra]] — $\mu^*$ from a cover is an outer measure, so $\Sigma$ is a $\sigma$-algebra, $\mu^*|_\Sigma$ a measure.
2. **$\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma$.** For $A\in\mathcal{A}$, $B$ arbitrary: cover $B$ by $K_j\in\mathcal{A}$ near-optimally; split each $K_j$ by $A$ using *finite* additivity of $\widetilde\mu$; conclude $\mu^*(B)\ge\mu^*(B\cap A)+\mu^*(B\setminus A)$.
   - *Hint:* only "$\geq$" is needed; "$\le$" is free subadditivity.
3. **$\sigma(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\Sigma$.** $\Sigma$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{A}$; minimality of $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$.
4. **Faithfulness $\mu^*|_\mathcal{A}=\widetilde\mu$.** "$\le$": trivial cover. "$\ge$": disjointify any algebra-cover inside $\mathcal{A}$, apply countable-additivity-within-$\mathcal{A}$, infimise.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: The algebra is Carathéodory-measurable
> **Statement:** $A\in\mathcal{A}\implies A\in\Sigma$.
>
> **Hint:** Split a near-optimal cover of the test set $B$ set-by-set using finite additivity of $\widetilde\mu$.
>
> **Why needed:** This is statement (2); with step 3 it gives $\sigma(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\Sigma$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Fix $A\in\mathcal{A}$, $B\subseteq X$, $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $K_j\in\mathcal{A}$ with $B\subseteq\bigcup_j K_j$ and $\sum_j\widetilde\mu(K_j)\le\mu^*(B)+\varepsilon$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ is an algebra, $K_j\cap A,\ K_j\setminus A\in\mathcal{A}$, and by finite additivity $\widetilde\mu(K_j)=\widetilde\mu(K_j\cap A)+\widetilde\mu(K_j\setminus A)$. The family $(K_j\cap A)$ covers $B\cap A$ and $(K_j\setminus A)$ covers $B\setminus A$, so by the definition of $\mu^*$ and finiteness of the algebra operations,
> > $$\mu^*(B\cap A)+\mu^*(B\setminus A)\le\sum_j\widetilde\mu(K_j\cap A)+\sum_j\widetilde\mu(K_j\setminus A)=\sum_j\widetilde\mu(K_j)\le\mu^*(B)+\varepsilon.$$
> > Let $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$; with subadditivity ("$\le$") this gives the Carathéodory equality. $\square$

> [!note]- Lemma 2: Faithfulness on the algebra
> **Statement:** $\mu^*(A)=\widetilde\mu(A)$ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}$.
>
> **Hint:** "$\ge$" needs disjointifying the cover *inside* $\mathcal{A}$ and the pre-measure's countable additivity.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > "$\le$": take the single-set cover $K_1=A$, cost $\widetilde\mu(A)$. "$\ge$": let $K_j\in\mathcal{A}$ cover $A$. Disjointify: $\widetilde K_1=K_1$, $\widetilde K_j=K_j\setminus\bigcup_{l<j}K_l\in\mathcal{A}$. The sets $\widetilde K_j\cap A\in\mathcal{A}$ are pairwise disjoint with $\bigsqcup_j(\widetilde K_j\cap A)=A\in\mathcal{A}$. By countable additivity *within $\mathcal{A}$* (the pre-measure axiom),
> > $$\widetilde\mu(A)=\sum_j\widetilde\mu(\widetilde K_j\cap A)\le\sum_j\widetilde\mu(\widetilde K_j)\le\sum_j\widetilde\mu(K_j),$$
> > using $\widetilde K_j\cap A\subseteq\widetilde K_j\subseteq K_j$ and monotonicity of $\widetilde\mu$ on $\mathcal{A}$. Infimise over covers: $\widetilde\mu(A)\le\mu^*(A)$. $\square$

> [!note]- Lemma 3 (input): Elementary volume is a pre-measure
> **Statement:** On $\mathbb{R}^n$, the elementary-volume function $\widetilde\lambda$ on the algebra of [[Def - Interval and Elementary Figure|elementary figures]] is a pre-measure.
>
> **Hint:** Countable additivity-within-$\mathcal{A}$ reduces, via Heine–Borel, to finite additivity.
>
> **Why needed:** Without this the extension theorem has no input for the Lebesgue construction.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof sketch
> > "$\ge$" in countable additivity is finite additivity plus monotonicity. For "$\le$" when an interval $I=\bigsqcup_k I_k$: shrink $I$ to a compact $\overline I_L$, enlarge each $I_k$ to an open $I_k^\varepsilon$ with $\widetilde\lambda(I_k^\varepsilon)<\widetilde\lambda(I_k)+\varepsilon 2^{-k}$. The $I_k^\varepsilon$ open-cover the compact $\overline I_L$; Heine–Borel extracts a *finite* subcover; finite subadditivity then bounds $\widetilde\lambda(\overline I_L)\le\sum\widetilde\lambda(I_k)+\varepsilon$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$, $L\to\infty$. $\square$

---

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> By [[Thm - Carathéodory's σ-Algebra|Carathéodory's theorem]] applied to the cover $\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{A}$, $\mu^*$ is an outer measure, $\Sigma$ a $\sigma$-algebra, $\mu=\mu^*|_\Sigma$ a measure — statement (1). Lemma 1 gives $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma$; since $\Sigma$ is a $\sigma$-algebra and $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ is the smallest containing $\mathcal{A}$, $\sigma(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\Sigma$ — statement (2). Lemma 2 gives $\mu^*|_\mathcal{A}=\widetilde\mu$, and since $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma$, $\mu|_\mathcal{A}=\mu^*|_\mathcal{A}=\widetilde\mu$ — statement (3). $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

The same theorem, fed different pre-measures, builds: **Lebesgue–Stieltjes measures** (pre-measure $(a,b]\mapsto F(b)-F(a)$ for a [[Def - Distribution Function|distribution function]] $F$ — this is how *every* law on $\mathbb{R}$ is constructed); **product measures** ([[Thm - Product Measure and Fubini-Tonelli]]); **Hausdorff measures** (covers by small balls); and, via Kolmogorov's extension theorem, the **law of Brownian motion** on path space. Recognising "pre-measure on an algebra" in a new setting is recognising that a measure can be built there.
