## §2.4 Convergence Theorems — Exercises

The exercises of §2.4 drill the three convergence theorems (MCT, Fatou, DCT) and their interchanges of limit with integral. Term-by-term integration of series follows from MCT for non-negative summands and from DCT under absolute summability; the three escape mechanisms (vertical, horizontal, oscillating) make Fatou strict; and differentiation under the integral sign is the special case of DCT applied to a difference quotient with a uniform bound from the mean value theorem. The unifying observation: a dominating function (or monotonicity) is what makes limit-integral interchange legal.

- [[Ex - Term-by-term integration of series]] (⭐⭐) — interchanging $\sum$ and $\int$: MCT for free, DCT under absolute summability ([[Thm - Monotone Convergence Theorem]], [[Thm - Dominated Convergence Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Strict inequality in Fatou's lemma]] (⭐) — the three escape mechanisms making Fatou strict ([[Thm - Fatou's Lemma]], [[Thm - Dominated Convergence Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Differentiation under the integral sign]] (⭐⭐) — $\frac{d}{dt}\int=\int\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$ via DCT and the mean value theorem ([[Thm - Dominated Convergence Theorem]])
