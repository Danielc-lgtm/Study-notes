## §2.6 Lp Spaces — Exercises

The exercises of §2.6 drill the $L^p$ spaces and their convergence theory. Inclusions among $L^p$ spaces run in opposite directions on finite-measure vs counting-measure spaces (a calibration on the role of the measure); $L^p$-convergence implies convergence in measure plus an a.e.-convergent subsequence (the standard $L^p \Rightarrow$ a.e. machinery); and Cauchy-Schwarz makes $L^2$ a Hilbert space, with orthogonal projection identifying conditional expectation. The unifying observation: the $L^p$ spaces are the natural Banach-space hosts for measurable functions, with $L^2$ uniquely admitting Hilbert-space geometry.

- [[Ex - Lp inclusions and a power-law criterion]] (⭐⭐) — inclusions run opposite ways on finite-measure vs counting-measure spaces; $|x|^{-\alpha}\in L^p\iff\alpha p<1$ ([[Def - Lp Spaces]], [[Thm - Hölder and Minkowski Inequalities]])
- [[Ex - Lp convergence and almost-everywhere subsequences]] (⭐⭐) — $L^p$-convergence gives convergence in measure and an a.e.-convergent subsequence ([[Def - Lp Spaces]], [[Thm - Completeness of Lp Spaces]], [[Def - Convergence in Measure]])
- [[Ex - The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and L2 geometry]] (⭐⭐) — $L^2$ as a Hilbert space; orthogonal projection is conditional expectation ([[Def - Lp Spaces]], [[Thm - Hölder and Minkowski Inequalities]], [[Thm - Completeness of Lp Spaces]])
