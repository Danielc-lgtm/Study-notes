## §2.3 The Integral — Exercises

The exercises of §2.3 drill the Lebesgue integral and its working properties. Markov's inequality converts averages into tail bounds (the foundation of Chebyshev and concentration); monotone convergence justifies the standard machine of building integrals from simple-function approximations; and the Dirichlet function calibrates the gap between Lebesgue and Riemann integration — Lebesgue handles convergence in measure where Riemann fails.

- [[Ex - Markov's inequality]] (⭐) — the average controls the tail; Chebyshev and "$\int f=0\Rightarrow f=0$ a.e." ([[Def - The Integral]], [[Thm - Properties of the Integral]])
- [[Ex - Computing an expectation by monotone convergence]] (⭐⭐) — the geometric mean $\mathbb{E}[X]=1/p$, with MCT justifying "integral $=$ series" ([[Def - The Integral]], [[Thm - Monotone Convergence Theorem]], [[Thm - Approximation by Simple Functions]])
- [[Ex - The Riemann integral as a special case]] (⭐⭐) — the Dirichlet function: Lebesgue-integrable, not Riemann; the integral commutes with limits ([[Def - The Integral]], [[Def - Lebesgue Measure]], [[Thm - Dominated Convergence Theorem]])
