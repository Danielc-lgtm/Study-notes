## §2.5 Vitali's Theorem — Exercises

The exercises of §2.5 drill convergence in measure and Vitali's theorem. The typewriter sequence converges in measure but not a.e. (the subsequence rescue: a.e. convergence is recoverable along a subsequence); $L^p$-boundedness ($p > 1$) and domination both give uniform integrability (the two standard routes); and Vitali strictly contains DCT — a "wandering tall bump" converges in $L^1$ without any dominating function, but its uniform integrability makes Vitali apply.

- [[Ex - The typewriter sequence]] (⭐⭐) — convergence in measure without a.e. convergence; the subsequence rescue ([[Def - Convergence in Measure]], [[Def - Almost Everywhere]])
- [[Ex - Lp boundedness implies uniform integrability]] (⭐⭐) — domination and $L^p$-boundedness ($p>1$) are the two routes to uniform integrability ([[Def - Absolute Continuity and Density]], [[Def - Lp Spaces]], [[Thm - Vitali Convergence Theorem]])
- [[Ex - L1 convergence without a dominating function]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Vitali strictly contains DCT; wandering tall bumps ([[Thm - Vitali Convergence Theorem]], [[Thm - Dominated Convergence Theorem]], [[Def - Absolute Continuity and Density]])
