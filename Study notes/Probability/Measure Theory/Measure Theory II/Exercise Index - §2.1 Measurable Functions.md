## §2.1 Measurable Functions — Exercises

The exercises of §2.1 drill the working properties of measurable functions. Measurability need only be checked on a generating $\pi$-system (the standard minimality trick); the Doob-Dynkin lemma identifies $\sigma(f)$-measurable functions as exactly the Borel functions of $f$; and pointwise limits of measurable functions are measurable on the convergence set. The unifying observation: measurability is closed under all the operations one needs to define new functions from old ones.

- [[Ex - Measurability via a generating set]] (⭐) — measurability need only be checked on a generating family; the minimality principle ([[Def - Measurable Function]], [[Def - Generated σ-Algebra]])
- [[Ex - The σ-algebra generated by a function]] (⭐⭐) — Doob–Dynkin: $\sigma(f)$-measurable $=$ a function of $f$ ([[Def - Measurable Function]], [[Thm - Approximation by Simple Functions]])
- [[Ex - Pointwise limits and the convergence set]] (⭐) — the set where $f_n$ converges is measurable ([[Def - Measurable Function]], [[Thm - Operations Preserve Measurability]])
