## §2.2 Measurability vs Continuity — Exercises

The exercises of §2.2 drill the relationship between measurability and continuity via Lusin and Egorov. Lusin says every measurable function is continuous *off a small set* (the Dirichlet function as the extreme case); Egorov says a.e. convergence is *uniform off a small set* (the bridge from a.e. to uniform); both require finite measure to prevent escape-to-infinity counterexamples. The unifying observation: measurable functions and a.e. convergence are "almost" continuous and uniform, with the gap controlled by small exceptional sets.

- [[Ex - Egorov fails without finite measure]] (⭐⭐) — escape to infinity defeats Egorov on $(\mathbb{R},\lambda)$ ([[Thm - Egorov's Theorem]], [[Def - Almost Everywhere]])
- [[Ex - Lusin for the Dirichlet function]] (⭐⭐) — a nowhere-continuous function made continuous off a small set ([[Thm - Lusin's Theorem]], [[Def - Measurable Function]])
- [[Ex - Almost-everywhere convergence is uniform off a small set]] (⭐⭐) — Egorov converts a.e. convergence into convergence in measure and bounded convergence ([[Thm - Egorov's Theorem]], [[Def - Almost Everywhere]])
