## §4.2 Lebesgue Decomposition and Radon–Nikodym — Exercises

The exercises of §4.2 drill the Radon-Nikodym theorem and the Lebesgue decomposition. Each exercise illustrates either the *positive* content — Radon-Nikodym constructs a density whenever absolute continuity holds and both measures are $\sigma$-finite — or a structural limitation: a Dirac mass has no density (it is mutually singular to Lebesgue), and counting measure on $[0, 1]$ admits no density of Lebesgue measure (because the reference measure fails $\sigma$-finiteness). The key conceptual move is recognising conditional expectation as a Radon-Nikodym derivative on the sub-$\sigma$-algebra.

- [[Ex - The Dirac mass has no density]] (⭐) — a point mass is singular to Lebesgue measure; the Lebesgue decomposition of a mixed measure ([[Def - Absolute Continuity and Density]], [[Def - Mutual Singularity]], [[Thm - Radon-Nikodym Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Conditional expectation as a Radon-Nikodym derivative]] (⭐⭐⭐) — $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$ is the density of $\int_\cdot X\,d\mathbb{P}$ on the sub-$\sigma$-algebra ([[Thm - Radon-Nikodym Theorem]], [[Def - Absolute Continuity and Density]])
- [[Ex - Radon-Nikodym fails without σ-finiteness]] (⭐⭐) — counting measure vs Lebesgue: $\ll$ holds, no density exists ([[Thm - Radon-Nikodym Theorem]], [[Def - σ-Finite Measure]], [[Def - Absolute Continuity and Density]])
