## §4.1 Differentiation of the Lebesgue Integral — Exercises

The exercises of §4.1 drill the Lebesgue differentiation theorem and its consequences. Each exercise practices the use of the differentiation theorem in a different mode: as an indicator-function statement (the density theorem), as an upgrade to absolute averages (Lebesgue points), and as a calibration of *why* the Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality must be weak-type rather than strong. The unifying observation: differentiation of the integral lifts pointwise behaviour from a.e. averages of $f$ to a.e. averages of $|f - f(x)|$, ruling out cancellation effects.

- [[Ex - The Lebesgue density theorem]] (⭐⭐) — a.e. point of a measurable set has density $0$ or $1$ ([[Thm - Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem]], [[Def - Lebesgue Measure]])
- [[Ex - Lebesgue points of an integrable function]] (⭐⭐) — a.e. point controls the average deviation $\frac{1}{\lambda(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-f(x)|$, not just the average ([[Thm - Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem]])
- [[Ex - The maximal function is not integrable]] (⭐⭐) — $f^*\notin L^1$; the maximal inequality must be weak-type, and that suffices ([[Def - Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Function]], [[Def - The Integral]])
