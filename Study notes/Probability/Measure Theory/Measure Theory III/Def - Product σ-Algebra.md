# Notation

$(X_1,\mathcal{A}_1)$, $(X_2,\mathcal{A}_2)$ are [[Def - Measurable Space|measurable spaces]]. $X=X_1\times X_2$; $Z_i:X\to X_i$ the coordinate projections. $\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2$ the product $\sigma$-algebra.

---

# Axiom Motivation

To do measure theory on a product space $X_1\times X_2$ — to integrate functions of two variables, to build the law of a pair of random variables — we need a $\sigma$-algebra on the product. What should it be?

The guiding demand: the two **coordinate projections** $Z_1,Z_2$ should be [[Def - Measurable Function|measurable]]. That is the minimal requirement for "a measurable function of the pair restricts to a measurable function of each coordinate." Measurability of $Z_i$ means $Z_i^{-1}(A_i)\in\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2$ for $A_i\in\mathcal{A}_i$ — i.e. the "slabs" $A_1\times X_2$ and $X_1\times A_2$ must be measurable. Taking the *smallest* $\sigma$-algebra containing them (the [[Def - Generated σ-Algebra|generated]] one) gives the product $\sigma$-algebra.

Equivalently — and this is the form one uses — it is generated by the **measurable rectangles** $A_1\times A_2$, since $A_1\times A_2=Z_1^{-1}(A_1)\cap Z_2^{-1}(A_2)$. The rectangles form a [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|π-system]] (closed under intersection: $(A_1\times A_2)\cap(B_1\times B_2)=(A_1\cap B_1)\times(A_2\cap B_2)$), which is exactly what makes uniqueness of the [[Thm - Product Measure|product measure]] and the slice arguments of [[Thm - Fubini-Tonelli Theorem|Fubini]] work. This is the same "initial structure making the projections nice" recipe as the product topology — and the compatibility $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^m)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})$ shows the two product constructions agree where both apply.

---

# The Definition

Let $(X_1,\mathcal{A}_1)$, $(X_2,\mathcal{A}_2)$ be measurable spaces, $X=X_1\times X_2$. A **measurable rectangle** is a set $A_1\times A_2$ with $A_i\in\mathcal{A}_i$. The **product $\sigma$-algebra** is
$$\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2=\sigma\big(\{A_1\times A_2:A_i\in\mathcal{A}_i\}\big)=\sigma(Z_1,Z_2),$$
the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the measurable rectangles — equivalently, by the coordinate projections $Z_1,Z_2$. It is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra on $X$ making both projections measurable.

For $E\subseteq X$ and $x_1\in X_1$, the **slice** $E_{x_1}=\{x_2:(x_1,x_2)\in E\}$ satisfies: $E\in\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2\implies E_{x_1}\in\mathcal{A}_2$ for every $x_1$ (and symmetrically). The product extends to finitely (and, for σ-finite spaces, countably) many factors associatively.

---

# Categorical Definition

$(X_1\times X_2,\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2)$ is the **categorical product** in $\mathbf{Meas}$: it carries projections $Z_1,Z_2$, and for any measurable space $Y$ with measurable maps $f_i:Y\to X_i$ there is a *unique* measurable $f:Y\to X_1\times X_2$ with $Z_i\circ f=f_i$, namely $f=(f_1,f_2)$. The product $\sigma$-algebra is precisely the structure that makes this universal property hold — the *initial* $\sigma$-algebra for the projections, exactly as the product topology is initial for them.

---

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The product $\sigma$-algebra is the measure-theoretic analogue of the **product topology** — both are "the coarsest structure making the projections continuous/measurable" — and the compatibility $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^m)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})$ confirms the analogy. In probability it is the $\sigma$-algebra on the space of a *pair* of random variables; **independence** of $X_1,X_2$ is precisely the statement that their joint law is the [[Thm - Product Measure|product]] of the marginals on $\mathcal{A}_1\otimes\mathcal{A}_2$. The infinite product $\bigotimes_i\mathcal{A}_i$ is the $\sigma$-algebra of *path space*, on which stochastic processes live.

# Examples / Corollaries

$\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ — the Borel sets of the plane are the product of the Borel sets of the line. Every open set of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a countable union of open boxes, so $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^2)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$; conversely rectangles are Borel.

Caution: the *Lebesgue* $\sigma$-algebras do **not** multiply correctly — $\mathcal{B}^*(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}^*(\mathbb{R})\subsetneq\mathcal{B}^*(\mathbb{R}^2)$, because the product is not complete (a product of a null set with a non-measurable set need not be in the product $\sigma$-algebra). One completes after taking the product.

Corollary (slices): every slice of a product-measurable set is measurable — proved by the [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|π–λ]] argument, the foundation of [[Thm - Fubini-Tonelli Theorem|Fubini]].

Calibration: (i) Is $\{(x,y):x=y\}$ in $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$? Yes — the diagonal is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$, hence Borel. (ii) Is every measurable rectangle measurable? Yes, by definition of the generators. (iii) Do the rectangles form a $\sigma$-algebra? No — only a $\pi$-system; one must generate.
