# Problem Statement

Let $\mathcal{G}=\sigma(G_1,G_2,\dots)$ be generated by a countable partition $\Omega=\bigsqcup_n G_n$ into events with $\mathbb{P}(G_n)>0$, and $X\in L^1$.

**(a)** Show that the [[Def - Conditional Expectation|conditional expectation]] is the *block-average* random variable
$$\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]=\sum_n\frac{\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]}{\mathbb{P}(G_n)}\,\mathbf{1}_{G_n}.$$

**(b)** Confirm it is constant on each block, equal there to the elementary $\mathbb{E}[X\mid G_n]$.

**(c)** Recover, as the two extreme cases, $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\{\emptyset,\Omega\}]=\mathbb{E}[X]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{F}]=X$ (when $X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable, the partition into points).

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** verifying a *candidate formula* for conditional expectation against the two characterising properties.

**Assumption pattern:** the proposed $Y$ is built to be constant on each $G_n$ — so it is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable; one then checks the averaging identity on the generators $G_n$ and extends.

**Theorem routing:** $Y$ $\mathcal{G}$-measurable $+$ $\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_A]=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_A]$ for $A\in\mathcal{G}$ $\Rightarrow$ ([[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of Conditional Expectation|uniqueness]]) $Y=\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$.

---

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Guess the formula, verify the two properties.**
2. **Check the averaging identity on generators**, extend by $\sigma$-additivity.

---

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> Every $A\in\mathcal{G}$ is a union of blocks $G_n$. A $\mathcal{G}$-measurable function is constant on each block.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Check $\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]$ for the generators $G_m$; the candidate is designed so this is an identity.

---

# Solution

**Step 1 — (a),(b).** Let $Y=\sum_n c_n\mathbf{1}_{G_n}$ with $c_n=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]/\mathbb{P}(G_n)$ — note $c_n=\mathbb{E}[X\mid G_n]$, the elementary conditional expectation. $Y$ is constant ($=c_n$) on each block $G_n$, hence **$\mathcal{G}$-measurable** (its level sets are unions of blocks). It is integrable: $\mathbb{E}|Y|\le\sum_n|c_n|\mathbb{P}(G_n)\le\sum_n\mathbb{E}[|X|\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]=\mathbb{E}|X|<\infty$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Averaging identity.* For a generator $G_m$: $\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]=c_m\mathbb{P}(G_m)=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]$ — exactly by the definition of $c_m$. A general $A\in\mathcal{G}$ is a disjoint union $A=\bigsqcup_{n\in I}G_n$; by $\sigma$-additivity (or [[Thm - Monotone Convergence Theorem|MCT]] on partial sums), $\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_A]=\sum_{n\in I}\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]=\sum_{n\in I}\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_A]$.
> So $Y$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable, integrable, and satisfies the averaging identity — by [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of Conditional Expectation|uniqueness]], $Y=\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$. It is constant on $G_n$, equal to $\mathbb{E}[X\mid G_n]$ — the conditional expectation is literally the block-by-block elementary average.

**Step 2 — (c) Extremes.** *Trivial $\sigma$-algebra* $\{\emptyset,\Omega\}$: one block $G_1=\Omega$, so $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\{\emptyset,\Omega\}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_\Omega]}{\mathbb{P}(\Omega)}=\mathbb{E}[X]$ — no information, the global mean. *Discrete full $\sigma$-algebra*: if $\Omega$ is countable and $\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}=2^\Omega$, the blocks are singletons $\{\omega\}$, and $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{F}]=\sum_\omega\frac{X(\omega)\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\})}{\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\})}\mathbf{1}_{\{\omega\}}=X$ — total information, $X$ itself.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> (a),(b) $Y=\sum_n c_n\mathbf{1}_{G_n}$, $c_n=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_n}]/\mathbb{P}(G_n)$, is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable and integrable; $\mathbb{E}[Y\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]=c_m\mathbb{P}(G_m)=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{G_m}]$, extending by $\sigma$-additivity to all $A\in\mathcal{G}$; uniqueness gives $Y=\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$, constant $=\mathbb{E}[X\mid G_n]$ on $G_n$. (c) One block $\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}[X]$; singleton blocks $\Rightarrow X$. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**In the discrete case, conditional expectation is literally the block-average: constant on each piece of the conditioning partition, equal there to the elementary $\mathbb{E}[X\mid G_n]$.** This is the picture to *keep in mind* for the general definition — $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$ is "$X$ with the within-block randomness averaged out, the which-block randomness retained." The two extremes calibrate the notion: the trivial $\sigma$-algebra gives the constant mean (no information), the full one gives $X$ (total information), and every $\mathcal{G}$ interpolates.

**The proof is the universal method for conditional expectations — guess the formula, verify the two characterising properties, invoke [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of Conditional Expectation|uniqueness]].** One never computes $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$ from "the definition" directly; one *proposes* a $\mathcal{G}$-measurable candidate (here, dictated by the partition structure) and *checks* it has the right $\mathcal{G}$-integrals — on the generators, then extended. This "propose and verify" is exactly how the [[Ex - The Gaussian conditional expectation|Gaussian]] and [[Ex - Conditional expectation via the conditional density|density]] conditional expectations are computed too.
