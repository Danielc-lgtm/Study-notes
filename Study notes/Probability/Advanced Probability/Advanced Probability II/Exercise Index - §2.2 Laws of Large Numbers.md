## §2.2 Laws of Large Numbers — Exercises

The exercises of §2.2 drill the strong law and its applications. The elementary SLLN via fourth moments converts a moment bound into a summability condition for Borel-Cantelli; Monte Carlo integration realises integrals as sample means and uses the CLT to estimate convergence rates; and Borel's normal number theorem applies the SLLN to digit sequences to derive a number-theoretic almost-everywhere statement. The unifying observation: the SLLN converts statistical facts into deterministic almost-sure / almost-everywhere statements about long-run averages.

- [[Ex - The strong law via fourth moments]] (⭐⭐⭐) — the elementary SLLN: an $O(n^{-2})$ moment bound is summable, forcing a.s. convergence ([[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers]], [[Thm - Borel-Cantelli Lemmas]], [[Ex - Markov's inequality]])
- [[Ex - Monte Carlo integration]] (⭐⭐) — integrals as sample means; SLLN for consistency, CLT for the [[Def - Dimension|dimension]]-free $n^{-1/2}$ rate ([[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers]], [[Thm - Central Limit Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Borel's normal number theorem]] (⭐⭐) — almost every real is base-$2$ normal; a deterministic fact proved by the SLLN ([[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers]], [[Def - Lebesgue Measure]])
