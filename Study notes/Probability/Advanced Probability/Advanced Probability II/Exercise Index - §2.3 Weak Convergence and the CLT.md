## §2.3 Weak Convergence and the Central Limit Theorem — Exercises

The exercises of §2.3 drill weak convergence, characteristic functions, and the CLT. The Gaussian's characteristic function $e^{-t^2/2}$ is its own (essentially) Fourier transform, making it a fixed point under convolution; the de Moivre-Laplace CLT illustrates the characteristic-function proof in the simplest binomial setting; and Prokhorov's theorem ties weak convergence to tightness, with moment bounds providing the standard route to tightness via Markov's inequality. The unifying observation: characteristic functions linearise convolution, and weak convergence reduces to pointwise convergence of characteristic functions.

- [[Ex - Characteristic functions and moments]] (⭐⭐) — the Gaussian's $\varphi=e^{-t^2/2}$; moments as derivatives; sums of independent Gaussians ([[Def - Characteristic Function]], [[Ex - Differentiation under the integral sign]])
- [[Ex - The de Moivre-Laplace central limit theorem]] (⭐⭐) — the CLT for coin tosses; the characteristic-function proof in miniature ([[Thm - Central Limit Theorem]], [[Def - Characteristic Function]])
- [[Ex - Tightness and the escape of mass]] (⭐⭐) — weak limits fail only by escape; a moment bound gives tightness, hence subsequential limits ([[Def - Weak Convergence]], [[Thm - Prokhorov's Theorem]], [[Ex - Markov's inequality]])
