# Notation

$(X_n)_{n\ge1}$ independent [[Def - Random Variable|random variables]]; $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$; $\mathcal{T}_n=\sigma(X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\dots)$; the **tail $\sigma$-algebra** $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{n}\mathcal{T}_n$.

---

# Motivation

Some events depend on the *entire* infinite sequence $(X_n)$ but are unaffected by changing any *finite* number of the $X_n$ — for instance "$\sum X_n$ converges," "$\limsup X_n>5$," "$\frac1n\sum_{k\le n}X_k\to0$." These are the **tail events**. Kolmogorov's 0–1 law makes a startling assertion: for an *independent* sequence, **every tail event has probability $0$ or $1$**. There is no genuine uncertainty in the tail — asymptotic behaviour is deterministic, even though the individual $X_n$ are random. This is why the [[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers|strong law]] *converges to a constant*, why a random series either converges a.s. or diverges a.s., and why "$A_n$ infinitely often" was bound to be $0$ or $1$ in [[Thm - Borel-Cantelli Lemmas|Borel–Cantelli]].

---

# Sources and Targets

**Sources.** Hypothesis: $(X_n)$ [[Def - Independence|independent]]. The key recognition is *which events are tail events* — those measurable with respect to $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_n\sigma(X_{n+1},\dots)$, equivalently unchanged by altering finitely many coordinates: convergence of a series, of Cesàro averages, $\limsup/\liminf$ of $X_n$, $\{A_n\text{ i.o.}\}$ for $A_n\in\sigma(X_n)$.

**Targets.** "$\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$ for tail $A$" yields: any tail *random variable* is a.s. constant (its distribution function jumps from $0$ to $1$) — so $\limsup_n X_n$, $\limsup\frac{S_n}{n}$, the radius of convergence of $\sum X_n z^n$ are all a.s. constant. This is what makes the limit in the [[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers|SLLN]] a genuine number, and it tells us in advance that a [[Thm - Borel-Cantelli Lemmas|Borel–Cantelli]] $\limsup$ is $0$ or $1$ before computing which.

---

# Statement

Let $(X_n)_{n\ge1}$ be independent random variables and $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_{n\ge1}\sigma(X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\dots)$ the **tail $\sigma$-algebra**. Then $\mathcal{T}$ is **trivial**:
$$\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}\qquad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal{T}.$$
Consequently any $\mathcal{T}$-measurable random variable is almost surely constant.

---

# Why Is It True

The proof's punchline is paradoxical and exact: **a tail event is independent of itself**, and an event independent of itself has probability $0$ or $1$ (from $\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A\cap A)=\mathbb{P}(A)^2$).

Why is a tail event $A\in\mathcal{T}$ independent of itself? In three moves.

*Move 1 — $A$ is independent of every finite past.* $A\in\mathcal{T}\subseteq\sigma(X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\dots)$, while the past $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is generated by $X_1,\dots,X_n$. Since *all* the $X_k$ are independent, the past block $X_1,\dots,X_n$ is independent of the future block $X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\dots$ — hence $\mathcal{F}_n$ is independent of $\sigma(X_{n+1},\dots)\supseteq\mathcal{T}$. So $A$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n$ for *every* $n$.

*Move 2 — $A$ is independent of the whole past.* The events $\bigcup_n\mathcal{F}_n$ form a [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|π-system]] (the $\mathcal{F}_n$ increase, so any two events lie in a common $\mathcal{F}_n$) generating $\mathcal{F}_\infty=\sigma(X_1,X_2,\dots)$. "$A$ independent of each $\mathcal{F}_n$" means $A$ is independent of this $\pi$-system; by [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|Dynkin's lemma]], $A$ is independent of the generated $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}_\infty$.

*Move 3 — $A$ is independent of itself.* But $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\sigma(X_1,X_2,\dots)=\mathcal{F}_\infty$, so $A\in\mathcal{F}_\infty$. Thus $A$ is independent of a $\sigma$-algebra *containing $A$* — in particular independent of $A$. Hence $\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A\cap A)=\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(A)$, forcing $\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$.

The slogan: **the tail lies in the future, hence is independent of every finite past; the finite pasts $\pi$-generate the whole space, so the tail is independent of everything — including itself.** The $\pi$–$\lambda$ theorem is the bridge from "independent of each finite past" to "independent of all of $\mathcal{F}_\infty$."

---

# What Makes This Hard

The argument is short but every step is a small surprise. (i) "$A$ independent of every finite past" — easy, by blockwise independence. (ii) The leap to "independent of the *whole* past $\mathcal{F}_\infty$" needs the [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|π–λ theorem]] — one cannot just say "take $n\to\infty$"; the $\pi$-system $\bigcup_n\mathcal{F}_n$ must be identified and Dynkin invoked. (iii) The self-independence punchline relies on the *inclusion* $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_\infty$ — the tail is part of the past it is independent of. Missing any of the three, or trying to argue without $\pi$–$\lambda$, is where people stall.

---

# Rederivation Scaffold

**High-level strategy.** Show the tail $A$ is independent of every finite past $\mathcal{F}_n$; upgrade to independence of $\mathcal{F}_\infty$ via $\pi$–$\lambda$; note $A\in\mathcal{F}_\infty$, so $A$ is independent of itself, forcing $\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$.

**Subgoal decomposition.**

1. **Tail $\perp$ finite past.** $A\in\mathcal{T}\subseteq\sigma(X_{n+1},\dots)$, independent of $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ by blockwise independence.
2. **Tail $\perp$ whole past.** $\bigcup_n\mathcal{F}_n$ is a $\pi$-system generating $\mathcal{F}_\infty$; [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|Dynkin]] $\Rightarrow A$ independent of $\mathcal{F}_\infty$.
3. **Self-independence.** $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_\infty$, so $A\in\mathcal{F}_\infty$; $A$ independent of $A$ $\Rightarrow\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A)^2\Rightarrow\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$.

---

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: The tail is independent of every finite past
> **Statement:** For each $n$, $\mathcal{T}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Independence of the whole family $(X_k)$ implies the block $(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is independent of the block $(X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\dots)$ — checked on the generating $\pi$-systems of rays via [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|Dynkin]]. Hence $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is independent of $\sigma(X_{n+1},\dots)$, and $\mathcal{T}=\bigcap_m\sigma(X_{m+1},\dots)\subseteq\sigma(X_{n+1},\dots)$, so $\mathcal{T}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n$. $\square$

> [!note]- Lemma 2: The tail is independent of itself
> **Statement:** Every $A\in\mathcal{T}$ satisfies $\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > By Lemma 1, $\mathcal{T}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n$ for all $n$. The union $\bigcup_n\mathcal{F}_n$ is a $\pi$-system (the $\mathcal{F}_n$ increase) generating $\mathcal{F}_\infty=\sigma(X_1,X_2,\dots)$; "$\mathcal{T}$ independent of each $\mathcal{F}_n$" says $\mathcal{T}$ is independent of this $\pi$-system, so by [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem|Dynkin's lemma]] $\mathcal{T}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_\infty$. But $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_\infty$. So any $A\in\mathcal{T}$, being also in $\mathcal{F}_\infty$, is independent of itself: $\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A\cap A)=\mathbb{P}(A)^2$, whence $\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$. $\square$

---

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Lemmas 1–2 give $\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}$ for all $A\in\mathcal{T}$. If $Z$ is $\mathcal{T}$-measurable, then $\{Z\le t\}\in\mathcal{T}$ has probability $0$ or $1$ for each $t$, so the distribution function of $Z$ is a $\{0,1\}$-valued non-decreasing right-continuous function — a single jump — i.e. $Z$ is a.s. constant. $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

The 0–1 law guarantees, *before any computation*, that for i.i.d. $X_n$ the limit $\lim_n\frac1n\sum_{k\le n}X_k$ (if it exists) is a.s. *constant* — which is half of why the [[Thm - Strong Law of Large Numbers|strong law]] can even be stated. It shows a random power series $\sum X_n z^n$ has an a.s.-constant radius of convergence, and that $\sum X_n$ either converges a.s. or diverges a.s. — never "sometimes." The Hewitt–Savage 0–1 law extends it to *exchangeable* events.
