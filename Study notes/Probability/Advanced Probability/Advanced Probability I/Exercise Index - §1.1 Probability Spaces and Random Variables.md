## §1.1 Probability Spaces, Random Variables, Expectation — Exercises

The exercises of §1.1 drill the foundational language of probability: random variables, laws, and expectations. The "expectation depends only on the law" identity converts every probabilistic computation into an integral against the push-forward measure; the quantile transform realises every distribution as a function of one uniform random variable (inverse-transform sampling); and linearity of expectation, requiring no independence, makes the indicator method one of the most-used tools for combinatorial counting via expectations.

- [[Ex - Expectation via the law]] (⭐⭐) — $\mathbb{E}[h(X)]=\int h\,d\mu_X$; everything depends only on the law ([[Def - Random Variable]], [[Def - Expectation and Moments]], [[Thm - Monotone Convergence Theorem]])
- [[Ex - The quantile transform]] (⭐⭐) — every law is a function of one uniform variable; inverse-transform sampling ([[Def - Distribution Function]], [[Def - Lebesgue Measure]], [[Def - Random Variable]])
- [[Ex - Linearity of expectation and the indicator method]] (⭐⭐) — linearity needs no independence; the indicator method; variance is not additive ([[Def - Expectation and Moments]], [[Thm - Properties of the Integral]], [[Def - Independence]])
