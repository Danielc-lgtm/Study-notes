## §4.3 Martingale Convergence Theorems — Exercises

The exercises of §4.3 drill the two martingale convergence theorems — a.s. convergence (under $L^1$-boundedness) and the $L^1$ upgrade (under uniform integrability). Each exercise lives at a calibration point: the product martingale converges a.s. but not in $L^1$ (showing the gap between the two theorems), Pólya's urn converges in $L^1$ to a *random* limit (showing convergence need not mean a deterministic value), and Lévy's upward theorem characterises closed martingales as those that converge to the conditional expectation given the limit $\sigma$-algebra.

- [[Ex - A martingale converging a.s. but not in L1]] (⭐⭐) — the product martingale: a.s. limit $0$ while $\mathbb{E}[X_n]\equiv1$; uniform integrability fails ([[Thm - Almost Sure Martingale Convergence]], [[Thm - Lp and L1 Martingale Convergence]], [[Def - Uniform Integrability]])
- [[Ex - Polya's urn]] (⭐⭐⭐) — a bounded martingale converging a.s. and in $L^1$ to a *random* (uniform) limit ([[Def - Martingale]], [[Thm - Almost Sure Martingale Convergence]], [[Thm - Lp and L1 Martingale Convergence]])
- [[Ex - Closed martingales and Levy's 0-1 law]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Lévy's upward theorem and 0–1 law; the Kolmogorov 0–1 law as a corollary ([[Thm - Lp and L1 Martingale Convergence]], [[Def - Conditional Expectation]], [[Thm - Dynkin's π-λ Theorem]])
