# Notation

$(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ a [[Def - Probability Space|probability space]]; $(\mathcal{F}_n)_{n\ge0}$ a filtration; $(X_n)$ a stochastic process.

---

# Axiom Motivation

To model a process *evolving in time* one needs to formalise "the information available at time $n$." A sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}$ already represents [[Def - Conditional Expectation|a state of partial information]]; a **filtration** is a *time-indexed family* of these, $\mathcal{F}_0\subseteq\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq\mathcal{F}_2\subseteq\cdots$, the chain *increasing* because information, once acquired, is never lost — at time $n$ one knows everything one knew at time $n-1$, and more.

The filtration is the stage on which all of dynamic probability is set. Against it, two compatibility notions for a process $(X_n)$ arise. **Adaptedness** — $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable — says "$X_n$ is observable by time $n$": its value is known once the time-$n$ information is in. This is the minimal sanity condition; a process that revealed future information would be unphysical. The **natural filtration** $\mathcal{F}_n^X=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is the smallest filtration making $(X_n)$ adapted — "exactly the information generated by observing the process itself." A general filtration may carry *more* (side information, an independent source).

The filtration is what gives [[Def - Martingale|martingales]], [[Def - Stopping Time|stopping times]], and the [[Thm - Optional Stopping Theorem|optional stopping theorem]] their meaning: a martingale is "fair given the filtration," a stopping time is "a decision using only past information," and the whole theory is the calculus of [[Def - Conditional Expectation|conditional expectation]] indexed by this growing information.

---

# The Definition

A **filtration** on $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ is an increasing sequence of sub-$\sigma$-algebras
$$\mathcal{F}_0\subseteq\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq\mathcal{F}_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F},$$
with $\mathcal{F}_\infty:=\sigma\big(\bigcup_n\mathcal{F}_n\big)$. The quadruple $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_n),\mathbb{P})$ is a **filtered probability space**.

A **stochastic process** (discrete time) is a sequence $(X_n)_{n\ge0}$ of [[Def - Random Variable|random variables]]. It is:
- **adapted** (to $(\mathcal{F}_n)$) if $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable for every $n$;
- **integrable** if $X_n\in L^1$ for every $n$;
- **predictable** if $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable (known one step early).

The **natural filtration** of $(X_n)$ is $\mathcal{F}_n^X=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ — the smallest filtration to which $(X_n)$ is adapted.

---

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

A filtration is the formalisation of *accumulating information* — a "resolution of knowledge that sharpens with time." In information theory each $\mathcal{F}_n$ is a finer partition of $\Omega$; in dynamical systems it is the pullback of a refining sequence of observations. *Adaptedness* is the non-anticipation / causality condition — "no peeking at the future" — central to the theory of [[Def - Martingale|martingales]] and to the Itô integral in continuous time. *Predictability* is the slightly stronger "known an instant in advance," the right notion for the integrand in a discrete stochastic integral (martingale transform).

# Examples / Corollaries

**Random walk.** $S_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n$ with the natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$: $(S_n)$ is adapted.

**A larger filtration.** If $\mathcal{F}_n=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n,Z)$ for some extra variable $Z$, then $(S_n)$ is still adapted, but the filtration carries side information $Z$ from the start — adaptedness is relative to the chosen filtration.

**Predictable.** A betting strategy $C_n$ that decides the stake for round $n$ using only rounds $1,\dots,n-1$ is $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable — predictable.

Calibration: (i) Must a filtration increase? Yes — information is not lost. (ii) Is every process adapted to *some* filtration? Yes — its natural one. (iii) Is the natural filtration the largest making $(X_n)$ adapted? No — the *smallest*; one can always adjoin more.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Martingales and stopping times
> A [[Def - Martingale|martingale]] is an adapted integrable process with $\mathbb{E}[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_n]=X_n$; a [[Def - Stopping Time|stopping time]] is a random time decidable from the filtration. Both are meaningless without a fixed filtration.
