## §3.1 Jordan Measure and the Riemann Integral — Exercises

The exercises of §3.1 drill Jordan measurability and Riemann integrability. The disk is Jordan-measurable via the content-zero boundary criterion; the rational points of the cube are *not* Jordan-measurable (upper content $1$, lower content $0$) — calibrating that Jordan measure lacks countable additivity; and the Lebesgue criterion for Riemann integrability is "discontinuity set has outer Lebesgue measure zero." The unifying observation: Riemann integration is enough for nice (content-zero-boundary) regions but breaks for the wild sets that motivate Lebesgue measure.

- [[Ex - A Jordan-measurable region]] (⭐) — certify that the closed unit disk is a legitimate domain of integration by recognizing its boundary circle as the union of two graphs of continuous functions, each content zero, and invoking the boundary characterization of Jordan measurability ([[Def - Jordan Measure]], [[Def - The Riemann Integral in Several Variables]], [[Thm - The Lebesgue Criterion for Riemann Integrability]]).

- [[Ex - A bounded set that is not Jordan measurable]] (⭐⭐) — exhibit the rational points of the unit cube as a bounded set with upper content $1$ and lower content $0$, driven by density versus empty interior, and use that it is a countable union of measure-zero singletons to show Jordan measure fails countable additivity ([[Def - Jordan Measure]], [[Def - The Riemann Integral in Several Variables]]).

- [[Ex - Integrability of a function with a discontinuity set]] (⭐⭐) — decide Riemann integrability for three functions by locating and measuring their discontinuity sets — a content-zero boundary curve (integrable), a dense everywhere-discontinuity set (not integrable), and a countable union of measure-zero lines (integrable) — illustrating why the criterion needs outer measure rather than content ([[Thm - The Lebesgue Criterion for Riemann Integrability]], [[Def - The Riemann Integral in Several Variables]], [[Def - Jordan Measure]]).
