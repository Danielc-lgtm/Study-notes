## §17 The Baire Category Theorem — Exercises

The exercises of §17 drill the Baire category theorem and its applications to existence proofs. Baire-generic ("residual") sets are dense $G_\delta$ subsets; their complements are meager. Each exercise practices a different genericity statement: $\mathbb{Q}$ is meager so irrationals are non-meager (existence of irrationals without cardinality); the nowhere differentiable functions are residual in $C[0,1]$ (existence of exotic continuous functions); and pointwise limits of continuous functions are continuous on a residual set (the discontinuity structure of Baire class 1 functions). The unifying recipe: write the bad set as a countable union of nowhere-dense pieces, then Baire forces the complement to be dense.

- [[Ex - The rationals are first category in R]] (⭐) — Show $\mathbb{Q}$ is meager (countable union of nowhere dense singletons) and apply Baire to conclude the irrationals are non-meager ([[Def - Nowhere Dense and Meager]], [[Thm - Baire Category Theorem]], [[Def - Cauchy Sequence and Complete Metric Space]]).

- [[Ex - A continuous nowhere differentiable function exists]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Use Baire to show the nowhere differentiable functions are residual in $C[0, 1]$, via density of zig-zag approximations ([[Thm - Baire Category Theorem]], [[Def - Nowhere Dense and Meager]], [[Def - Cauchy Sequence and Complete Metric Space]]).

- [[Ex - Pointwise limit of continuous functions has dense continuity set]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Show a pointwise limit of continuous functions on a complete metric space is continuous on a residual subset, via the uniform-convergence-decomposition of the discontinuity set ([[Thm - Baire Category Theorem]], [[Def - Nowhere Dense and Meager]], [[Def - Continuous Map]], [[Def - Cauchy Sequence and Complete Metric Space]]).
