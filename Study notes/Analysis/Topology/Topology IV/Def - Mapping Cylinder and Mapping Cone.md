# Notation

$X, Y$ are topological spaces, $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous map, $I = [0, 1]$ is the unit interval. $X \sqcup Y$ is the disjoint union with the standard topology (a set is open iff its intersection with each piece is). $M_f$ is the mapping cylinder, $C_f$ the mapping cone, $CX = X \times I / (X \times \{1\})$ the cone on $X$. The full registry is on the topic page.

---

# Axiom Motivation

In homotopy theory one wants to replace an arbitrary continuous map $f : X \to Y$ by an *inclusion* — a map that embeds the source as a subspace. The reason is structural: many theorems (homotopy extension, fibration lifting, cofibrations) are stated for inclusions, not for arbitrary maps, and would be much weaker if they applied only to maps that *happen* to be inclusions. Mapping cylinders solve this problem in a single move.

The construction is forced if we ask two things. First, we need a slightly larger space $M_f$ containing $X$ as a subspace and homotopy-equivalent to $Y$, with the inclusion $X \hookrightarrow M_f$ playing the role of $f$. Second, the construction should be functorial and well-defined up to homotopy. The recipe: take $X \times I$ — a cylinder over $X$ — and glue the top end $X \times \{0\}$ to $Y$ along $f$, leaving the bottom $X \times \{1\} \cong X$ as a copy of $X$ sitting in $M_f$. Pictorially, $M_f$ is the space obtained by attaching a cylinder to $Y$ with the attaching map $f$. The bottom $X \times \{1\}$ is a closed subspace; the top $X \times \{0\}$ has been *identified* with $f(X) \subseteq Y$. The retraction $r : M_f \to Y$ that slides everything down the cylinder is a strong deformation retract, so $M_f \simeq Y$. And $f$ has been replaced by the inclusion $X \cong X \times \{1\} \hookrightarrow M_f$.

The **mapping cone** $C_f = M_f / (X \times \{1\})$ collapses the "free" end of the cylinder — the copy of $X$ — to a single point. This gives a homotopy-theoretic measure of how nontrivial $f$ is: $C_f$ is contractible if and only if $f$ extends to a continuous map from $CX$ into $Y$, i.e., $f$ is *null-homotopic*. So the topology of $C_f$ records the homotopy class of $f$.

Why glue at $X \times \{0\}$ and not $X \times \{1\}$? Convention. Bredon glues at the bottom so the top is the free copy of $X$; some texts (e.g. Hatcher) glue at the top. The choice is cosmetic — the resulting spaces are homeomorphic by flipping $t \to 1 - t$.

# The Definition

**Mapping cylinder.** Let $f : X \to Y$ be a continuous map. The **mapping cylinder** is the quotient space
$$M_f := (X \times I) \sqcup Y \, \big/ \, \sim$$
where the equivalence relation is generated by $(x, 0) \sim f(x)$ for each $x \in X$. We equip $M_f$ with the quotient topology induced by the projection $(X \times I) \sqcup Y \twoheadrightarrow M_f$.

There is a canonical inclusion $j : X \hookrightarrow M_f$ given by $j(x) = [(x, 1)]$, embedding $X$ as a closed subspace via the "free" end of the cylinder. There is a canonical retraction $r : M_f \to Y$ given by $r[(x, t)] = f(x)$ and $r[y] = y$; the original map $f$ factors as $f = r \circ j$.

**Mapping cone.** The **mapping cone** of $f$ is the further quotient
$$C_f := M_f / (X \times \{1\}),$$
collapsing the free end of the cylinder to a single point (the **cone point**).

**Cone on a space.** The **cone** on $X$ is $CX := (X \times I) / (X \times \{1\})$. Equivalently, $CX = C_{1_X}$ — the mapping cone of the identity.

---

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The mapping cylinder is the **homotopy pushout** of the diagram $Y \xleftarrow{f} X \xrightarrow{1} X$ in the category of topological spaces — the canonical replacement of a non-cofibration $f$ by a cofibration $j$. It is the universal way to make $f$ "act like an inclusion" without changing its homotopy class. Dually, the mapping cocylinder (path space) replaces a non-fibration by a fibration.

In categorical language, the mapping cone is the cofibre of $f$ in the topological model structure: every map $f$ extends to a cofibre sequence $X \xrightarrow{f} Y \to C_f \to \Sigma X \to \cdots$, where $\Sigma X = CX / X$ is the **suspension**. This is the Puppe sequence and is the topological precursor of long exact sequences in homology.

# Examples and Corollaries

**Is an instance — the cone $CX$.** Take $Y$ a point and $f : X \to *$ the unique map. Then $M_f = (X \times I) \sqcup * / (x, 0) \sim *$ is just $X \times I$ with one endpoint collapsed — the cone $CX$.

**Is an instance — the suspension.** The suspension $\Sigma X = CX \cup_X CX$ (two cones glued along their bases) is homotopy equivalent to $C_f / *$ for $f : X \to *$. For $X = S^n$, $\Sigma S^n = S^{n+1}$.

**Is an instance — the mapping cylinder of a homotopy equivalence.** If $f$ is a homotopy equivalence, then $M_f$ is homotopy equivalent to $Y$ in two ways: by the retraction $r$, and also via the homotopy inverse. The two homotopy equivalences are themselves homotopic.

**Is NOT an instance of a mapping cylinder — a generic gluing.** Not every adjunction space is a mapping cylinder. The torus $T^2$ as a quotient of $I^2$ is an [[Def - Adjunction Space|adjunction space]] but cannot be written as the cylinder of any single map.

**Corollary — $M_f$ deformation retracts to $Y$.** The retraction $r : M_f \to Y$ has the explicit deformation $H : M_f \times I \to M_f$ given by $H([(x, t)], s) = [(x, (1-s)t)]$, $H([y], s) = [y]$. At $s = 0$, $H$ is the identity; at $s = 1$, every cylinder point has been slid down to $Y$. The points of $Y$ are fixed throughout, so $r$ is a **strong** deformation retract. This is [[Thm - Mapping Cylinder is Deformation Retract of Target]].

**Corollary — $f$ factors as inclusion followed by homotopy equivalence.** The factorization $f = r \circ j$ writes any $f : X \to Y$ as the composition $X \xhookrightarrow{j} M_f \xrightarrow{r} Y$ where $j$ is an inclusion of a closed subspace and $r$ is a strong deformation retraction. This is the homotopy-theoretic factorization.

**Corollary — $C_f$ is contractible iff $f$ is null-homotopic.** If $f \simeq c_{y_0}$ (constant), the homotopy extends to a contraction of $C_f$. Conversely, if $C_f$ is contractible, the inclusion $X \hookrightarrow C_f$ is null-homotopic, and composing with the cone-point retraction shows $f$ itself is.

**Calibration check.** Verify: $M_{1_X} \cong X \times I$ (mapping cylinder of identity); $C_{1_X} = CX$ (cone of identity is the cone on $X$); for $f : S^1 \to S^1$ the squaring map $z \mapsto z^2$, $C_f$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}P^2$ (this is a problem in §14). If these check out, you have understood the construction.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Cofibration *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> A **cofibration** is an inclusion with the homotopy extension property — a homotopy on the subspace can always be extended to the ambient space. The inclusion $X \hookrightarrow M_f$ is the universal example: every map $f$ becomes a cofibration after replacing $Y$ by $M_f$.

> [!tip] Puppe Sequence *(from Stable Homotopy Theory)*
> Iterating the cofibre construction $f \mapsto C_f$ gives the **Puppe sequence** $X \to Y \to C_f \to \Sigma X \to \Sigma Y \to \Sigma C_f \to \cdots$, an infinite sequence of spaces and maps whose application of any cohomology theory yields a long exact sequence.
