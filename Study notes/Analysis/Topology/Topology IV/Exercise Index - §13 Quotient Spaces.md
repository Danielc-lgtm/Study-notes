## §13 Quotient Spaces — Exercises

The exercises of §13 drill the "construct + descend + upgrade" recipe for identifying quotient spaces with known objects. The torus and projective plane illustrate how multiple definitions of the same space (as quotient of $\mathbb{R}^n$, of a fundamental polygon, as a product, or as an embedded submanifold) are reconciled via universal property + compact-to-Hausdorff. The pathological $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ shows that quotients can destroy *all* topological structure when equivalence classes are dense — a cautionary calibration for "well-behaved" quotients.

- [[Ex - The torus has four definitions, all equivalent]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Identify $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2 \cong [0,1]^2/\sim \cong S^1 \times S^1 \cong$ anchor ring via the descend-and-upgrade pattern ([[Def - Quotient Topology and Identification Map]], [[Thm - Universal Property of the Quotient]], [[Thm - Compact to Hausdorff Continuous Bijection is Homeomorphism]], [[Def - Product Topology]]).

- [[Ex - Projective plane as a quotient of disk and sphere]] (⭐⭐) — Identify $\mathbb{R}P^2 = S^2/\{\pm x\} \cong D^2/\{u \sim -u \text{ on } S^1\}$ via the upper-hemisphere embedding ([[Def - Quotient Topology and Identification Map]], [[Thm - Universal Property of the Quotient]], [[Thm - Compact to Hausdorff Continuous Bijection is Homeomorphism]]).

- [[Ex - A quotient with trivial topology]] (⭐⭐) — Show $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ has uncountably many points but the indiscrete topology, via density of $\mathbb{Q}$-cosets ([[Def - Quotient Topology and Identification Map]], [[Def - Topological Space]]).
