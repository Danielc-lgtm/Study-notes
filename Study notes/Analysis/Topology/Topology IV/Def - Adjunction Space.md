# Notation

$X, Y$ are topological spaces, $A \subseteq X$ a closed subset, $f : A \to Y$ a continuous map (the **attaching map**). $X \sqcup Y$ is the disjoint union. The adjunction space is denoted $Y \cup_f X$ (Bredon's notation) — read as "$X$ attached to $Y$ along $f$". The full registry is on the topic page.

---

# Axiom Motivation

The adjunction space is the formal version of *pasting* one space onto another along a designated subset. We have $X$ (the piece we want to attach), $Y$ (the base), $A \subseteq X$ (the part of $X$ that gets identified), and $f : A \to Y$ (the gluing recipe — which point of $Y$ does each $a \in A$ get glued to). The result $Y \cup_f X$ is the new space: $X$ and $Y$ disjoint, then identified along the relation $a \sim f(a)$.

Why is this the right construction? Two reasons. First, *closed* attachment matters: the closure of $A$ in $X$ should match the closure of $f(A)$ in $Y$, so that the resulting space has a well-defined topology consistent with both pieces. Requiring $A$ closed in $X$ guarantees that $Y$ embeds as a closed subspace of $Y \cup_f X$ and $X \setminus A$ embeds as an open subspace — the topological pieces inherit their original topologies. Second, the universal property: any pair of compatible continuous maps $g_X : X \to Z$ and $g_Y : Y \to Z$ (compatible meaning $g_X|_A = g_Y \circ f$) extends uniquely to a continuous map $Y \cup_f X \to Z$. This makes the adjunction space the **pushout** of the diagram $X \xleftarrow{i} A \xrightarrow{f} Y$ where $i$ is the inclusion.

If we *weaken* by dropping closedness of $A$, we lose the clean topological inheritance: the resulting space might collapse $Y$ in unintended ways. If we *strengthen* by requiring more (say, $A$ compact or $f$ a homeomorphism onto its image), we exclude the most important application: building CW complexes, where the attaching map of an $n$-cell is the map $S^{n-1} \to X^{(n-1)}$ from the boundary of $D^n$ to the lower skeleton, and is generally neither injective nor a homeomorphism.

The closed-attaching axiom is what makes the iterated construction work: a CW complex is built one dimension at a time by attaching cells via boundary maps, and at each step $A = S^{n-1}$ is closed in $X = D^n$. The mapping cylinder $M_f$ is also a special case: take $X = X_{\text{old}} \times I$, $A = X_{\text{old}} \times \{0\}$, attach to $Y$ via $f$.

# The Definition

Let $X, Y$ be topological spaces, $A \subseteq X$ a **closed** subspace, $f : A \to Y$ a continuous map. The **adjunction space** $Y \cup_f X$ is the quotient
$$Y \cup_f X := (X \sqcup Y) \, / \, \sim$$
where the equivalence relation is generated by $a \sim f(a)$ for each $a \in A$. Equivalently, for points $u, v \in X \sqcup Y$, $u \sim v$ iff one of the following holds:

1. $u = v$;
2. $u, v \in A$ and $f(u) = f(v)$;
3. $u \in A$ and $v = f(u) \in Y$ (or symmetrically with roles swapped).

The space carries the quotient topology induced by the projection $X \sqcup Y \twoheadrightarrow Y \cup_f X$.

**Embedding properties** (Proposition 13.14 in Bredon):

- The canonical map $Y \hookrightarrow Y \cup_f X$ is an embedding onto a closed subspace.
- The canonical map $X \setminus A \hookrightarrow Y \cup_f X$ is an embedding onto an open subspace.

**Universal property.** A function $g : Y \cup_f X \to Z$ is continuous if and only if $g|_X : X \to Z$ and $g|_Y : Y \to Z$ are both continuous and agree on the identification: $g|_X(a) = g|_Y(f(a))$ for every $a \in A$.

---

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The adjunction space is the **pushout** in the category of topological spaces of the diagram $X \xleftarrow{i} A \xrightarrow{f} Y$. It is dual to the **pullback** (fibre product) and is the categorical mechanism for gluing along a shared boundary.

In algebra, the analogous construction is the **amalgamated free product**: groups $G$ and $H$ glued along a common subgroup, with $G *_K H$ the amalgamation. Both constructions solve the same universal problem in their respective categories.

In differential geometry, gluing manifolds with boundary along a common boundary diffeomorphism is an adjunction; the resulting space is a closed manifold (one whose boundary has become interior).

# Examples and Corollaries

**Is an instance — the mapping cylinder.** Take $X = X_{\text{old}} \times I$, $A = X_{\text{old}} \times \{0\}$, $f : A \to Y$ the original map. Then $Y \cup_f X = M_f$. The mapping cylinder is an adjunction space par excellence.

**Is an instance — collapsing a subspace.** Take $Y = \{*\}$ a point and $f : A \to *$ the unique map. Then $Y \cup_f X = X/A$. Collapsing $A$ to a point is the special case "$Y$ is a point".

**Is an instance — attaching an $n$-cell.** Given a space $X_{\text{old}}$ and a map $\phi : S^{n-1} \to X_{\text{old}}$, the space $X_{\text{old}} \cup_\phi D^n$ is the result of attaching an $n$-cell. Iterating: a **CW complex** of dimension $n$ is built from a CW complex of dimension $n-1$ by attaching a disjoint union of $n$-cells.

**Is an instance — the projective plane.** $\mathbb{R}P^2 = D^2 \cup_\phi *$ where $\phi : S^1 \to S^1 \to *$ is *not* the standard one — instead, $\mathbb{R}P^2 = * \cup_\phi D^2$ where $\phi : S^1 \to *$ is constant and we identify pairs of antipodes first. Equivalently, $\mathbb{R}P^2$ is the result of attaching a 2-cell to $\mathbb{R}P^1 = S^1$ via the degree-2 map $z \mapsto z^2$ on $S^1$ — see [[Ex - Projective plane as a quotient of disk and sphere]].

**Is NOT an instance — attaching along a non-closed subset.** If $A$ is not closed, the construction is still defined as a set, but the topology degenerates: $Y$ no longer embeds as a closed subspace, and the universal property may fail. Bredon's definition explicitly requires closedness; many failures of intuition about adjunction stem from accidentally working with a non-closed $A$.

**Corollary — adjunction preserves normality.** If $X, Y$ are normal and $A$ is closed, then $Y \cup_f X$ is normal (Bredon, §13 Problem 2). The proof uses Urysohn's lemma on both pieces.

**Corollary — adjunction preserves Hausdorffness for nice $f$.** If $X, Y$ are Hausdorff and $f$ is a closed map, then $Y \cup_f X$ is Hausdorff. The condition rules out pathological gluings that bring distinct points arbitrarily close.

**Calibration check.** Verify: the torus $T^2$ is $S^1 \vee S^1 \cup_\phi D^2$ where $\phi : S^1 \to S^1 \vee S^1$ is the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Verify the Klein bottle is similarly an adjunction of $D^2$ to $S^1 \vee S^1$ via $abab^{-1}$. Verify the disk attachment $D^n \cup_{1_{S^{n-1}}} D^n$ along the identity of the boundary gives $S^n$.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] CW Complex *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> A **CW complex** is a space built by inductively attaching cells of increasing dimension via adjunction. The combinatorial data (cells, attaching maps) determines the homotopy type, and the algebraic invariants — fundamental group, homology, cohomology — can be computed directly from this data via cellular techniques.

> [!tip] Pushout *(from Category Theory)*
> The adjunction is the topological realization of the **pushout** square — a colimit construction available in any category with the right structure. The pushout in $\mathbf{Set}$ glues sets along a common subset; in $\mathbf{Grp}$ it produces amalgamated free products; in $\mathbf{Top}$ it produces adjunction spaces. The universal property is identical in each setting.
