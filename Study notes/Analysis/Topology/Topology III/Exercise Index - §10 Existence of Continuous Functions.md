## §10 Existence of Continuous Functions — Exercises

The exercises of §10 drill Urysohn's lemma and the Tietze extension theorem, the principal tools for constructing continuous separators and extending continuous functions on normal spaces. Metric spaces give an explicit Urysohn formula via the distance ratio; Tietze for vector-valued functions reduces to scalar Tietze coordinate-by-coordinate; and the Tychonoff plank witnesses that *without* normality, Tietze (and even Urysohn) genuinely fail. The unifying observation: normality is *exactly* the separation axiom that makes continuous functions abundant enough to separate disjoint closed sets.

- [[Ex - Urysohn's lemma in a metric space]] (⭐⭐) — explicit Urysohn function $f(x) = d(x, F)/(d(x, F) + d(x, G))$ for disjoint closed $F, G$ in a metric space ([[Thm - Urysohn's Lemma]], [[Def - Metric Space]])
- [[Ex - Tietze extension for vector-valued functions]] (⭐⭐) — reduce coordinate-by-coordinate to scalar Tietze, plus range-preservation in closed convex targets via metric projection ([[Thm - Tietze Extension Theorem]], [[Def - Product Topology]])
- [[Ex - Failure of Tietze without normality]] (⭐⭐⭐) — the Tychonoff plank as a Hausdorff non-normal space, with the ordinal pressing-down argument showing no continuous separator exists ([[Thm - Tietze Extension Theorem]], [[Thm - Urysohn's Lemma]], [[Def - Separation Axioms|Def - Hausdorff Space]])
