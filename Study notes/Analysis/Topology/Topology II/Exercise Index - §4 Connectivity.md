## §4 Connectivity — Exercises

The exercises of §4 drill the connectedness/path-connectedness distinction and the standard tools for verifying each. Direct supremum arguments establish $[0, 1]$ is connected (the order-completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ being the active hypothesis); the topologist's sine curve calibrates connected-but-not-path-connected; local path-connectedness emerges as the gap-bridging hypothesis. The unifying observation: connected components and path-components agree exactly when the local structure is rich enough to support continuous path construction.

- [[Ex - The interval [0,1] is connected]] (⭐⭐) — Direct proof from the open-cover definition using the supremum technique; the order-completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ converts the negation of connectedness into a contradictory critical point. ([[Def - Connected Space]], [[Def - Topological Space]], [[Def - Continuous Map]])

- [[Ex - The topologist's sine curve]] (⭐⭐⭐) — The canonical example of a connected space that is not path-connected; oscillation of $\sin(1/x)$ near $0$ is the discontinuity engine that prevents any path crossing from the graph piece to the segment piece. ([[Def - Connected Space]], [[Def - Path-Connected Space]], [[Def - Closure, Interior, and Boundary]], [[Def - Continuous Map]], [[Thm - Continuous Image of a Connected Space]])

- [[Ex - Components versus path-components]] (⭐⭐) — Exhibits a space whose connected components and path-components differ (the topologist's sine curve), and shows that local path-connectedness is the cleanest sufficient condition for them to coincide via the clopen-subset-of-connected-is-trivial argument. ([[Def - Connected Space]], [[Def - Path-Connected Space]], [[Def - Connected Components]])
