## §7 Compactness — Exercises

- [[Ex - The unit interval is compact]] — Direct proof from the open-cover definition using the supremum of "well-covered prefixes"; the order-completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ pushes the sup to $1$ and openness near $1$ extends the finite cover to reach $1$. Prototype for compactness in $\mathbb{R}^n$ via scaling, Tychonoff for finite products, and closed-subset-of-compact-is-compact. ([[Def - Compact Space]], [[Def - Topological Space]])

- [[Ex - Compact + Hausdorff implies normal]] — The iterated finite-subcover-assembly proof: Hausdorff gives pointwise separation, compactness of closed subsets gives finite-subcover extraction, and applying the recipe twice (once for regularity, once for normality) gives the full separation between disjoint closed sets. ([[Def - Compact Space]], [[Def - Separation Axioms]], [[Thm - Closed Subset of Compact is Compact]])

- [[Ex - A non-Hausdorff space where compact subsets are not closed]] — In the cofinite topology on $\mathbb{R}$, every subset is compact but only finite sets and $\mathbb{R}$ are closed; the open interval $(0, 1)$ is compact but not closed, demonstrating that Hausdorff is essential in the theorem "compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed". ([[Def - Compact Space]], [[Def - Separation Axioms]], [[Thm - Compact Subset of Hausdorff is Closed]])
