## §2.6 Isolated Zeros and Identity — Exercises

The exercises of §2.6 drill the identity theorem and its consequences: agreement of two holomorphic functions on any set with an accumulation point inside a connected domain forces global agreement. Each exercise practices either *applying* the theorem (to pin down a function from partial data) or *extending* a holomorphic function across a boundary (Schwarz reflection). The crucial check is always "is the accumulation point *inside* the domain?"

- [[Ex - The identity theorem in action]] (⭐⭐) — agreement on $\{1/n\}$ with $0 \in D$ implies global agreement ([[Thm - Identity Theorem (Uniqueness of Analytic Continuation)]], [[Def - Domain in the Complex Plane]])
- [[Ex - Uniqueness of sin from real values]] (⭐⭐) — entire extension of real $\sin$ is unique by identity theorem ([[Thm - Identity Theorem (Uniqueness of Analytic Continuation)]], [[Def - Complex Exponential and Trigonometric Functions]])
- [[Ex - Schwarz reflection principle (mini-version)]] (⭐⭐⭐) — extend holomorphic $f$ from upper half-disc to full disc when real-valued on the diameter ([[Thm - Morera's Theorem]], [[Thm - Identity Theorem (Uniqueness of Analytic Continuation)]])
