## §2.5 Analytic = Holomorphic — Exercises

The exercises of §2.5 drill the equivalence of holomorphic and analytic and its consequences. Each exercise practices a structural move: computing a Taylor series by recognising the function as the derivative of a known geometric expansion, or by termwise integration; using Cauchy estimates with $R \to \infty$ to read off polynomial structure from growth bounds; and verifying holomorphy of a parameter-dependent integral via Morera. The radius of convergence is *always* the distance to the nearest singularity — a Hardy-style maximum-principle observation in disguise.

- [[Ex - Power series expansion of 1 over (1-z) around 0]] (⭐) — geometric series, two methods, radius equals distance to nearest singularity ([[Thm - Holomorphic Functions are Analytic (Local Power Series Expansion)]])
- [[Ex - Power series expansion of log(1+z) around 0]] (⭐⭐) — termwise integration of geometric series, constant of integration ([[Thm - Holomorphic Functions are Analytic (Local Power Series Expansion)]], [[Def - Branch of the Logarithm]], [[Thm - Power Series is Holomorphic with Termwise Derivative]])
- [[Ex - Cauchy estimates bound polynomial degree]] (⭐⭐) — polynomial growth of an entire function forces polynomial form ([[Thm - Cauchy Estimates]], [[Thm - Higher Derivatives via CIF]], [[Thm - Holomorphic Functions are Analytic (Local Power Series Expansion)]])
- [[Ex - Continuity from Morera (specific instance)]] (⭐⭐) — Cauchy-type integral $\int \phi(t)/(t - z)\,dt$ is holomorphic in $z$ off the path ([[Thm - Morera's Theorem]], [[Thm - Holomorphic Dependence on a Parameter]])
