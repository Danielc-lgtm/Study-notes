## §1.4 Exp, Log, Powers — Exercises

The exercises of §1.4 drill the complex exponential and logarithm. Each exercise calibrates a structural feature: Euler's formula collapses trigonometric identities into single-line algebraic computations on $e^{i\theta}$; the logarithm is multi-valued, with $2\pi i \mathbb{Z}$ ambiguity that is selected by a branch cut; and no continuous global branch of $\log$ exists on the punctured plane $\mathbb{C}^\times$ — a topological obstruction tied to non-simply-connectedness.

- [[Ex - Euler's formula and trigonometric identities]] (⭐) — deriving the trig addition formulas from $e^{i(\theta+\phi)} = e^{i\theta}e^{i\phi}$ ([[Thm - Properties of the Complex Exponential]], [[Def - Complex Exponential and Trigonometric Functions]])
- [[Ex - Computing log(-1) and log(i)]] (⭐) — principal vs. alternative branches, the full multivalued set ([[Def - Branch of the Logarithm]], [[Def - Complex Exponential and Trigonometric Functions]])
- [[Ex - Failure of log existence on the punctured plane]] (⭐⭐⭐) — topological obstruction to a continuous branch on $\mathbb{C}^\times$, with two proofs ([[Def - Branch of the Logarithm]], [[Thm - Existence of a Logarithm on Simply Connected Domains]], [[Thm - Properties of the Complex Exponential]])
