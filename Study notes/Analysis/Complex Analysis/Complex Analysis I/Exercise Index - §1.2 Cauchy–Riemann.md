## §1.2 Cauchy–Riemann — Exercises

The exercises of §1.2 drill the Cauchy-Riemann equations and the gap between "complex differentiable" and "real differentiable with CR." Each exercise calibrates a structural fact: standard holomorphic functions ($z^2$, $e^z$) satisfy CR routinely; $\bar z$ violates CR everywhere (and so is nowhere holomorphic); and a function can have all partials existing and satisfying CR at a point *without* being complex differentiable there — the missing hypothesis is *continuity* of the partials.

- [[Ex - Verifying Cauchy–Riemann for z2 and exp(z)]] (⭐) — direct CR verification for polynomials and exponential, with derivative computation ([[Thm - Cauchy–Riemann Equations]], [[Def - Holomorphic Function]], [[Def - Complex Exponential and Trigonometric Functions]])
- [[Ex - The function f(z) = bar z is not differentiable]] (⭐) — showing CR failure for $\bar z$ via both partials and the two-direction limit test ([[Thm - Cauchy–Riemann Equations]], [[Def - Holomorphic Function]])
- [[Ex - A function with all partials but not differentiable]] (⭐⭐⭐) — counterexample showing partial-derivative existence + CR is *strictly weaker* than holomorphicity ([[Thm - Cauchy–Riemann Equations]], [[Def - Holomorphic Function]])
