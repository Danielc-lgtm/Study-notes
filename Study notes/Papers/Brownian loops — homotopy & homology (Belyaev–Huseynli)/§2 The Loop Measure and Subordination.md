**What §2 buys you.** §2 builds the measures every later section computes with: the $\sigma$-finite Brownian **loop measure** $\mu_X$ on unrooted unparametrised loops (total mass $\infty$, but finite on each non-trivial class), its Dirichlet-form generalisation $\mu^E_X$, and the $\phi$-subordinate versions $\mu^\phi_X$. It pins down the class of clocks $\phi$ admissible for subordination (**Bernstein** $+$ **Assumption 2.3**), packages the subordinator's whole occupation into a single $\sigma$-finite measure $V_\phi$ on proper time, and proves the collapse **Lemma 2.11** that later turns a class-mass double integral into one integral against $V_\phi$.

# A. Standing setup

Everything in §2 is built from one diffusion on a surface and one random clock. The constructions of §2.1–§2.4 hold on any complete Riemannian surface; the destination — the hyperbolic quotient whose closed geodesics carry the masses of §3–§7 — is fixed below so that the distance $L$ appearing in $I_\phi$ has a meaning on this page.

**The surface and its diffusion.** $(X,g)$ is a complete orientable Riemannian surface (with Dirichlet conditions on $\partial X$ if $\partial X\neq\varnothing$, i.e. Brownian motion killed on first hitting the boundary). $\Delta_X=-\operatorname{div}_g\operatorname{grad}_g\ge0$ is the positive Laplace–Beltrami operator; **speed-$2$ Brownian motion** on $X$ is the diffusion generated by $-\Delta_X$. Its **heat kernel** $p_X(t,x,y)>0$ is the integral kernel of the heat semigroup, $(e^{-t\Delta_X}f)(x)=\int_X p_X(t,x,y)f(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(y)$; on-diagonal $p_X(t,x,x)\sim1/(4\pi t)$ as $t\downarrow0$.

**The destination geometry ($\mathbb H^2$).** $\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb R)$ is a discrete, torsion-free group of isometries of the hyperbolic plane $\mathbb H^2$ (upper half-plane $\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$, metric $|dz|/\operatorname{Im}z$), acting **freely** — $\forall h\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}\ \forall z\in\mathbb H^2:\ hz\neq z$ (no non-identity isometry fixes a point) — and **properly discontinuously** — $\forall K\Subset\mathbb H^2:\ \#\{h\in\Gamma:hK\cap K\neq\varnothing\}<\infty$ (each compact set meets only finitely many of its $\Gamma$-translates). Under exactly these two conditions the quotient $X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^2$ (points of $\mathbb H^2$ glued when one is a $\Gamma$-image of the other) is a smooth hyperbolic surface and $\pi:\mathbb H^2\to X$ is a **covering map** (a local isometry under which $\mathbb H^2$ wraps around $X$) with **deck group** $\Gamma$ (the isometries permuting the sheets over each point), $\Gamma\cong\pi_1(X)$. $X$ is **geometrically finite**: $\Gamma$ finitely generated (equivalently $X$ has a finite-sided fundamental polygon); the only consequence used is that the length spectrum is locally finite, $\#\{\gamma:\ell_\gamma\le R\}<\infty$ for every $R$. A closed geodesic $\gamma$ is **primitive** if it is not a repeated traversal of a shorter one; $\ell_\gamma>0$ is its length, and $L:=m\ell_\gamma$ ($m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$) the length of its $m$-fold iterate. A **free-homotopy class** of loops is an equivalence class of loops under basepoint-free deformation ($\exists$ continuous $H:S^1\times[0,1]\to X$ between two loops); on $X$ these correspond bijectively to conjugacy classes $[h]=\{qhq^{-1}:q\in\Gamma\}$ in $\Gamma$, and the **non-trivial, non-peripheral** ones (not the constant loop, not a loop around a cusp) to pairs $(\gamma,m)\in\mathcal P_X\times\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$ — the class $\mathcal C_X(\gamma^m)=[\tau^m]$ winding $m$ times around $\gamma$. This indexing is used from §3 on; §2 needs only that each such class is a measurable set of loops. **The only geometric input to $I_\phi$ is the single positive number $L$.**

**Standing conventions.** $\Delta_X\ge0$ (geometer's sign; $\operatorname{spec}\Delta_X\subseteq[0,\infty)$); Brownian motion at speed $2$ (generator $-\Delta_X$). Three time-like variables are kept typographically distinct, deviating from the paper's single "$s$": the **spectral parameter** $s$; the **subordination / proper-time** variable $u$ (integrated in $I_\phi$ and $V_\phi$); the **loop duration** $t$ (integrated $\mathrm{d}t/t$). Spectral $s$ and killing $\kappa$ are linked by $s=\tfrac12+\sqrt{\tfrac14+\kappa}\iff\kappa=s(s-1)$, with $\kappa\ge-\tfrac14$ (at $\kappa=-\tfrac14$, $s=\tfrac12=\inf\operatorname{spec}\Delta_{\mathbb H^2}$). **Total mass** always means the sum over **non-trivial, non-peripheral** free-homotopy classes: the trivial (contractible) class carries infinite mass and is excluded, and peripheral (cusp) classes have no closed geodesic and are excluded.

§2 is foundational: it consumes no object from an earlier section, so there are no earlier-section recall chips. Every unfamiliar token is expanded at its first load-bearing use below.

# B. Spine of §2 (skim layer)

Five constructions and one lemma, in paper order. Read this list and you have §2's logical content.

1. **§2.1 — $\mu_X$ (Def 2.1).** *Given* speed-$2$ Brownian motion on $(X,g)$ with heat kernel $p_X$ ⊢ *produce* the $\sigma$-finite loop measure $\mu_X=\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int_X W^t_{x\to x}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g$ on unrooted unparametrised loops; total mass $\infty$.
2. **§2.2 — $\mu^E_X$ (Def 2.2).** *Given* any regular symmetric Dirichlet form $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ with heat kernel $p^E$ ⊢ *produce* the same construction, $\mu^E_X$; the only property used later is "it has a heat kernel."
3. **§2.3 — $\phi$ Bernstein $+$ Assumption 2.3.** *Isolate* the admissible clocks: Bernstein $\phi$ with $b>0$ or $\nu(0,\infty)=\infty$, i.e. a strictly increasing subordinator whose law has no atom at $0$.
4. **§2.4 — $\mu^\phi_X$ (Def 2.8).** *Given* $\phi$ admissible ⊢ *produce* the subordinate process $e^{-t\phi(A)}$ with kernel $p^\phi(t,\cdot,\cdot)=\int p^E(u,\cdot,\cdot)\psi^\phi_t(du)$ and its loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$.
5. **§2.4.1 — $V_\phi$ (Def 2.9).** *Collapse* the subordinator's occupation over all durations against $dt/t$ into one $\sigma$-finite measure $V_\phi$ on $(0,\infty)$; four explicit values.
6. **§2.4.1 — Lemma 2.11.** *Given* Assumption 2.3 ⊢ *produce* $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int h\,d\psi^\phi_t=\int h\,dV_\phi$ — the collapse of the $(t,u)$ double integral into a single integral against $V_\phi$.

# C. The results

## §2.1  The Brownian loop measure $\mu_X$ (Definition 2.1)

*New symbols:* $W^t_x,\ W^t_{x\to y},\ C^*_X,\ C_X,\ \mu^*_X,\ \mu_X$.

**Expansions.**

*Used here —* the loop is stripped of both a marked start-point and a clock, keeping only its oriented image; this is what makes $\mu_X$ a measure on geometric loops, on which "winds $m$ times around $\gamma$" (a purely geometric predicate) is measurable.
- **unrooted, unparametrised loop** — an equivalence class of a continuous map $\omega:[0,t]\to X$ with $\omega(0)=\omega(t)$, under (i) **circular time-shift** $\omega\mapsto\omega(\,\cdot+s_0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ t))$ — forgetting which point is the basepoint ("unrooted") — and (ii) increasing continuous reparametrisation of the circle — forgetting the clock ("unparametrised") — retaining the **orientation**. $C^*_X=\{(t,\omega):t\ge0,\ \omega\in C([0,t],X),\ \omega(0)=\omega(t)\}$ is the rooted parametrised loops; $C_X=C^*_X/\!\sim$ is their unrooted unparametrised quotient.

*Used here —* $W^t_{x\to x}$ is exactly the "return to $x$ in time $t$" ingredient integrated over $x$ and $t$; its total mass being the heat kernel is what makes $\mu_X$ computable.
- **unnormalised bridge measure** $W^t_{x\to y}$ — the (sub-probability) measure on paths $\omega\in C([0,t],X)$ with $\omega(0)=x,\ \omega(t)=y$ obtained by disintegrating the law $W^t_x$ of speed-$2$ Brownian motion from $x$ by its endpoint, $W^t_x=\int_X W^t_{x\to y}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(y)$. Its total mass is $\lvert W^t_{x\to y}\rvert=p_X(t,x,y)$, and $W^t_{x\to y}/p_X(t,x,y)$ is the ordinary Brownian bridge (the conditional law given $\omega(t)=y$).

> **Definition 2.1 (Brownian loop measure).** With $p_X$ the speed-$2$ heat kernel of $(X,g)$, the rooted parametrised Brownian loop measure on $C^*_X$ is
> $$\mu^*_X:=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_X W^t_{x\to x}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(x),$$
> and the **Brownian loop measure** $\mu_X$ is its pushforward to $C_X=C^*_X/\!\sim$. It is $\sigma$-finite, with **infinite** total mass.

> [!warning] Why the weight is $dt/t$ and not $dt$
> $dt/t$ is the multiplicative Haar measure on $(0,\infty)$: it is the unique (up to scale) weight under which $\mu^*_X$ is invariant under the circular time-shift, so that after quotienting by reparametrisation the pushforward $\mu_X$ is well-defined on unrooted unparametrised loops (Le Jan). Replace $dt/t$ by $dt$ and the pushforward to $C_X$ is not defined.

> [!note]- Why $\sigma$-finite but total mass $\infty$ (skippable)
> Total mass $=\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int_X p_X(t,x,x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(x)$. As $t\downarrow0$, $p_X(t,x,x)\sim1/(4\pi t)$, so the integrand $\sim1/t^2$ and the $t$-integral diverges at $0$ — the divergence is carried entirely by short, contractible (trivial-class) loops, which is why the trivial class is excluded from "total mass" throughout. It is $\sigma$-finite: cut $(0,\infty)$ into $\{t\ge1/n\}$ and restrict to loops of diameter $\ge1/n$; each piece has finite mass and the pieces exhaust $C_X$. Every non-trivial, non-peripheral class has **finite** mass (§3, Theorem 3.5).

## §2.2  The Dirichlet-form loop measure $\mu^E_X$ (Definition 2.2)

*New symbols:* $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F),\ A,\ p^E,\ \mu^E_X$.

**Expansions.** The construction of §2.1 needs nothing about $X$ beyond a symmetric heat semigroup with a kernel. Any regular symmetric Dirichlet form supplies one.

> [!def]+ regular symmetric Dirichlet form
> On $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$: a symmetric bilinear form $\mathcal E(u,v)$ with dense domain $\mathcal F\subseteq L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ that is (i) **non-negative**, $\mathcal E(u,u)\ge0$; (ii) **closed** — $\mathcal F$ is complete in the norm $\lVert u\rVert^2_{\mathcal E_1}:=\mathcal E(u,u)+\lVert u\rVert^2_{L^2}$; (iii) **Markovian** — for the unit contraction $v:=(0\vee u)\wedge1$ one has $v\in\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal E(v,v)\le\mathcal E(u,u)$ (squeezing a function into $[0,1]$ does not raise its energy — the analytic fingerprint of a process that cannot leave the state space); (iv) **regular** — $\mathcal F\cap C_c(X)$ is dense both in $\mathcal F$ ($\mathcal E_1$-norm) and in $C_c(X)$ (sup-norm), i.e. the form has a core of compactly supported continuous functions.

*Used here —* only the downstream consequence: such a form has a heat kernel, hence a loop measure. No finer property of $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ is ever used again.
> [!import]- (Fukushima) regular symmetric Dirichlet form $\Rightarrow$ Hunt process with kernel — Says / Needs / Gives
> **Says.** A regular symmetric Dirichlet form $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ determines a self-adjoint $A\ge0$ with $\mathcal F=\operatorname{Dom}(A^{1/2})$ and $\mathcal E(u,v)=\langle A^{1/2}u,A^{1/2}v\rangle$, hence a strongly continuous symmetric Markov semigroup $e^{-tA}$; it is the transition semigroup of a $\mathrm{vol}_g$-symmetric Hunt process on $X$, unique up to quasi-everywhere equivalence.
> **Needs.** $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ regular symmetric Dirichlet form on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$; and (assumed throughout) that $e^{-tA}$ has a jointly measurable symmetric density $p^E(t,x,y)$: $(e^{-tA}f)(x)=\int_X p^E(t,x,y)f(y)\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(y)$.
> **Gives.** Unnormalised bridge measures $W^{t,E}_{x\to y}$ of mass $p^E(t,x,y)$ (on càdlàg paths $D([0,t],X)$, to allow jumps), hence a loop measure. Assume freely; nothing here re-proves it. Functional-analytic fact (Fukushima's correspondence); anchor-adjacent, **not a genuine gap**.

> **Definition 2.2 (Dirichlet-form loop measure).** For a regular symmetric Dirichlet form $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ with heat kernel $p^E$, the parametrised loop measure on $C^*_X$ (now càdlàg loops) is
> $$\mu^{*,E}_X:=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_X W^{t,E}_{x\to x}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(x),$$
> invariant under the circular time-shift; its pushforward to $C_X$ is the loop measure $\mu^E_X$. Taking $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ the Dirichlet energy $\int|\nabla u|^2$ recovers $\mu_X$ of Definition 2.1.

## §2.3  Admissible clocks: Bernstein functions and Assumption 2.3

*New symbols:* $\phi,\ (a,b,\nu),\ T_t,\ \psi^\phi_t$.

**Expansions.** Subordination will run the process on a random clock $T_t$. The clock's laws are exactly the Bernstein functions; the paper's four processes are all of this form. This is §2's T1 home for the notion.

*Used here —* only that $\phi$ Bernstein with Assumption 2.3 yields a well-defined $I_\phi$ (§2.4.1); no other property of $\phi$ is used anywhere later.
> [!def]+ $\phi$ a Bernstein function satisfying Assumption 2.3
> $\phi:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ is **Bernstein** if it is $C^\infty$ with $(-1)^{n-1}\phi^{(n)}(\lambda)\ge0$ for all $n\ge1,\lambda>0$ (its derivative completely monotone). Equivalently (Lévy–Khintchine) $\phi(\lambda)=a+b\lambda+\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-\lambda u})\,\nu(\mathrm du)$ for a unique triple $(a,b,\nu)$ with $a,b\ge0$ and $\int_0^\infty(1\wedge u)\,\nu(\mathrm du)<\infty$ — the Laplace exponent of a **subordinator** (an increasing random clock $T_t\ge0$, $\mathbb E[e^{-\lambda T_t}]=e^{-t\phi(\lambda)}$): $a$ = killing rate, $b$ = drift, $\nu$ = jump intensity. **Assumption 2.3:** $b>0$ **or** $\nu(0,\infty)=\infty$ — the clock strictly increases, so its law has no atom at $0$ and the subordinate heat kernel is a genuine density. *(It says nothing about $a$: killing is allowed — $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda+\kappa$ has $a=\kappa$, passes via $b=1$.)* The four instances: $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda,\ \lambda+\kappa,\ \lambda^{\alpha/2},\ (\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$.

> [!note]- The four clocks and their laws $\psi^\phi_t$ (skippable)
> $\psi^\phi_t$ is the law of $T_t$ on $[0,\infty)$; $\lvert\psi^\phi_t\rvert=e^{-at}$ (mass lost at killing rate $a$). (i) **Brownian**, $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda$, $(a,b,\nu)=(0,1,0)$: $\psi^\phi_t=\delta_t$ (identity clock). (ii) **Killing** $\kappa>0$, $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda+\kappa$, $(a,b,\nu)=(\kappa,1,0)$: $\psi^\phi_t=e^{-\kappa t}\delta_t$. (iii) **$\alpha$-stable**, $\alpha\in(0,2)$, $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha/2}$, $(a,b,\nu)=(0,0,\nu_\alpha)$ with $\nu_\alpha(\mathrm du)=\tfrac{\alpha/2}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)}u^{-1-\alpha/2}\mathrm du$: pure-jump, $\nu(0,\infty)=\infty$ so Assumption 2.3 holds via the jump clause. (iv) **shifted stable** $(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$. Assumption 2.3 excludes only the compound-Poisson clocks (finite $\nu$, $b=0$), whose semigroups have no density.

## §2.4  The subordinate Brownian loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$ (Definition 2.8)

*New symbols:* $p^\phi,\ \mu^\phi_X,\ (\mathcal E^\phi,\mathcal F^\phi)$.

**Expansions.** Subordinating a symmetric semigroup by $\phi$ produces a new symmetric Markov semigroup; the paper cites this as the Phillips (Bochner–Phillips) functional calculus.

*Used here —* the subordinate kernel $p^\phi$ is a $\psi^\phi_t$-mixture of $p^E$; this exact shape is what Lemma 2.11 collapses.
> [!import]- (Phillips) subordination of a symmetric semigroup — Says / Needs / Gives
> **Says.** For $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ with generator $A\ge0$, semigroup $e^{-tA}$ and kernel $p^E$, and $\phi$ Bernstein with subordinator law $\psi^\phi_t$: $\ e^{-t\phi(A)}=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-uA}\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$ is again a strongly continuous symmetric Markov semigroup, with generator $-\phi(A)$, $\ \phi(A)=aI+bA+\int_0^\infty(I-e^{-uA})\,\nu(\mathrm du)$, and transition density
> $$p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{[0,\infty)}p^E(u,x,y)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du).$$
> **Needs.** $\phi$ Bernstein (triple $(a,b,\nu)$), subordinator independent of the base process; $(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$ regular symmetric Dirichlet form.
> **Gives.** The subordinate process is again a $\mathrm{vol}_g$-symmetric Hunt process with regular symmetric Dirichlet form $(\mathcal E^\phi,\mathcal F^\phi)$, same reference measure $\mathrm{vol}_g$; every $\mathrm{vol}_g$-symmetry / isometry-invariance of $p^E$ transfers to $p^\phi$ (in particular $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb R)$-invariance on $\mathbb H^2$). Assume freely; nothing here re-proves it. Functional-calculus fact; anchor-adjacent, **not a genuine gap**.

> **Definition 2.8 (subordinate Brownian loop measure).** Fix $\phi$ Bernstein with Assumption 2.3, with subordinate form $(\mathcal E^\phi,\mathcal F^\phi)$, semigroup $e^{-t\phi(A)}$ and density $p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{[0,\infty)}p^E(u,x,y)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$. Applying Definition 2.2 to $(\mathcal E^\phi,\mathcal F^\phi)$, the **subordinate Brownian loop measure** is
> $$\mu^\phi_X:=\text{pushforward to }C_X\text{ of }\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_X W^{t,\phi}_{x\to x}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g(x),\qquad\lvert W^{t,\phi}_{x\to y}\rvert=p^\phi(t,x,y).$$
> It is $\sigma$-finite with infinite total mass; each non-trivial non-peripheral class has finite mass.

## §2.4.1  The weighted potential measure $V_\phi$ (Definition 2.9)

*New symbols:* $V_\phi,\ I_\phi$.

**Expansions.** $p^\phi$ is a $\psi^\phi_t$-mixture of $p^E(u,\cdot,\cdot)$, so the loop measure carries a **double** integral in $(t,u)$. Collapsing the $t$-integral against $dt/t$ turns the pair $(\psi^\phi_t)_t$ into a single measure on the proper-time variable $u$. This is §2's home for $V_\phi$ (and for the shape of the $\mathbb H^2$ integral $I_\phi$ that §3 will build from it).

*Used here —* only its **value as a function of $L$** (via the four closed forms of $V_\phi$); its internals are not touched by later sections. $I_\phi$ is the ONLY factor of the class mass that depends on the process — all geometry sits in the prefactor.
> [!def]+ $I_\phi$, the weighted heat-kernel integral, and $V_\phi$ (Definition 2.9 / 3.6)
> $\displaystyle I_\phi(L):=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-u/4}\,e^{-L^2/(4u)}}{2\sqrt{\pi u}}\,V_\phi(\mathrm du)\in(0,\infty)$, $\ L>0$, where the kernel $e^{-u/4}e^{-L^2/(4u)}/(2\sqrt{\pi u})$ is the speed-$2$, mass-shifted Brownian heat kernel on $\mathbb H^2$ at proper time $u$ between two points a hyperbolic distance $L$ apart, and $V_\phi$ — the **weighted potential measure** — is the $\sigma$-finite measure on $(0,\infty)$ got by integrating the subordinator's time-$t$ law $\psi^\phi_t$ over all durations, $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=\int_0^\infty\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)\,\mathrm dt/t$. Four values: $\mathrm du/u$ (Brownian), $e^{-\kappa u}\mathrm du/u$ (killing $\kappa$), $\tfrac\alpha2\mathrm du/u$ ($\alpha$-stable), $\tfrac\alpha2 e^{-\kappa u}\mathrm du/u$ (shifted stable). $I_\phi$ is the ONLY factor of the class mass that depends on the process.

> **Definition 2.9 (weighted potential measure).** $V_\phi$ is the $\sigma$-finite measure on $(0,\infty)$ characterised by
> $$\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,V_\phi(\mathrm du)=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)\tag{7}$$
> for every measurable $h\ge0$ with finite right-hand side. When $V_\phi$ is absolutely continuous (all four cases here) we write $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=V_\phi(u)\,\mathrm du$.

> [!note]- Verification of the four values of $V_\phi$ (skippable; every number reproducible here)
> **(a) Brownian**, $\psi^\phi_t=\delta_t$: the $t$-integral localises at $u=t$, so $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}h(t)=\int_0^\infty h(u)\tfrac{\mathrm du}{u}$, giving $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=\mathrm du/u$. **(b) Killing $\kappa$**, $\psi^\phi_t=e^{-\kappa t}\delta_t$: same localisation with weight $e^{-\kappa t}$, so $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=e^{-\kappa u}\,\mathrm du/u$. **(c) $\alpha$-stable**, $\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)=t^{-2/\alpha}g_{\alpha/2}(u\,t^{-2/\alpha})\,\mathrm du$ ($g_{\alpha/2}$ the standard $\alpha/2$-stable density, a probability density): substitute $r=u\,t^{-2/\alpha}$ in $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}t^{-2/\alpha}g_{\alpha/2}(u\,t^{-2/\alpha})=\tfrac{\alpha}{2u}\int_0^\infty g_{\alpha/2}(r)\,dr=\tfrac{\alpha}{2u}$, so $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=\tfrac\alpha2\,\mathrm du/u$ — the multiplicative Haar measure preserved up to the constant $\alpha/2$. **(d) shifted stable** combines (b) and (c): $V_\phi(\mathrm du)=\tfrac\alpha2 e^{-\kappa u}\,\mathrm du/u$.

## §2.4.2  The collapse lemma (Lemma 2.11)

*New symbols:* none (uses $\psi^\phi_t,\ V_\phi,\ p^E,\ p^\phi$).

**Statement.** The lemma collapses the $(t,u)$ double integral of $\mu^\phi_X$ into a single integral against $V_\phi$. It is the move that later turns §3's Theorem 3.2 into Theorem 3.5.

> **Lemma 2.11 (collapse of the time integral).** Assume:
> **(H1)** $\phi$ is Bernstein with Assumption 2.3 (so $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=0$ for all $t>0$).
> Then for every measurable $h\ge0$ on $(0,\infty)$,
> $$\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm dt}{t}\int_{[0,\infty)}h(u)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)=\int_0^\infty h(u)\,V_\phi(\mathrm du).\tag{8}$$

*Used here —* taking $h(u)=p^E(u,x,y)$ and using $p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int p^E(u,x,y)\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$ (Phillips import), (8) becomes $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{\mathrm dt}{t}\,p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{(0,\infty)}p^E(u,x,y)\,V_\phi(\mathrm du)$ — the identity §3 unfolds into $I_\phi$.

**Discharge (the typecheck).**

| step | apply | to | get |

| 1 | (H1) Assumption 2.3 | $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=0\ \forall t$ | integrand supported on $(0,\infty)$: $\int_{[0,\infty)}h\,d\psi^\phi_t=\int_{(0,\infty)}h\,d\psi^\phi_t$ |
| 2 | Tonelli ($h\ge0$, all measures $\sigma$-finite) | $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$ | integral well-defined in $[0,\infty]$, order immaterial |
| 3 | Definition 2.9, eq (7) | the double integral of step 2 | $=\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,V_\phi(\mathrm du)$ |

> [!note]- Proof (skippable — the Discharge is the proof)
> By Assumption 2.3, $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=0$ for every $t>0$, so the atom at $0$ never contributes and $\int_{[0,\infty)}h\,d\psi^\phi_t=\int_{(0,\infty)}h\,d\psi^\phi_t$; this is exactly why Assumption 2.3 is needed. Since $h\ge0$ and both $\tfrac{dt}{t}$ on $(0,\infty)$ and $\psi^\phi_t$ are $\sigma$-finite, Tonelli's theorem makes $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$ a well-defined element of $[0,\infty]$ independent of the order of integration. This is precisely the right-hand side of the defining property (7) of $V_\phi$, whose left-hand side is $\int_{(0,\infty)}h(u)\,V_\phi(\mathrm du)$. Equating gives (8). Specialising $h(u)=p^E(u,x,y)$ and inserting Phillips' $p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int p^E(u,x,y)\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm du)$ yields $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{(0,\infty)}p^E(u,x,y)V_\phi(\mathrm du)$. $\square$

> [!warning] Why Assumption 2.3 is the load-bearing hypothesis
> Drop it (a compound-Poisson clock, $b=0$ and $\nu(0,\infty)<\infty$) and $\psi^\phi_t$ has an atom at $0$: $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=e^{-\nu(0,\infty)t}>0$. Then $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}e^{-\nu(0,\infty)t}=\infty$ diverges, and worse, $p^\phi(t,x,y)$ fails to be a density (a positive-mass "no-jump" atom sits on the diagonal). Assumption 2.3 is exactly the condition making $V_\phi$ a genuine $\sigma$-finite measure on $(0,\infty)$ and $p^\phi$ a genuine kernel — no atom, nothing to collapse incorrectly.

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> The whole section is one idea applied twice. Le Jan's loop measure $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}\int_X W^t_{x\to x}$ is "sum every return-to-start Brownian excursion, over every start-point and every duration, weighted by the scale-invariant $dt/t$." The weight is not cosmetic: it is the exact multiplicative Haar factor that lets you forget the clock and the basepoint and still have a measure — remove it and there is no object on $C_X$ at all. The price is that the total mass diverges, but the divergence is entirely short contractible loops, which is why "total mass" always means the sum over non-trivial classes and the trivial class is quarantined.
>
> The second half swaps Brownian motion for a subordinate process — a Brownian motion watched on a random increasing clock $T_t$. The clocks that work are exactly the Bernstein functions, and Assumption 2.3 is the single sanity condition (the clock really moves, so no atom sits at proper time $0$). Subordination replaces one integral by two — average $p^E(u,\cdot,\cdot)$ against the clock's law $\psi^\phi_t$, then integrate $t$ — and the one genuinely useful lemma of §2, Lemma 2.11, observes that the $\int_0^\infty\tfrac{dt}{t}$ commutes past the mixing and lands as a single measure $V_\phi$ on proper time. That is the whole mechanism: $\int\tfrac{dt}{t}\int\psi^\phi_t=\int V_\phi$. Everything process-dependent in the entire paper is now packaged in $V_\phi$, whose four values are trivial to read off, and every geometric fact still to come will meet the process only through $\int(\text{heat kernel})\,V_\phi=I_\phi$.
