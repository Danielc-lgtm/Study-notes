# Definition

> **Definition (centraliser).** $C_\Gamma(h):=\{q\in\Gamma:qh=hq\}$, a subgroup of $\Gamma$.

> **Proposition (C) — the centraliser of a power of a primitive.** Let $\Gamma$ be torsion-free and discrete in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ or $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, $\tau\in\Gamma$ primitive hyperbolic (loxodromic), $m\geq1$. Then
> $$C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle=\{\tau^k:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cong\mathbb{Z},$$
> generated by the **primitive** $\tau$ — **not** by $\tau^m$.

> **Proposition (E) — coset enumeration.** For $h_1,h_2\in\Gamma$,
> $$h_1\tau^mh_1^{-1}=h_2\tau^mh_2^{-1}\iff h_1^{-1}h_2\in C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle\iff h_1\langle\tau\rangle=h_2\langle\tau\rangle .$$
> Consequently $r\mapsto r\tau^mr^{-1}$ is a **bijection** $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\to[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, and
> $$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}=\bigsqcup_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\{r\tau^mr^{-1}\}\tag{10}$$
> — **one distinct conjugate per coset**.

> [!note]- Proof of (C) and (E) (skippable)
> **(C).** Let $q\tau^m=\tau^mq$. Then $q$ permutes $\operatorname{Fix}(\tau^m)\subseteq\partial$: if $\tau^m\xi=\xi$ then $\tau^m(q\xi)=q\tau^m\xi=q\xi$. As $\tau$ is hyperbolic (loxodromic), $\operatorname{Fix}(\tau^m)$ is exactly the two fixed points of $\tau$, so $q$ preserves $\operatorname{axis}(\tau)$ setwise. Torsion-freeness excludes elliptics, and $\Gamma$ consists of orientation-preserving isometries, so an element preserving a geodesic setwise must **translate** along it. The elements of $\Gamma$ translating along a fixed geodesic form a discrete subgroup of $(\mathbb{R},+)$, hence infinite cyclic; its generator is the shortest such translation, which is the primitive $\tau$. So $q\in\langle\tau\rangle$. The reverse inclusion is immediate since $\langle\tau\rangle$ is abelian.
>
> **(E).** $h_1\tau^mh_1^{-1}=h_2\tau^mh_2^{-1}\iff(h_1^{-1}h_2)\tau^m(h_1^{-1}h_2)^{-1}=\tau^m\iff h_1^{-1}h_2\in C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$. By (C) this is $h_1\langle\tau\rangle=h_2\langle\tau\rangle$. So the map is well defined on cosets and injective; surjective by definition of $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. $\;\square$

**Dimension-independence.** The proof of (C) uses only: $\operatorname{Fix}(\tau^m)=\operatorname{Fix}(\tau)$; torsion-freeness; orientation-preservation; discreteness. **None is two-dimensional**, so (C) and (E) hold verbatim for $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and this page serves §7 as well as §3.

# Type card

> [!abstract] Type card — (C), (E)
> **Given.** **(H1)** $\Gamma$ torsion-free discrete in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ or $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$. **(H2)** $\tau\in\Gamma$ **primitive** hyperbolic/loxodromic. **(H3)** $m\geq1$.
>
> **Produces.** $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}$; and a bijection $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\to[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, i.e. an enumeration of the conjugacy class **without repetition**.
>
> **Lets you.** Unfold: index the conjugacy-class sum by $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$, then use [[Def - Fundamental Region|(R)]] to reassemble $\bigsqcup_r r^{-1}F$ into a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$. **The class sum and the region enlargement are the same combinatorial fact seen twice, so they cancel exactly.**

# Checks

**Instance (abelian extreme).** $\Gamma=\langle\tau\rangle$: $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ is a single coset, so (10) says $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}=\{\tau^m\}$ — correct for an abelian group. Then $F$ is already a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$, the unfolding does nothing, and Theorem 3.2 reduces to its own hypothesis. The general case interpolates between this and a free group, where the cosets are numerous.

**Non-instance (fails primitivity).** Suppose one wrote $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau^m\rangle$. Then $\Gamma/\langle\tau^m\rangle$ has $m$ times as many cosets and (10) lists each conjugate $m$ times. The mass in Theorem 3.2 comes out $m$ times too large, and the Brownian formula becomes $\frac{1}{e^L-1}$ instead of $\frac1m\frac{1}{e^L-1}$ — losing exactly the $1/m$ that makes $\sum_m$ a logarithm and hence produces the Selberg zeta function. **The $1/m$ in every mass formula in this paper traces to the word "primitive" in (C).**

**Non-instance (fails torsion-freeness).** With an elliptic rotation by $\pi$ about a point of $\operatorname{axis}(\tau)$ available, the stabiliser of the axis is infinite **dihedral**, not infinite cyclic, and the argument in (C) breaks at "must translate along it". (The centraliser of $\tau^m$ is unchanged, since that element conjugates $\tau$ to $\tau^{-1}$ and does not commute with $\tau^m$; but the quotient is then an orbifold and §3's setting fails earlier.)

# Commentary

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> To integrate a sum over a conjugacy class one needs to list it without repetition, and the list is indexed by cosets of the centraliser. The general principle is elementary; the content here is *which* subgroup the centraliser is, and the answer is as small as it could be — infinite cyclic, generated by the primitive element.
>
> The payoff is the unfolding move, which is the only structural argument in the paper and happens twice, in §3 and §7. In Theorem 3.2 one has an integral over a fundamental region for $\Gamma$ of a sum over a conjugacy class. Writing the class as one term per coset, moving each $r$ onto the region by $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel, and reassembling $\bigsqcup_r r^{-1}F$, one gets a fundamental region for a *cyclic* group — whose fundamental regions are horizontal bands one can integrate over explicitly. The conjugacy-class sum and the group-sized region annihilate each other.
