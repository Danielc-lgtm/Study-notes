# Notation

- $\Gamma$ — a torsion-free Fuchsian group (or, in §7, a torsion-free Kleinian group); $\tau\in\Gamma$ a primitive hyperbolic (loxodromic) element
- $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\{h\in\Gamma : h\tau^m = \tau^m h\}$ — the centraliser of $\tau^m$ in $\Gamma$
- $\langle\tau\rangle=\{\tau^k : k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ — the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by $\tau$
- $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}=\{h\tau^m h^{-1} : h\in\Gamma\}$ — the conjugacy class
- $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ — the set of **left** cosets $r\langle\tau\rangle$; $r$ a coset representative
- $\bigsqcup$ — disjoint union

---

# In plain language

To integrate a sum over a conjugacy class, one needs to list the class **without repetition**. The list is indexed by cosets, and this page is that observation together with the geometric fact that makes it work.

The general principle is elementary: two elements $h_1,h_2$ conjugate $\tau^m$ to the same thing exactly when $h_1^{-1}h_2$ commutes with $\tau^m$, that is when they lie in the same left coset of the centraliser. So the conjugacy class is in bijection with $\Gamma/C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$, one conjugate per coset.

The specific fact is what the centraliser actually is here, and it is as small as it could be: $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle$, infinite cyclic, generated by the **primitive** $\tau$. The geometric reason is short. Anything commuting with $\tau^m$ must preserve the fixed-point set of $\tau^m$, hence the axis of $\tau$; the elements of $\Gamma$ preserving a given geodesic form a discrete group of translations along it, hence infinite cyclic; and its generator is the primitive translation, which is $\tau$. Torsion-freeness is used to rule out an orientation-reversing or elliptic contribution.

**The payoff, and the reason this page exists.** In [[Thm - General Homotopy Class Decomposition for Hyperbolic Surfaces|Theorem 3.2]] one has an integral over a fundamental region $F$ for $\Gamma$ of a sum over the conjugacy class. Writing the class as one term per coset $r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$, moving each $r$ onto the region by invariance of the kernel, and reassembling $\bigsqcup_r r^{-1}F$, one obtains a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ — a *cyclic* group, whose fundamental regions are horizontal bands one can integrate over explicitly. **The conjugacy-class sum and the group-sized fundamental region annihilate each other, leaving one term and a band.** That is the unfolding move, it is the only structural argument in the paper, and it happens twice: §3 and §7.

# The definition

> **Definition (centraliser).** For $h\in\Gamma$, the **centraliser** is $C_\Gamma(h)=\{q\in\Gamma : qh=hq\}$, a subgroup of $\Gamma$.

> **Proposition (the centraliser of a power of a primitive hyperbolic).** Let $\Gamma$ be a torsion-free Fuchsian group, $\tau\in\Gamma$ primitive hyperbolic, and $m\geq1$. Then
> $$C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle = \{\tau^k : k\in\mathbb{Z}\},$$
> an infinite cyclic subgroup generated by the primitive translation $\tau$ — **not** by $\tau^m$.

> **Proposition (coset enumeration).** Two elements $h_1,h_2\in\Gamma$ produce the same conjugate, $h_1\tau^m h_1^{-1}=h_2\tau^m h_2^{-1}$, if and only if $h_1^{-1}h_2\in C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle$, that is if and only if $h_1$ and $h_2$ lie in the same left coset of $\langle\tau\rangle$ in $\Gamma$. Consequently
> $$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \bigsqcup_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\{r\tau^m r^{-1}\},\tag{10}$$
> **with one distinct conjugate per coset.**

> [!note]- Proof (skippable)
> **The centraliser.** Suppose $q\tau^m=\tau^m q$. Then $q$ maps the fixed-point set of $\tau^m$ on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$ to itself: if $\tau^m\xi=\xi$ then $\tau^m(q\xi)=q\tau^m\xi=q\xi$. Since $\tau$ is hyperbolic, $\tau^m$ has exactly the two fixed points of $\tau$, so $q$ preserves that pair and hence preserves the axis of $\tau$ setwise. Because $\Gamma$ is torsion-free it contains no elliptic elements, and because it consists of orientation-preserving isometries, an element preserving a geodesic setwise must translate along it. The elements of $\Gamma$ translating along a fixed geodesic form a discrete subgroup of $(\mathbb{R},+)$, hence infinite cyclic; its generator is the shortest such translation, which is the primitive $\tau$. Therefore $q\in\langle\tau\rangle$. The reverse inclusion is immediate since $\langle\tau\rangle$ is abelian.
>
> **The enumeration.** $h_1\tau^m h_1^{-1}=h_2\tau^m h_2^{-1}$ if and only if $(h_1^{-1}h_2)\tau^m(h_1^{-1}h_2)^{-1}=\tau^m$, that is $h_1^{-1}h_2\in C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$. By the above that means $h_1\langle\tau\rangle=h_2\langle\tau\rangle$. So $r\mapsto r\tau^m r^{-1}$ is a well-defined injection $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\to[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, and it is surjective by definition of the conjugacy class. $\;\square$

# Types and signatures

- $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$ — a subgroup of $\Gamma$; here isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$
- $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ — a set of left cosets, countably infinite (unless $\Gamma=\langle\tau\rangle$, when it is a single coset)
- $r\mapsto r\tau^m r^{-1}$ — a **bijection** $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\to[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, well defined on cosets
- $\bigsqcup_{r} r^{-1}F$ — a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ acting on $\mathbb{H}^2$, assembled from $\Gamma$-translates of a fundamental region $F$ for $\Gamma$

---

# Example

The abelian extreme. If $\Gamma=\langle\tau\rangle$ is itself infinite cyclic — the hyperbolic cylinder — then $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ is a single coset, and (10) says the conjugacy class of $\tau^m$ is the single element $\{\tau^m\}$. Correct: an abelian group has singleton conjugacy classes. The fundamental region $F$ for $\Gamma$ is already a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$, so the unfolding step of Theorem 3.2 does nothing, and the theorem reduces to its own hypothesis. The general case interpolates between this and a free group, where the cosets are numerous.

**Near-miss non-example — why primitivity is essential.** Suppose one wrote $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau^m\rangle$ instead. Then the cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau^m\rangle$ would be $m$ times as numerous, and the enumeration (10) would list each conjugate $m$ times. The mass computed in Theorem 3.2 would come out $m$ times too large, and the Brownian formula would be $\frac{1}{e^L-1}$ instead of $\frac1m\frac{1}{e^L-1}$ — losing exactly the $1/m$ that makes $\sum_m$ into a logarithm and hence produces the Selberg zeta function. **The factor $1/m$ in every mass formula in this paper traces back to the word "primitive" in this proposition.**

**Second near-miss.** If $\Gamma$ had torsion, an elliptic element rotating by $\pi$ about a point on the axis would conjugate $\tau$ to $\tau^{-1}$ and preserve the axis while reversing it, so the stabiliser of the axis would be an infinite dihedral group rather than $\mathbb{Z}$. The centraliser of $\tau^m$ would be unchanged (that element does not commute with $\tau^m$), but the geometric argument above would need care, and the quotient would be an orbifold. Torsion-freeness keeps the argument as short as it is.
