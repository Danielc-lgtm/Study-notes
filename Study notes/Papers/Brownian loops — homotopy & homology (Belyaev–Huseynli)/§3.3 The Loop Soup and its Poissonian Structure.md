# Exports

> **(E1)** For any $\sigma$-finite $\mu^\phi_X$ and any $c>0$ the soup $\mathcal{L}_c$ exists, and $\mathbb{E}[N_A]=c\,\mu^\phi_X(A)$ for measurable $A$. *(Definition 3.7; [[Constr - The Loop Soup|(P1),(P2)]].)*
>
> **(E2)** $\mu^\phi_X(A)<\infty\Rightarrow N_A\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(c\,\mu^\phi_X(A))$. *([[Constr - The Loop Soup|(P2)]].)*
>
> **(E3)** Distinct free homotopy classes are disjoint, hence their counts are jointly independent. *([[Thm - Poissonian Structure of Homotopy Classes|Prop 3.8]].)*
>
> **(E4)** For jump $\phi$, $\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)$ is not a measurable set of càdlàg loops; the soup is taken on **marked** loops carrying $(B,S)$, with the same intensity. *([[Constr - The Loop Soup|(P4)]], Remark 3.1.)*
>
> **(E5)** Every mass formula of §3.1 and §4 is therefore also a **distributional** statement. Explicitly, combining (E2) with [[Thm - Mass of the Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure on Surfaces|Thm 3.5]]:
> $$N_{\gamma,m}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\Big(\tfrac{c}{m}\tfrac{e^{(1-s)L}}{e^L-1}\Big),\qquad L=m\ell_\gamma,\ s=\tfrac12+\sqrt{\tfrac14+\kappa}.$$

# Commentary

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> The section is two pages of the paper and one idea: **a $\sigma$-finite measure is the intensity of a Poisson point process, so any mass computation is secretly a distributional computation.** Nothing about the loop measure changes; what changes is the kind of question one may ask. Before §3.3 the paper can say "the expected number of loops in this class is $\frac1m\frac{1}{e^L-1}$"; after it, "the number of loops in this class is Poisson with that mean, independently of every other class".
>
> The finiteness that makes the soup interesting is not automatic and is not proved here — that the *total* over non-trivial classes is finite is [[Thm - Finiteness of the Total Mass|Corollary 4.7]], one section later, and it is exactly what turns $\#\mathcal{L}^*_\lambda$ into an honest Poisson variable with mean $-\lambda\log Z_X(s)$. The Selberg zeta function appearing as a *mean number of loops* is the first place the analytic and probabilistic sides of the paper touch.
