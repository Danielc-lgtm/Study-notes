# Notation

- $\phi$ — a Bernstein function satisfying [[Constr - Assumption 2.3 (Strictly Increasing Subordinator)|Assumption 2.3]]; $\mu^\phi_X$ the subordinate Brownian loop measure on $\mathcal{C}_X$
- $c>0$ — the intensity of the soup
- $\mathcal{L}_c$ — the subordinate Brownian **loop soup**: the Poisson point process of loops on $X$ with intensity measure $c\,\mu^\phi_X$; a random countable collection of loops
- $N_A := \#\{\eta\in\mathcal{L}_c : \eta\in A\}$ — the count of soup loops in a measurable set $A$ of loops with $\mu^\phi_X(A)<\infty$
- $\mathcal{L}^*_\lambda$ — (used in §6.2) the loops of $\mathcal{L}_\lambda$ that are non-contractible and not homotopic into a cusp

# What this section is for

Two paragraphs and one proposition whose proof is a single line. The content is a change of viewpoint that pays off twice later, and it rests on one fact: **any $\sigma$-finite measure can serve as the intensity of a Poisson point process.**

Up to this point $\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$ has been a number — the mass of a class. Because $\mu^\phi_X$ is $\sigma$-finite, it is also the intensity of a Poisson point process, and then that number becomes the *mean of a Poisson random variable*: the number of loops of the soup lying in the class. This upgrades every mass computation in §3 from a statement about an expectation to a statement about a distribution. It is what lets §6 speak about the *law* of a topological quantity rather than only its average, and it is the mechanism behind [[Thm - Distribution of the Total Homology of the Loop Soup|Proposition 6.7]], where the total homology of the soup gets a closed-form characteristic function.

The construction follows Lawler and Werner, who introduced the Brownian loop soup, and Le Jan. Fix $c>0$ and let $\mathcal{L}_c$ be the Poisson point process with intensity $c\,\mu^\phi_X$. Then $\mathcal{L}_c$ is a random countable collection of loops on $X$, and for any measurable set $A$ of loops with $\mu^\phi_X(A)<\infty$ the count $N_A$ is Poisson with mean $c\,\mu^\phi_X(A)$. The parameter $c$ is the intensity of the soup, and note that it multiplies everything linearly — all the geometry sits in $\mu^\phi_X$.

**One caveat, and it is the same caveat as everywhere else in the paper.** When the process has jumps, $\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)$ is not a measurable set of càdlàg loops, by [[Constr - Loop Mass in a Homotopy Class for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1]] — there is nothing to count. The fix is the same fix: take $\mathcal{L}_c$ to be the Poisson point process of *marked* loops carrying the pair $(B,S)$, on which the monodromy class is measurable, and whose intensity for the class is the same number $c\,\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$. Every statement in this section is to be read on the marked space in the jump case.

# Results

## Proposition 3.8 — Poissonian structure of homotopy classes

> [!abstract] Type card — Proposition 3.8
> **Given.** An intensity $c>0$; the loop soup $\mathcal{L}_c$ with intensity measure $c\,\mu^\phi_X$ for a Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3; a primitive closed geodesic $\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X$ and a winding number $m\geq1$. For jump processes, $\mathcal{L}_c$ is the process of marked loops of [[Constr - Loop Mass in a Homotopy Class for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1]].
>
> **Produces.** The number of loops of $\mathcal{L}_c$ in the free homotopy class $\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)$ is a **Poisson random variable of mean $c\,\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$**; and for any finite collection of pairwise distinct classes, these variables are **jointly independent**.
>
> **Lets you.** Upgrade every mass computation of §3 to a distributional statement, so that questions like "what is the probability that the soup contains no loop in this class?" have closed-form answers — here $\exp(-c\,\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)))$, which by Theorem 3.5 is an explicit function of $\ell_\gamma$ and $m$.

**Strategy.** Distinct free homotopy classes are disjoint measurable sets; both statements are then immediate from the defining properties of a Poisson point process.

Full statement: [[Thm - Poissonian Structure of Homotopy Classes]].

The independence half is worth dwelling on for a moment, because it is stronger than it looks. Free homotopy classes on a hyperbolic surface are geometrically entangled — geodesics in different classes intersect, and the loops realising them share regions of the surface. Yet the *counts* are exactly independent, with no correction term. That is not a fact about hyperbolic geometry; it is a fact about Poisson processes, which are independent across disjoint sets no matter how the sets are arranged in space. The geometry enters only through the means.

# What to carry forward

**The dictionary.** Mass of a class $\leftrightarrow$ mean of a Poisson count. Everywhere §3 wrote a number, one may now write a distribution.

**The exponential formula**, which is what §6.2 actually uses: for a Poisson process of intensity $\lambda\mu$ and measurable $F$ on loops,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\eta\in\mathcal{L}_\lambda} e^{F(\eta)}\Big] = \exp\Big(\lambda\int\big(e^{F(\eta)}-1\big)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\eta)\Big).$$
Proposition 6.7 is this formula with $e^{F(\eta)}=\chi([\eta])$, a unitary character evaluated on the homology class of a loop. Having the formula in hand is the whole of that proof.

**The marked-space convention** for jump processes, inherited from Remark 3.1 and used silently thereafter.
