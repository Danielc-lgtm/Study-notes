# Notation

- $\tau\in\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ — a hyperbolic element
- $\ell_\gamma>0$ — its translation length; $\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X$ the corresponding primitive oriented closed geodesic
- $\mathrm{axis}(\tau)\subset\mathbb{H}^2$ — the geodesic joining the two fixed points of $\tau$ on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$
- $\operatorname{tr}$ — the trace of a lift to $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, well defined up to sign, so $|\operatorname{tr}|$ is well defined on $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
- $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ — hyperbolic distance

---

# In plain language

An isometry of $\mathbb{H}^2$ is one of three kinds, and the trichotomy is visible in $|\operatorname{tr}|$. **Elliptic** ($|\operatorname{tr}|<2$) fixes a point inside $\mathbb{H}^2$ and rotates about it. **Parabolic** ($|\operatorname{tr}|=2$, not the identity) fixes exactly one point on the boundary circle and no interior point. **Hyperbolic** ($|\operatorname{tr}|>2$) fixes exactly two boundary points, and translates along the geodesic joining them.

A torsion-free Fuchsian group contains no elliptic elements at all — an elliptic element of a discrete group has finite order. So its non-identity elements are parabolic or hyperbolic, and the two correspond to the two possible ends of the quotient: parabolics give cusps, hyperbolics give closed geodesics and funnels.

A hyperbolic $\tau$ carries a number: its **translation length** $\ell_\gamma$, the distance it moves points along its axis. Downstairs on $X$ the axis projects to a closed geodesic of exactly that length. **Primitive** means $\tau$ is not a proper power of anything in $\Gamma$; equivalently the closed geodesic is traversed once rather than $m$ times. Every hyperbolic element is a power of a unique primitive one, which is why the paper indexes everything by a pair $(\gamma,m)$ with $\gamma$ primitive.

**Why the paper cares about primitivity.** Two reasons, both structural. The centraliser computation of the next page — $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle$, generated by the *primitive* $\tau$ and not by $\tau^m$ — is what makes the coset enumeration correct, and it fails if $\tau$ is not primitive. And the Euler product defining the [[Def - Selberg Zeta Function|Selberg zeta function]] runs over primitive geodesics only, with the iterates $m\geq1$ supplied by the logarithmic expansion — exactly mirroring how the Riemann zeta function's Euler product runs over primes.

# The definition

> **Definition (hyperbolic element and translation length).** A non-identity $\tau\in\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ is **hyperbolic** if $|\operatorname{tr}\tau|>2$; equivalently, if it has exactly two fixed points on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$ and none in $\mathbb{H}^2$. The geodesic joining its fixed points is its **axis**, and $\tau$ translates along the axis by a distance
> $$\ell_\gamma := \min_{z\in\mathbb{H}^2} d(z,\tau z) > 0,$$
> its **translation length**, with the minimum attained exactly on the axis. Equivalently $|\operatorname{tr}\tau| = 2\cosh(\ell_\gamma/2)$.

> **Definition (primitive).** A hyperbolic $\tau\in\Gamma$ is **primitive** if it is not a proper power in $\Gamma$: $\tau=\sigma^k$ with $\sigma\in\Gamma$ and $k\geq1$ forces $k=1$. Every hyperbolic element of a torsion-free Fuchsian group is $\tau^m$ for a unique primitive $\tau$ and unique $m\geq1$, up to the choice of orientation.

> **Correspondence with geodesics.** $\mathcal{P}_X$ denotes the set of **primitive oriented closed geodesics** on $X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$. Each $\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X$ corresponds to a primitive hyperbolic conjugacy class in $\Gamma$ with translation length $\ell_\gamma$: the axis of any representative $\tau$ projects under $\pi$ onto $\gamma$, and the length of $\gamma$ is $\ell_\gamma$.

# Types and signatures

- $\tau$ — an element of $\Gamma$, that is a Möbius transformation of $\mathbb{H}^2$; $|\operatorname{tr}\tau|>2$
- $\ell_\gamma\in(0,\infty)$ — a positive real number, an invariant of the **conjugacy class** of $\tau$ (conjugation moves the axis but preserves the displacement)
- $\mathrm{axis}(\tau)$ — a complete geodesic in $\mathbb{H}^2$, preserved setwise by $\tau$ and by everything commuting with it
- $\mathcal{P}_X$ — a countable set, with $N_X(R)=\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X : \ell_\gamma\leq R\}<\infty$ for every $R$

# Example

$\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell}z$, the [[Constr - Standard-Form Representative and the Fundamental Strip|standard form]] used throughout §3. Its matrix in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ is $\begin{pmatrix}e^{\ell/2}&0\\0&e^{-\ell/2}\end{pmatrix}$, with $|\operatorname{tr}|=2\cosh(\ell/2)>2$; its fixed points are $0$ and $\infty$; its axis is the imaginary half-line. For $z=iy$ on the axis, $d(iy,\tau(iy))=d(iy,ie^{\ell}y)=\ell$, and for $z$ off the axis the displacement is strictly larger — the displacement function $z\mapsto d(z,\tau z)$ is a strictly convex function of the distance to the axis. So $\ell_\gamma=\ell$ and the minimum is attained exactly on the axis. Every hyperbolic element is conjugate in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ to one of this form, which is what licenses the standard-form normalisation.

**Near-miss non-example.** The parabolic $z\mapsto z+1$ has $|\operatorname{tr}|=2$, one fixed point ($\infty$), no axis, and $\inf_z d(z,z+1)=0$ **not attained** — the displacement tends to $0$ as $\operatorname{Im}(z)\to\infty$. So a parabolic has no translation length, its class contains no closed geodesic, and every formula in §3 involving $\ell_\gamma$ is meaningless for it. This is the group-theoretic face of "peripheral", and it is why parabolic classes are excluded by the standing convention rather than handled separately.

**Second near-miss.** $\tau^2$ for $\tau$ primitive is hyperbolic with translation length $2\ell_\gamma$, same axis, and is **not** primitive. Its centraliser in $\Gamma$ is $\langle\tau\rangle$ — generated by $\tau$, not by $\tau^2$ — which is precisely the point exploited on the next page. Getting this wrong would over-count the conjugacy class by a factor of $2$.

# Where this sits in my DAG

The classification of isometries of $\mathbb{H}^2$ by trace, the existence of the axis, and the identity $|\operatorname{tr}\tau|=2\cosh(\ell_\gamma/2)$ are anchors via the Riemannian-geometry strand together with [[Def - The Hyperbolic Space H^n]]; they are also thoroughly standard and quoted from Katok or Buser. Conjugation and powers in a group are elementary — [[Def - Conjugacy Class]].

The one fact worth flagging as quoted rather than derived: **that an elliptic element of a discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ has finite order**, which is what makes "torsion-free" equivalent to "no elliptics" and hence to "the action is free". It is standard and its proof is short, but it is the hinge on which [[Def - Fuchsian Group and the Quotient Surface]]'s manifold-versus-orbifold distinction turns.
