# Notation

- $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}:=\min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X}\ell_\gamma$ — the systole
- $N_{\mathrm{sys}}:=\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X : \ell_\gamma=\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}\}$ — its multiplicity
- $\mathcal{P}_X$ — the primitive **oriented** closed geodesics; $N_X(R)$ the counting function
- $\bar\gamma$ — the orientation reversal of $\gamma$

---

# In plain language

The **systole** is the length of the shortest closed geodesic on $X$. The minimum is attained rather than merely approached, because $N_X(R)<\infty$ for every $R$ — only finitely many geodesics are shorter than any given bound — and $\mathcal{P}_X$ is non-empty for a surface with non-abelian fundamental group.

Two uses in the paper, and they are quite different.

**As a uniform bound.** In [[Thm - Finiteness of the Total Mass|Corollary 4.7]], the systole supplies the estimate $e^L-1\geq(1-e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})e^L$ valid for every $L\geq\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, which converts the awkward factor $1/(e^L-1)$ into a constant multiple of $e^{-L}$. That is what reduces the convergence question to $\sum_\gamma e^{-s\ell_\gamma}$, where the prime geodesic theorem can be applied. The systole enters only through the constant, and its precise value is irrelevant.

**As the limit of the typical length.** In [[Thm - Concentration on Systolic Classes|§6.1]], as the killing rate grows the probability measure $\mathbb{P}_s$ concentrates on the *shortest* classes, so $\mathbb{E}_s[L]\to\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and the systole becomes recoverable from the asymptotics of $-\log Z_X(s)$. Here its precise value is the whole point.

**The multiplicity is at least two, and the reason is orientation.** $\mathcal{P}_X$ consists of *oriented* primitive closed geodesics, and a hyperbolic element of a torsion-free Fuchsian group is never conjugate to its inverse — an element conjugating $\tau$ to $\tau^{-1}$ would have to reverse the axis, which no orientation-preserving element of a torsion-free discrete group does. So $\gamma$ and $\bar\gamma$ are distinct elements of $\mathcal{P}_X$ of equal length, and $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\geq2$ always. **This is why the $s\to\infty$ limit of $\mathbb{P}_s$ is uniform on at least two atoms rather than a point mass.**

# The definition

> **Definition (systole and its multiplicity).** For a hyperbolic surface $X$ with $\mathcal{P}_X\neq\emptyset$, the **systole** is
> $$\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} := \min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X}\ell_\gamma,$$
> the length of the shortest closed geodesic, and its **multiplicity** is
> $$N_{\mathrm{sys}} := \#\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X : \ell_\gamma=\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}\}\;\geq\;2.$$

The minimum is attained because $N_X(R)=\#\{\gamma : \ell_\gamma\leq R\}$ is finite for every $R$, so only finitely many geodesics compete. The bound $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\geq2$ holds because $\mathcal{P}_X$ consists of oriented geodesics and $\gamma\neq\bar\gamma$ in $\mathcal{P}_X$, since a hyperbolic element of a torsion-free Fuchsian group is never conjugate to its inverse.

---

# Types and signatures

- $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}\in(0,\infty)$ — a positive real number, attained
- $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\in\{2,3,4,\dots\}$ — a finite integer, at least $2$, always even when every systolic geodesic is non-self-reverse
- $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ — invariants of the **metric**, not of the topology; they move as the point of Teichmüller space moves

---

# Example

The hyperbolic cylinder $\langle\tau\rangle\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ with $\tau : z\mapsto e^\ell z$. Its primitive oriented closed geodesics are the core geodesic and its reverse, both of length $\ell$; every other class is a proper power and has length $m\ell>\ell$. So $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}=\ell$ and $N_{\mathrm{sys}}=2$ — the minimum possible, and it is achieved by the orientation pair alone.

**Near-miss non-example — forgetting orientation.** If one counted *unoriented* closed geodesics, the cylinder would have $N_{\mathrm{sys}}=1$, and the $s\to\infty$ limit of $\mathbb{P}_s$ would be a point mass. That would be wrong: the loop measure is a measure on **oriented** loops, by the construction of [[Def - The Space of Unrooted Unparametrised Loops]], whose equivalence relation uses *increasing* reparametrisations only. The factor of $2$ is not bookkeeping — it is the same orientation-sensitivity that makes free homotopy classes correspond to conjugacy classes in the first place.

**Second near-miss — a surface with no closed geodesic.** $\mathbb{H}^2$ itself, or a hyperbolic surface with $\Gamma$ generated by a single parabolic (a cusp with no core geodesic), has $\mathcal{P}_X=\emptyset$ and no systole. Every result in §4 and §6 tacitly assumes $\mathcal{P}_X\neq\emptyset$, which holds whenever $\Gamma$ contains a hyperbolic element — that is, for every surface with a non-peripheral non-trivial class, which is exactly the standing hypothesis.

# Where this sits in my DAG

Reduces to [[Def - Primitive Hyperbolic Element and Translation Length]] for the notion of geodesic length, and to [[Def - Free Homotopy Class and Conjugacy Class Correspondence]] for the orientation-sensitivity of $\mathcal{P}_X$ and hence for $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\geq2$. Finiteness of $N_X(R)$ comes from discreteness of $\Gamma$ and is quantified by [[Def - Critical Exponent and the Prime Geodesic Theorem]].

The fact quoted rather than derived is that **a hyperbolic element of a torsion-free Fuchsian group is never conjugate to its inverse.** It is short — a conjugating element would preserve the axis while swapping its endpoints, hence be an orientation-reversing isometry or an elliptic rotation by $\pi$, and a torsion-free group of orientation-preserving isometries contains neither — but it is the entire justification for $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\geq2$, so it is worth having explicitly rather than assuming.
