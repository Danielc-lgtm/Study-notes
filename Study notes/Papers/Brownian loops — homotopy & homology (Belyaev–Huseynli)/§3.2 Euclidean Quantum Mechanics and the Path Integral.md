# Notation

**Standing conventions.** Units are chosen so that $\hbar^2/2m = 1$, and $\hbar$ is absorbed into the potential. The Laplacian is positive, $\Delta_X\geq0$, so the Hamiltonian $\widehat H = \Delta_X + V$ with $V\geq0$ is a non-negative self-adjoint operator and $e^{-t\widehat H}$ is a contraction semigroup for $t\geq0$.

- $\psi$ — the wave function; $\widehat H = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta_X + V(x)$ the Hamiltonian, self-adjoint
- $K(t,x,y) = \langle y|e^{-it\widehat H/\hbar}|x\rangle$ — the propagator, or transition amplitude
- $t = -i\tau$ — the Wick rotation; the Euclidean time $\tau$ is the diffusion time of §2, and is written $t$ from that point on
- $p^V(t,x,y)$ — the integral kernel of $e^{-t\widehat H}$: the transition density of Brownian motion killed at rate $V$
- $S[\omega] = \int_0^t\big(\tfrac14|\dot\omega(r)|^2 + V(\omega(r))\big)\,\mathrm{d}r$ — the Euclidean action; $\mathcal{D}\omega$ the formal Lebesgue measure on paths, which does not exist as a measure
- $\varphi$ — a free real scalar field of mass $\sqrt\kappa$ on $X$; $S_E[\varphi]$ its Euclidean action
- $Z^\kappa_X$ — the Euclidean partition function; $\Gamma^{(1)}_X(\kappa) = -\log Z^\kappa_X$ the one-loop effective action
- $|\mu^\kappa_X|_{\mathrm{reg}}$ — the *regularised* total mass of the killing loop measure over all loops, including the divergent contractible and peripheral contributions

# What this section is for

This is a digression, and the paper labels it as one. Skipping it costs no theorem. What it buys is the answer to a question the reader will otherwise carry unasked through §5: *why should a measure on loops have anything to do with a determinant of the Laplacian?*

The chain of identifications is short and each link is standard. **Wick rotation** turns the unitary group $e^{-it\widehat H/\hbar}$ of quantum mechanics into the contraction semigroup $e^{-t\widehat H}$ of diffusion; the propagator becomes a heat kernel; Euclidean time is diffusion time. **Feynman–Kac** makes the correspondence rigorous: $p^V(t,x,y)=\int e^{-\int_0^t V(\omega(r))\,\mathrm{d}r}\,W^t_{x\to y}(\mathrm{d}\omega)$, each Brownian bridge weighted by its probability of surviving killing along its own trajectory. For the constant potential $V=\kappa$ the weight is just $e^{-\kappa t}$, recovering $p^\kappa(t,x,y)=e^{-\kappa t}p(t,x,y)$ — which is exactly the killing case of §2.3. **So $\mu^\kappa_X$ is the intensity of the loop ensemble of a Euclidean quantum particle in a constant potential**, and the loop soup of §3.3 is that ensemble.

The second half of the section does the field-theoretic version and is where §5 gets its motivation. Take a free real scalar field $\varphi$ of mass $\sqrt\kappa$ on $X$, with Euclidean action $S_E[\varphi]=\tfrac12\int_X(|\nabla\varphi|^2+\kappa\varphi^2)\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g = \tfrac12\langle\varphi,(\Delta_X+\kappa)\varphi\rangle$. The action is quadratic, so the path integral is Gaussian and gives $Z^\kappa_X = \int\mathcal{D}\varphi\,e^{-S_E[\varphi]}\propto\det(\Delta_X+\kappa)^{-1/2}$; the one-loop effective action is $\Gamma^{(1)}_X(\kappa)=\tfrac12\log\det(\Delta_X+\kappa)$. Express that determinant through the heat semigroup by the [[Def - Schwinger Proper-Time Representation|Schwinger proper-time representation]],
$$-\log\det(\Delta_X+\kappa) = \int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,e^{-\kappa t}\operatorname{Tr}\big(e^{-t\Delta_X}\big),$$
and when $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ is trace class, $\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_X}) = \int_X p(t,x,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g(x)$ — the heat trace is built out of Brownian paths that start and return to the same point.

Now compare structures. The right-hand side integrates a diagonal heat kernel over $X$, then over $t$ with the Haar weight $\mathrm{d}t/t$ and the killing weight $e^{-\kappa t}$. **That is, term for term, the definition of the Brownian loop measure with killing.** So
$$-\log\det(\Delta_X+\kappa) = \big|\mu^\kappa_X\big|_{\mathrm{reg}},$$
the total mass of the killing loop measure over *all* loops — including the regularised divergent contribution of the contractible class, and of the peripheral classes when present. Equivalently
$$Z^\kappa_X \propto \exp\Big(\tfrac12\big|\mu^\kappa_X\big|_{\mathrm{reg}}\Big),$$
so the partition function of a free real scalar field of mass $\sqrt\kappa$ is, up to normalisation, the exponential of *half* the regularised total mass of Brownian loops on $X$ with killing rate $\kappa$. The factor $\tfrac12$ is the power $\det^{-1/2}$ of a single real field, and it is worth checking that against the counting: a complex field would give $\det^{-1}$ and no factor of a half.

The word doing the work in every displayed equation above is **regularised**. The mass is infinite, for the reason §2.1 identified — small loops. §5 is the business of making the regularisation precise, and its choice of truncation (by the length spectrum, following Wang–Xue) is one of several possible ones; the alternative in the literature truncates by quadratic variation instead.

# Remark 3.3 — the homotopy theorem for path integrals

The decomposition of §3 has a physical reading too, and this is the other half of the digression.

For a path integral on a topological space $X$, the *homotopy theorem for path integration* writes a transition amplitude as
$$K = \sum_{[\alpha]\in\pi_1(X,x)}\chi(\alpha)\,K_\alpha,$$
a sum over homotopy classes of a partial amplitude $K_\alpha$ from paths in the class $[\alpha]$, weighted by a unitary character $\chi$ of $\pi_1(X,x)$. §3's decomposition is the Wick-rotated version of this: the masses $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$ play the role of the partial amplitudes, conjugacy classes replace based classes because the loops are unrooted, and the character is trivial, $\chi\equiv1$.

On a multiply connected space — a hyperbolic surface of genus $g$ certainly qualifies — different choices of $\chi$ give physically *different* theories, not different presentations of one theory. The corresponding move on the probabilistic side is to twist the loop measure by a flat unitary bundle on $X$, equivalently by a unitary representation of $\pi_1(X)$, which replaces the periodisation $p^{\mathcal{E}}_X=\sum_h p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z,h\tilde w)$ by its twisted form $\sum_h \chi(h)\,p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z,h\tilde w)$.

**This is not idle: the paper does exactly this twice later.** [[Thm - Twisted Ruelle Zeta Identity|Corollary 4.6]] twists by a finite-dimensional representation $\rho$ of $\Gamma$ and produces the twisted Ruelle zeta function; [[Def - Selberg L-Function|§6.2]] twists by a unitary character of $H_1(X,\mathbb{Z})$ — a one-dimensional abelian representation — and produces the Selberg $L$-function, which is what makes the homology decomposition computable at all. So the correct way to read §6 is: *it is the $\chi\neq1$ case of Remark 3.3*.

For the graph and compact-surface versions, the paper points to Le Jan's *Random walks and physical fields* for physical interpretations of twisted loop measures, and to Perruchaud–Sauzedde for the detailed mathematical construction.

# What to carry forward

**The identification $-\log\det(\Delta_X+\kappa) = |\mu^\kappa_X|_{\mathrm{reg}}$**, and the fact that it is *formal* until §5 supplies a regularisation. This is the reason §5 exists and the reason killing rates rather than plain Brownian motion are the natural parameter throughout §4–§6.

**That the killing loop measure is the loop ensemble of a Euclidean quantum particle in a constant potential**, via Feynman–Kac with $V=\kappa$. This makes the loop soup of §3.3 a physically meaningful object rather than a probabilistic convenience.

**That twisting by a character is the general form of the decomposition**, with §3 the trivial-character case and §6 the abelian-character case. If you read only one paragraph of this section, read Remark 3.3.
