# Exports

> **(E1)** For $V\equiv\kappa$, $\;p_\kappa(t,x,y)=e^{-\kappa t}p(t,x,y)$. Hence $\mu^\kappa_X$ is the intensity of the loop ensemble of a Euclidean quantum particle in a constant potential. *(From [[Ext - Feynman–Kac Formula|(FK)(F1)]]; recovers Example 2.6.)*
>
> **(E2)** $\mathcal{D}\omega$ **does not exist** as a measure on path space. The rigorous content of the Euclidean path integral is (FK), where the kinetic part of the action sits inside $W^t_{x\to y}$ and only $e^{-\int V}$ is an explicit weight.
>
> **(E3)** Formally, $-\log\det(\Delta_X+\kappa)=\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}e^{-\kappa t}\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_X})=\lvert\mu^\kappa_X\rvert_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Divergent at $t\downarrow0$; regularised in §5.
>
> **(E4)** $Z^\kappa_X\propto\exp\big(\tfrac12\lvert\mu^\kappa_X\rvert_{\mathrm{reg}}\big)$ for a free real scalar field of mass $\sqrt\kappa$ on $X$, with $\kappa=m^2>0$.

# Commentary

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> The digression answers a question a reader is entitled to ask after Theorem 3.5: *why should anyone care about $\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}$ against a heat kernel?* The answer is that this is, verbatim, the Schwinger proper-time representation of a one-loop determinant — and the "loop" of "one-loop" is the same loop the measure is built from.
>
> The chain is short. A quantum particle in a potential has propagator $\langle y\rvert e^{-it\widehat H/\hbar}\lvert x\rangle$; Wick rotation turns it into a heat kernel with killing; (FK) writes that heat kernel as a weighted bridge integral; integrating the diagonal against $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g$ and $\mathrm{d}t/t$ is the loop measure; and the same integral is the proper-time representation of $\log\det$. Every arrow is standard. What is not standard is reading the *decomposition* of that total mass over free homotopy classes, which is the actual content of the paper — the physics literature computes the total, §3 computes the summands.
>
> Two honest caveats the paper itself flags. The Schwinger integral diverges at short proper time, at the rate $\mathrm{vol}_g(X)/4\pi t^2$, so (E3) is an equality between two undefined things until §5 fixes both sides. And the field-theory packaging is one *real* scalar; a complex field would give $\det^{-1}$ and no factor $\tfrac12$.
