# Notation

- $\mathbb{H}^2=\{z\in\mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}$ — the hyperbolic plane in the upper half-plane model, with metric $|\mathrm{d}z|^2/\operatorname{Im}(z)^2$ and area measure $\rho=\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$
- $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\{\pm I\}$ — the orientation-preserving isometry group of $\mathbb{H}^2$, acting by Möbius transformations $z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}$
- $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ — a Fuchsian group; assumed torsion-free throughout
- $X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ — the quotient surface; $\pi:\mathbb{H}^2\to X$ the projection; $\rho_X$ the induced area measure
- $F\subset\mathbb{H}^2$ — a fundamental region for $\Gamma$
- $\Lambda(\Gamma)\subset\partial\mathbb{H}^2$ — the limit set

---

# In plain language

A Fuchsian group is a discrete group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic plane. Quotienting $\mathbb{H}^2$ by one produces a hyperbolic surface, and — when the group is torsion-free, so that the action is free — that quotient is a smooth surface with $\mathbb{H}^2$ as its universal cover and $\Gamma$ as its deck group.

That last sentence is the entire reason the paper works. It means every topological question about loops on $X$ becomes a group-theoretic question about $\Gamma$, and every analytic object on $X$ (heat kernel, loop measure) can be written as a $\Gamma$-indexed sum of objects upstairs. §3 is nothing but the systematic exploitation of that translation.

Two conditions on the action, and both are load-bearing. **Properly discontinuous** is what makes the quotient a reasonable space at all — orbits do not accumulate, so the quotient topology is Hausdorff and the projection is a covering map. **Free** — no non-identity element has a fixed point — is what makes the quotient a *manifold* rather than an orbifold, and it is equivalent to torsion-freeness for a discrete group of hyperbolic isometries. If $\Gamma$ had an element of finite order, that element would fix a point, the quotient would have a cone point there, and the covering-space dictionary would fail exactly at that point.

# The definition

> **Definition (Fuchsian group).** A **Fuchsian group** is a discrete subgroup $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$. Discreteness in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ is equivalent to the action on $\mathbb{H}^2$ being **properly discontinuous**: every compact $K\subset\mathbb{H}^2$ meets $hK$ for only finitely many $h\in\Gamma$.

> **Definition (the quotient surface).** Let $\Gamma$ be **torsion-free**, so that the action on $\mathbb{H}^2$ is additionally **free**: $hz=z$ for some $z$ forces $h=\mathrm{id}$. Then the quotient
> $$X := \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$$
> is a smooth complete orientable hyperbolic surface, the projection $\pi:\mathbb{H}^2\to X$ is a covering map, and $\mathbb{H}^2$ is the universal cover of $X$ with deck transformation group $\Gamma$.

The hyperbolic area measure descends: $\rho_X$ is defined so that $\int_X(\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_X = \int_F(\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ for any fundamental region $F$ of $\Gamma$. **That identity is the "unfolding" step used in [[Thm - General Homotopy Class Decomposition for Hyperbolic Surfaces|Theorem 3.2]]**, and it is worth recognising it here as a definition rather than meeting it as a trick there.

# Types and signatures

- $\Gamma$ — a countable discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$; countable because discrete in a second-countable group
- the action $\Gamma\times\mathbb{H}^2\to\mathbb{H}^2$ — by isometries, free and properly discontinuous
- $\pi : \mathbb{H}^2\to X$ — a covering map, and a local isometry
- $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$, $\rho_X$ — measures on $\mathbb{H}^2$ and on $X$; $\rho_X(X)=\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(F)$, possibly infinite
- $F\subset\mathbb{H}^2$ — a fundamental region: a Borel set meeting each $\Gamma$-orbit exactly once, up to a $\rho$-null set

---

# Example

The simplest non-trivial case, and the one that makes §3 concrete. Take $\Gamma=\langle\tau\rangle$ with $\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell}z$ for a fixed $\ell>0$ — an infinite cyclic group generated by a single hyperbolic translation along the imaginary axis. It is discrete, torsion-free, and acts freely. The quotient $X=\langle\tau\rangle\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ is a **hyperbolic cylinder**: it has exactly one primitive closed geodesic, the image of the imaginary axis, of length $\ell$, together with its iterates and its orientation reversal. A fundamental region is the horizontal band $\{1\leq\operatorname{Im}(z)<e^\ell\}$ — which is precisely the [[Constr - Standard-Form Representative and the Fundamental Strip|fundamental strip]] $F_\tau$ that Theorem 3.2 integrates over. **This example is not incidental: §3's whole strategy is to reduce a general $\Gamma$ to this cylinder, one primitive geodesic at a time.**

**Near-miss non-example.** Take $\Gamma$ generated by an elliptic element — a rotation by $2\pi/n$ about a point $z_0\in\mathbb{H}^2$. This is discrete and acts properly discontinuously, so it is a Fuchsian group; but it has torsion (the generator has order $n$) and does not act freely (it fixes $z_0$). The quotient is a hyperbolic *orbifold* with a cone point of angle $2\pi/n$: not a manifold, not covered by $\mathbb{H}^2$ in the deck-group sense near that point, and the correspondence between free homotopy classes and conjugacy classes breaks there. Excluding torsion is what buys the whole of §3.
