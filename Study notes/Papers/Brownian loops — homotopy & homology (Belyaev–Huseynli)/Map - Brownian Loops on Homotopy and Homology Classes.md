# What this paper does

There is a $\sigma$-finite measure on the space of loops in a Riemannian surface — the Brownian loop measure — which assigns a mass to any measurable family of loops without ever normalising to a probability. On a hyperbolic surface $X = \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}^2$ the loops sort themselves into free homotopy classes, and each class is a measurable family, so each class has a mass. The paper computes that mass in closed form, and it turns out to depend on the class only through the length $m\ell_\gamma$ of its closed geodesic representative. Summing the masses over all classes produces $-\log Z_X(s)$, the logarithm of the Selberg zeta function. Because that sum is finite exactly when the spectral parameter $s$ exceeds the critical exponent $\delta$, one can divide by it, and the masses become a genuine probability measure on free homotopy classes whose every moment is a derivative of $\log Z_X$.

The paper does all of this not for Brownian motion alone but for any process obtained from it by [[Def - Subordinator and Subordination of a Semigroup|subordination]] — running the diffusion on the clock of an independent increasing Lévy process. The device that makes this cost one line rather than one paper per case is the [[Constr - The Weighted Potential Measure Vϕ|weighted potential measure]] $V_\phi$, which absorbs the whole subordination into a single measure on $(0,\infty)$. The central identity of the paper, from which almost everything downstream follows, is

$$\mu_X^\phi\big(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)} \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-s/4}\, e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}\, V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s), \qquad L = m\ell_\gamma .$$

In words: the mass of loops winding $m$ times around the primitive geodesic $\gamma$ is a purely geometric prefactor $\ell_\gamma / 2\sinh(L/2)$ multiplied by a purely analytic integral in which the entire choice of process sits inside $V_\phi$. Change the process and only $V_\phi$ changes. That is the whole architecture of §3 onwards.

**Source.** `paper_source/A PROBABILITY MEASURE ON HOMOTOPY & HOMOLOGY CLASSES VIA BROWNIAN LOOPS (1).pdf` — Dmitry Belyaev and Farhad Huseynli, *A Probability Measure on Homotopy & Homology Classes via Brownian Loops*.

# Notation registry

**Standing conventions.** The Laplacian is the *positive* one, $\Delta_X = -\operatorname{div}_g \operatorname{grad}_g$, so its spectrum lies in $[0,\infty)$ and $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ is a contraction semigroup; this is the opposite sign to the analyst's $\Delta = \partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2$ and is worth checking every time a formula is compared against another source. Brownian motion is run at **speed 2**, meaning it is the diffusion generated by $-\Delta_X$ rather than by $-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_X$; this is what puts the $e^{-s/4}$ rather than $e^{-s/8}$ in the heat-kernel factor above. Where $\partial X \neq \emptyset$, Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed, so the process is killed on first hitting the boundary. The spectral parameter $s$ and the killing rate $\kappa$ are always linked by $s = \tfrac{1}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac14 + \kappa}$, equivalently $\kappa = s(s-1)$; when one appears, the other is implied.

## §2.1–2.2 Loop measures → [[§2.1–2.2 Loop Measures — Brownian and Dirichlet Form]]

- **[[Constr - The Brownian Loop Measure|Definition 2.1 — the Brownian loop measure]]** — *Given* a complete orientable Riemannian surface $(X,g)$, its heat kernel and its unnormalised bridge measures. *Produces* a $\sigma$-finite measure $\mu_X$ on the space of unrooted unparametrised oriented loops, of infinite total mass. *Lets you* assign a mass to a family of loops — in particular to a free homotopy class — without any probability normalisation, and inherit two structural properties (restriction, conformal invariance) that no probability measure on paths would give you.
- **[[Constr - The Dirichlet-Form Loop Measure|Definition 2.2 — the Dirichlet-form loop measure]]** — *Given* a regular symmetric Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ whose semigroup has a jointly measurable symmetric transition density. *Produces* a $\sigma$-finite measure $\mu_X^{\mathcal{E}}$ on càdlàg loops. *Lets you* run the whole construction for jump processes as well as diffusions, at the cost of losing conformal invariance.

## §2.3–2.4 Subordination → [[§2.3–2.4 Subordination and the Weighted Potential Measure]]

- **[[Constr - Assumption 2.3 (Strictly Increasing Subordinator)|Assumption 2.3]]** — *Given* a Bernstein function $\phi$ with triple $(a,b,\nu)$. *Produces* the standing hypothesis $b > 0$ or $\nu(0,\infty) = \infty$, equivalently $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\}) = 0$ for every $t > 0$. *Lets you* assume the subordinate semigroup has a transition density, which every formula in §3 onwards silently uses; it is exactly what excludes compound Poisson subordinators.
- **[[Constr - The Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure|Definition 2.8 — subordinate Brownian loop measure]]** — *Given* a Bernstein function $\phi$ and the subordinate Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E}^\phi, \mathcal{F}^\phi)$. *Produces* the $\sigma$-finite loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$ on $\mathcal{C}_X$. *Lets you* treat Brownian motion, Brownian motion with killing, and the $\alpha$-stable processes as four instances of one object rather than four separate calculations.
- **[[Constr - The Weighted Potential Measure Vϕ|Definition 2.9 — the weighted potential measure]]** — *Given* a Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3. *Produces* a $\sigma$-finite measure $V_\phi$ on $(0,\infty)$, absolutely continuous in every case treated. *Lets you* replace the double integral $\int_0^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_{[0,\infty)} \cdots \psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$ by the single integral $\int_{(0,\infty)} \cdots V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$; this is the assumption carried through every theorem of §3.
- **[[Thm - Collapsing the Time Integral into the Weighted Potential Measure|Lemma 2.11]]** — *Given* a Bernstein function $\phi$, its subordinate transition density $p^\phi$ and the base density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$. *Produces* the identity $\int_0^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,p^\phi(t,x,y) = \int_{(0,\infty)} p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,x,y)\,V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$, valid for every $x,y \in X$. *Lets you* perform the collapse in one step wherever a $\mathrm{d}t/t$ integral meets a subordinated kernel — the workhorse of both Theorem 3.5 and Theorem 7.2.

## §3, §3.1, §3.4 Homotopy-class decomposition → [[§3 Decomposition over Homotopy Classes]]

- **[[Constr - The Periodised Kernel|Equation (11) — the periodised kernel]]** — *Given* a $\Gamma$-invariant kernel $p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ on the universal cover with fast enough decay to beat the orbit growth of $\Gamma$. *Produces* the kernel $p^{\mathcal{E}}_X(t,z,w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma} p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde w)$ on the quotient, independent of the lifts chosen. *Lets you* index the terms of the downstairs heat kernel by deck transformations, which is what makes a *topological* decomposition of an *analytic* object possible at all.
- **[[Constr - Standard-Form Representative and the Fundamental Strip|Equations (9) and (12) — standard form and the strip]]** — *Given* a primitive hyperbolic conjugacy class in $\Gamma$ with translation length $\ell_\gamma$. *Produces* a representative $\tau : z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$ with axis the imaginary half-line, and the fundamental strip $F_\tau = \{z \in \mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \leq \operatorname{Im}(z) < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$, a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$. *Lets you* turn an unmanageable integral over a fundamental region for $\Gamma$ into an explicit integral over a horizontal band.
- **[[Constr - Loop Mass in a Homotopy Class for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1 — jump-process convention]]** — *Given* a pure-jump subordinate process, whose càdlàg sample loops have no free homotopy class. *Produces* a *definition* (13) of $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$ as the periodisation restricted to the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. *Lets you* state Theorems 3.5 and 7.2 uniformly across diffusions and jump processes — at the price that in the jump case the left-hand side is a definition, not a measured quantity.
- **[[Thm - General Homotopy Class Decomposition for Hyperbolic Surfaces|Theorem 3.2]]** — *Given* a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form whose kernel periodises, a primitive closed geodesic $\gamma$ with standard-form representative $\tau$, and a winding number $m \geq 1$. *Produces* the identity $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_{F_\tau} p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,\tau^m z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z)$ — a non-negative number, possibly infinite. *Lets you* compute a homotopy-class mass upstairs on $\mathbb{H}^2$ against a single group element, with the group summed away.
- **[[Thm - The Wang–Xue Fundamental-Strip Identity|Lemma 3.4 (Wang–Xue)]]** — *Given* $s > 0$, $m \geq 1$ and the hyperbolic Brownian heat kernel. *Produces* the closed form $\int_{F_\tau} p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(s,z,e^{L}z)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)}\cdot\frac{e^{-s/4}e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}$, a positive real number. *Lets you* discharge the spatial integral of Theorem 3.2 completely, leaving only a one-dimensional integral in the time variable.
- **[[Thm - Mass of the Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure on Surfaces|Theorem 3.5]]** — *Given* a Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3, a primitive closed geodesic $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ and a winding number $m \geq 1$. *Produces* a closed-form value for $\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$, a non-negative number, as a single integral of an explicit heat-kernel factor against $V_\phi$. *Lets you* replace the double $(t,s)$ integral by one integral against $V_\phi$, which is what makes every later special case a one-line substitution.
- **[[Constr - The Weighted Heat-Kernel Integral Iϕ|Definition 3.6 — the integral Iϕ]]** — *Given* $\phi$ and a length $L > 0$. *Produces* the number $I_\phi(L) = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-s/4}e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$, so that Theorem 3.5 reads $\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)}I_\phi(L)$. *Lets you* state the Selberg zeta criterion of Lemma 4.2 as a condition on a single function of one variable.
- **[[Def - Polar Set|Polar sets]]** and **[[Thm - Length-Spectrum Identity under Puncturing|Theorem 3.9 (Wang–Xue)]]** — *Given* a complete hyperbolic surface $X$ without boundary and a non-empty closed polar set $P \subset X$, with $X' = X \setminus P$ carrying its own complete hyperbolic metric. *Produces* the identity $\frac{1}{m}\frac{1}{e^{m\ell_\gamma}-1} = \sum \frac{1}{m'}\frac{1}{e^{m'\ell_{\gamma'}}-1}$ between geodesic length spectra of two different surfaces. *Lets you* trade conformal invariance of $\mu_X$ for an exact relation between length spectra — and shows why the identity collapses to a triviality for any nonlinear subordination.
- **[[Def - Marked Length Spectrum|Definition 3.10]]** and **[[Thm - Loop Masses Determine the Marked Length Spectrum|Proposition 3.11]]** — *Given* the mass $\mu_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma))$, or $\mu^\kappa_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma))$ for $\kappa \geq -\tfrac14$. *Produces* the geodesic length as $\ell_\gamma = \log(1 + 1/\mu_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma)))$, or via strict monotonicity in the killing case. *Lets you* recover the entire marked length spectrum from the loop masses — an information-theoretic statement that the masses lose nothing.
- **[[Thm - Loop Masses Determine the Hyperbolic Surface|Corollary 3.12]]** — *Given* two hyperbolic metrics $g_1,g_2$ on a closed surface $X$ with equal loop masses in every class, and $\kappa \geq -\tfrac14$. *Produces* an isometry between them isotopic to the identity, hence equality of points in Teichmüller space. *Lets you* read the loop masses as a complete invariant of the marked hyperbolic structure.

## §4 Zeta identities and finiteness → [[§4 Zeta Identities and Finiteness of the Total Mass]]

- **[[Def - Selberg Zeta Function|Definition 4.1]]**, **[[Def - Critical Exponent and the Prime Geodesic Theorem|the critical exponent δ]]** — the objects the rest of §4 is stated in.
- **[[Thm - Selberg Zeta Criterion|Lemma 4.2]]** — *Given* the function $I_\phi$ and constants $C > 0$, $s > \delta$ independent of $L$ with $\frac{L}{2\sinh(L/2)}I_\phi(L) = C\frac{e^{(1-s)L}}{e^L - 1}$ for all $L > 0$. *Produces* the identity $\sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)) = -C\log Z_X(s)$. *Lets you* certify a zeta identity for a new Bernstein function by checking one scalar functional equation — no geometry required.
- **[[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity (Killing Case)|Corollary 4.3]]** — *Given* a killing rate $\kappa \geq -\tfrac14$, set $s = \tfrac12 + \sqrt{\tfrac14+\kappa}$, and assume $s > \delta$. *Produces* the identity: total mass of the killing loop measure over all non-trivial classes $= -\log Z_X(s)$. *Lets you* read the total loop mass straight off the Selberg zeta function, and at $\kappa = 0$, $s = 1$ recover the Brownian total mass as $-\log Z_X(1)$.
- **[[Def - Ruelle Zeta Function and its Twist|Definition 4.5]]** and **[[Thm - Twisted Ruelle Zeta Identity|Corollary 4.6]]** — *Given* a finite-dimensional representation $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}(V_\rho)$ and $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \max(c_\rho, \tfrac12)$. *Produces* $-\log R_X(s,\rho)$ as a $\rho$-weighted sum of *differences* $\mu^{\kappa_-(s)}_X - \mu^{\kappa_+(s)}_X$ of loop masses at the two killing rates $s(s-1)$ and $s(s+1)$. *Lets you* see which zeta functions the loop measure reaches, and why the Selberg one is the natural one: the Ruelle identity needs a difference of two loop measures where Selberg needs one.
- **[[Thm - Finiteness of the Total Mass|Corollary 4.7]]** — *Given* any of the paper's Bernstein functions with spectral parameter $s(\phi)$, and the prime geodesic theorem for $\Gamma$. *Produces* finiteness of $\sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\phi_X(\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m))$ exactly when $s(\phi) > \delta$. *Lets you* know in advance whether a normalisation is available: infinite-area surfaces have $\delta < 1$ and need nothing, finite-area surfaces have $\delta = 1$ and need either a killing rate or the renormalisation of §5.

# Local prerequisite DAG

The paper stands on four pillars: heat kernels and semigroups on Riemannian manifolds; measure-theoretic probability with Brownian bridges and disintegration; the covering-space picture of a hyperbolic quotient, which converts topology into group theory; and the analytic theory of the Selberg zeta function. The first two are anchors outright. The third bottoms out in covering-space theory and elementary group theory, both of which reduce quickly. The fourth is the deepest branch: the Selberg zeta function, the critical exponent, the prime geodesic theorem and the zeta-regularised determinant form a chain that reduces to complex analysis and spectral theory but does not reduce to anything more elementary within this paper.

The full backchain, with every leaf either marked as an anchor or linked to a page in this folder, is on [[Prereq DAG - Brownian Loops on Homotopy and Homology Classes]]. Read that page before the section pages — it is the only page whose purpose is to tell you whether the floor is solid.

# Suggested reading order

There are three useful passes, and they are genuinely different activities.

**First pass — the spine, in twenty minutes.** Read only the type index above, top to bottom, plus the "What this paper does" paragraph. Do not open a section page. The point is to leave with the shape: a loop measure exists; it decomposes over homotopy classes; each class's mass is a geometric prefactor times an analytic integral; the analytic integral is where the choice of process lives; summing over classes gives the Selberg zeta function; dividing by that sum gives a probability measure; the whole thing transports to three dimensions. If after this pass you cannot say why $V_\phi$ exists, re-read the two §2.3–2.4 cards — everything downstream depends on that single collapse.

**Second pass — the sections, in order.** [[§2.1–2.2 Loop Measures — Brownian and Dirichlet Form]] then [[§2.3–2.4 Subordination and the Weighted Potential Measure]] are genuinely prerequisite and should be read in full; they are short and they set every convention. [[§3 Decomposition over Homotopy Classes]] is the heart of the paper and deserves the most time. After §3, the sections fan out and can be read in any order: [[§4 Zeta Identities and Finiteness of the Total Mass|§4]] is the analytic payoff, [[§5 Zeta-Regularised Determinants and the Polyakov Anomaly|§5]] the renormalisation, [[§6 Probability Measures on Homotopy and Homology Classes|§6]] the probabilistic payoff, [[§7 Brownian Loops on Hyperbolic 3-Manifolds|§7]] the extension.

**Skippable on a first reading.** [[§3.2 Euclidean Quantum Mechanics and the Path Integral]] is a digression and is labelled as one in the paper; skipping it costs you the physical motivation for why killing rates and determinants belong in the same story, which is worth having but is not used in any proof. [[§3.3 The Loop Soup and its Poissonian Structure]] is two paragraphs and one proposition whose proof is one line; read it whenever you want, but read it before §6.2, which uses the soup. §5.2 (the non-compact case) can be deferred until you actually care about cusps.

**Where dropping into a proof pays.** Three places, and only three. The proof of [[Thm - General Homotopy Class Decomposition for Hyperbolic Surfaces|Theorem 3.2]] — the unfolding over cosets of the cyclic centraliser is the one genuinely structural argument in the paper, and it recurs verbatim in §7. The proof of [[Thm - Finiteness of the Total Mass|Corollary 4.7]] — the integration by parts against the counting function is the template for every "does this converge" question here. And the derivation of the [[Thm - The H3 Fundamental-Slab Heat-Kernel Identity|ℍ³ slab identity]] — it is the paper's one original computation, and it is short. Everything else can be taken on faith from its type card and strategy line.

# What this paper leaves open

The paper's own stated open problem is an **intrinsic geometrisation of a càdlàg loop** ([[Constr - Loop Mass in a Homotopy Class for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1]]). For a pure-jump subordinate process the free homotopy class is not recoverable from the càdlàg path: the deleted segments can be filled in by continuous paths of differing monodromy. The paper defines the class-mass by fiat, on the marked space carrying the pair $(B,S)$, and conjectures that any canonical continuous interpolation of the jumps would break the closed-form mass formulas. That is a concrete question — is there an interpolation scheme, and does it necessarily break (90)?

Two things the note-set noticed. First, the $\alpha$-stable case **collapses** to a constant multiple of the Brownian case, $\mu^\alpha_X = \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\mu_X$, and the paper explains why: self-similar subordinators are exactly the stable ones, the weight $\mathrm{d}t/t$ is itself scale-invariant, and the only measure on $(0,\infty)$ compatible with both scalings is a constant multiple of $\mathrm{d}s/s$. This is a genuine obstruction, not an accident of the calculation, and it says that *any* scale-invariant subordination is invisible to this construction. The shifted $\alpha$-stable case $\phi(\lambda) = (\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ breaks the scaling and is the paper's own suggested repair — but note that it produces the same functional form as killing, with an extra $\alpha/2$; so the search for a subordination whose loop mass has a genuinely different *shape* in $L$ is still open.

Second, §7 stops short. Having transported the homotopy-class decomposition to $\mathbb{H}^3$ and computed the mass, the paper does not run §4–§6 in three dimensions: there is no Selberg-zeta identity, no finiteness criterion, no probability measure on homotopy classes of a hyperbolic 3-manifold. The obstruction is visible in [[Thm - Mass of Brownian Loop Measure in a Class on 3-Manifolds|Corollary 7.3]] — the mass is $\frac1m|e^{mL_\gamma}-1|^{-2}$, which is not of the form $\frac{C}{m}\frac{e^{(1-s)L}}{e^L-1}$ that [[Thm - Selberg Zeta Criterion|Lemma 4.2]] demands, so the criterion does not apply as stated. The right object is presumably a Selberg zeta function for $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ built from complex lengths, and asking which functional equation replaces (33) is the natural next question.

**If you want drills.** This note-set deliberately carries no exercises — that is `polymath-notes` and `exercise-builder` territory. The three computations most worth drilling by hand are the coset unfolding in Theorem 3.2, the $\int_0^\infty s^{-3/2}e^{-as-b/s}\,\mathrm{d}s = \sqrt{\pi/b}\,e^{-2\sqrt{ab}}$ identity that discharges four different special cases, and the change of variables $r \mapsto u$ in the $\mathbb{H}^3$ slab computation.
