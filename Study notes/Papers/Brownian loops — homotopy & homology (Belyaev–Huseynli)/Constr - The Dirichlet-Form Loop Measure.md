# Notation

- $X$ — a Riemannian surface, taken as the state space from §2.2.1 onward; $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ a regular symmetric [[Def - Dirichlet Form and the Hunt Process Correspondence|Dirichlet form]] on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$
- $p^{\mathcal{E}}(t,x,y)$ — its transition density against $\mathrm{vol}_g$; jointly measurable, symmetric
- $D([0,t];X)$ — the space of càdlàg paths, containing $C([0,t];X)$
- $W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_x$, $W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_{x\to y}$ — the path law and the unnormalised bridge measure on $D([0,t];X)$, with $W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_x=\int_X W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_{x\to y}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g(y)$ and $|W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_{x\to y}|=p^{\mathcal{E}}(t,x,y)$
- $\mathcal{C}^*_X$, $\mathcal{C}_X$ — the càdlàg rooted and unrooted loop spaces, which from here on denote the càdlàg versions
- $\mu^{*,\mathcal{E}}_X$, $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X$ — the parametrised and the unrooted Dirichlet-form loop measures
- $\mathcal{E}_{X'}$ — the part form on an open $X'\subseteq X$

# In plain language

The same construction as the Brownian loop measure, run on any symmetric Markov process instead of Brownian motion. Continuous paths become càdlàg paths so that jumps are allowed; the heat kernel becomes the transition density of the form; nothing else changes.

The reason to do this is not generality for its own sake. §2.3 will manufacture a whole family of processes from Brownian motion by [[Def - Subordinator and Subordination of a Semigroup|subordination]], and most of them jump. Having stated the loop-measure construction at the level of Dirichlet forms means every theorem in §3 is proved once for the whole family rather than four times.

The cost is exactly one property: **conformal invariance does not survive**. For a subordinate process the operator is $\phi(\Delta_{X,g})$, which depends on the metric $g$ itself and not just on its conformal class. Restriction does survive, and for a good reason — the part form on an open subset is by definition the form of the process killed on leaving it.

# The construction

> **Definition 2.2 (Dirichlet form loop measure).** Let $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ be a regular symmetric Dirichlet form on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ with transition density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$. The **parametrised loop measure** on $\mathcal{C}^*_X$ is the $\sigma$-finite measure
> $$\mu^{*,\mathcal{E}}_X := \int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_X W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_{x\to x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g(x),$$
> and the **loop measure** $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X$ is its pushforward to $\mathcal{C}_X$.

Two things make the definition well posed, and both are inherited rather than re-proved. The bridge measures exist by disintegration of $W^{t,\mathcal{E}}_x$ with respect to the endpoint, and carry mass $p^{\mathcal{E}}(t,x,y)$. And $\mu^{*,\mathcal{E}}_X$ is invariant under the circular time-shift — Le Jan, [LJ11, Chapter 2] — which is what allows the pushforward to the quotient $\mathcal{C}_X$ of [[Def - The Space of Unrooted Unparametrised Loops]]; the equivalence relation is the one of §2.1, generated by circular shifts and increasing continuous reparametrisations, both of which preserve the càdlàg property.

The total mass is
$$\big|\mu^{*,\mathcal{E}}_X\big| = \int_0^\infty\frac1t\int_X p^{\mathcal{E}}(t,x,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g(x)\,\mathrm{d}t,$$
generally infinite, for the same small-$t$ reason as in the Brownian case.

> **Given.** A regular symmetric [[Def - Dirichlet Form and the Hunt Process Correspondence|Dirichlet form]] $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ whose semigroup admits a jointly measurable symmetric transition density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$ against $\mathrm{vol}_g$.
>

>
> **Lets you.** Run the loop-measure construction for jump processes as well as diffusions, so that §3's theorems are proved once for the whole subordinate family. Restriction is retained; **conformal invariance is not**.

# Properties relied on later

**Restriction — retained.** For an open $X'\subseteq X$, the part form $\mathcal{E}_{X'}$ is associated with the process killed on leaving $X'$, so its bridge measures are the ambient ones restricted to paths that stay inside $X'$. Hence $\mathrm{d}\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_{X'}(\eta)=\mathbf{1}_{\eta\subseteq X'}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X(\eta)$, exactly as in the Brownian case. This is what §3.4 uses for the killing process.

**Conformal invariance — lost.** Not a technical gap: for a subordinate process the operator $\phi(\Delta_{X,g})$ depends on $g$ and not only on $[g]$, because $\phi(e^{-2\sigma}\Delta_{X,g})\neq e^{-2\sigma}\phi(\Delta_{X,g})$ unless $\phi$ is linear. Consequence: from §2.3 onwards $X$ is a **Riemannian** surface, not a Riemann surface, and §3.4's length-spectrum identity collapses to a triviality for every nonlinear $\phi$.

**Jumps break the topological reading.** For a pure-jump process the sample loops are càdlàg maps into $X$ with no free homotopy class and no canonical lift, so $\mathcal{C}_X(\gamma^m)$ is not a measurable set of loops. The repair — defining the class mass by restricting the periodisation — is [[Constr - Loop Mass in a Homotopy Class for Jump Processes]], and every $\alpha$-stable statement in the paper is to be read through it.
