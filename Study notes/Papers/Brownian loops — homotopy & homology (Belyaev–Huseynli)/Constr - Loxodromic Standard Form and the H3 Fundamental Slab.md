# In plain language

The three-dimensional version of [[Constr - Standard-Form Representative and the Fundamental Strip|§3's standard form and strip]], and the point is how little changes.

**The standard form.** Every loxodromic element of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is conjugate within $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ to
$$\tau : (z,y)\longmapsto\big(e^{L_\gamma}z,\ e^{\ell_\gamma}y\big),$$
the isometry whose axis is the vertical geodesic from $0$ to $\infty$, acting along it by translation through $\ell_\gamma$ and rotation through $\theta_\gamma$. The horizontal coordinate is multiplied by the *complex* number $e^{L_\gamma}=e^{\ell_\gamma}e^{i\theta_\gamma}$, which scales and rotates; the height is multiplied by the *real* number $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, which only scales.

**The fundamental slab.** That last observation is the whole construction. Since only the height is scaled, and by the real factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, each $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit meets the slab
$$F_\tau = \big\{(z,y)\in\mathbb{H}^3 : 1\leq y<e^{\ell_\gamma}\big\}$$
in exactly one point — the powers of $\tau$ shift $\log y$ by multiples of $\ell_\gamma$, and exactly one lands in $[0,\ell_\gamma)$. **The rotation $\theta_\gamma$ acts within each slab and does not affect which slab a point lies in.** So $F_\tau$ is a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ acting on $\mathbb{H}^3$, and the holonomy angle is invisible to the choice of region.

Everything else — the centraliser computation and the coset enumeration — is verbatim §3, with "hyperbolic" replaced by "loxodromic". Since $\Gamma$ is torsion-free and discrete, anything commuting with $\tau^m$ preserves the axis of $\tau$, and the elements of $\Gamma$ preserving that axis form an infinite cyclic subgroup generated by the primitive $\tau$; so $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)=\langle\tau\rangle$ and
$$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \bigsqcup_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\{r\tau^m r^{-1}\},$$
one distinct conjugate per coset. **The argument does not notice that the dimension changed.**

# The construction

> **Construction (equation (82) — loxodromic standard form).** We use the upper half-space model $\mathbb{H}^3=\{(z,y) : z\in\mathbb{C},\ y>0\}$. Fixing a representative $\tau\in\Gamma$ of the primitive loxodromic conjugacy class of $\gamma$ and conjugating in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, we may place $\tau$ in the **standard form**
> $$\tau : (z,y)\longmapsto\big(e^{L_\gamma}z,\ e^{\ell_\gamma}y\big),\tag{82}$$
> the loxodromic isometry of $\mathbb{H}^3$ whose axis is the vertical geodesic from $0$ to $\infty$, acting along it by translation through $\ell_\gamma$ and rotation through $\theta_\gamma$.

> **Construction (equation (84) — the fundamental slab).** In the standard form, $\tau$ scales the height by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$ and rotates the horizontal coordinate by $\theta_\gamma$. The height is scaled by the **real** factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, so each orbit of $\langle\tau\rangle$ meets the slab $1\leq y<e^{\ell_\gamma}$ in exactly one point, and we take
> $$F_\tau := \big\{(z,y)\in\mathbb{H}^3 : 1\leq y<e^{\ell_\gamma}\big\}\tag{84}$$
> as a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ acting on $\mathbb{H}^3$. Note that the rotation $\theta_\gamma$ acts within each slab and does not affect which slab a point lies in.

> **The centraliser and coset enumeration (equation (83)).** Since $\Gamma$ is torsion-free and discrete, anything commuting with $\tau^m$ preserves the axis of $\tau$, and the elements of $\Gamma$ preserving that axis form an infinite cyclic subgroup generated by the primitive element $\tau$:
> $$C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle = \{\tau^k : k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.$$
> Two elements $h_1,h_2\in\Gamma$ produce the same conjugate if and only if $h_1^{-1}h_2\in\langle\tau\rangle$, that is if and only if they lie in the same left coset. Consequently
> $$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \bigsqcup_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\{r\tau^m r^{-1}\},\tag{83}$$
> with one distinct conjugate per coset.

It is also assumed that the heat kernel $p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^3}$ decays fast enough in its spatial variables that, together with the discreteness of $\Gamma$, the periodisation converges absolutely — the three-dimensional version of the hypothesis on [[Constr - The Periodised Kernel]].

# Type card

> [!abstract] Type card — loxodromic standard form and the slab
> **Given.** A primitive loxodromic conjugacy class in a torsion-free Kleinian group $\Gamma$, with complex length $L_\gamma=\ell_\gamma+i\theta_\gamma$; the freedom to conjugate within $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$.
>
> **Produces.** A representative $\tau:(z,y)\mapsto(e^{L_\gamma}z,e^{\ell_\gamma}y)$; the region $F_\tau=\{1\leq y<e^{\ell_\gamma}\}\subset\mathbb{H}^3$, a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ of infinite hyperbolic volume; and the coset enumeration (83) of the conjugacy class.
>
> **Lets you.** Run [[Thm - General Homotopy Class Decomposition for Hyperbolic 3-Manifolds|Theorem 7.1]]'s unfolding exactly as in §3, and then compute the resulting slab integral in closed form — which is what [[Thm - The H3 Fundamental-Slab Heat-Kernel Identity|equations (88)–(89)]] do.

# Properties relied on later

**$\tau^m(z,y)=(e^{L}z,\ e^{m\ell_\gamma}y)$ with $L=mL_\gamma$.** The explicit action is what makes the distance $d(w,\tau^mw)$ computable, and hence what makes the slab identity possible. Note the asymmetry: the horizontal coordinate picks up the full complex $e^L$, the vertical only the real $e^{m\ell_\gamma}$.

**$\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance of the integrand.** As in §3: $\tau$ commutes with $\tau^m$ and the kernel is $\Gamma$-invariant, so $w\mapsto p^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t,w,\tau^mw)$ is $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant and its integral over any fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ is the same. This licenses replacing the reassembled region $\bigsqcup_r r^{-1}F$ by the slab.

**The slab ranges over all $z\in\mathbb{C}$**, with volume element $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}=y^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}A(z)\,\mathrm{d}y$. Its hyperbolic volume is infinite, which is fine because the integrand decays as $w$ moves away from the axis.

**The distance depends on $z$ only through $|z|$.** In the standard form,
$$\cosh u = 1+\frac{|z-e^Lz|^2+(y-e^{m\ell_\gamma}y)^2}{2e^{m\ell_\gamma}y^2} = \cosh(m\ell_\gamma)+\frac{|e^L-1|^2|z|^2}{2e^{m\ell_\gamma}y^2},$$
so in polar coordinates $z=re^{i\varphi}$ the angular integral contributes a bare factor $2\pi$ and only $r$ matters. **This is why the three-dimensional computation is as tractable as the two-dimensional one**, and it is a consequence of the axis being vertical.

**The holonomy sits only in $|e^L-1|^2$.** It enters the distance through that factor and nowhere else, so it ends up in the geometric prefactor of [[Thm - The H3 Fundamental-Slab Heat-Kernel Identity|(88)]] and never touches the analytic factor. That is why the subordination machinery of §2 goes through unchanged.

Directly parallel to [[Constr - Standard-Form Representative and the Fundamental Strip]], one dimension up. The group theory is [[Def - Centraliser and Coset Enumeration of a Conjugacy Class]] with $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ substituted — **the same page, since the argument is dimension-independent** — and the geometry is [[Def - Kleinian Group and Loxodromic Complex Length]].

Anchors below: $\mathbb{H}^3$, its upper half-space model, the volume element $y^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}y$, the distance formula, and the classification of isometries of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ — all standard, via the Riemannian-geometry strand and [[Def - The Hyperbolic Space H^n]].
