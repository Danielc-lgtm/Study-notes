# Notation

- $C([0,t];X)$, $D([0,t];X)$ — continuous and càdlàg path spaces on the time interval $[0,t]$ with values in $X$
- $\mathcal{C}^*_X$ — the space of parametrised, oriented, **rooted** loops
- $\mathcal{C}_X = \mathcal{C}^*_X/\!\sim$ — the space of unparametrised, oriented, **unrooted** loops
- $(t,\omega)$ — a rooted loop: a duration $t\geq0$ together with a path $\omega$ on $[0,t]$ with $\omega(0)=\omega(t)$
- $\mathrm{shift}_{s_0}$ — the circular time-shift, $\mathrm{shift}_{s_0}(t,\omega)=(t,\omega^{s_0})$ with $\omega^{s_0}(s)=\omega(s+s_0 \bmod t)$
- $i$ — an increasing continuous bijection of parametrising circles $[0,t]/0\!\sim\!t\to[0,s]/0\!\sim\!s$

---

# In plain language

A loop, as a geometric object, is a closed curve in $X$. A loop, as an object a stochastic process hands you, is a *path* — it comes with a starting point and a clock. The two extra pieces of data are artefacts of how the loop was produced, and the loop measure is built on the space where they have been quotiented away.

So: $\mathcal{C}^*_X$ is the space of loops-with-artefacts, and $\mathcal{C}_X$ is the space of loops. The equivalence relation forgets the root and the parametrisation, and **remembers the orientation** — traversing a loop backwards gives a genuinely different point of $\mathcal{C}_X$.

The one subtlety worth noticing is that the slice $\{t=0\}$ is not empty: it consists of the constant point-loops, one for each point of $X$. They contribute nothing to any of the paper's mass computations, but they are part of the space.

---

# The definition

> **Definition (rooted loop space).** The space of parametrised, oriented, rooted loops in $X$ is
> $$\mathcal{C}^*_X := \big\{(t,\omega) : t\geq0,\ \omega\in C([0,t];X),\ \omega(0)=\omega(t)\big\}.$$
> From §2.2 onwards the same definition is used with $C([0,t];X)$ replaced by the càdlàg space $D([0,t];X)$, which contains it; both are written $\mathcal{C}^*_X$.
>
> **Definition (the equivalence, and the unrooted loop space).** For $(t,\omega),(s,\tilde\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^*_X$, write $(t,\omega)\sim(s,\tilde\omega)$ whenever there is an increasing continuous bijection $i : [0,t]/0\!\sim\!t\to[0,s]/0\!\sim\!s$ of the parametrising circles with $\tilde\omega(i(r))=\omega(r)$ for all $r$. The space of unparametrised, oriented, unrooted loops is the quotient
> $$\mathcal{C}_X := \mathcal{C}^*_X/\!\sim.$$

Such maps $i$ are generated by the circular time-shift action: for $t>0$,
$$\mathrm{shift}_{s_0}(t,\omega) := (t,\omega^{s_0}),\qquad \omega^{s_0}(s):=\omega(s+s_0 \bmod t),$$
together with increasing continuous reparametrisations. Both preserve the càdlàg property, which is why the same quotient works in the jump setting.

---

# Types and signatures

- $\mathcal{C}^*_X$ — a set fibred over $[0,\infty)$; the fibre over $t>0$ is $\{\omega\in C([0,t];X) : \omega(0)=\omega(t)\}$, and the fibre over $t=0$ is a copy of $X$ (the constant point-loops)
- $\sim$ — an equivalence relation on $\mathcal{C}^*_X$; it does **not** identify loops of different duration with one another unless a reparametrisation carries one to the other
- $\mathcal{C}_X$ — the quotient set, with the quotient $\sigma$-algebra; the map $\mathcal{C}^*_X\to\mathcal{C}_X$ is the pushforward used in [[Constr - The Brownian Loop Measure|Definition 2.1]] and [[Constr - The Dirichlet-Form Loop Measure|Definition 2.2]]
- $\mathrm{shift}_{s_0} : \mathcal{C}^*_X\to\mathcal{C}^*_X$ for each $s_0$ — a measurable bijection preserving each fibre $\{t=\text{const}\}$

# Example

Take $X=S^1=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ and the loop $\omega(r)=r$ on $[0,2\pi]$, so $(2\pi,\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^*_{S^1}$ traverses the circle once at unit speed starting at $0$. Its equivalence class in $\mathcal{C}_{S^1}$ contains: every rotation of the starting point, $\omega^{s_0}(r)=r+s_0$; and every increasing reparametrisation, for instance $(1,\omega')$ with $\omega'(r)=2\pi r$, which traverses the same circle in unit time. So one point of $\mathcal{C}_{S^1}$ corresponds to "once around, anticlockwise", which is exactly the geometric object one wants.

**Near-miss non-example.** The path $\tilde\omega(r)=-r$ on $[0,2\pi]$ — once around clockwise — is **not** equivalent to $\omega$. The map $i$ is required to be *increasing*, and an orientation reversal is decreasing. This is the clause that makes $\mathcal{C}_X$ a space of *oriented* loops, and it is not decoration: the whole of §3 relies on it, because free homotopy classes of *oriented* closed curves correspond to conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$, and the observation in §6.1 that $N_{\mathrm{sys}}\geq2$ is exactly the statement that a hyperbolic element is never conjugate to its inverse — so $\gamma$ and $\bar\gamma$ are two distinct classes of the same length.
