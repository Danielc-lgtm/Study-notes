# Notation

- $\phi$ — a Bernstein function, $\phi : (0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$; smooth with $(-1)^{n-1}\phi^{(n)}(\lambda)\geq 0$ for all $n\geq 1$
- $(a,b,\nu)$ — its Lévy–Khintchine triple: $a\geq 0$ the killing rate, $b\geq 0$ the drift, $\nu$ the Lévy measure on $(0,\infty)$ with $\int_0^\infty(1\wedge s)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}s)<\infty$
- $S_t$ — the subordinator, an increasing Lévy process on $[0,\infty)$ with $\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda S_t}]=e^{-t\phi(\lambda)}$
- $\psi^\phi_t$ — the law of $S_t$, a measure on $[0,\infty)$ with total mass $|\psi^\phi_t| = e^{-at}$; a probability measure exactly when $a=0$
- $A$ — the generator of the base Dirichlet form, non-negative self-adjoint on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$; $\phi(A)$ its subordinate, defined by functional calculus
- $(\mathcal{E}^\phi,\mathcal{F}^\phi)$ — the subordinate Dirichlet form; $p^\phi(t,x,y)$ its transition density
- $\rho$ — the hyperbolic area measure on $\mathbb{H}^2$, written $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ when the space needs naming
- $V_\phi$ — the weighted potential measure, a $\sigma$-finite measure on $(0,\infty)$; written $V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)=V_\phi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ when absolutely continuous, which it is in every case treated
- $\eta^\alpha_t(s)$ — the density of the $\alpha/2$-stable subordinator law, $\eta^\alpha_t(s)=t^{-2/\alpha}g_{\alpha/2}(st^{-2/\alpha})$ with $g_{\alpha/2}$ the standard $\alpha/2$-stable density on $(0,\infty)$
- $\kappa$ — a constant killing rate; $\alpha\in(0,2)$ — the stability index

# What this section is for

§2.2 said: give me any regular symmetric Dirichlet form and I will give you a loop measure. That is a licence to be greedy, and §2.3 spends it. The question is how to *manufacture* new Dirichlet forms from one you already have, in a way that is parametrised by something you can compute with.

The answer is subordination: run the process on a random clock. Take an independent increasing Lévy process $S_t$ — a **subordinator** — and look at $Y_u = B_{S_u}$. Because $S$ is increasing, $Y$ is again a Markov process; because $S$ is independent of $B$, its effect on the semigroup is pure averaging,
$$e^{-t\phi(A)} = \int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-sA}\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s),$$
and the generator turns from $-A$ into $-\phi(A)$ where $\phi$ is the Laplace exponent of $S$. The class of admissible $\phi$ is exactly the [[Def - Bernstein Function and the Lévy–Khintchine Representation|Bernstein functions]], and the reason to know the Lévy–Khintchine triple $(a,b,\nu)$ is that it reads off, from $\phi$ alone, what the new process does: $a$ is a constant killing rate, $b$ is the surviving diffusive part, and $\nu$ is the jump structure. If $\nu\neq 0$ the subordinate process **jumps**, even though the process you started with was a diffusion. This is why §2.2 needed càdlàg paths.

Three examples carry the whole paper. $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda$ is the identity: no subordination, $\psi^\phi_t=\delta_t$, plain Brownian motion. $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda+\kappa$ adds a constant killing rate; $\psi^\phi_t = e^{-\kappa t}\delta_t$ and the transition density is simply $e^{-\kappa t}p(t,z,w)$. And $\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ for $\alpha\in(0,2)$ gives the fractional Laplacian $\Delta^{\alpha/2}$ and a pure-jump $\alpha$-stable process, recovering Brownian motion in the boundary case $\alpha=2$. The paper adds a fourth, $\phi(\lambda)=(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$, precisely to break a degeneracy that shows up in §3.1.3.

**Remark 2.4 is worth reading rather than skipping**, because it distinguishes subordination from the other way of running a process on a different clock. A time change by a positive continuous additive functional — Liouville Brownian motion is the canonical example — builds the clock from the process's own trajectory, so it is adapted to the filtration, it reparametrises the same path (continuity is preserved), and it changes the *reference measure* to the Revuz measure of the additive functional while leaving the paths alone. Subordination does the opposite in every respect: the clock is independent and non-adapted, the reference measure $\mathrm{vol}_g$ is untouched, the Dirichlet form changes, and continuity is destroyed. Two operations that sound the same and act on orthogonal parts of the data $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{vol}_g)$.

Then §2.4 does the one computational move that makes the rest of the paper cheap. Because $p^\phi$ is itself an average of $p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,\cdot,\cdot)$ against $\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$, any loop measure built from $p^\phi$ carries a *double* integral, in $t$ and in $s$. But the outer variable $t$ appears nowhere except inside $\psi^\phi_t$. So integrate it out first, against its $\mathrm{d}t/t$ weight, and what is left is a single measure on the subordination variable $s$. That measure is $V_\phi$, and [[Thm - Collapsing the Time Integral into the Weighted Potential Measure|Lemma 2.11]] is the statement that the collapse is legitimate. Everything after this point in the paper is the same calculation with a different $V_\phi$ plugged in.

# Results

## Assumption 2.3 — the standing hypothesis

> [!abstract] Type card — Assumption 2.3
> **Given.** A [[Def - Bernstein Function and the Lévy–Khintchine Representation|Bernstein function]] $\phi$ with triple $(a,b,\nu)$.
>
> **Produces.** The standing hypothesis $b>0$ or $\nu(0,\infty)=\infty$; equivalently $S_t>0$ almost surely for every $t>0$, equivalently $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=0$.
>
> **Lets you.** Assume the subordinate semigroup admits a transition density, which every formula from §2.4 onwards uses without further comment. It is exactly the condition that excludes compound Poisson subordinators, whose laws have an atom at the origin and whose semigroups therefore have no density.

Full discussion, including what fails without it: [[Constr - Assumption 2.3 (Strictly Increasing Subordinator)]].

## Definition 2.8 — the subordinate Brownian loop measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.8 (subordinate Brownian loop measure)
> **Given.** A Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3, and the [[Def - Subordinator and Subordination of a Semigroup|subordinate Dirichlet form]] $(\mathcal{E}^\phi,\mathcal{F}^\phi)$ on $L^2(X,\mathrm{vol}_g)$ with transition density $p^\phi$.
>
> **Produces.** The $\sigma$-finite loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$ on $\mathcal{C}_X$, as the pushforward of $\mu^{*,\phi}_X = \int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_X W^{t,\phi}_{x\to x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_g(x)$.
>
> **Lets you.** Treat Brownian motion, Brownian motion with killing, the $\alpha$-stable processes and the shifted $\alpha$-stable processes as four instances of a single object, so that every theorem in §3 is proved once.

## Definition 2.9 — the weighted potential measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.9 (weighted potential measure)
> **Given.** A Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3, with subordinator laws $\psi^\phi_t$.
>
> **Produces.** A $\sigma$-finite measure $V_\phi$ on $(0,\infty)$, characterised by $\int_{(0,\infty)} h(s)\,V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s) = \int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_{(0,\infty)}h(s)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$ for every non-negative measurable $h$ making the right side finite. Absolutely continuous in every case the paper treats.
>
> **Lets you.** Replace the double integral $\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_{[0,\infty)}(\cdots)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$ by the single integral $\int_{(0,\infty)}(\cdots)\,V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$. This is the hypothesis carried through every theorem of §3 and §7.

The four cases, computed on [[Constr - The Weighted Potential Measure Vϕ]] and worth memorising because they are substituted repeatedly:

| $\phi(\lambda)$ | process | $\psi^\phi_t$ | $V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\lambda$ | Brownian motion | $\delta_t$ | $\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $\lambda+\kappa$ | Brownian with killing | $e^{-\kappa t}\delta_t$ | $e^{-\kappa s}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ | $\alpha$-stable | $\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ | $\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ | shifted $\alpha$-stable | $e^{-\kappa s}\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ | $\tfrac{\alpha}{2}e^{-\kappa s}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |

The pattern is worth naming out loud, because it explains a collapse in §3.1.3 before it happens. **In the two Dirac cases the $\mathrm{d}t/t$ integral localises at $s=t$ and $V_\phi$ inherits the Haar measure verbatim. In the stable case the Haar measure is preserved up to the constant $\alpha/2$ — and that is not luck.** Self-similar subordinators are exactly the stable ones; $\mathrm{d}t/t$ is itself scale-invariant; and the only measure on $(0,\infty)$ compatible with both scalings is a constant multiple of $\mathrm{d}s/s$. Adding the shift $\kappa$ is the cheapest available way to break the scale invariance, which is exactly why the paper introduces the fourth line of the table.

## Lemma 2.11 — collapsing the time integral

> [!abstract] Type card — Lemma 2.11
> **Given.** A Bernstein function $\phi$ satisfying Assumption 2.3, the base transition density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$, and the subordinate density $p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{[0,\infty)}p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,x,y)\,\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$.
>
> **Produces.** The identity, valid for all $x,y\in X$ as an equality in $[0,\infty]$:
> $$\int_0^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,p^\phi(t,x,y) \;=\; \int_{(0,\infty)} p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,x,y)\,V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s).$$
>
> **Lets you.** Perform the collapse in one step wherever a $\mathrm{d}t/t$ integral meets a subordinated kernel. This is the workhorse of both [[Thm - Mass of the Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure on Surfaces|Theorem 3.5]] and [[Thm - Mass of the Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure on 3-Manifolds|Theorem 7.2]], and it is invoked in both with the *same* structure: identify the inner spatial integral as some $h(s)$, then apply the lemma to that $h$.

**Strategy.** Substitute the subordination formula for $p^\phi$, exchange the two integrals by Tonelli — legitimate because everything in sight is non-negative — and then read the result off Definition 2.9 with $h(s)=p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,x,y)$. The lemma is one line; its value is entirely in being the right one line to have named.

Full statement and discussion: [[Thm - Collapsing the Time Integral into the Weighted Potential Measure]].

# Worked special cases

The subordinate heat kernels on $\mathbb{H}^2$, which is where §3 will need them.

**Killing ($\phi(\lambda)=\lambda+\kappa$, $\kappa>0$).** The form is $\mathcal{E}^\kappa(f,f)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^2}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}\rho + \kappa\int_{\mathbb{H}^2}f^2\,\mathrm{d}\rho$ and the kernel is $p^\kappa_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)=e^{-\kappa t}p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$. This is the trivial case in that the paths are unchanged; only their weight decays.

**$\alpha$-stable ($\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha/2}$, $\alpha\in(0,2)$).** The operator is the fractional Laplacian $\Delta^{\alpha/2}_{\mathbb{H}^2}$, the kernel is $p^\alpha_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)=\int_0^\infty p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(s,z,w)\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$, and the process is pure-jump for every $\alpha\in(0,2)$. Since $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ is $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$-invariant and $\phi$ acts by functional calculus, $p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ is $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$-invariant too — which is exactly the hypothesis §3 will need in order to periodise over $\Gamma$.

**Shifted $\alpha$-stable ($\phi(\lambda)=(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$).** This $\phi$ is a complete Bernstein function, being the composition of $\lambda\mapsto\lambda+\kappa$ with $u\mapsto u^{\alpha/2}$, so the framework applies unchanged. Its subordinator law is an exponential tilt of the stable law:

> [!note]- Calculation (skippable)
> Write the Laplace exponent as an integral against the stable density and read off the tilt:
> $$e^{-t(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}} = \int_0^\infty e^{-u(\lambda+\kappa)}\eta^\alpha_t(u)\,\mathrm{d}u = \int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s}\,e^{-\kappa s}\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,$$
> so $\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)=e^{-\kappa s}\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$. Substituting into Definition 2.9 and applying the stable collapse to $h(s)e^{-\kappa s}$ rather than to $h(s)$ gives $V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)=\tfrac{\alpha}{2}e^{-\kappa s}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$.

# What to carry forward

**$V_\phi$, and only $V_\phi$.** From §3 onwards the choice of process enters the formulas in exactly one place, as the measure integrated against. If you remember the four-line table above you can generate every special case in §3.1 and §7 without re-reading a proof.

**Lemma 2.11 as a move, not a fact.** The trigger is: a $\mathrm{d}t/t$ integral in front of a subordinated kernel, with the spatial integral already discharged. The action is: name the discharged spatial integral $h(s)$ and collapse. It happens twice in the paper, at (22) and at the proof of Theorem 7.2, and it is the same move both times.

**Assumption 2.3 as a hypothesis you must keep quoting.** It is the reason a transition density exists, and every theorem downstream that says "let $\phi$ be a Bernstein function satisfying Assumption 2.3" is quoting it because without a density there is no kernel to periodise in §3.

**The scale-invariance warning.** Stable subordination gives $V_\phi = \tfrac\alpha2\,\mathrm{d}s/s$, so $\mu^\alpha_X = \tfrac\alpha2\mu_X$ exactly, and nothing new is learned about the geometry of $X$. Remembering this now saves the disappointment of §3.1.3 and explains why the shifted case exists.
