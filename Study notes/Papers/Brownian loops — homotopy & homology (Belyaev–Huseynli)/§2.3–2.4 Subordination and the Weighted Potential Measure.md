# Results

## Assumption 2.3 — the standing hypothesis

> [!abstract] Type card — (A2.3)
> **Given.** **(H1)** $\phi$ Bernstein with triple $(a,b,\nu)$.
>
> **Produces.** The proposition $b>0$ or $\nu(0,\infty)=\infty$; equivalently $S_t>0$ a.s. for all $t>0$; equivalently $\psi^\phi_t(\{0\})=0$.
>
> **Lets you.** Assume $p^\phi$ exists, by [[Ext - Phillips Subordination of Semigroups and Dirichlet Forms|(PH)(C3)]]. Excludes compound Poisson subordinators, whose semigroups have no density.

Full page: [[Constr - Assumption 2.3 (Strictly Increasing Subordinator)]].

## Definition 2.8 — the subordinate Brownian loop measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.8
> **Given.** **(H1)** $\phi$ Bernstein satisfying (A2.3). **(H2)** $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ regular symmetric with density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$. **(H3)** subordinator $\perp$ base process.
>
> **Produces.** $\mu^\phi_X$: $\sigma$-finite on $\mathcal{C}_X$, infinite total mass; for jump processes, on càdlàg loops where homotopy classes are **not** measurable.
>
> **Lets you.** Treat the four processes as four values of one parameter; §3 is proved once.

## Definition 2.9 — the weighted potential measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.9
> **Given.** **(H1)** $\phi$ Bernstein satisfying (A2.3), laws $\{\psi^\phi_t\}$.
>
> **Produces.** $V_\phi$: $\sigma$-finite measure on $(0,\infty)$, **not finite**; absolutely continuous in every case here. Characterised by $\int h\,\mathrm{d}V_\phi=\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int h\,\mathrm{d}\psi^\phi_t$ for measurable $h\geq0$.
>
> **Lets you.** Replace the double $(t,s)$ integral by one integral in $s$. Carried as a hypothesis through every theorem of §3 and §7.

Full page: [[Constr - The Weighted Potential Measure Vϕ]].

## Lemma 2.11 — collapsing the time integral

> [!abstract] Type card — Lemma 2.11
> **Given.** **(H1)** $\phi$ Bernstein satisfying (A2.3). **(H2)** $p^{\mathcal{E}}$ jointly measurable, symmetric. **(H3)** $x,y\in X$.
>
> **Produces.** An identity in $[0,\infty]$ — no integrability hypothesis:
> $$\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,p^\phi(t,x,y)=\int_{(0,\infty)}p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,x,y)\,V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s).$$
>
> **Lets you.** Perform the collapse in one step. Used twice, identically: discharge the spatial integral, name it $h(s)$, collapse.

**Strategy.** Substitute [[Ext - Phillips Subordination of Semigroups and Dirichlet Forms|(PH)(C3)]] for $p^\phi$; exchange by [[Thm - Fubini-Tonelli Theorem|Tonelli]] (non-negative); read off (7). · Full page: [[Thm - Collapsing the Time Integral into the Weighted Potential Measure]].

# Special cases

## The four subordinations

| $\phi(\lambda)$ | $(a,b,\nu)$ | process | $\psi^\phi_t$ | $V_\phi(\mathrm{d}s)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\lambda$ | $(0,1,0)$ | Brownian motion | $\delta_t$ | $\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $\lambda+\kappa$ | $(\kappa,1,0)$ | Brownian with killing | $e^{-\kappa t}\delta_t$ | $e^{-\kappa s}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ | $(0,0,\nu_\alpha)$, $\nu_\alpha(0,\infty)=\infty$ | $\alpha$-stable, pure jump | $\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ | $\tfrac\alpha2\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |
| $(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ | composition | shifted $\alpha$-stable | $e^{-\kappa s}\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ | $\tfrac\alpha2 e^{-\kappa s}\,\mathrm{d}s/s$ |

Calculations: [[Constr - The Weighted Potential Measure Vϕ]].

## Subordinate kernels on $\mathbb{H}^2$

| $\phi$ | operator | kernel |
|---|---|---|
| $\lambda+\kappa$ | $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^2}+\kappa$; $\mathcal{E}^\kappa(f,f)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^2}\lvert\nabla f\rvert^2\mathrm{d}\rho+\kappa\int_{\mathbb{H}^2}f^2\mathrm{d}\rho$ | $p^\kappa_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)=e^{-\kappa t}p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$ |
| $\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ | $\Delta^{\alpha/2}_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ (fractional Laplacian) | $p^\alpha_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)=\int_0^\infty p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(s,z,w)\eta^\alpha_t(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$; pure jump for all $\alpha\in(0,2)$; $\alpha=2$ recovers BM |
| $(\lambda+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ | $(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^2}+\kappa)^{\alpha/2}$; complete Bernstein, being $\lambda\mapsto\lambda+\kappa$ then $u\mapsto u^{\alpha/2}$ | exponential tilt of the previous row |

**Invariance transfers.** $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ is $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$-invariant and $\phi$ acts by functional calculus, so $p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ is $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$-invariant — [[Ext - Phillips Subordination of Semigroups and Dirichlet Forms|(PH)(C5)]]. **This is the hypothesis §3 needs to periodise over $\Gamma$.**

# Commentary

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> §2.2 licensed a loop measure for any regular symmetric Dirichlet form; §2.3 spends that licence by manufacturing forms. The machine is subordination — run the process on an independent random clock — and the parametrisation is by a single function of one real variable, small enough to compute with.
>
> Remark 2.4 distinguishes subordination from time change by a positive continuous additive functional, and the distinction is sharper than it sounds: the two act on orthogonal parts of the data $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},\operatorname{vol}_g)$. Subordination has an *independent, non-adapted* clock, changes the form, leaves the reference measure alone, and destroys path continuity. A PCAF clock is built from the process's own trajectory, is adapted, reparametrises the same paths (continuity preserved), and replaces the reference measure by the Revuz measure. The canonical example of the second is Liouville Brownian motion. Full table on [[Def - Subordinator]].
>
> §2.4 then does the one computational move that makes the rest of the paper cheap. Because $p^\phi$ is an average of $p^{\mathcal{E}}(s,\cdot,\cdot)$ against $\psi^\phi_t(\mathrm{d}s)$, a subordinate loop measure carries a double integral — and $t$ appears nowhere except inside $\psi^\phi_t$. So integrate $t$ out first. What is left is $V_\phi$, and everything after this point is the same calculation with a different $V_\phi$ plugged in.
>
> The pattern in the $V_\phi$ table is worth naming before §3.1.3 makes it a problem. In the two Dirac rows the $\mathrm{d}t/t$ integral localises at $s=t$ and the Haar measure passes through verbatim. In the stable row it is preserved up to the constant $\alpha/2$ — and that is forced, not lucky: self-similar subordinators are exactly the stable ones, $\mathrm{d}t/t$ is itself scale-invariant, and the only measures compatible with both scalings are constant multiples of $\mathrm{d}s/s$. Adding the shift $\kappa$ is the cheapest way to break the scaling, which is why the fourth row exists.
