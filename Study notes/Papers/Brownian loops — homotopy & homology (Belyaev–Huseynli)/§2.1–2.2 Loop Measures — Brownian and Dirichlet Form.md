# Results

## Definition 2.1 — the Brownian loop measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.1
> **Given.**
> **(H1)** $(X,g)$ complete orientable Riemannian surface, Dirichlet conditions on $\partial X$.
> **(H2)** $p_X$ exists ([[Def - Transition Density and Heat Kernel|(D1)–(D3)]]); hence $\{W^t_{x\to y}\}$ exists.
> **(H3)** [[Ext - Le Jan Shift-Invariance of the Parametrised Loop Measure|(LJ)]], so $q_*$ is well defined.
>
> **Produces.** $\mu_X$: $\sigma$-finite measure on $\mathcal{C}_X$, $\mu_X(\mathcal{C}_X)=\infty$. Not normalisable.
>
> **Lets you.** Assign mass to a family of loops with no normalisation; use $\mu_X$ as a Poisson intensity; inherit (P1) restriction and (P2) conformal invariance.

## Definition 2.2 — the Dirichlet-form loop measure

> [!abstract] Type card — Definition 2.2
> **Given.**
> **(H1)** $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ a [[Def - Regular Symmetric Dirichlet Form|regular symmetric Dirichlet form]] on $L^2(X,\operatorname{vol}_g)$.
> **(H2)** $e^{-tA}$ admits a jointly measurable symmetric density $p^{\mathcal{E}}$ against $\operatorname{vol}_g$.
> **(H3)** [[Ext - Le Jan Shift-Invariance of the Parametrised Loop Measure|(LJ)]].
>
> **Produces.** $\mu^{\mathcal{E}}_X$: $\sigma$-finite measure on the **càdlàg** loop space $\mathcal{C}_X$, rooted total mass $\int_0^\infty\frac1t\int_Xp^{\mathcal{E}}(t,x,x)\,\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}_g(x)\,\mathrm{d}t$.
>
> **Lets you.** Run the construction for jump processes; retains (P1), loses (P2).

# The two structural properties

Imported from [[Ext - Lawler–Werner Restriction and Conformal Invariance|(LW1),(LW2)]], and their scopes differ.

| property | statement | holds for | consumed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(P1)** restriction | $\mathrm{d}\mu_{X'}(\eta)=\mathbf{1}_{\{\eta\subseteq X'\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_X(\eta)$, $X'\subseteq X$ open | **every** Dirichlet-form loop measure, since $\mathcal{E}_{X'}$ is the form of the killed process | [[Ext - Wang–Xue Length-Spectrum Identity]] |
| **(P2)** conformal invariance | $\mu_{X,e^{2\sigma}g}=\mu_{X,g}$ | **only** 2D Brownian motion; fails whenever $\phi(\lambda)\neq c\lambda$ | [[Ext - Wang–Xue Length-Spectrum Identity]], [[Thm - Polyakov's Formula via Brownian Loop Measure]] |

The obstruction to (P2) is exact:
$$\Delta_{X,e^{2\sigma}g}=e^{-2\sigma}\Delta_{X,g},\qquad \phi\big(e^{-2\sigma}\Delta_{X,g}\big)\neq e^{-2\sigma}\phi\big(\Delta_{X,g}\big)\ \text{ unless }\phi(\lambda)=c\lambda .$$
**Consequence:** from §2.3 on, $X$ is a Riemannian surface, not a Riemann surface.

# Exports

**(E1)** *The recipe.* Every loop measure in the paper is $q_*\Big(\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int_X W^{t,\bullet}_{x\to x}\,\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}_g(x)\Big)$ with a different transition density substituted. → §2.3, §3, §7.

**(E2)** *Class mass has the shape* $\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\int(\text{diagonal kernel})$. The $\mathrm{d}t/t$ is untouched until [[Thm - Collapsing the Time Integral into the Weighted Potential Measure|Lemma 2.11]] eats it. → §3, §7.

**(E3)** *(P1) survives everything; (P2) survives only $\phi(\lambda)=c\lambda$.* → §3.4, §5.1.1, §7.

**(E4)** *Total mass is infinite, divergent at $t\downarrow0$ like $\int_0t^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}t$.* → §5 (renormalisation).

# Commentary

> [!note]- Commentary (skippable)
> Everything in the paper is a statement about the mass of a set of loops, so the first thing to build is a measure on loops — and the striking fact, the reason the subject exists, is that the natural such measure is not a probability measure and could not usefully be one.
>
> The difficulty it solves: to say "the loops in this free homotopy class have such-and-such weight", one starts from a Brownian bridge conditioned to return to its start, and has then made two arbitrary choices — the duration $t$ and the basepoint $x$. Both are artefacts of a parametrisation. Integrating out the basepoint costs $\int_X\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}_g$; integrating out the duration forces the weight $\mathrm{d}t/t$, the unique measure up to scale invariant under $t\mapsto\lambda t$.
>
> The price is (E4). Both the $1/t$ from the Haar weight and the $1/4\pi t$ from the short-time diagonal compound to $\int_0t^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}t$: a surface contains overwhelmingly many very small loops, which is a correct statement rather than a defect. What survives is $\sigma$-finiteness — enough for Tonelli, for pushforwards, and for a Poisson intensity. What is lost is "pick a loop at random", and §4–§6 earn it back on a sub-family of finite mass.
>
> §2.2 then generalises, and the reason is not generality for its own sake: §2.3 manufactures a whole family of processes by subordination, most of which jump, so stating the construction at Dirichlet-form level means §3 is proved once rather than four times. The cost is exactly (P2), and §3.4 is where that cost is itemised.
