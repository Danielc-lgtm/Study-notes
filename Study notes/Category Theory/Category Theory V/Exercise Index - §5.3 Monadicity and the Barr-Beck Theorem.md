## §5.3 Monadicity and the Barr-Beck Theorem — Exercises

This section drills the [[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem|Barr–Beck monadicity theorem]] in both directions and its comonadic dual. The deciding skill is to run the three-item checklist — left adjoint, conservativity, creation of split coequalizers — and to know which condition is the bottleneck: algebraic categories pass all three by stereotyped one-liners, while topology fails conservativity and fields lack a left adjoint entirely. The recognition exercise goes the other way, certifying a concrete category as a category of [[Def - Algebra for a Monad|algebras]] by identifying its monad. The descent exercise is the algebraic-geometry payoff: faithfully flat descent *is* the comonadic Barr–Beck theorem, with the cocycle condition being comonad coassociativity. Throughout, the unifying slogan is "monadic = algebraic."

- [[Ex - Which forgetful functors are monadic]] (⭐⭐⭐) — deciding monadicity for $\mathbf{Grp}, \mathbf{Ring}$ (yes), $\mathbf{Top}$ (conservativity fails), $\mathbf{Field}$ (no left adjoint); monadicity as the meaning of "algebraic" ([[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem]], [[Def - Algebra for a Monad]], [[Def - Monad and Comonad]], [[Def - Group]])
- [[Ex - Recognizing a category of algebras]] (⭐⭐) — certifying $M$-sets and pointed sets as categories of algebras by identifying the monad and applying Barr–Beck ([[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem]], [[Def - Algebra for a Monad]], [[Def - Monad and Comonad]])
- [[Ex - Descent via comonadicity]] (⭐⭐⭐) — faithfully flat descent for modules as comonadicity of base change; descent data as coalgebras, the cocycle as coassociativity ([[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem]], [[Def - Monad and Comonad]], [[Def - Module]], [[Def - Ring]])
