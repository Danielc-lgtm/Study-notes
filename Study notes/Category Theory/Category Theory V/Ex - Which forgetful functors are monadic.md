# Problem Statement

For each forgetful functor below to $\mathbf{Set}$, decide whether it is **monadic**, and justify using the [[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem|Barr–Beck theorem]]:

**(a)** $U : \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}$ (forget the group structure).

**(b)** $U : \mathbf{Ring} \to \mathbf{Set}$ (forget the ring structure).

**(c)** $U : \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Set}$ (forget the topology).

**(d)** $U : \mathbf{Field} \to \mathbf{Set}$ (forget the field structure).

Identify, in each negative case, *which* Barr–Beck condition fails and *why*.

**Recall:**

![[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem#Statement]]

A functor is **conservative** if it reflects isomorphisms. It **creates coequalizers of $U$-split pairs** if every parallel pair whose image has a split coequalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$ has a coequalizer in the domain, preserved and reflected by $U$.

---

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** A "decide monadicity" problem — applying the Barr–Beck checklist to concrete forgetful functors. The skill is knowing which condition is the bottleneck in each case.

**Assumption pattern:** Each functor either has a free object (left adjoint exists) or not, and either reflects isos or not. The assumption to leverage is that *algebraic* categories (defined by operations and equations) pass all three conditions, while topology and fields fail a specific one (legal operation 5).

**Theorem routing:** Route every case through [[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem]]: check (1) left adjoint, (2) conservativity, (3) creation of split coequalizers. For positive cases, all three hold; for negatives, locate the failure — conservativity for $\mathbf{Top}$, the left adjoint for $\mathbf{Field}$.

**Key decision point:** The discriminating insight is that monadicity *is* the precise meaning of "algebraic." Groups and rings are algebraic (operations + equations), so monadic. Topology is *not* algebraic — a topology is not a tuple of operations — and concretely $U : \mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}$ fails conservativity: a continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism. Fields fail even earlier: there is no free field, so no left adjoint.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> For (a),(b): algebraic categories have free objects (free group, free ring), so condition (1) holds; a bijective homomorphism has a homomorphism inverse, so condition (2) holds; coequalizers are computed as quotients, and the split condition supplies the structure on the quotient, so condition (3) holds.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> For (c) $\mathbf{Top}$: the left adjoint exists (discrete topology) — but is $U$ conservative? Consider a continuous bijection from a discrete space to an indiscrete one on the same set.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> A continuous bijection $f : X \to Y$ with $f$ a bijection of underlying sets is *not* necessarily a homeomorphism (its inverse need not be continuous). So $Uf$ is an iso in $\mathbf{Set}$ while $f$ is not an iso in $\mathbf{Top}$: conservativity fails.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For (d) $\mathbf{Field}$: is there a "free field on a set"? The field generated by a set of elements depends on characteristic and algebraic relations; there is no left adjoint to $U$. Condition (1) fails outright.

# Solution

The plan: confirm $\mathbf{Grp}$ and $\mathbf{Ring}$ pass all three Barr–Beck conditions (algebraic, hence monadic); show $\mathbf{Top}$ fails conservativity via a continuous bijection that is not a homeomorphism; show $\mathbf{Field}$ fails the left-adjoint condition because no free field exists. The unifying theme: monadicity is exactly "algebraic."

**Step 1 (a),(b): Groups and rings are monadic.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Left adjoint:* the free group $F(X)$ and free ring $\mathbb{Z}\langle X\rangle$ exist, so $U$ has a left adjoint in both cases (condition 1).
>
> *Conservative:* a bijective group (or ring) homomorphism $f$ has a set-inverse $f^{-1}$, which is automatically a homomorphism (e.g. $f^{-1}(xy) = f^{-1}(x)f^{-1}(y)$ follows by applying $f$). So $Uf$ iso $\Rightarrow f$ iso: conservative (condition 2).
>
> *Creates split coequalizers:* given a $U$-split pair, the coequalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$ is a quotient by a generated congruence; the split provides a section that lets the group/ring structure descend uniquely to the quotient, and $U$ preserves it. So $U$ creates coequalizers of $U$-split pairs (condition 3).
>
> All three hold, so by [[Thm - The Barr-Beck Monadicity Theorem|Barr–Beck]], $U : \mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ and $U : \mathbf{Ring}\to\mathbf{Set}$ are monadic. (This is also visible directly: $\mathbf{Grp} \simeq \mathbf{Set}^T$ for the free-group monad, as in [[Ex - Algebras for the free-group monad are groups]].)

**Step 2 (c): Topology is not monadic — conservativity fails.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> The forgetful $U : \mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}$ *does* have a left adjoint, the discrete-topology functor $X \mapsto X_{\mathrm{disc}}$ (every function out of a discrete space is continuous), so condition (1) holds. But conservativity fails. Let $X = (\{0,1\}, \text{discrete})$ and $Y = (\{0,1\}, \text{indiscrete})$, and let $f = \mathrm{id}$ on the underlying set. Then $f : X \to Y$ is continuous (every map into the indiscrete space is), and $Uf = \mathrm{id}$ is a bijection, hence an isomorphism in $\mathbf{Set}$. But $f$ is *not* a homeomorphism: its inverse $Y \to X$ is not continuous (the point $\{0\}$ is open in $X$ but its preimage is not open in $Y$). So $Uf$ is iso while $f$ is not: $U$ is **not conservative**, condition (2) fails, and $\mathbf{Top}$ is not monadic over $\mathbf{Set}$.
>
> Conceptually: the monad induced by the discrete–forgetful adjunction is the *identity* monad ($UF = U(X_{\mathrm{disc}}) = X$), whose only algebras are bare sets. The topology is invisible to the monad — topology is not an algebraic structure.

**Step 3 (d): Fields are not monadic — no left adjoint.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> The forgetful $U : \mathbf{Field}\to\mathbf{Set}$ has **no left adjoint**, so condition (1) fails at the first step. There is no "free field on a set $X$": a left adjoint would require a field $F(X)$ with $\mathbf{Field}(F(X), K) \cong \mathbf{Set}(X, UK)$ naturally, but the field generated by $|X|$ elements depends on the characteristic (which a field-homomorphism out of $F(X)$ would have to fix, yet $X$ alone cannot determine it) and on which algebraic relations the images satisfy. Already for $X = \varnothing$ there is no initial field — $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{F}_p$ admit no homomorphisms between them, so no field maps to all fields. With no left adjoint there is no induced monad and no comparison functor, so monadicity is undefined/false: $\mathbf{Field}$ is **not** monadic over $\mathbf{Set}$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **(a),(b)** Monadic. Free group / free ring give a left adjoint (1); a bijective homomorphism has a homomorphism inverse, so $U$ is conservative (2); the split structure on a $U$-split pair descends the algebraic structure to the set-coequalizer, so $U$ creates such coequalizers (3). By Barr–Beck, monadic.
>
> **(c)** Not monadic. $U$ has a left adjoint (discrete topology) but is *not conservative*: $\mathrm{id} : \{0,1\}_{\mathrm{disc}} \to \{0,1\}_{\mathrm{indisc}}$ is a continuous bijection that is not a homeomorphism, so $Uf$ iso $\not\Rightarrow f$ iso. Condition (2) fails. (The induced monad is the identity, whose algebras are bare sets.)
>
> **(d)** Not monadic. $U$ has *no left adjoint* — there is no free field on a set (the generated field depends on characteristic and algebraic relations, and there is not even an initial field). Condition (1) fails. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting: "all forgetful functors are monadic"
> Because algebraic forgetful functors are monadic so reliably, it is tempting to assume *every* "forget structure" functor is. The two negative cases show the two distinct ways this fails: $\mathbf{Top}$ has free objects but is not conservative (structure that a bijection can destroy is not algebraic), and $\mathbf{Field}$ has no free objects at all (a structure with no free construction cannot be presented by a monad). The repair is to actually run the three-item checklist; the failures are diagnostic of *what kind* of structure each category carries.

# Key Takeaways

**Monadicity is the precise definition of "algebraic."** The deepest takeaway is that "is this forgetful functor monadic?" and "is this category defined by operations and equations?" are the *same question*. Groups, rings, modules, lattices, Boolean algebras — anything presentable by a signature of operations satisfying equational axioms — is monadic over $\mathbf{Set}$, and this is *why* they uniformly have free objects, presentations by generators and relations, and limits computed set-wise. When you meet a new "category of structured sets," asking whether it is algebraic is asking whether Barr–Beck applies, and the answer predicts its entire structural behaviour.

**Conservativity is the test for "structure a bijection cannot destroy."** The $\mathbf{Top}$ failure isolates conservativity as the condition that a structure-preserving map that is bijective underneath is automatically invertible. For algebraic structures this holds because the inverse of a bijective homomorphism is a homomorphism; for topology it fails because a continuous bijection can have a non-continuous inverse — the topology adds information that the bijection does not respect. The transferable diagnostic: if you can build a structure-preserving bijection that is not an isomorphism, the functor is not conservative, hence not monadic, and the category is not algebraic. This single example ($\mathrm{id}$ from discrete to indiscrete) is the canonical witness and worth memorizing.

**No free object means no monad — check the left adjoint first.** The $\mathbf{Field}$ failure is the cleanest: monadicity requires a left adjoint, and a left adjoint is exactly a free-object construction. Fields have no free objects because the field "generated" by a set is not determined by the set alone (characteristic and algebraic relations intervene), and there is not even an initial field. The lesson is to check condition (1) *first*, because it is often the quickest disqualifier and it is conceptually primary — without free objects there is no monad to compare against, so the question of monadicity does not even get off the ground. This is why the category of fields shares so few of the good properties of $\mathbf{Grp}, \mathbf{Ring}, \mathbf{Mod}_R$: it is not algebraic at the most basic level. See [[Ex - Recognizing a category of algebras]] for the positive direction, certifying a concrete category *is* a category of algebras.
