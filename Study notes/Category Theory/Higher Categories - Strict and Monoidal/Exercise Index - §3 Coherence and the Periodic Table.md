## §3 Coherence and the Periodic Table — Exercises

This section is the chapter's payoff: coherence makes the bracketing-free notation honest, [[Thm - Strictification of Monoidal Categories|strictification]] lets you assume the tensor is strict in low [[Def - Dimension|dimensions]], and the [[Ex - The Eckmann-Hilton argument|Eckmann–Hilton argument]] climbs the periodic table by manufacturing commutativity from interchange. The exercises drill the unifying frame — *going up a dimension and looking at one object is the same as gaining a multiplication* — at increasing depth: first the algebraic engine (Eckmann–Hilton), then the bottom row of the table (monoidal = one-object bicategory), then the full ascent and the stabilization hypothesis. The deepest takeaway is that strictification is a dimension-$\leq 2$ miracle whose failure at dimension three (the surviving braiding) is the structural reason weak higher categories exist.

- [[Ex - The Eckmann-Hilton argument]] (⭐⭐) — two unital interchanging operations are equal, associative, and commutative; the source of abelian higher [[Def - Homotopy|homotopy]] [[Def - Group|groups]] and of braidings up the periodic table ([[Def - Strict n-Category and Strict ω-Category]], [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory]], [[Def - Monoidal Category]])
- [[Ex - A monoidal category is a one-object unbiased bicategory]] (⭐⭐) — the precise biased and unbiased dictionary for "monoidal = one-object bicategory"; the bottom row of the periodic table ([[Def - Unbiased Monoidal Category]], [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory]], [[Def - Monoidal Category]])
- [[Ex - Climbing the periodic table by stabilization]] (⭐⭐⭐) — verifying the first two columns; braided before symmetric; the stabilization hypothesis $k\geq n+2$ and its $\infty$-categorical refinement ([[Def - Monoidal Category]], [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory]], [[Def - Strict n-Category and Strict ω-Category]], [[Def - Unbiased Monoidal Category]])
