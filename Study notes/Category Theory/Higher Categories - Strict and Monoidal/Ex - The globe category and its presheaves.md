# Problem Statement

Let $\mathbb{G}$ be the **globe category**: objects $[0], [1], [2], \dots$, generated by arrows $\sigma_n, \tau_n : [n-1] \to [n]$ subject to the co-globularity relations $\sigma_n \sigma_{n-1} = \tau_n \sigma_{n-1}$ and $\sigma_n \tau_{n-1} = \tau_n \tau_{n-1}$.

(a) Show that a [[Def - Globular Set|globular set]] — a family of sets $X_n$ with maps $s, t : X_n \to X_{n-1}$ satisfying $ss = st$ and $ts = tt$ — is exactly a [[Def - Presheaf|presheaf]] $X : \mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$, and that morphisms of globular sets are natural transformations.

(b) Compute the hom-sets $\mathbb{G}([m], [n])$ for small $m, n$, and describe the representable presheaf $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-, [n])$ (the **standard $n$-globe**). How many cells does $G_n$ have in each dimension?

(c) Using the [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]], show that for any globular set $X$ there is a natural bijection $\mathbf{GSet}(G_n, X) \cong X_n$ — maps out of the standard $n$-globe pick out $n$-cells.

**Recall:**

A [[Def - Globular Set|globular set]] is, by the definition we are asked to reformulate, a tower of sets and source/target maps:

![[Def - Globular Set#The Definition]]

A [[Def - Presheaf|presheaf]] on a small category $\mathcal{D}$ is a [[Def - Functor|functor]] $\mathcal{D}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$; a morphism of presheaves is a [[Def - Natural Transformation|natural transformation]]. The [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]] states that for a presheaf $X$ on $\mathcal{D}$ and an object $d$, evaluation at the identity gives a natural bijection $[\mathcal{D}^{op},\mathbf{Set}](\mathcal{D}(-,d), X) \cong X(d)$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is a *presentation-identification* problem of the kind described in the topic page's problem-solving strategy — recognising hand-given cellular data as a presheaf category. The whole point is to convert "a tower of sets with structure maps and equations" into "a functor on a shape category," after which the standard presheaf toolkit (limits, colimits, representables, Yoneda) becomes available for free.

**Assumption pattern:** The given data are the sets $X_n$, the maps $s, t$, and the globularity equations $ss = st$, $ts = tt$. The unlock is that these equations are *exactly* the relations a functor must respect when the domain category has the matching co-relations. Recognising the equations as the contravariant image of the relations in $\mathbb{G}$ is what makes the reformulation work; the contravariance (presheaf = functor on $\mathbb{G}^{op}$) is why $ss = st$ matches $\sigma\sigma = \tau\sigma$ with the order of composition reversed.

**Theorem routing:** Part (a) is a direct unwinding of the definition of a [[Def - Functor|functor]] on $\mathbb{G}^{op}$. Part (b) is a computation in $\mathbb{G}$ using the co-globularity relations to count distinct arrows. Part (c) routes through the [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]], whose statement specializes to $\mathbf{GSet}(G_n, X) = \mathbf{GSet}(\mathbb{G}(-,[n]), X) \cong X([n]) = X_n$.

**Key decision point:** The non-obvious choice is to verify that *every* arrow of $\mathbb{G}$ is a composite of $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s reduced by the co-globularity relations to a normal form, so that a functor on $\mathbb{G}^{op}$ carries *no more and no less* data than $s$, $t$, and the equations. The tempting error is to think there might be extra arrows in $\mathbb{G}$ imposing extra conditions; the co-globularity relations are precisely what collapse the would-be extra arrows to the two iterated boundaries $s_p, t_p$.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Operation 1 from the topic page (recognise cellular data as a presheaf on a shape category).** This is the entire content of part (a): we identify the globular-set data with a functor $\mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$, after which everything in (b) and (c) is standard presheaf theory.

2. **Yoneda evaluation (a use of the topic-page principle that representables are the standard globes).** In part (c) we apply the [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]] to the representable $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-,[n])$, identifying maps out of the free globular set on an $n$-cell with the $n$-cells of the target — the higher-dimensional analogue of "a map out of the free object on one generator is an element."

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> A functor $X : \mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$ assigns a set $X([n]) =: X_n$ to each object and a function to each arrow. The generating arrows are $\sigma_n, \tau_n : [n-1]\to[n]$, which become functions $X(\sigma_n), X(\tau_n) : X_n \to X_{n-1}$ (contravariance reverses the direction). Call these $s$ and $t$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Functoriality forces $X$ to respect the relations of $\mathbb{G}$. The relation $\sigma_n\sigma_{n-1} = \tau_n\sigma_{n-1}$ becomes, after applying $X$ (which reverses composition order), $X(\sigma_{n-1})X(\sigma_n) = X(\sigma_{n-1})X(\tau_n)$, i.e. $s \circ s = s \circ t$. That is the first globularity equation; the second comes from the other relation.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For (b): every arrow $[m]\to[n]$ in $\mathbb{G}$ with $m < n$ is a composite of $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s; the co-globularity relations let you push all $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s into a normal form, leaving exactly *two* arrows $[m]\to[n]$ for each pair $m < n$ (the iterated all-source and all-target), plus the identity when $m = n$, and none when $m > n$.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For (c): $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-,[n])$ is a presheaf, so Yoneda applies verbatim: $\mathbf{GSet}(\mathbb{G}(-,[n]), X) \cong X([n]) = X_n$. Geometrically, a map from the standard $n$-globe into $X$ is exactly the data of where the single top $n$-cell of $G_n$ goes, and the boundary is then determined.

# Solution

The solution has three parts mirroring the problem. Part (a) unwinds the definition of a functor on $\mathbb{G}^{op}$ and matches the relations to the globularity equations. Part (b) counts arrows in $\mathbb{G}$ using the co-globularity relations to find that $G_n$ has two cells in each dimension below $n$ and one on top. Part (c) is a one-line specialization of Yoneda. The non-obvious move is the normal-form count in (b), which certifies that the presheaf data is neither more nor less than the globular-set data.

**Step 1: A globular set is a presheaf on $\mathbb{G}$.**

A functor $X : \mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$ assigns $X_n := X([n])$ and, to the generators $\sigma_n, \tau_n : [n-1]\to[n]$, functions $s := X(\sigma_n), t := X(\tau_n) : X_n \to X_{n-1}$; functoriality on the co-globularity relations yields exactly $ss = st$ and $ts = tt$. Conversely these data assemble into a functor. Naturality of a transformation is commutation with $s, t$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> A [[Def - Functor|functor]] $X : \mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$ consists of: an object-assignment $[n]\mapsto X_n$, and an arrow-assignment sending each $f : a \to b$ in $\mathbb{G}$ to $X(f) : X_b \to X_a$ (contravariant), preserving identities and composition: $X(g\circ f) = X(f)\circ X(g)$.
>
> On the generators $\sigma_n, \tau_n : [n-1]\to[n]$ this gives $s := X(\sigma_n) : X_n \to X_{n-1}$ and $t := X(\tau_n) : X_n \to X_{n-1}$. Now apply $X$ to the co-globularity relation $\sigma_n\circ\sigma_{n-1} = \tau_n\circ\sigma_{n-1}$ (an equality of arrows $[n-2]\to[n]$):
> $$X(\sigma_n\circ\sigma_{n-1}) = X(\sigma_{n-1})\circ X(\sigma_n) = s\circ s, \qquad X(\tau_n\circ\sigma_{n-1}) = X(\sigma_{n-1})\circ X(\tau_n) = s\circ t.$$
> (The composition order reverses because $X$ is contravariant.) Equality of the relation forces $s\circ s = s\circ t$, the first globularity equation. The other relation $\sigma_n\tau_{n-1} = \tau_n\tau_{n-1}$ gives $t\circ s = t\circ t$. So a functor produces a globular set.
>
> Conversely, given a globular set $(X_n, s, t)$, define $X([n]) = X_n$ and $X$ on each arrow of $\mathbb{G}$ by composing the assigned $s$'s and $t$'s; the globularity equations are exactly what make this well-defined on the relations, so $X$ is a functor. The two constructions are mutually inverse. A [[Def - Natural Transformation|natural transformation]] $\varphi : X\Rightarrow Y$ has components $\varphi_n : X_n \to Y_n$, and naturality squares for $\sigma_n, \tau_n$ are precisely $\varphi_{n-1}s = s\varphi_n$ and $\varphi_{n-1}t = t\varphi_n$ — a morphism of globular sets. Hence $\mathbf{GSet} = [\mathbb{G}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$.

**Step 2: Arrows of $\mathbb{G}$ and the standard globe $G_n$.**

For $m < n$ there are exactly two arrows $[m]\to[n]$ in $\mathbb{G}$ (the iterated all-source $\sigma^{(n-m)}$ and all-target $\tau^{(n-m)}$); for $m = n$ there is one (the identity); for $m > n$ there are none. Hence $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-,[n])$ has $(G_n)_m = 2$ for $m < n$, $(G_n)_n = 1$, and $(G_n)_m = \emptyset$ for $m > n$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> An arrow $[m]\to[n]$ is a word in $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s raising the index from $m$ to $n$, i.e. a sequence of $n-m$ choices "$\sigma$ or $\tau$" at each step up. A priori there are $2^{\,n-m}$ such words. The co-globularity relations $\sigma_n\sigma_{n-1} = \tau_n\sigma_{n-1}$ and $\sigma_n\tau_{n-1} = \tau_n\tau_{n-1}$ say that the *outermost* (last-applied, highest-index) letter is irrelevant: whatever the letter just below, replacing the top $\sigma$ by $\tau$ (or vice versa) does not change the arrow, as long as there is at least one letter below it. Iterating, every word is equal to one of just two normal forms — "all $\sigma$ except possibly the bottom letter" collapses, and one finds the arrow is determined entirely by its *bottom* letter $\sigma_{m+1}$ or $\tau_{m+1}$. Thus exactly two arrows $[m]\to[n]$ for $m < n$: $s_m := \sigma_{m+1}\sigma_{m+2}\cdots\sigma_n$ (collapsing to the bottom-$\sigma$ form) and $t_m := \tau_{m+1}\sigma_{m+2}\cdots$, the iterated all-source and all-target. For $m = n$ only $\mathrm{id}_{[n]}$; for $m > n$ none, since the generators only raise the index.
>
> Therefore $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-,[n])$ has $(G_n)_m = \mathbb{G}([m],[n])$ of size $2$ for $m < n$, size $1$ for $m = n$, and empty for $m > n$. Geometrically: a single top $n$-cell, whose source and target are two $(n-1)$-cells, each with two $(n-2)$-cell faces, and so on down to two $0$-cells. For $n=2$: two $0$-cells $a, b$, two parallel $1$-cells $f, g : a\to b$, one $2$-cell $\alpha : f\Rightarrow g$ — the bigon.

**Step 3: Yoneda — maps out of $G_n$ are $n$-cells.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> The [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]] for the presheaf category $[\mathbb{G}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$ states that for any presheaf $X$ and object $[n]$, evaluation at the identity gives a natural bijection
> $$[\mathbb{G}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]\big(\mathbb{G}(-,[n]),\, X\big) \;\cong\; X([n]).$$
> Since $G_n = \mathbb{G}(-,[n])$ and $X([n]) = X_n$, this reads $\mathbf{GSet}(G_n, X)\cong X_n$. Concretely, a morphism $G_n \to X$ is determined by the image of the unique top cell $1_{[n]}\in (G_n)_n$, which can be any $n$-cell of $X$; the rest of the map (on the boundary cells) is then forced by naturality. So the standard globe is the *free globular set on a single $n$-cell*, exactly as the standard simplex $\Delta^n$ is the free [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial set]] on an $n$-simplex.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **(a)** A functor $X:\mathbb{G}^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$ is the assignment $X_n := X([n])$ together with $s := X(\sigma_n)$, $t := X(\tau_n) : X_n\to X_{n-1}$. Contravariant functoriality applied to $\sigma_n\sigma_{n-1}=\tau_n\sigma_{n-1}$ gives $s\circ s = s\circ t$, and applied to $\sigma_n\tau_{n-1}=\tau_n\tau_{n-1}$ gives $t\circ s = t\circ t$ — the globularity equations. Conversely a globular set defines such a functor by composing $s,t$ along words, well-defined by the equations. Natural transformations are families $\varphi_n$ with $\varphi_{n-1}s=s\varphi_n$, $\varphi_{n-1}t=t\varphi_n$, i.e. morphisms of globular sets. Hence $\mathbf{GSet}=[\mathbb{G}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$.
>
> **(b)** Words in $\sigma,\tau$ from $[m]$ to $[n]$ ($m<n$) are reduced by the co-globularity relations (the top letter is irrelevant given a letter below) to exactly two normal forms, the iterated all-source $s_m$ and all-target $t_m$. So $\mathbb{G}([m],[n])$ has $2$ elements for $m<n$, $1$ for $m=n$, $0$ for $m>n$. Thus $G_n=\mathbb{G}(-,[n])$ has two cells in each dimension $<n$, one in dimension $n$, none above; for $n=2$ it is the bigon $a\overset{f}{\underset{g}{\rightrightarrows}}b$, $\alpha:f\Rightarrow g$.
>
> **(c)** By the [[Thm - The Yoneda Lemma|Yoneda lemma]], $\mathbf{GSet}(G_n,X)=[\mathbb{G}^{op},\mathbf{Set}](\mathbb{G}(-,[n]),X)\cong X([n])=X_n$, naturally in $X$. So $G_n$ is the free globular set on a single $n$-cell. $\qquad\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**The shape category turns hand-given cellular data into a presheaf, and presheaf theory then runs for free.** The single most reusable move in higher category theory is the one performed in part (a): whenever you are handed a tower of sets with structure maps satisfying some equations, look for a small category whose arrows and relations *are* those structure maps and equations, and recognise the data as a presheaf. The payoff is enormous — all [[Def - Limit and Colimit|limits and colimits]] (computed dimensionwise), cartesian closure, the topos structure, and the free objects (the representables) come at no cost. The same pattern identifies simplicial sets as presheaves on $\Delta$, cubical sets as presheaves on the box category, and opetopic sets as presheaves on the opetopes; learning to spot the shape category is learning to spot the whole toolkit.

**Contravariance is why the source/target equations match the co-relations with composition reversed.** A subtle but crucial point in part (a) is that a presheaf is a *contravariant* functor, so the relation $\sigma\sigma=\tau\sigma$ in $\mathbb{G}$ becomes $s\,s = s\,t$ — note the composition order flips. Getting the variance right is what makes the two presentations match exactly rather than approximately; a covariant functor on $\mathbb{G}$ would give "co-globular sets" with the maps pointing the wrong way. The trigger to internalise: whenever converting between "data with maps going down a tower" and "a functor on a category with arrows going up," the functor is contravariant, and every relation's composition order reverses.

**Representables are the basic shapes, and Yoneda says maps out of them probe cells.** The standard globe $G_n$, computed in part (b) and characterised in part (c), is the free globular set on one $n$-cell, and Yoneda makes "a map out of $G_n$ is an $n$-cell" precise. This is the higher-dimensional version of the universal property of a free object on one generator, and it is the template for building and probing presheaves everywhere: the standard simplices $\Delta^n$ play the identical role for simplicial sets, and "fill a horn" / "fill a globe" arguments are all maps-out-of-representables. Whenever you want to *specify* or *detect* a cell, reach for a map out of the corresponding representable; whenever you want to *build* a presheaf, glue representables along their boundaries via colimits.
