# Problem Statement

Let $\iota : \mathbf{Ab}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Grp}$ be the inclusion of abelian groups into all groups. For a group $G$, let $[G,G]$ be the **commutator subgroup**, generated by all commutators $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $G^{ab} := G/[G,G]$ the **abelianisation**.

**(a)** Show $[G,G]\trianglelefteq G$ and that $G^{ab}$ is abelian.

**(b)** Prove the universal property: every homomorphism $G\to A$ with $A$ abelian factors uniquely through the quotient map $\eta_G : G\to G^{ab}$.

**(c)** Conclude that $G\mapsto G^{ab}$ is left adjoint to $\iota$, so $\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [[Def - Reflective Subcategory|reflective subcategory]] of $\mathbf{Grp}$, and identify the unit and counit.

**Recall:**

An [[Def - Abelian Group|abelian group]] is a group whose operation commutes. A subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ is [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal]] if $gNg^{-1}=N$ for all $g$. A [[Def - Reflective Subcategory|reflective subcategory]] is a full subcategory whose inclusion has a left adjoint (the reflector). The [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]]: a homomorphism factors as a quotient followed by an injection.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is a "prove a subcategory is reflective by exhibiting the reflector and its universal property" problem (⭐⭐⭐), combining a group-theoretic construction (commutator subgroup) with the categorical recognition of a reflector.

**Assumption pattern:** The key assumptions: a homomorphism to an abelian group must *kill all commutators* (since $\phi(ghg^{-1}h^{-1})$ is a commutator in an abelian group, hence $e$), so $[G,G]\subseteq\ker\phi$; and $[G,G]$ is normal (generated by a conjugation-stable set). These two facts produce both the well-definedness of the quotient and the universal factorization.

**Theorem routing:** Show $[G,G]\trianglelefteq G$ (so $G^{ab}$ exists) $\Rightarrow$ any $\phi : G\to A$ ($A$ abelian) kills $[G,G]$ $\Rightarrow$ by the [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|universal property of the quotient]], $\phi$ factors through $G^{ab}$ uniquely $\Rightarrow$ this is the universal arrow, so $(-)^{ab}\dashv\iota$ by [[Def - Reflective Subcategory|definition]] and the [[Thm - Equivalence of the Definitions of Adjunction|universal-arrow form]].

**Key decision point:** The crux is identifying *exactly* $[G,G]$ as the kernel to quotient by — not less (you must kill all commutators for the target to be abelian) and not more (killing more would destroy the universal property, since $G^{ab}$ must be the *largest* abelian quotient). The commutator subgroup is the smallest normal subgroup with abelian quotient, which is precisely what makes the quotient universal.

# Solution

The abelianisation is the quotient by the commutator subgroup. Step 1 builds the object; Step 2 proves the universal property (every map to an abelian group factors through it); Step 3 reads this as the reflector adjunction. The thread: a map to an abelian group must kill commutators, and $[G,G]$ is exactly the commutators.

**Step 1: $[G,G]$ is normal and $G^{ab}$ is abelian (part a).**

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Normality.** $[G,G] = \langle ghg^{-1}h^{-1} : g,h\in G\rangle$. Conjugation by $x$ sends a generating commutator to another commutator:
> $$x(ghg^{-1}h^{-1})x^{-1} = (xgx^{-1})(xhx^{-1})(xgx^{-1})^{-1}(xhx^{-1})^{-1},$$
> so conjugation maps the generating set into itself, hence $x[G,G]x^{-1} = [G,G]$. Thus $[G,G]\trianglelefteq G$ and the [[Def - Quotient Group|quotient]] $G^{ab} = G/[G,G]$ is a group.
>
> **Abelian.** Writing $\overline{g}$ for the coset of $g$, $\overline{g}\,\overline{h}\,\overline{g}^{-1}\,\overline{h}^{-1} = \overline{ghg^{-1}h^{-1}} = \overline{e}$ because $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\in[G,G]$. So $\overline{g}\,\overline{h} = \overline{h}\,\overline{g}$ for all $g,h$: $G^{ab}$ is abelian.

**Step 2: The universal property (part b).**

Every $\phi : G\to A$ with $A$ abelian factors uniquely as $\phi = \overline{\phi}\circ\eta_G$ for a unique homomorphism $\overline{\phi} : G^{ab}\to A$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $A$ be abelian and $\phi : G\to A$. For any $g, h$, $\phi(ghg^{-1}h^{-1}) = \phi(g)\phi(h)\phi(g)^{-1}\phi(h)^{-1} = e$ because $A$ is abelian (the commutator of any two elements of an abelian group is trivial). Since the commutators generate $[G,G]$ and $\phi$ is a homomorphism, $[G,G]\subseteq\ker\phi$.
>
> By the universal property of the quotient (a homomorphism whose kernel contains the subgroup being quotiented descends to the quotient — [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]] machinery), there is a unique homomorphism $\overline{\phi} : G/[G,G]\to A$ with $\overline{\phi}\circ\eta_G = \phi$, namely $\overline{\phi}(\overline{g}) = \phi(g)$ (well-defined precisely because $[G,G]\subseteq\ker\phi$). Uniqueness holds because $\eta_G$ is surjective.

**Step 3: The reflector adjunction (part c).**

$(-)^{ab}\dashv\iota$, so $\mathbf{Ab}$ is reflective in $\mathbf{Grp}$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Step 2 says: for each $G$, the map $\eta_G : G\to\iota(G^{ab})$ is a [[Def - Universal Property and Universal Arrow|universal arrow]] from $G$ to $\iota$ — every map $G\to\iota A$ factors uniquely through it. By the [[Thm - Equivalence of the Definitions of Adjunction|universal-arrow form of an adjunction]], this is exactly the statement that $(-)^{ab}$ is left adjoint to $\iota$:
> $$\mathbf{Ab}(G^{ab}, A)\cong\mathbf{Grp}(G, \iota A), \qquad \overline{\phi}\mapsto\overline{\phi}\circ\eta_G.$$
> Since $\iota$ is a full inclusion, $\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [[Def - Reflective Subcategory|reflective subcategory]] with reflector $(-)^{ab}$.
>
> **Unit** $\eta_G : G\to G^{ab}$ is the quotient (abelianisation) map. **Counit** $\varepsilon_A : (\iota A)^{ab}\to A$ for $A$ abelian: since $A$ is already abelian, $[A,A] = \{e\}$, so $(\iota A)^{ab} = A/\{e\}\cong A$ and $\varepsilon_A$ is this isomorphism. The counit being an isomorphism confirms reflectivity and idempotence: abelianising an abelian group does nothing.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **(a)** $[G,G]\trianglelefteq G$: conjugating a generating commutator yields a commutator, so the generating set is conjugation-stable. $G^{ab} = G/[G,G]$ is abelian since $\overline{g}\,\overline{h}\,\overline{g}^{-1}\,\overline{h}^{-1} = \overline{ghg^{-1}h^{-1}} = \overline{e}$.
>
> **(b)** For abelian $A$ and $\phi : G\to A$, every commutator maps to $e$, so $[G,G]\subseteq\ker\phi$; the quotient's universal property gives a unique $\overline{\phi} : G^{ab}\to A$ with $\overline{\phi}\circ\eta_G = \phi$.
>
> **(c)** Thus $\eta_G : G\to G^{ab}$ is a universal arrow to $\iota$, so $(-)^{ab}\dashv\iota$ and $\mathbf{Ab}$ is reflective. Unit: the abelianisation quotient map. Counit: the isomorphism $(\iota A)^{ab}\cong A$ for abelian $A$. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**The reflector is the universal quotient that forces the defining property — and the commutator subgroup is exactly the right thing to kill.** Abelianisation works because $[G,G]$ is the *smallest* normal subgroup whose quotient is abelian: killing fewer elements leaves a non-abelian quotient, killing more loses the universal property. The pattern generalizes to every "force the structure" reflector: to reflect $\mathcal{C}$ into the subcategory of objects satisfying property $P$, quotient by the smallest congruence forcing $P$. For abelianisation the property $P$ is "commutative" and the congruence is $[G,G]$; for the maximal separated quotient of a topological space the property is "$T_0$" and one quotients by topological indistinguishability; for the reflection of rings into commutative rings one quotients by the ideal generated by $xy - yx$. The trigger is any "the universal X-ification" — find the minimal relations forcing the property, and the quotient is the reflector.

**A reflector's unit is a genuine quotient (not an inclusion), unlike free-forgetful units.** Here the unit $\eta_G : G\to G^{ab}$ is *surjective* — it throws information away — whereas the unit of a free-forgetful adjunction (insertion of generators) is *injective*. This is the signature of a reflective subcategory: the reflector approximates an object by an object *already in* the subcategory, and the unit is the universal map *to* that approximation, typically a quotient or a completion-style map. The counit, by contrast, is always an isomorphism for a reflective subcategory (an abelian group is its own abelianisation), which is the defining feature distinguishing reflective inclusions from general adjunctions. When you meet an "-ification", check: is the unit a quotient/completion (reflector) and is the counit an isomorphism (reflective)? If so, you inherit closure under limits and the colimit formula "reflect the ambient colimit".

**Recognising the reflector imports the abstract theorems for free.** Once $(-)^{ab}\dashv\iota$ is established, [[Thm - Right Adjoints Preserve Limits|RAPL]] tells you the inclusion preserves limits — so a product of abelian groups, computed in $\mathbf{Grp}$, is automatically abelian and is the product in $\mathbf{Ab}$ — and LAPC tells you the reflector preserves colimits, so the coproduct of abelian groups (the direct sum) is the abelianisation of the free product. You also get [[Thm - Adjoints are Unique up to Natural Isomorphism|uniqueness]] of the abelianisation up to canonical isomorphism and the idempotence $((G^{ab})^{ab}\cong G^{ab})$. This is the payoff of the categorical viewpoint: a single recognition ("this is a reflector") replaces case-by-case verification of all these properties, and the same dividends apply to completion, sheafification, and the Stone–Čech compactification. The companion exercise [[Ex - Sheafification as a reflective localization|Sheafification as a reflective localization]] runs the identical recognition where the "forced property" is the sheaf gluing axiom.
