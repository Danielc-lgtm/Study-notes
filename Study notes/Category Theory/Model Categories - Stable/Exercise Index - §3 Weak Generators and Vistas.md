## §3 Weak Generators and Vistas — Exercises

These exercises drill the recognition theory that reduces a triangulated category to algebra: the notion of a [[Def - Compact Weak Generator|compact weak generator]] and the Schwede–Shipley conclusion that one such generator presents the category as modules over an endomorphism ring spectrum. The first two are the two *poles* of the theorem run in full detail — the sphere spectrum $\mathbb{S}$ in $\mathcal{SH}$ (where $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S})$ is a genuine ring spectrum with infinitely many nonzero homotopy groups) and the free module $R$ in $D(R)$ (where $\mathrm{End}(R) = R$ is an ordinary ring). The third is a consolidation: matching all four canonical triangulated categories — $D(R)$, $\mathcal{SH}$, the stable module category, and the derived category of coherent sheaves — to their generators, deciding compactness, and seeing the full range of endomorphism objects from ordinary rings to dg-algebras. Throughout, the operative distinction is generation (cheap) versus compactness (the real content).

- [[Ex - The sphere spectrum is a compact generator of the stable homotopy category]] (⭐⭐⭐) — $\mathbb{S}$ generates and is compact; $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}) = \mathbb{S}$ is a ring spectrum, the maximally homotopical pole ([[Def - Compact Weak Generator]], [[Def - Triangulated Category]])
- [[Ex - R is a compact generator of its derived category]] (⭐⭐) — $R$ generates $D(R)$ via $[R[n], X] = H_n(X)$; $\mathrm{End}(R) = R$ is an ordinary ring, the degenerate pole ([[Def - Compact Weak Generator]], [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy]], [[Def - Module]])
- [[Ex - Identifying the triangulated examples]] (⭐⭐) — matching $D(R)$, $\mathcal{SH}$, $\underline{\mathbf{Mod}}_\Lambda$, $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}\,X)$ to generators, compactness, and endomorphism objects ([[Def - Triangulated Category]], [[Def - Compact Weak Generator]], [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy]])
