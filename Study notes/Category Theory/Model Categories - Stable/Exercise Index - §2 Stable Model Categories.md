## §2 Stable Model Categories — Exercises

These exercises drill the single defining property of a [[Def - Stable Model Category|stable model category]] — invertibility of suspension — from three angles. The first *verifies* stability in the calibration example, chain complexes, where the suspension is a literal degree shift and invertibility is a one-line relabeling; this is the template for "is this model category stable?". The second *refutes* stability for pointed spaces, the canonical non-example, by defeating essential surjectivity with a non-simply-connected space — the template for "this is only pre-triangulated." The third proves the *formal equivalence* of the several formulations of stability (equivalence, adjoint equivalence, $\Omega$ an equivalence) by adjunction yoga, justifying why the definition page lists them interchangeably. Verify-refute-formalize is the full toolkit for stability.

- [[Ex - Chain complexes form a stable model category]] (⭐⭐) — suspension is the degree shift $X[1]$, invertible on $D(R)$; the template for verifying stability ([[Def - Stable Model Category]], [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy]], [[Def - Module]])
- [[Ex - A pointed model category that is not stable]] (⭐⭐) — pointed spaces: $\Sigma$ is not essentially surjective ($S^1$ is not a suspension), so not stable, only pre-triangulated ([[Def - Stable Model Category]], [[Def - Topological Space]], [[Thm - Characterization of Stable Model Categories]])
- [[Ex - Suspension and loop are inverse equivalences in the stable case]] (⭐⭐⭐) — adjoint-equivalence formalism showing the formulations of stability coincide and $\Sigma^{-1} = \Omega$ is forced ([[Def - Stable Model Category]], [[Def - Adjunction]])
