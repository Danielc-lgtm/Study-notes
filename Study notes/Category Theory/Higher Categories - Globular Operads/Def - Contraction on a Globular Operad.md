# Notation

Let $P \xrightarrow{d} T1$ be a [[Def - Globular Operad|globular operad]] over the [[Def - The Free Strict ω-Category Monad|free strict ω-category monad]] $T$, with $T1 = \mathrm{pd}$ the globular set of **pasting diagrams** and $\partial : \mathrm{pd}(m+1) \to \mathrm{pd}(m)$ the boundary. For a globular set $X$ and $m \geq 1$, two cells $\alpha^-, \alpha^+ \in X(m)$ are **parallel** if either $m = 0$, or $m \geq 1$ and $s(\alpha^-) = s(\alpha^+)$ and $t(\alpha^-) = t(\alpha^+)$ — they share a common source and a common target one dimension down. For an operation $\theta \in P(\pi)$ over a pasting diagram $\pi \in \mathrm{pd}(m)$, its source and target are $s(\theta), t(\theta) \in P(\partial\pi)$. Given $\pi \in \mathrm{pd}(m)$ with $m \geq 1$, the set of **parallel pairs over the boundary of $\pi$** is
$$
\mathrm{Par}_P(\pi) = \{ (\alpha^-, \alpha^+) \in P(\partial\pi) \times P(\partial\pi) : \alpha^-, \alpha^+ \text{ are parallel} \}.
$$
A contraction is written $\chi$, with components $\chi_\pi : \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi) \to P(\pi)$. The full symbol registry is on [[Higher Categories — Globular Operads and Weak n-Categories]].

The notions **globular set**, **strict ω-category**, and **tame map** belong to chapters whose pages are not yet in the vault; they appear in bold and are restated as needed.

# Axiom Motivation

Here is the difficulty a contraction solves, and it is the central conceptual move of the entire Batanin–Leinster definition. We have the [[Def - The Free Strict ω-Category Monad|free strict ω-category monad]] $T$, whose terminal operad gives *strict* $\omega$-categories: exactly one composite per pasting diagram. We want *weak* $\omega$-categories, where composition is associative and unital only up to coherent higher cells. The question is: how do we say "up to coherent higher cells" operadically, without writing down an infinite tower of explicit coherence axioms (the path that makes weak $3$-categories already monstrous and weak $4$-categories essentially unmanageable)?

The answer is to demand, of the operad $P$, a *uniform supply of coherence cells*, generated by one simple closure condition. Picture what coherence cells are. In a weak $2$-category, the two ways of composing three $1$-cells, $(hg)f$ and $h(gf)$, are not equal but are related by an *associator* $2$-cell. Operadically, both bracketings are operations in $P(\partial\pi)$ over the boundary $\partial\pi$ of a $2$-dimensional pasting diagram $\pi$, and they are **parallel** (same source $0$-cell, same target $0$-cell). The associator is an operation in $P(\pi)$ whose source is $(hg)f$ and whose target is $h(gf)$. So a coherence cell is exactly: *a chosen operation over $\pi$ filling in between a parallel pair of operations over $\partial\pi$.* The same description covers unitors, interchangers, and every higher coherence: each is a lift of a parallel pair to one dimension up.

This is the desideratum, and it is what forces the definition. A contraction $\chi$ on $P$ should provide, for **every** pasting diagram $\pi$ and **every** parallel pair $(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)$ of operations over $\partial\pi$, a chosen operation $\chi_\pi(\alpha^-, \alpha^+) \in P(\pi)$ with source $\alpha^-$ and target $\alpha^+$. Three boundary conditions are then *non-negotiable*, and dropping any one destroys the meaning:

The **source condition** $s(\chi_\pi(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)) = \alpha^-$ and the **target condition** $t(\chi_\pi(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)) = \alpha^+$ are what make the chosen cell genuinely a coherence cell *between the given pair* rather than a random operation over $\pi$. Drop them and $\chi$ would assign cells with no relationship to the parallel pair, witnessing nothing; the associator might fail to go from $(hg)f$ to $h(gf)$, which is precisely the data we wanted it to carry. The **shape condition** $d(\chi_\pi(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)) = \pi$ keeps the lift in the correct fibre: a coherence cell witnessing two operations over $\partial\pi$ must itself be an operation over $\pi$, one dimension up. Drop it and the lift could land over the wrong pasting diagram, breaking compatibility with composition.

Now the deep point, the one that makes the definition powerful rather than merely a long list. **Why a uniform contraction, and not just "some" coherence cells?** Because demanding *all* parallel pairs be lifted, in *every* dimension, is what makes the coherence cells themselves cohere. A coherence cell between two coherence cells (a "coherence of coherences", such as the pentagon $3$-cell relating different ways of reassociating four $1$-cells) is *again a lift of a parallel pair, one dimension higher*. The single condition "every parallel pair lifts" therefore generates the entire infinite coherence tower automatically: the pentagon is not a separate axiom, it is the lift of a parallel pair of associator-composites, and its own coherence is the lift one dimension above that, ad infinitum. This is the genius of the contraction: an infinite tower of coherence data is compressed into one closure property. A reader who has fought through the explicit definition of a tricategory will appreciate that the contraction *replaces* all of that by a single clause.

What if we *strengthen* the condition — demand the lift be *unique*? In the infinite-dimensional case this is too strong: it would force coherence cells to be equalities one dimension down, collapsing weakness back to strictness. (In the finite-dimensional, $n$-category case the situation is subtler and is handled by **tameness** and **precontractions**: in the top dimension $n$ there is no room above to defer the relation, so one demands the source-target map be *injective* there — distinct parallel top-cells with the same image are equated — which is exactly what "$P$ is tame" means.) What if we *weaken* — demand lifts only in low dimensions? Then coherence runs out: the associativity $2$-cells would exist but the pentagon relating them would not be forced to exist, and the structure would not be coherent. The contraction must reach every dimension.

The test of the motivation: a reader who pictures "coherence cell = chosen filler of a parallel pair, one dimension up" and "coherence-of-coherences = the same, one dimension higher" should be able to reinvent the contraction as the demand that *all* such fillers exist uniformly, and should predict that this single demand encodes both weak composition (lifting a not-yet-composed parallel pair to its composite) and full coherence (lifting between competing composites).

# The Definition

Let $q : X \to Y$ be a map of **globular sets**. For $m \geq 1$ and $\beta \in Y(m)$, write
$$
\mathrm{Par}_q(\beta) = \{ (\alpha^-, \alpha^+) \in X(m-1) \times X(m-1) : \alpha^-, \alpha^+ \text{ parallel},\ q(\alpha^-) = s(\beta),\ q(\alpha^+) = t(\beta) \}.
$$

> A **contraction $\chi$ on the map $q$** is a family of functions
> $$
> \chi_\beta : \mathrm{Par}_q(\beta) \longrightarrow X(m), \qquad m \geq 1,\ \beta \in Y(m),
> $$
> such that for all $m \geq 1$, all $\beta \in Y(m)$, and all $(\alpha^-, \alpha^+) \in \mathrm{Par}_q(\beta)$,
> $$
> s\big(\chi_\beta(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)\big) = \alpha^-, \qquad t\big(\chi_\beta(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)\big) = \alpha^+, \qquad q\big(\chi_\beta(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)\big) = \beta.
> $$
> A map is **contractible** if it admits a contraction.

> A **contraction on a globular operad** $P \xrightarrow{d} T1$ is a contraction on the underlying map $d$. Concretely, writing $\mathrm{Par}_P(\pi) = \mathrm{Par}_d(\pi)$, it is a family of functions
> $$
> \chi_\pi : \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi) \longrightarrow P(\pi), \qquad \pi \in \mathrm{pd}(m),\ m \geq 1,
> $$
> assigning to each pasting diagram $\pi$ and each parallel pair $(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)$ of operations over the boundary $\partial\pi$ an operation $\chi_\pi(\alpha^-, \alpha^+) \in P(\pi)$ with source $\alpha^-$, target $\alpha^+$, and shape $\pi$. The operad $P$ is **contractible** if such a $\chi$ exists.

A map admitting a contraction is, in Leinster's phrase, *"injective on homotopy in an oriented sense"*: parallel cells downstairs that should be witnessed as equivalent are supplied with a witnessing cell upstairs.

In the finite-dimensional ($n$-category) setting one refines this: a **precontraction** on a map of $n$-globular sets satisfies the same source/target/shape conditions in dimensions $1 \leq m \leq n$; a map is **tame** if any two parallel top-dimensional ($n$-)cells with the same image are equal; and a **contraction** is a precontraction on a tame map. Tameness supplies the missing top-dimensional rigidity that the infinite case defers upward.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

Structurally, a contraction is a system of **lifts against the boundary**, and the cleanest way to see this is through the source-target pairing. For each pasting diagram $\pi$ there is a function
$$
(s, t) : P(\pi) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi), \qquad \theta \longmapsto (s(\theta), t(\theta)),
$$
sending an operation to its (parallel) source-target pair over the boundary. A **contraction is a section of every such pairing simultaneously**: it is a choice, for each $\pi$, of a one-sided inverse $\chi_\pi$ with $(s,t) \circ \chi_\pi = \mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Par}_P(\pi)}$. So "$P$ admits a contraction" says exactly "$(s,t)$ is surjective for every $\pi$, coherently chosen" — every parallel pair downstairs is hit by an operation upstairs.

This recasts contractibility as a **lifting property**, the same shape of condition that pervades homotopy theory. Form the boundary inclusion of globular sets and ask: does $P$ lift parallel pairs against it? A contractible operad is one for which all these lifts exist; the choice of lifts *is* the contraction. The analogy to a [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve|Kan complex]] is exact and not accidental: a Kan complex is a simplicial set in which every horn admits a filler, modelling a space; a contractible globular operad is one in which every parallel boundary admits a coherence-cell filler, modelling the coherence of a higher category. In both, "weakness handled correctly" is encoded by "all the right fillers exist".

The structural significance of the finite-dimensional refinement is illuminating here. In the infinite case the pairing $(s,t)$ need only be **surjective** (lifts exist; uniqueness is deferred upward forever). In the $n$-truncated case, in the top dimension $n$ there is no "upward" left, so one demands $(s,t)$ be a **bijection** in dimension $n$: surjective (a precontraction) *and* injective (tameness). Thus a contractible $n$-operad is *entirely determined by its $(n-1)$-dimensional part*: in top dimension $P(\pi)$ must be exactly the set $\mathrm{Par}_P(\partial^{-1}\dots)$ of parallel pairs below. This is why the finite case needs tameness and the infinite case does not — there is no top dimension in the infinite case to pin down.

There is a final structural reading via **homotopy**. The slogan "$P$ is contractible" matches the topological meaning of contractibility: the fibres of $d : P \to T1$ are, in an oriented homotopical sense, contractible — any two parallel cells are connected by a cell one dimension up, which is the directed analogue of "any two points are joined by a path, any two paths by a homotopy, and so on". The Batanin–Leinster operad $L$ is then "the free oriented-contractible operad over $T1$", the directed analogue of the free contractible space on a point.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

A contraction is the operadic incarnation of a **lifting/extension property**, and it is the directed cousin of two familiar conditions. In simplicial homotopy theory, a [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve|Kan complex]] $X$ is one where every horn $\Lambda^n_k \to X$ extends to $\Delta^n \to X$; this "all inner-and-outer horns fill" condition is what makes $X$ model a homotopy type, with composition defined only up to contractible choice. A contraction on a globular operad is the same philosophy in globular, directed, *algebraic* form: instead of asking that fillers merely exist (a property), the contraction *chooses* them (structure), and instead of horns it lifts parallel boundary pairs. The contrast "fillers as property (Kan/quasi-category) vs. fillers as chosen structure (contraction)" is exactly the divide between the geometric/non-algebraic and the algebraic definitions of higher category.

**True name:** *a contraction is "a coherent system of chosen coherence cells, generated by the single rule that every parallel pair of operations lifts to a cell one dimension up."* Operationally, when you see "$P$ is contractible" do not think of the formal source/target/shape equations — think "$P$ has all the associators, unitors, interchangers, pentagons, and their infinite tower of higher coherences, supplied uniformly by one closure condition." The lifting of a *not-yet-composed* parallel pair gives weak *composites*; the lifting *between two competing composites* gives *coherence*; both are the same operation, which is the whole compression.

The compression to one idea: *weak composition and coherence are the same phenomenon — a lift of a parallel pair against the boundary — and a contraction is the demand that all such lifts exist.* This single sentence replaces the entire explicit coherence apparatus of bicategories, tricategories, and beyond. It is the reason the Batanin–Leinster definition of weak $\omega$-category fits on one line ("an $L$-algebra") where the explicit definition of even a weak $4$-category does not fit in a paper.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the unique contraction on the terminal operad in low dimensions, failing in general.** The terminal globular operad $1$ has a single operation per shape, so for any pasting diagram $\pi$ and parallel pair $(\alpha^-, \alpha^+)$ over $\partial\pi$ there is exactly one candidate lift (the unique operation of shape $\pi$). The source/target/shape conditions then *force* $\alpha^- = \alpha^+ =$ the unique operation over $\partial\pi$; so the terminal operad admits a contraction only when distinct parallel pairs never occur, which in the infinite case fails. The corollary: the strict operad $1$ is *not* contractible in general, which is why strict $\omega$-categories are *not* the same as weak ones — strictness has no room to house non-trivial associators.

**Is an instance — the Batanin–Leinster operad $L$.** By construction $L$ is the *initial* globular operad carrying a contraction; it admits a contraction $\chi$ as part of its very definition, and its fibres $L(\pi)$ are exactly large enough to house all the freely-generated coherence cells and no more. This is the example that matters: a [[Def - Weak ω-Category and Weak n-Category (Batanin-Leinster)|weak ω-category]] is an $L$-algebra. See [[Thm - The Initial Contractible Globular Operad Exists]].

**Is an instance — an endomorphism operad of a contractible globular set.** If $X$ is a **contractible globular set** (any two parallel $n$-cells are joined by an $(n+1)$-cell), then its endomorphism operad $\mathrm{End}(X)$ admits a contraction: given a parallel pair of operations over $\partial\pi$, the contractibility of $X$ supplies a filling cell, and one assembles these into a contraction. Consequently $X$ acquires a weak $\omega$-category structure (pull back the contraction along $L \to \mathrm{End}(X)$). This is the directed analogue of "a contractible space is canonically an $\infty$-groupoid".

**Is NOT an instance — a discrete operad over a globular set with non-trivial parallel pairs.** Take a globular operad $P$ whose fibre $P(\pi)$ over some $2$-dimensional $\pi$ is *empty* while $P(\partial\pi)$ contains two distinct parallel operations. Then $\mathrm{Par}_P(\pi)$ is non-empty but $P(\pi)$ is empty, so no function $\chi_\pi : \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi) \to P(\pi)$ exists. This $P$ is not contractible: there is simply nowhere to put the coherence cell. It illustrates that contractibility is a real constraint, demanding the higher fibres be large enough.

**Is NOT an instance — a precontraction that is not a contraction (failure of tameness).** In the $n$-category setting, consider an $n$-operad with a precontraction whose top-dimensional source-target map $(s,t) : P(\pi) \to \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi)$ is surjective but *not* injective — two distinct parallel $n$-operations share the same boundary pair. This is precontractible but not contractible, because the map $d$ is not **tame**. Leinster notes such examples exist; they are precisely why the finite-dimensional definition needs tameness on top and the infinite-dimensional one does not.

**Calibration check.** Verify that a contraction makes the source-target pairing $(s,t) : P(\pi) \to \mathrm{Par}_P(\pi)$ surjective for every $\pi$ (the lift is a section). Confirm that in the top dimension of an $n$-operad, contractibility forces $(s,t)$ to be a *bijection* there, so that $P(\pi)$ in top dimension is determined as the set of parallel pairs below. Finally, explain in one sentence how the *same* lift mechanism produces both a weak composite (lift a parallel pair that has not yet been composed) and a coherence cell (lift between two competing composites) — if you can, you have understood why a contraction encodes weak composition and coherence simultaneously.

> [!tip] Weak ω-Category and Weak n-Category *(from this chapter)*
> The category $\mathbf{OC}$ of **globular operads-with-contraction** has pairs $(P, \chi)$ as objects and contraction-preserving operad maps as morphisms. Its **initial** object $(L, \chi)$ is the Batanin–Leinster operad, and a **weak ω-category** is an $L$-algebra; a **weak $n$-category** is its $n$-truncated analogue. See [[Def - Weak ω-Category and Weak n-Category (Batanin-Leinster)]].

> [!tip] System of Compositions and Coherence *(from Higher Operads)*
> Refinements of the definition split the contraction into a **system of compositions** (which fixes the weak *composites* — a chosen composite for each pasting diagram) and a **coherence** (which supplies the coherence cells between them). The initial operad-with-a-system-of-compositions-and-coherence is a variant of $L$; **Batanin's original definition** and Leinster's coincide on the resulting algebras.

> [!tip] Grothendieck ∞-Groupoids and the Homotopy Hypothesis *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> Replacing "operad with contraction" by **Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis coherators** yields **Grothendieck ∞-groupoids**, conjecturally equivalent to topological spaces — the **homotopy hypothesis**. The contraction here is the directed, operadic shadow of the coherator's "all fillers exist" condition; a contractible globular set being a weak $\omega$-category is the algebraic mirror of "a contractible space is an $\infty$-groupoid."
