## §2 Globular Operads — Exercises

This section installs the central object of the chapter — the globular operad — and the three exercises drill the three faces you must hold simultaneously: an operad's *algebras* (what structures it presents), its *maps* (and the workhorse identity "$P$-algebra $=$ map into the endomorphism operad"), and the *substitution product* that makes "globular operad $=$ monoid" well-posed and pins down exactly why cartesianness is required. Begin with the terminal-operad exercise to anchor "operad-algebra $=$ higher category" in the strict case; then the operad-map exercise for the identity used throughout §3; then the substitution-product exercise, the hardest, which exposes the machinery beneath the definition.

- [[Ex - Algebras for the terminal globular operad are strict omega-categories]] (⭐⭐) — unwind a terminal-operad algebra as "one composite per labelled shape" and recognize it as a strict $\omega$-category, learning "fibre size $=$ amount of weakness" ([[Def - Globular Operad]], [[Def - The Free Strict ω-Category Monad]], [[Def - Algebra for a Monad]])
- [[Ex - A globular operad map is determined by its action on operations]] (⭐) — characterize operad maps as collection maps plus equations and prove "$P$-algebra $=$ globular-operad map $P \to \mathrm{End}(X)$" by currying ([[Def - Globular Operad]], [[Def - The Free Strict ω-Category Monad]])
- [[Ex - The substitution product and why cartesianness is needed]] (⭐⭐⭐) — construct the substitution product $\otimes$ on collections, prove its associativity uses cartesianness, and break it with the non-cartesian multiset monad ([[Def - Globular Operad]], [[Def - The Free Strict ω-Category Monad]], [[Def - Pullback and Pushout]])
