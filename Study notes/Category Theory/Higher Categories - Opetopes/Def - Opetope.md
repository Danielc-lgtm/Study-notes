# Notation

We write $I = 1$ for the **identity operad** — the terminal operad, with one object and exactly one operation in each arity $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ — and $C^+$ for the [[Def - The Slice of a Generalized Multicategory|slice]] of a multicategory $C$. The $n$-fold iterated slice is $I^{+\cdots+}$ ($n$ plus-signs); we abbreviate the chain as $I, I^+, I^{++}, I^{+++}, \dots$. The set of **$n$-opetopes** is written $\mathcal{O}_n$. A general opetope is denoted $O$, its arity (number of source faces, when relevant) by $n$. The combinatorial encoding of an opetope is a finite, **rooted, planar tree**: the root edge is the output, the leaf edges are the inputs, the internal vertices carry lower-dimensional opetopes. The full symbol registry is on the parent page [[Higher Categories — Opetopes and Opetopic Sets]].

Standing convention: trees are **planar** (the branches at each vertex carry a fixed left-to-right order), because we work with non-symmetric multicategories whose operations have ordered inputs. Non-planar trees arise from symmetric multicategories and give a coarser, different theory.

# Axiom Motivation

An opetope is supposed to be a *cell shape* — the abstract geometry of an $n$-dimensional composition cell. The design problem is: what should the shapes be, in every dimension, so that they correctly model "many things composing to one thing", and so that the shapes in dimension $n$ are built canonically from the shapes in dimension $n-1$? Get this right and you have a uniform vocabulary for weak $n$-categories; get it wrong and the dimensions do not fit together.

Here is the constraint that drives everything. A composition cell takes a *configuration* of lower cells (its source) to a *single* lower cell (its target): a string of $1$-cells composes to one $1$-cell, a diagram of $2$-cells pastes to one $2$-cell. So the shape of an $n$-cell must record (i) a configuration of $(n-1)$-cells on the source side and (ii) a single $(n-1)$-cell on the target side. The source configuration is itself a pasting diagram — a *tree* of $(n-1)$-cells — and this is the recursion: the shape of an $n$-cell is "a pasting diagram of $(n-1)$-shapes, all composing to one $(n-1)$-shape." An $n$-opetope is a pasting diagram of $(n-1)$-opetopes. The recursion needs a base, and the base is forced too: the $0$-opetope is the **point** (a $0$-cell has no source and no target, it is just a cell), and the $1$-opetope is the **arrow** (a $1$-cell has one source point and one target point). From the arrow up, the recursion takes over.

Could a reader invent this? Yes, if they take seriously the demand that shapes-in-dimension-$n$ be *generated* from shapes-in-dimension-$(n-1)$ by the operation "pasting diagram of". The only non-obvious step is to recognise that "pasting diagram of operations of $C$" is exactly what the [[Def - The Slice of a Generalized Multicategory|slice C⁺]] packages, so the recursion "shapes up one dimension = pasting diagrams of shapes below" is literally "the slice of the previous level". That is why the official definition is "iterate the slice on $I$": it is the recursion made into a single repeated construction.

Why start from the **identity operad** $I = 1$ and not from some other base? Because $I$ is the simplest possible composition law — one operation in each arity, nothing to choose — so the shapes it generates are *pure* shapes, untainted by any extra structure. If we started from a richer operad, its operations would carry data beyond their shape (labels, relations), and the slices would inherit that data; the opetopes would no longer be bare cell-shapes but shapes-with-decoration. Starting from the terminal/identity operad is the choice that makes the tower *canonical*: there is exactly one such operad, so there is exactly one resulting hierarchy of opetopes. If we instead started from the empty or initial operad, slicing would produce nothing usable — there would be no operations to become objects one level up. The terminal operad is the unique base with "all arities, no choices", which is precisely what a shape generator should be.

What goes wrong with nearby alternatives? If you used **globes** as your base shapes — one source face, one target face, one shape per dimension — you would get globular sets and Batanin–Leinster weak $\omega$-categories, a perfectly good but *different* theory, in which composition is not native to the cell shape (you must specify pasting separately via an operad on top). If you used **simplices**, you would get the simplicial/quasi-category world, where cells are totally ordered chains and composition is encoded by horn-filling. The opetopic choice — many-in, one-out, generated by slicing — is the one that makes "several things compose to one thing" the *primitive* notion built into the shape itself, so that no extra pasting structure has to be bolted on. That is the payoff, and it is what would be lost by any of the alternatives.

# The Definition

An **opetope** is an object appearing in the iterated slice of the identity operad. Precisely, form the sequence of multicategories
$$I = 1, \quad I^+, \quad I^{++}, \quad I^{+++}, \quad \dots$$
where each term is the [[Def - The Slice of a Generalized Multicategory|slice]] of the previous. For each $n \geq 0$, the set of **$n$-opetopes** is the object-set of the $n$-th term:
$$\mathcal{O}_n \;:=\; \mathrm{ob}\big(I^{+\cdots+}\big) \quad (n \text{ plus-signs}; \ \mathcal{O}_0 := \mathrm{ob}(I)).$$
An **opetope** is an $n$-opetope for some $n$.

Unwinding the bottom of the recursion:

- The **$0$-opetope** is the single object of $I$ — the **point**. There is exactly one: $|\mathcal{O}_0| = 1$.
- The **$1$-opetope** is an object of $I^+$, i.e. an operation of $I$. Since $I$ has one operation per arity and the relevant cell is the unary one, the canonical $1$-opetope is the **arrow** (one source point, one target point). There is exactly one $1$-opetope: $|\mathcal{O}_1| = 1$.
- A **$2$-opetope** is an object of $I^{++}$, i.e. an operation of $I^+$, i.e. a pasting diagram of $1$-opetopes (arrows) composing to one arrow. These are the **many-in, one-out** cells: for each $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ there is one $2$-opetope with $n$ source arrows and one target arrow. So $\mathcal{O}_2$ is countably infinite, indexed by arity.
- An **$n$-opetope** (for $n \geq 1$) is a pasting diagram of $(n-1)$-opetopes — a configuration of $(n-1)$-opetopes on the source side, all pasting to a single $(n-1)$-opetope on the target side.

**Combinatorial form (Appendix E).** Each opetope corresponds to a finite, rooted, planar tree, in which the root edge is the output face, the leaf edges are the input faces, and each internal vertex is labelled by a lower-dimensional opetope. The face maps (which lower opetopes form the source and target of $O$) are read off from the tree. Several equivalent definitions of "tree" — as planar embeddings, as combinatorial species, as elements of a free operad — all yield the same notion of opetope (this is the content of Appendix E's equivalence).

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

Opetopes sit in a triad of "cell-shape" notions, each the index category for a flavour of higher-categorical presheaf. **Simplices** $[n] = \{0 < \dots < n\}$ give totally ordered chains and index [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial sets]]; **globes** give one-in, one-out cells and index globular sets; **opetopes** give many-in, one-out cells and index [[Def - Opetopic Set|opetopic sets]]. The compression is that all three are "the smallest cell-shapes closed under the composition pattern you care about": for simplices, composing along a shared vertex in a linear order; for globes, composing one cell after another in the same dimension; for opetopes, composing a configuration of cells into one. An opetope is the many-in, one-out member of this family.

**True name:** an opetope is *a pasting diagram of lower opetopes, recorded as a tree* — the root is what it composes to, the leaves are what it is composed of. When you need to think about a specific opetope, draw its tree; the tree *is* the opetope.

There is a further compression with **free operads**: the operations of a free operad are exactly trees, and the opetopes are exactly the trees that arise from iterating the slice on $I$. So "opetope" and "tree of trees of ... of points" are the same object viewed combinatorially, and the apparent novelty of the definition dissolves into the very old combinatorics of rooted planar trees.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the unique $0$- and $1$-opetopes.** There is exactly one $0$-opetope (the point) and exactly one $1$-opetope (the arrow), because $I$ has one object and the relevant cell of $I^+$ is unique. These are the two "atoms" from which all higher opetopes are pasted; in any opetopic set they index the $0$-cells (objects) and $1$-cells (morphisms).

**Is an instance — the $2$-opetope of arity $n$.** For each natural number $n$ there is a $2$-opetope with $n$ source arrows $f_1, \dots, f_n$ arranged head-to-tail and one target arrow $g$; it is the shape of "the $2$-cell witnessing that $g$ is the composite of $f_1, \dots, f_n$". Its tree is a single vertex (the target $2$-cell) with $n$ incoming leaf edges (the sources) and one root edge (the target). The arity-$2$ case is the familiar globular-looking 2-cell $f_2 \circ f_1 \Rightarrow g$; the arity-$0$ case is the shape of a unit (a $2$-cell with *no* source arrows, witnessing an identity), which has no globular analogue.

**Is an instance — a $3$-opetope.** A $3$-opetope is a pasting diagram of $2$-opetopes: a tree whose vertices are arity-$n$ $2$-cells, glued along shared arrows, all composing to a single $2$-cell. For example, take two arity-$2$ source $2$-cells stacked so the output arrow of one is an input arrow of the next, composing to one arity-$3$ target $2$-cell; this $3$-opetope is the associativity-style coherence cell relating the two ways of composing three $1$-cells. The proliferation is real: $\mathcal{O}_3$ is infinite, parametrised by all such trees of $2$-cells.

**Is NOT an instance — the $2$-globe is not a $2$-opetope.** The $2$-globe has exactly one source $1$-cell and one target $1$-cell (one-in, one-out). The arity-$2$ opetope, by contrast, has *two* source arrows and one target. Only the arity-$1$ opetope coincides with the $2$-globe. So globes are the arity-$1$ slice of opetopes, and the genuinely opetopic cells (arity $\neq 1$) have no globular counterpart. Treating a $2$-opetope as a globe loses the many-in structure that is the whole point.

**Is NOT an instance — a non-planar tree is not a (plain) opetope.** A tree in which the branches at a vertex are *unordered* (a non-planar tree) encodes an operation of a *symmetric* multicategory, not a non-symmetric one. It is not a plain opetope; it belongs to the symmetric (Baez–Dolan original) theory, which identifies opetopes that the planar theory keeps distinct. The order of the inputs is genuine data here.

**Calibration check.** Verify: (1) that $|\mathcal{O}_0| = |\mathcal{O}_1| = 1$ but $\mathcal{O}_2$ is infinite, by counting the slices; (2) that the arity-$2$ opetope is the only $2$-opetope coinciding with the $2$-globe; (3) that you can draw, as trees, the $2$-opetopes of arities $0, 1, 2, 3$ (a vertex with $0, 1, 2, 3$ leaves and one root) and at least one $3$-opetope (a tree of two such vertices). If you can do these, you have understood that an opetope is a tree, generated by slicing.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Opetopic Set *(from this chapter)*
> The opetopes assemble into a category $\mathbb{O}$, and a presheaf on it is an [[Def - Opetopic Set|opetopic set]] — a collection of cells of every opetopic shape, glued along faces. This is the exact analogue of a [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial set]] (presheaf on the simplex category) and of a globular set.

> [!tip] Opetopic Weak n-Categories *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> An [[Thm - Baez-Dolan Opetopic Weak n-Categories|opetopic weak n-category]] is an opetopic set in which the many-in, one-out niches have universal fillers. The opetopes are the shapes; the filler condition is the structure that turns a bare collection of shapes into a weak higher category.

> [!tip] The Periodic Table and Stabilization *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> Opetopes were introduced to phrase the **periodic table** of $k$-tuply monoidal $n$-categories and the **stabilization hypothesis**. Having a canonical supply of cell-shapes in every dimension is the prerequisite for stating those structural conjectures.
