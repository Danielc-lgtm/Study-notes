## §1.2 Duality and the Opposite Category — Exercises

These exercises drill the [[Def - Opposite Category and Duality|opposite category]] and the [[Thm - The Duality Principle|duality principle]] it powers: the formal "reverse all arrows" operation that halves the labour of the subject. The first verifies by hand the single translation — mono in $\mathcal{C}$ becomes epi in $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ — that the duality principle then automates for every statement. The second instantiates $(-)^{\mathrm{op}}$ on the two degenerate kinds of category, finding that posets reverse their order while groups are self-opposite via inversion. The third is the hard impossibility argument that $\mathbf{Set} \not\simeq \mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}$, locating an in/out asymmetry no equivalence could match.

- [[Ex - Monos in a category are epis in the opposite]] (⭐) — translating a cancellation property across $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$; mono and epi as a dual pair ([[Def - Opposite Category and Duality]], [[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism]])
- [[Ex - The opposite of a poset and of a group]] (⭐) — computing $(-)^{\mathrm{op}}$ on a poset (order reverses) and a group (self-opposite via $g \mapsto g^{-1}$) ([[Def - Opposite Category and Duality]], [[Def - Group]], [[Def - Category]])
- [[Ex - Set is not equivalent to its opposite]] (⭐⭐⭐) — impossibility via a preserved invariant; the initial/terminal asymmetry of $\mathbf{Set}$ ([[Def - Opposite Category and Duality]], [[Def - Equivalence of Categories]], [[Def - Full, Faithful, and Essentially Surjective Functor]])
