## §1.5 Equivalence of Categories — Exercises

These exercises drill [[Def - Equivalence of Categories|equivalence]] and the [[Thm - Characterization of Equivalence|full + faithful + essentially surjective]] criterion, the working definition of "the same category". The first is the canonical application: finite-dimensional [[Def - Vector Space|vector spaces]] are equivalent — but not isomorphic — to the category of matrices, proved by the three local conditions rather than a quasi-inverse. The second separates equivalence from isomorphism with a minimal example, locating the gap in object-count. The third shows equivalences preserve and reflect every categorical property ([[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism|monos, epis]], limits), the formal content of "categorically the same".

- [[Ex - Finite-dimensional vector spaces are equivalent to the category of matrices]] (⭐⭐) — proving an equivalence by the three local conditions; $\mathbf{Mat}_k$ as a skeleton of $\mathbf{FinVect}_k$ ([[Def - Equivalence of Categories]], [[Thm - Characterization of Equivalence]], [[Def - Full, Faithful, and Essentially Surjective Functor]])
- [[Ex - Equivalence versus isomorphism of categories]] (⭐⭐) — weakening equality of round-trips to natural isomorphism; the object-count gap ([[Def - Equivalence of Categories]], [[Def - Functor]], [[Def - Natural Transformation]])
- [[Ex - Equivalences preserve monos epis and limits]] (⭐⭐) — transporting categorical properties via full faithfulness and essential surjectivity ([[Def - Equivalence of Categories]], [[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism]], [[Def - Full, Faithful, and Essentially Surjective Functor]])
