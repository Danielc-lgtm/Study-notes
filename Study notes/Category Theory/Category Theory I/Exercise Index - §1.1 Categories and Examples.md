## §1.1 Categories and Examples — Exercises

These exercises drill the basic anatomy of a [[Def - Category|category]]: what the axioms forbid, how familiar algebraic structures are categories in disguise, and how to manufacture new categories from old. The first probes the gap between the [[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism|mono/epi/iso]] notions — the lesson that "injective and surjective" need not mean "invertible" outside $\mathbf{Set}$. The second collapses the number of objects to recover a monoid, installing the "category is a many-object monoid" frame. The third constructs slice and arrow categories, the first instances of "diagrams of a fixed shape form a category" and the gateway to limits and functor categories.

- [[Ex - Mono and epi need not imply iso]] (⭐⭐) — verifying mono/epi by cancellation, not by elements; an epimorphism that is not surjective ([[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism]], [[Def - Ring Homomorphism]], [[Def - Category]])
- [[Ex - A monoid is a one-object category]] (⭐) — the object-count dial; a category as a many-object monoid, a [[Def - Group|group]] as a one-object groupoid ([[Def - Category]], [[Def - Group]], [[Def - Groupoid]])
- [[Ex - Slice and arrow categories]] (⭐⭐) — building new categories by inheriting composition and adding a commuting constraint; the arrow category as $[\mathbf{2}, \mathcal{C}]$ ([[Def - Category]], [[Def - Commutative Diagram]], [[Def - Functor Category]])
