## §1.4 Natural Transformations and Functor Categories — Exercises

These exercises drill the [[Def - Natural Transformation|naturality square]] and the structure of the [[Def - Functor Category|functor category]]. The first is the example that named the subject: the [[Def - Dual Space|double dual]] is a natural transformation while the single dual is not, with the difference traced to variance. The second shows the [[Def - Determinant|determinant]] is natural because it is "the same polynomial in every ring" — the lesson that a uniform formula is automatically a natural transformation. The third unwinds components and naturality squares on the [[Def - Category|walking arrow]] and proves the componentwise characterization of isomorphisms in a functor category, the standard route to natural isomorphisms.

- [[Ex - The double dual natural transformation]] (⭐⭐) — verifying a naturality square by chasing an element; double dual is natural, single dual is not (variance) ([[Def - Natural Transformation]], [[Def - Dual Space]], [[Def - Dual Map]])
- [[Ex - The determinant is a natural transformation]] (⭐⭐) — naturality of a uniform formula; ring maps commute with the determinant polynomial ([[Def - Natural Transformation]], [[Def - Determinant]], [[Def - Functor]])
- [[Ex - Components and naturality squares]] (⭐) — components and squares on the walking arrow; isomorphisms in $[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$ are exactly natural isomorphisms ([[Def - Natural Transformation]], [[Def - Functor Category]], [[Def - Functor]])
