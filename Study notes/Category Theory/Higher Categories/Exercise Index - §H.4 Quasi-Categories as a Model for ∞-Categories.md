## §H.4 Quasi-Categories as a Model for ∞-Categories — Exercises

These exercises drill the inner-horn-filling condition that defines $\infty$-categories and the taxonomy it produces. The first proves that every nerve is a quasi-category with *unique* inner fillers — the case where composition is single-valued — re-proving the forward half of the nerve characterisation by interpreting horns as composable strings. The second shows Kan complexes are quasi-categories by index-set containment, and identifies the inner/outer horn split as the $\infty$-category / $\infty$-groupoid split. The third verifies that the homotopy category of a quasi-category is genuinely a category, the technical crux being that composition is well-defined up to homotopy via higher inner fillers. The shared technique is "translate horn-filling into a statement about composites", with the inner/outer distinction as the recurring fork.

- [[Ex - The nerve of a category is a quasi-category]] (⭐⭐⭐) — unique inner fillers from single-valued composition and associativity ([[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve]], [[Def - Quasi-Category]], [[Def - Category]])
- [[Ex - Every Kan complex is a quasi-category]] (⭐⭐) — inner horns ⊆ all horns; outer filling = invertibility; the converse fails ([[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve]], [[Def - Quasi-Category]])
- [[Ex - The homotopy category of a quasi-category]] (⭐⭐⭐) — homotopy is an equivalence relation, composition independent of the filler, associativity via a $3$-horn ([[Def - Quasi-Category]], [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Quasi-Category]], [[Def - Category]])
