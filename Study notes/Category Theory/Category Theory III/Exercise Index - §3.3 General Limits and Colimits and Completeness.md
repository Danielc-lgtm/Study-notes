## §3.3 General Limits and Colimits and Completeness — Exercises

These exercises step up from special shapes to the general notions of [[Def - Limit and Colimit|limit and colimit]] and the property of [[Def - Complete and Cocomplete Category|completeness]]. The techniques drilled are: decoding universal properties in a thin (poset) category so that limits become meets and joins; verifying completeness via the [[Thm - Products and Equalizers Give All Limits|reduction theorem]] ("products + equalizers ⇒ complete") rather than per-shape; and recognising set operations like intersection as limits computed by pullbacks. Together they install the habit of computing any limit as "the product of vertices cut down by the edge equations" — compatible families.

- [[Ex - Limits and colimits in a poset are meets and joins]] (⭐) — in a thin category universal properties degenerate to order relations; product = meet, coproduct = join, complete category = complete lattice ([[Def - Limit and Colimit]], [[Def - Product and Coproduct]], [[Def - Category]])
- [[Ex - Set is complete and cocomplete]] (⭐⭐) — completeness by the two-check reduction; the explicit limit in $\mathbf{Set}$ is the set of compatible families ([[Def - Complete and Cocomplete Category]], [[Thm - Products and Equalizers Give All Limits]], [[Thm - Limits in Set and in Functor Categories]])
- [[Ex - An intersection is a pullback and a limit]] (⭐⭐) — intersection of subobjects is the pullback of inclusions, hence a limit; the mono hypothesis turns the fibre product into the intersection ([[Def - Pullback and Pushout]], [[Def - Limit and Colimit]], [[Def - Isomorphism, Monomorphism, Epimorphism]])
