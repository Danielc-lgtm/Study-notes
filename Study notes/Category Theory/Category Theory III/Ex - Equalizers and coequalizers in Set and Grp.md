# Problem Statement

Compute [[Def - Equalizer and Coequalizer|equalizers and coequalizers]] in $\mathbf{Set}$ and $\mathbf{Grp}$. Show that the equalizer of $f, g : A \rightrightarrows B$ in $\mathbf{Set}$ is the inclusion of the agreement-set $\{a : f(a) = g(a)\}$, and in $\mathbf{Grp}$ the inclusion of the agreement-subgroup. Show that the coequalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$ is the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by $\{(f(a), g(a))\}$, and in $\mathbf{Grp}$ the quotient $B / N$ by the [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal closure]] $N$ of $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\}$. Verify that every equalizer is a monomorphism.

**Recall:**

An **equalizer** of $f, g : A \rightrightarrows B$ is $e : E \to A$ with $fe = ge$, universal: every $z : Z \to A$ with $fz = gz$ factors uniquely through $e$. A **coequalizer** is $q : B \to Q$ with $qf = qg$, universal among maps out of $B$ coequalizing the pair.

![[Def - Quotient Group#The Definition]]

The **normal closure** of a subset $S \subseteq G$ is the smallest [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal subgroup]] containing $S$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is a paired "identify the (co)limit concretely" problem, with the equalizer (a subobject) and coequalizer (a quotient) treated together as duals. The routine: for the equalizer, exhibit the agreement-subobject and verify universality; for the coequalizer, exhibit the universal quotient.

**Assumption pattern:** The discriminating structure is, as with coproducts, whether the *quotient* must respect algebraic structure. The equalizer is easy and uniform — agreement-subobjects exist in both categories with the subspace structure. The coequalizer is where $\mathbf{Set}$ and $\mathbf{Grp}$ diverge: in $\mathbf{Grp}$ a quotient must be by a [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal]] subgroup, so the relation must be normalised.

**Theorem routing:** The equalizer verification routes through "a map factors through the agreement-subobject iff its image satisfies $f = g$". The $\mathbf{Set}$ coequalizer routes through "the universal coequalizing quotient is by the *generated* equivalence relation". The $\mathbf{Grp}$ coequalizer routes through "$B \to X$ coequalizes $f, g$ iff it kills each $f(a)g(a)^{-1}$ iff it factors through $B / N$ for $N$ the normal closure", connecting to the [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]] via $N = \ker q$.

**Key decision point:** The crux on the coequalizer side is recognising that in $\mathbf{Grp}$ you cannot quotient by an arbitrary subgroup — only normal ones give groups — so the universal coequalizing object must be $B$ modulo the *normal closure* of $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\}$, not merely the subgroup generated by those elements. The natural-but-wrong guess "quotient by the generated subgroup" fails to be a group unless that subgroup happens to be normal.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Verify a universal property of a candidate (from the topic page).** Exhibit the agreement-subobject (for the equalizer) and the universal quotient (for the coequalizer), and check the factorisation.

2. **Translate "coequalizes $f, g$" into "kills $f(a)g(a)^{-1}$" (algebraic rewriting).** In $\mathbf{Grp}$, $qf = qg \iff q(f(a)g(a)^{-1}) = e$ for all $a$, converting the coequalizer condition into a kernel condition and routing to the [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]].

3. **Use uniqueness to deduce the monomorphism property (operation: exploit universality).** Every equalizer is monic because two maps factoring identically through $e$ must coincide by uniqueness of the factorisation.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> For the equalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$, the candidate object is the literal set of points where $f$ and $g$ agree. A test map $z$ factors through it exactly when its image lands in the agreement set, i.e. exactly when $fz = gz$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> For the $\mathbf{Set}$ coequalizer, a quotient map $q$ coequalizes $f, g$ iff $q(f(a)) = q(g(a))$ for all $a$. The smallest quotient with this property identifies exactly $f(a) \sim g(a)$ — and everything that follows by transitivity.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For the $\mathbf{Grp}$ coequalizer, rewrite $qf = qg$ as $q(f(a)g(a)^{-1}) = e$. So $q$ must kill all $f(a)g(a)^{-1}$; the universal such $q$ kills exactly the smallest *normal* subgroup containing them.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> Monomorphism: suppose $e u = e u'$ for two maps into the equalizer. Both $u, u'$ are factorisations of the same map $e u$ through $e$; uniqueness forces $u = u'$.

# Solution

The plan is four computations. Equalizers in both categories are agreement-subobjects, verified identically. The $\mathbf{Set}$ coequalizer is the quotient by the generated equivalence relation; the $\mathbf{Grp}$ coequalizer is the quotient by the normal closure, because group quotients require normality. Finally, universality forces every equalizer to be monic.

**Step 1: Equalizers are agreement-subobjects in $\mathbf{Set}$ and $\mathbf{Grp}$.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $E = \{a \in A : f(a) = g(a)\}$ with inclusion $e : E \hookrightarrow A$. Then $fe = ge$ by construction. Given $z : Z \to A$ with $fz = gz$, every $z(w)$ satisfies $f(z(w)) = g(z(w))$, so $z(w) \in E$; hence $z$ corestricts to a unique $u : Z \to E$ with $eu = z$. So $E$ is the equalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$. In $\mathbf{Grp}$, if $f, g$ are homomorphisms then $E$ is a subgroup (closed under product and inverse, since $f, g$ agree on each), the inclusion is a homomorphism, and the same corestriction $u$ is a homomorphism; so $E$ is the equalizer in $\mathbf{Grp}$.

**Step 2: The coequalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$ is the quotient by the generated equivalence relation.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $\sim$ be the smallest equivalence relation on $B$ with $f(a) \sim g(a)$ for all $a$, and $q : B \to B/\!\sim$ the quotient map. Then $qf = qg$. Given $z : B \to X$ with $zf = zg$, we have $z(f(a)) = z(g(a))$, so $z$ is constant on each generating pair, hence (being a function) constant on each $\sim$-class; therefore $z$ factors as $z = u q$ for a unique $u : B/\!\sim \to X$. So $B/\!\sim$ is the coequalizer. The *generation* (transitive closure) is essential: $z$ respects the full equivalence relation, not just the raw pairs.

**Step 3: The coequalizer in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is the quotient by the normal closure.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> A homomorphism $q : B \to X$ satisfies $qf = qg$ iff $q(f(a)g(a)^{-1}) = e$ for all $a$, i.e. iff $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\} \subseteq \ker q$. Since $\ker q$ is [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal]], this holds iff $N \subseteq \ker q$, where $N$ is the *normal closure* of $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\}$. By the universal property of the [[Def - Quotient Group|quotient]] / [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]], such $q$ factor uniquely through the quotient map $B \to B/N$. Hence $B/N$, with the quotient map, is the coequalizer. Taking $g$ trivial recovers "$B$ modulo the normal closure of $\mathrm{im}\,f$".

**Step 4: Every equalizer is a monomorphism.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $e : E \to A$ be an equalizer and suppose $eu = eu'$ for $u, u' : Z \to E$. Then $w := eu = eu'$ satisfies $fw = ge$... more precisely $fw = f(eu) = (fe)u = (ge)u = gw$, so $w$ equalizes $f, g$ and factors uniquely through $e$. Both $u$ and $u'$ are such factorisations, so by uniqueness $u = u'$. Hence $e$ is monic. (Dually, every coequalizer is epic.)

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Equalizers.** $E = \{a : f(a) = g(a)\} \hookrightarrow A$ satisfies $fe = ge$; any $z$ with $fz = gz$ has image in $E$ and corestricts uniquely to $u : Z \to E$ with $eu = z$. In $\mathbf{Grp}$, $E$ is a subgroup and $u$ a homomorphism. So the equalizer is the agreement-subobject in both.
> **Coequalizer in $\mathbf{Set}$.** $B/\!\sim$ for $\sim$ the equivalence relation generated by $\{(f(a),g(a))\}$, with quotient map $q$; any $z$ with $zf = zg$ is constant on $\sim$-classes and factors uniquely through $q$.
> **Coequalizer in $\mathbf{Grp}$.** $B/N$ for $N$ the [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal closure]] of $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\}$; since $qf = qg \iff N \subseteq \ker q$, the [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]] gives the unique factorisation through $B/N$.
> **Monomorphism.** If $eu = eu'$, both factor the equalizing map $eu$ through $e$, so $u = u'$ by uniqueness; $e$ is monic. Each construction is unique up to unique isomorphism by [[Thm - Limits are Unique up to Unique Isomorphism|uniqueness of (co)limits]]. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**Equalizers are subobjects and coequalizers are quotients — restriction versus collapse.** The reusable framing is that the equalizer *selects* the part of the domain where two maps agree (a subobject, always monic), while the coequalizer *forces* two maps to agree by collapsing the codomain (a quotient, always epic). This over/under duality — equalizer over $A$, coequalizer under $B$ — is the geometric content, and it explains at a glance why kernels (subobjects) are equalizers and quotients are coequalizers. The trigger: "the locus where two things agree" calls for an equalizer; "the universal way to make two things equal" calls for a coequalizer.

**Quotients in algebra must be normal, and the coequalizer is exactly the universal normalisation.** The decisive lesson on the coequalizer side is that $\mathbf{Grp}$ does not let you quotient by an arbitrary relation: only [[Def - Normal Subgroup|normal]] subgroups give groups, so the universal coequalizing object is $B$ modulo the *normal closure* of $\{f(a)g(a)^{-1}\}$, not the plain subgroup they generate. This is the categorical reason normality is forced — it is the price of the coequalizer being a group. The transferable diagnostic: when computing a colimit (coequalizer, pushout, cokernel) in an algebraic category, the relation you impose must be closed under whatever the category requires (normal closure in $\mathbf{Grp}$, submodule in $\mathbf{Mod}_R$, ideal in $\mathbf{Ring}$), and the coequalizer automatically takes that closure.

**Universality alone forces monic/epic — no element-chasing needed.** The proof that every equalizer is a monomorphism uses *only* the uniqueness clause of the universal property, not any feature of the category. This is a template: many "structural" properties of universal constructions (equalizers are monic, coequalizers are epic, the pullback of a mono is a mono) follow from uniqueness of factorisations alone and hold in every category. Recognising that "uniqueness $\Rightarrow$ cancellability" is a one-line argument saves you from re-deriving these facts concretely each time, and it is the same move that proves [[Thm - Limits are Unique up to Unique Isomorphism|limits are unique up to unique isomorphism]]. See [[Ex - The first isomorphism theorem as a coequalizer]] for the deeper link between coequalizers and quotient maps.
