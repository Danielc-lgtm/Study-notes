## §3.2 Equalizers, Coequalizers, Pullbacks, and Pushouts — Exercises

These exercises develop the limits and colimits that *cut out* and *glue*: [[Def - Equalizer and Coequalizer|equalizers and coequalizers]] (subobjects where maps agree / quotients forcing them to agree) and [[Def - Pullback and Pushout|pullbacks and pushouts]] (fibre products and amalgamated sums). The unifying technique is to recognise a classical algebraic object — kernel, quotient, the first isomorphism theorem — as one of these universal constructions, which transports its structural properties (normality, monic/epic) for free. The hardest exercise reframes the [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem|first isomorphism theorem]] as an image factorisation, the categorical statement that quotients are coequalizers of kernel pairs.

- [[Ex - Equalizers and coequalizers in Set and Grp]] (⭐⭐) — equalizers are agreement-subobjects, coequalizers are quotients; in $\mathbf{Grp}$ the coequalizer needs the normal closure ([[Def - Equalizer and Coequalizer]], [[Def - Quotient Group]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Thm - Limits are Unique up to Unique Isomorphism]])
- [[Ex - The kernel as a pullback]] (⭐⭐) — the kernel is the pullback of $\varphi$ against the basepoint; normality is transported through the pullback ([[Def - Pullback and Pushout]], [[Def - Equalizer and Coequalizer]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Thm - Limits are Unique up to Unique Isomorphism]])
- [[Ex - The first isomorphism theorem as a coequalizer]] (⭐⭐⭐) — the quotient map is the coequalizer of the kernel pair; the first isomorphism theorem is the (regular epi, mono) image factorisation ([[Def - Equalizer and Coequalizer]], [[Def - Pullback and Pushout]], [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem]], [[Def - Quotient Group]])
