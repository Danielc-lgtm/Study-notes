# Problem Statement

Construct the [[Def - fc-Multicategory|fc-multicategory]] $\mathbb{B}\mathrm{im}$ whose four layers are:

- **objects:** [[Def - Ring|rings]] $R, S, T, \dots$ (associative, unital);
- **vertical $1$-cells** $R \to S$: ring homomorphisms;
- **horizontal $1$-cells** $R \nrightarrow S$: $(R, S)$-bimodules $M$ (left $R$-, right $S$-action, compatible);
- **$2$-cells** from a string $(M_1, \dots, M_n)$ — with $M_i$ an $(R_{i-1}, R_i)$-bimodule — to a single $(S_0, S_n)$-bimodule $P$, with vertical boundaries $f : R_0 \to S_0$ and $g : R_n \to S_n$: maps $\theta : M_1 \otimes_{R_1} M_2 \otimes_{R_2} \cdots \otimes_{R_{n-1}} M_n \to P$ that are *additive* and *equivariant* along the boundary homomorphisms, i.e. $\theta(r\, x) = f(r)\,\theta(x)$ and $\theta(x\, r) = \theta(x)\,g(r)$ for the outer actions.

Verify that substitution of $2$-cells is well-defined and associative, that identity $2$-cells exist, and that $\mathbb{B}\mathrm{im}$ is a genuinely non-trivial example — it has *more than one object* and *non-identity vertical $1$-cells*, so it is not a multicategory, monoidal category, or bicategory, though it is (more than that) a [[Def - Weak Double Category|weak double category]].

**Recall:**

![[Def - fc-Multicategory#The Definition]]

For [[Def - Ring|rings]] $R, S$, an **$(R,S)$-bimodule** is an abelian group $M$ with a left $R$-action and a right $S$-action that commute: $(r m) s = r (m s)$. For an $(R,S)$-bimodule $M$ and an $(S,T)$-bimodule $N$, the **[[Def - Tensor Product of Modules|tensor product]] over $S$**, $M\otimes_S N$, is the abelian group generated by symbols $m\otimes n$ modulo bilinearity and the balancing relation $ms\otimes n = m\otimes sn$; it is an $(R,T)$-bimodule. A map out of $M_1\otimes_{R_1}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n$ is the same as a map "multilinear and middle-balanced" in the $M_i$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** A *template-identification* problem at the hardest dial setting — *both* "many objects" and "non-trivial vertical structure" — verifying that the full four-layer fc-multicategory is genuinely needed. This is the existence-of-a-rich-example problem from the topic page's targets.

**Assumption pattern:** Rings carry two kinds of map of equal importance: homomorphisms (the "maps", which compose strictly — vertical) and bimodules (the "processes", composed by $\otimes$ — horizontal). The boundary equivariance condition on $2$-cells is what couples the two kinds, and it is exactly the data that a four-layer structure (and nothing smaller) can hold.

**Theorem routing:** This is the prototypical example behind [[Thm - Monoids and Modules Form a Bicategory]] (rings are monads in the one-object $\mathbf{Ab}$-bicategory, bimodules are their bimodules) and a non-trivial witness for [[Thm - fc-Multicategories Subsume Bicategories and Monoidal Categories]] showing the "many objects / non-trivial vertical" corner is inhabited. Substitution-associativity routes through associativity of $\otimes_S$ and of function composition.

**Key decision point:** The non-obvious choice is how to define a $2$-cell over a *string* of bimodules: not as an iterated map, but as a single map out of the *iterated tensor* $M_1\otimes_{R_1}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n$. This is what makes substitution associative for free (it is composition of maps after tensoring), and it is the precise sense in which "the string is the un-composed tensor". The tempting alternative — defining $2$-cells only over single bimodules — collapses to a double category and loses the substitution law.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Operation 1 (set the dials).** Here both dials are "on": many objects, non-trivial vertical structure. We confirm the example sits in the richest corner.

2. **Operation 2 (re-encode a composite as a universal $2$-cell out of a string).** The iterated tensor $M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_n$ is the universal target, so a $2$-cell over the string is a map out of it.

3. **Operation 6 (form the balanced tensor as a coequalizer).** The tensor over $R_i$ is the coequalizer balancing the adjacent actions; this is what makes the iterated tensor well-defined.

4. **Operation 8 (empty string for units).** The length-zero string at $R$ is represented by $R$ itself as an $(R,R)$-bimodule.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> A $2$-cell over $(M_1, \dots, M_n)$ should be a single map out of the iterated tensor $M_1\otimes_{R_1}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n$, *not* a list of maps. Why is a map out of the iterated tensor the same as a "middle-balanced multilinear map" in the $M_i$? Recall the universal property of $\otimes$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Substitution: given $\theta$ out of $M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_n$ and, for each $i$, a $2$-cell $\theta_i$ out of $\bigotimes S_i$ landing in $M_i$, you must produce a $2$-cell out of $\bigotimes(S_1\cdots S_n)$. Tensor the $\theta_i$ together (over the appropriate rings) and post-compose with $\theta$. Use that $\otimes$ is functorial and associative.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For associativity of substitution, lean on two facts: function composition is associative, and the tensor over rings is associative up to canonical iso, $(M\otimes_S N)\otimes_T P\cong M\otimes_S(N\otimes_T P)$. The identity $2$-cell $1_M$ is $\mathrm{id}_M : M\to M$ (length-one string). For non-triviality, just observe $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ are distinct objects and the inclusion $\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}$ is a non-identity vertical $1$-cell.

# Solution

We define the data, verify the fc-multicategory axioms, and confirm non-triviality.

**Step 1: $2$-cells over a string are maps out of the iterated tensor.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> For a string $R_0\xrightarrow{M_1}R_1\xrightarrow{M_2}\cdots\xrightarrow{M_n}R_n$ (each $M_i$ an $(R_{i-1},R_i)$-bimodule) and a target $(S_0,S_n)$-bimodule $P$ with boundaries $f : R_0\to S_0$, $g : R_n\to S_n$, a $2$-cell is a homomorphism of abelian groups
> $$\theta : M_1\otimes_{R_1}M_2\otimes_{R_2}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n\longrightarrow P$$
> equivariant along the *outer* actions: $\theta(r\cdot x)=f(r)\cdot\theta(x)$ for $r\in R_0$ and $\theta(x\cdot r)=\theta(x)\cdot g(r)$ for $r\in R_n$. By the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property of the tensor product]], a map out of the iterated tensor is exactly a map of the underlying $M_i$ that is additive in each variable and balanced at each interior ring $R_1,\dots,R_{n-1}$ (i.e. $m_i r\otimes m_{i+1}=m_i\otimes r m_{i+1}$). So the $2$-cells are precisely the "multilinear, middle-balanced, boundary-equivariant" maps. For $n=0$, the empty tensor at $R_0$ is the ring $R_0$ itself viewed as an $(R_0,R_0)$-bimodule, and a $2$-cell out of the empty string is an equivariant map $R_0\to P$.

**Step 2: Substitution is well-defined.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Given $\theta : M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_n\to P$ and, for each $i$, a $2$-cell $\theta_i : \bigotimes S_i\to M_i$ (with matching boundary homomorphisms forming a compatible chain $f_1,\dots,f_{n+1}$), tensor the $\theta_i$ over the appropriate rings to get
> $$\theta_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta_n : \textstyle\bigotimes(S_1\cdots S_n)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } (\bigotimes S_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes(\bigotimes S_n)\longrightarrow M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_n,$$
> using functoriality of $\otimes$ and the canonical associativity isomorphism to regroup. Post-compose with $\theta$:
> $$\theta\cdot(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_n) := \theta\circ(\theta_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta_n) : \textstyle\bigotimes(S_1\cdots S_n)\to P.$$
> This is additive, middle-balanced (each $\theta_i$ is, and balancing is preserved by tensoring), and equivariant along the outer boundaries $f\circ f_1$ and $g\circ f_{n+1}$ (composites of equivariant maps along composite homomorphisms). So it is a legitimate $2$-cell.

**Step 3: Associativity, identities, and non-triviality.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Associativity.* Substituting strings into strings into strings produces, in both bracketings, the same map $\bigotimes(\text{fully flattened string})\to P$: function composition is associative, and the regrouping isos cohere by the [[Thm - Mac Lane Coherence Theorem|pentagon]] for $\otimes$ (equivalently, $(M\otimes_S N)\otimes_T P\cong M\otimes_S(N\otimes_T P)$ canonically). This is exactly $\mathrm{fc}$'s associativity of path-concatenation realised on the tensor side.
>
> *Identities.* For each bimodule $M$, the identity $2$-cell $1_M : (M)\Rightarrow M$ is $\mathrm{id}_M$ with identity boundary homomorphisms; substituting $1_{M_i}$ into $\theta$ returns $\theta$, and substituting $\theta$ into $1_P$ (length one) returns $\theta$.
>
> *Non-triviality.* The objects include distinct rings $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$, so there is more than one object. The inclusion $\iota : \mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}$ is a vertical $1$-cell that is not an identity (and not invertible), so the vertical structure is non-trivial. Hence $\mathbb{B}\mathrm{im}$ is neither a multicategory (those have one object) nor a bicategory/monoidal category (those have trivial vertical structure). It is in fact a [[Def - Weak Double Category|weak double category]], since every string has the universal composite $M_1\otimes_{R_1}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n$ — but as a plain fc-multicategory it already witnesses the richest dial corner.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> Define $\mathbb{B}\mathrm{im}$: objects are rings; vertical $1$-cells are ring homomorphisms (strict composition); horizontal $1$-cells $R\nrightarrow S$ are $(R,S)$-bimodules; a $2$-cell from $(M_1,\dots,M_n)$ to $P$ with boundaries $f,g$ is an additive map $\theta : M_1\otimes_{R_1}\cdots\otimes_{R_{n-1}}M_n\to P$ with $\theta(rx)=f(r)\theta(x)$ and $\theta(xr)=\theta(x)g(r)$ on the outer actions (the empty string at $R$ giving $R$ as an $(R,R)$-bimodule). By the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property of the tensor product]], such $\theta$ are the multilinear middle-balanced boundary-equivariant maps. Substitution is $\theta\cdot(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_n)=\theta\circ(\theta_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta_n)$, well-defined since tensoring preserves additivity, balancing, and equivariance, and lands over the concatenated string by the regrouping iso. Associativity holds because function composition is associative and $\otimes$ is associative up to coherent iso (pentagon); the units are $\mathrm{id}_M$. The objects $\mathbb{Z}\neq\mathbb{Q}$ and the non-identity homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}$ show $\mathbb{B}\mathrm{im}$ has many objects and non-trivial vertical structure, so it inhabits the four-layer corner; representability of every string by the iterated tensor makes it a weak double category. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**Bimodules are the canonical reason all four layers are needed.** The structures studied earlier in the chapter — multicategories, monoidal categories, bicategories — each switch off some dial. The ring/bimodule example switches *every* dial on: ring homomorphisms are genuine non-invertible vertical maps that compose strictly, bimodules are genuine horizontal processes composed by a weak tensor, and the boundary-equivariance of $2$-cells couples the two. This is the example to keep in mind whenever someone asks "why bother with the full fc-multicategory rather than a bicategory?": because the world has objects with two equally-important kinds of map, and only the four-layer structure holds both plus their interaction. The transferable trigger is "two kinds of arrow of equal status on the same objects" $\Rightarrow$ "reach for a double category / fc-multicategory".

**A $2$-cell over a string is a map out of the iterated tensor — this is the substitution-makes-sense move.** The decisive modelling choice is to define $2$-cells over $(M_1,\dots,M_n)$ as single maps out of $M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_n$, rather than as lists of maps. This is what makes substitution associative essentially for free: it becomes composition of functions after tensoring, and tensoring is functorial and coherently associative. The general principle, recurring in [[Thm - Monoids and Modules Form a Bicategory]], is that "balanced multilinear map" $=$ "map out of the tensor" via the universal property, so the un-composed string and the iterated tensor are two views of one thing. When you next model a multi-input operation, model it as a map out of a universal object, and the composition law will write itself.

**Weakness is in the horizontal tensor, strictness in the vertical homomorphisms — and that asymmetry is forced by the mathematics, not chosen for convenience.** Ring homomorphisms compose strictly because they are honest functions; bimodule tensor associates only up to the canonical iso $(M\otimes_S N)\otimes_T P\cong M\otimes_S(N\otimes_T P)$ because $\otimes$ is built by a universal construction. So the chapter's standing convention "vertical strict, horizontal weak" is not a stipulation but an observation about where weakness genuinely lives. Recognising this tells you, in any new example, which direction to make strict (the maps) and which to make weak (the processes built by colimits/tensors). It is also exactly why the strict double category of rings and bimodules *does not exist* — see [[Ex - The double category of rings and bimodules is weak not strict]] — and why the honest home for bimodules is a *weak* structure.
