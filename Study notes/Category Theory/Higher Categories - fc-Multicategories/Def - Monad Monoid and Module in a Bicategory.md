# Notation

Throughout, $\mathcal{K}$ is a [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory|bicategory]]: it has objects (also called $0$-cells) $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \dots$; $1$-cells $s, t : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ between objects, composed by $\circ$ (or by juxtaposition $ts$ for $s : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ then $t : \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{C}$); and $2$-cells $\alpha : s \Rightarrow s'$ between parallel $1$-cells, composed *vertically* by $\cdot$ and *horizontally* by $\ast$. Each object $\mathcal{A}$ has an identity $1$-cell $1_{\mathcal{A}} : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$. Composition of $1$-cells is associative and unital only up to coherent invertible $2$-cells (the associator and unitors); we suppress these where harmless and write $tt$ for $t \circ t$ etc. The full registry is on [[Higher Categories — fc-Multicategories and Weak Double Categories]].

This is a compound page: it defines **four** interlocking notions — a *monad in a bicategory*, a *monoid* (the one-object special case of the same definition), a *module / bimodule* over such monads, and the *bicategory* $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$ they assemble into — because they are introduced together and none is fully usable without the others.

# Axiom Motivation

The ordinary notion of a [[Def - Monad and Comonad|monad]] on a category $\mathcal{C}$ is an endofunctor $T : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$ with a multiplication $\mu : TT \Rightarrow T$ and a unit $\eta : 1 \Rightarrow T$ satisfying associativity and unit laws. Stare at this data and strip away the word "category": what you actually used is that $T$ is a $1$-cell from an object to *itself*, that $TT$ is the composite of that $1$-cell with itself, that $\mu$ and $\eta$ are $2$-cells, and that there is an identity $1$-cell $1$. Every one of those ingredients makes sense in *any* [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory|bicategory]] $\mathcal{K}$, not just in the $2$-category $\mathbf{Cat}$ of categories, functors, and natural transformations. So the definition of a monad *wants* to be stated bicategorically — and the moment you state it that way, you discover that the *same three axioms* describe wildly different things depending on which bicategory you are in.

The driving desideratum is therefore generality with zero added cost: define "monad in $\mathcal{K}$" by transcribing the monad axioms with "$1$-cell" for "endofunctor" and "$2$-cell" for "natural transformation", and harvest the special cases. The harvest is spectacular. In $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Cat}$ you recover an ordinary monad — this is the consistency check, and it must hold or the generalisation is wrong. In $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ a monad turns out to be exactly a small [[Def - Category|category]]. In $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Rel}$ (sets and relations) a monad is exactly a preorder. These are not analogies; they are *equalities* once you unwind the definitions, and the fact that "category" and "monad" become the same word in the right bicategory is one of the genuinely surprising unifications in the subject.

Why a *monoid* and a *monad* by one definition? Because a monad in $\mathcal{K}$ is literally a monoid in the [[Def - Monoidal Category|monoidal category]] $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A})$ of endo-$1$-cells of a fixed object $\mathcal{A}$, with $\otimes = \circ$ (composition) and unit $I = 1_{\mathcal{A}}$. A [[Def - Monoid in a Monoidal Category|monoid]] in a monoidal category $(\mathcal{V}, \otimes, I)$ is an object $t$ with $\mu : t \otimes t \to t$ and $\eta : I \to t$ satisfying associativity and unit laws — transcribe with $\otimes = \circ$ and you have a monad. So "monoid" is the one-object view ("hom-monoidal-category view") and "monad" is the whole-bicategory view; the page treats them together because they are the same axioms read in two registers. This also explains why the definition has *exactly* a multiplication and a unit and *exactly* the associativity and unit laws: those are the monoid axioms, no more and no less, and any extra axiom would exclude legitimate examples (a category, after all, has only composition and identities — nothing else).

Now the modules. Given a monoid in algebra you immediately ask for its modules; the same reflex here is productive and forced. A **left module** over a monad $t$ on $\mathcal{A}$ should be a $1$-cell $x$ "into" $\mathcal{A}$ carrying an action $tx \Rightarrow x$ compatible with $\mu$ and $\eta$ — exactly the monoid-action axioms transcribed. A **bimodule** between a monad $s$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and a monad $t$ on $\mathcal{B}$ is a $1$-cell $m : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ with a left $t$-action and a right $s$-action that commute. The reason to bother is that *bimodules compose* — given a $(t,s)$-bimodule $m$ and an $(s,r)$-bimodule $n$, their "tensor over $s$" is a $(t,r)$-bimodule — and this composition is the engine of the next theorem: monads and bimodules in $\mathcal{K}$ form a new bicategory $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$ (see [[Thm - Monoids and Modules Form a Bicategory]]). If you dropped the compatibility axioms on the action, the tensor-over-$s$ would not be well-defined and $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$ would collapse — the action laws are precisely what make the bimodule tensor associative, the same role the module axioms play in ordinary ring theory.

What if we dropped the *unit* $\eta$ and kept only $\mu$? Then in $\mathbf{Cat}$ we would get a non-unital monad ("semigroup monad"), and in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ we would get a category *without identities* — a semicategory. These exist and are occasionally useful, but they break the bridge to ordinary categories (which have identities) and they break the bimodule unit, so $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$ would lose its identity $1$-cells. What if we dropped *associativity* of $\mu$? In $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ the would-be "category" would have a composition that does not associate — not a category at all. Each axiom is pinned by what it becomes in the example bicategories, which is the cleanest possible motivation: you cannot weaken the monad-in-a-bicategory axioms without breaking the very examples that justify the definition.

# The Definition

Fix a [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory|bicategory]] $\mathcal{K}$.

**Monad in $\mathcal{K}$.** A **monad** in $\mathcal{K}$ is an object $\mathcal{A}$ together with a $1$-cell $t : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$ and $2$-cells
$$\mu : t\, t \Longrightarrow t \qquad (\text{multiplication}), \qquad \eta : 1_{\mathcal{A}} \Longrightarrow t \qquad (\text{unit}),$$
satisfying the **associativity law** $\mu \cdot (\mu \ast 1_t) = \mu \cdot (1_t \ast \mu)$ and the **unit laws** $\mu \cdot (\eta \ast 1_t) = 1_t = \mu \cdot (1_t \ast \eta)$ (each equality holding up to the bicategory's coherence isomorphisms). When $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Cat}$, this is exactly an ordinary [[Def - Monad and Comonad|monad]] $(T, \mu, \eta)$ on the category $\mathcal{A}$.

**Monoid in $\mathcal{K}$.** A **monoid** in $\mathcal{K}$ is the same data viewed inside one hom-category: an object $\mathcal{A}$ and a [[Def - Monoid in a Monoidal Category|monoid]] $(t, \mu, \eta)$ in the [[Def - Monoidal Category|monoidal category]] $\big(\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A}),\, \circ,\, 1_{\mathcal{A}}\big)$. "Monad in $\mathcal{K}$" and "monoid in some hom-category of $\mathcal{K}$" are the same thing.

**Left module over a monad.** Given a monad $(t, \mu, \eta)$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and an object $\mathcal{X}$, a **left $t$-module** is a $1$-cell $x : \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{A}$ with an action $2$-cell $\lambda : t\, x \Rightarrow x$ satisfying $\lambda \cdot (\mu \ast 1_x) = \lambda \cdot (1_t \ast \lambda)$ (associativity) and $\lambda \cdot (\eta \ast 1_x) = 1_x$ (unitality). A **right module** is defined dually with an action $\rho : x\, t \Rightarrow x$.

**Bimodule.** Given monads $s$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and $t$ on $\mathcal{B}$, a **$(t, s)$-bimodule** is a $1$-cell $m : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ equipped with a left $t$-action $\lambda : t\,m \Rightarrow m$ and a right $s$-action $\rho : m\,s \Rightarrow m$ that are individually associative-and-unital and that **commute**: $\lambda \cdot (1_t \ast \rho) = \rho \cdot (\lambda \ast 1_s)$. A **module map** $(m, \lambda, \rho) \Rightarrow (m', \lambda', \rho')$ is a $2$-cell $\alpha : m \Rightarrow m'$ commuting with both actions.

**The bicategory $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$.** When $\mathcal{K}$ has the local coequalizers needed to form bimodule tensors, monads in $\mathcal{K}$, bimodules, and module maps assemble into a bicategory $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$: objects are monads, $1$-cells are bimodules with horizontal composition the *tensor over the middle monad*, and $2$-cells are module maps. The construction and its axioms are the content of [[Thm - Monoids and Modules Form a Bicategory]].

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The structural heart is the slogan **"a monad in $\mathcal{K}$ is a monoid in an endo-hom-monoidal-category of $\mathcal{K}$"**, and it is worth spelling out why this is a *definition*, not merely an analogy. Fix an object $\mathcal{A}$ of the [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory|bicategory]] $\mathcal{K}$. The hom-category $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A})$ of endo-$1$-cells and $2$-cells between them is a [[Def - Monoidal Category|monoidal category]]: its tensor is composition of $1$-cells, $s \otimes t := s \circ t$, its unit is $1_{\mathcal{A}}$, and the associator and unitors of the monoidal structure are inherited from those of $\mathcal{K}$. A [[Def - Monoid in a Monoidal Category|monoid]] in this monoidal category is an object $t$ (an endo-$1$-cell) with $\mu : t \otimes t \to t$ and $\eta : I \to t$ obeying the monoid axioms — which, translated back through $\otimes = \circ$ and $I = 1_{\mathcal{A}}$, are exactly the monad axioms. So *monad in $\mathcal{K}$ at the object $\mathcal{A}$* $=$ *monoid in $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A})$*, on the nose.

This reframing immediately delivers the module theory by transport of the ordinary algebraic notions. A left module over a monoid $t$ in a monoidal category $\mathcal{V}$ is an object $x$ with $t \otimes x \to x$ obeying action axioms; transported to $\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A})$ (a *left* module category over $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})$), it is precisely the left $t$-module above. Bimodules are objects of a hom-category $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B})$ that are simultaneously left-acted by the endo-monoid $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}) \ni t$ and right-acted by $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}) \ni s$ via the bicategory's composition, with the actions commuting. The tensor of bimodules over the shared monad is the coequalizer that, in the algebraic prototype, *is* $\otimes_S$. So the entire monoid/module apparatus of ordinary algebra lifts wholesale into any bicategory; the only new input is that "the ring" is now an endo-$1$-cell and "multiplication" is now $2$-cell composition.

There is a dual entry point worth recording: a monad in $\mathcal{K}$ is the same as a *lax functor from the terminal bicategory $\mathbf{1}$ into $\mathcal{K}$*. A lax functor $F : \mathbf{1} \to \mathcal{K}$ must pick an object $F(\ast) = \mathcal{A}$, a $1$-cell $F(1_\ast) = t$, and laxity $2$-cells $F(1_\ast)\,F(1_\ast) \Rightarrow F(1_\ast)$ and $1_{\mathcal{A}} \Rightarrow F(1_\ast)$ — which are exactly $\mu$ and $\eta$ — subject to the lax-functor coherence, which is exactly the monad associativity and unit laws. This is the cleanest one-line characterisation: **a monad in $\mathcal{K}$ is a lax functor $\mathbf{1} \to \mathcal{K}$.**

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The compression to carry away is that **"monoid", "monad", "category", and "preorder" are one definition read in four bicategories**. The definition is "object with associative unital multiplication" inside a monoidal hom-category; the four words are what that becomes in $\mathcal{V} = \mathbf{Set}$ (monoid), $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Cat}$ (monad), $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ (small category), $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Rel}$ (preorder). This is the same compression that drives the whole of Leinster's book: fix one structural template and vary the ambient bicategory.

In algebra, the prototype is unmistakable: a monad on a one-object setting is a [[Def - Ring|ring]] viewed as a monoid (forgetting addition), its modules are the ring's modules, bimodules are bimodules, and the tensor of bimodules is $\otimes_R$. The bicategory $\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{K})$ is the abstraction of "the bicategory of rings, bimodules, and bimodule maps", and the identification of small categories with monads in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ shows that *category theory itself* is a special case of the bimodule calculus — composing profunctors is tensoring bimodules over the relevant categories-as-monads.

**True name:** a monad in $\mathcal{K}$ is *a one-object lax functor into $\mathcal{K}$*, equivalently *a monoid in an endo-hom-monoidal-category*. When you meet "monad in a bicategory" in the wild, do not picture the three commuting diagrams; picture "I have chosen an object $\mathcal{A}$ and a multiplication on its endo-$1$-cell $t$, so $t$ behaves like a ring sitting at $\mathcal{A}$". Then its modules and bimodules are forced, and the question "what is this in concrete terms?" is answered by naming the bicategory $\mathcal{K}$.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — monad in $\mathbf{Cat}$ is an ordinary monad.** Take $\mathcal{K} = \mathbf{Cat}$, the $2$-category of categories, [[Def - Functor|functors]], and [[Def - Natural Transformation|natural transformations]]. An object is a category $\mathcal{A}$, the endo-$1$-cell $t$ is an endofunctor $T : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$, and $\mu, \eta$ are natural transformations $TT \Rightarrow T$, $1 \Rightarrow T$. The axioms are the ordinary monad laws, so this recovers [[Def - Monad and Comonad|the monad]] exactly — for instance the free-monoid monad on $\mathbf{Set}$, the power-set monad, or the [[Def - Monad and Comonad|free–forgetful]] monads. This is the consistency check that licenses the whole generalisation.

**Is an instance — monad in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ is a small category.** In the bicategory $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ an object is a set $C_0$, a $1$-cell $C_0 \to C_0$ is a span $C_0 \leftarrow C_1 \to C_0$ (i.e. a set $C_1$ with two maps $\mathrm{dom}, \mathrm{cod} : C_1 \to C_0$), composition of spans is by [[Def - Pullback and Pushout|pullback]], and the identity $1$-cell is the discrete span $C_0 = C_0 = C_0$. A monad on $C_0$ is then: a set $C_1$ of "arrows" with domain and codomain in $C_0$; a multiplication $\mu : C_1 \times_{C_0} C_1 \to C_1$ (the pullback is *composable pairs*), which is **composition of arrows**; and a unit $\eta : C_0 \to C_1$ picking out an **identity arrow** at each object. The associativity and unit laws are exactly the category axioms. So a monad in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ *is* a small [[Def - Category|category]] — objects $C_0$, arrows $C_1$, composition $\mu$, identities $\eta$.

**Is an instance — monad in $\mathbf{Rel}$ is a preorder.** In the bicategory $\mathbf{Rel}$ of sets and relations, a $1$-cell $X \to X$ is a relation $R \subseteq X \times X$, composition is relational composition, and the identity is the equality relation $\Delta_X$. A monad on $X$ is a relation $R$ with a multiplication $RR \Rightarrow R$ — which, since $2$-cells in $\mathbf{Rel}$ are inclusions, says $R \circ R \subseteq R$, i.e. **transitivity** ($xRy$ and $yRz$ imply $xRz$) — and a unit $\Delta_X \Rightarrow R$, i.e. $\Delta_X \subseteq R$, i.e. **reflexivity** ($xRx$). So a monad in $\mathbf{Rel}$ is a reflexive transitive relation: a **preorder**. (The associativity and unit laws are automatic because hom-sets of $\mathbf{Rel}$ are posets — at most one $2$-cell between two $1$-cells — so monad structure is a *property*, not extra data, which is exactly why a preorder is "a category with at most one arrow between objects".)

**Is an instance — a monoid is a monad in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ on a one-point set.** Specialise the category example to $C_0 = \{\ast\}$: then $C_1$ is a set $M$ with a unital associative multiplication $M \times M \to M$ and a chosen unit, i.e. an ordinary [[Def - Monoid in a Monoidal Category|monoid]]. This is the statement "a monoid is a one-object category", recovered as a special case.

**Is NOT an instance — a comonad is not a monad in $\mathcal{K}$.** A comonad in $\mathbf{Cat}$ has a *comultiplication* $\delta : T \Rightarrow TT$ and *counit* $\varepsilon : T \Rightarrow 1$ — the arrows point the *other way*. A comonad in $\mathcal{K}$ is a monad in $\mathcal{K}^{co}$ (the bicategory with $2$-cells reversed), not a monad in $\mathcal{K}$. So "monad in a bicategory" does **not** include comonads; you get them by dualising the bicategory. This is the one-direction-only nature of the definition: $\mu$ goes $tt \Rightarrow t$, never $t \Rightarrow tt$.

**Calibration check.** Verify that in $\mathbf{Span}(\mathbf{Set})$ the pullback $C_1 \times_{C_0} C_1$ really is the set of *composable* pairs of arrows (matching codomain to domain), so that $\mu$ is exactly composition. Verify that in $\mathbf{Rel}$ monad structure is a property (no choices) because the hom-posets are thin, and conclude that a preorder carries *exactly one* monad structure on a given relation when it has one. Finally, check the two unit laws for a left module reduce, in the ring case, to the familiar $1_R \cdot x = x$.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The bicategory Mod(𝒦) *(from this chapter)*
> Bundling monads in 𝒦 as objects, bimodules as $1$-cells (composed by tensor over the middle monad), and module maps as $2$-cells produces a new [[Def - 2-Category and Bicategory|bicategory]] **Mod(𝒦)** — see [[Thm - Monoids and Modules Form a Bicategory]]. Taking 𝒦 = **Span(Set)** turns small categories into objects and **profunctors** into $1$-cells; taking 𝒦 = **(rings as one-object Ab-categories)** recovers the bicategory of rings and bimodules. One construction, run on different 𝒦, generates the standard "categories of correspondences".

> [!tip] Formal monad theory and Eilenberg–Moore objects *(from 2-category theory)*
> Because the monad axioms are bicategorical, the whole Eilenberg–Moore and Kleisli machinery generalises: one can ask for **Eilenberg–Moore objects** and **Kleisli objects** of a monad in a $2$-category, recovering [[Def - Monad and Comonad|algebras and the Kleisli category]] when 𝒦 = **Cat**. This "formal theory of monads" (Street) is the abstract backbone of monad theory and the reason monadicity arguments transfer to enriched and internal settings.

> [!tip] Generalized multicategories as monads in a Kleisli-type bicategory *(from later in Leinster)*
> The very notion of a **generalized multicategory** for a cartesian monad $T$ is a monad in the bicategory of $T$-spans — so the [[Def - fc-Multicategory|fc-multicategory]] of this chapter is itself an instance of "monad in a bicategory", with 𝒦 the bicategory of $\mathrm{fc}$-spans on directed graphs. The definitions on this page are thus not a side topic but the structural template the whole chapter instantiates.
