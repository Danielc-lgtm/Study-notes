## §3 Algebras, Free Operads, and Examples — Exercises

These exercises drill the payoff of the chapter: how operads *act*, how they are *built freely*, and the deep examples that make the theory worth having. The first computes the [[Thm - The Free Operad|free operad]] on a single binary operation — planar binary trees, counted by Catalan numbers — and shows its algebras are magmas, with associativity recovered as a tree-collapsing quotient; this is the doorway to $A_\infty$-structures via the associahedra. The second realises every operad as a [[Def - Monad and Comonad|monad]] $T_P$ and matches their algebras, computing the free-monoid (lists) and free-commutative-monoid (multisets) monads as the associative and commutative cases. The third is the topological capstone: loop spaces are algebras over the little intervals operad, $E_1 \simeq \mathrm{Assoc}^{\mathrm{ns}}$ encodes homotopy-associativity, and [[Thm - May's Recognition Principle|May's recognition principle]] characterises which $E_1$-algebras are loop spaces. The recurring techniques: instantiating the free-operad tree formula, computing symmetric coinvariants, and recognising homotopy-coherent structure via the homotopy type of configuration spaces.

- [[Ex - An operad induces a monad whose algebras agree]] (⭐⭐⭐) — the operadic monad $T_P(X) = \coprod_n P(n)\times_{S_n} X^n$; lists versus multisets; equivariance and coinvariant well-definedness ([[Def - Operad]], [[Def - Algebra for an Operad]], [[Def - Monad and Comonad]], [[Def - Algebra for a Monad]])
