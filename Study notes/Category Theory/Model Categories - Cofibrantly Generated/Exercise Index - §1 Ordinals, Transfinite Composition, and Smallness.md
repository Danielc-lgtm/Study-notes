## §1 Ordinals, Transfinite Composition, and Smallness — Exercises

These exercises drill the one genuinely set-theoretic ingredient of the chapter: smallness, the termination certificate for the small object argument. The first establishes the topological prototype — compactness is smallness — by the compact-image-meets-finitely-many-cells argument, the form used to certify smallness in $\mathbf{Top}$. The second establishes the algebraic prototype — every set is small — by the cardinality-and-regularity supremum argument, the form used in every presheaf and locally presentable category. The third is a counterexample stress test: it builds explicit failures of smallness at too-short and at singular lengths, isolating regularity and cofinality as the load-bearing hypotheses. Together they teach the trigger "compact or finitely-presented domain ⇒ small ⇒ the small object argument runs," and the diagnostic that smallness is about cofinality, not raw size.

- [[Ex - Compact spaces are small relative to closed inclusions]] (⭐⭐) — converting compactness into bounded factorization via the compact-image-meets-finitely-many-cells argument ([[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness]], [[Def - Relative Cell Complex]], [[Def - Topological Space]])
- [[Ex - Every set is small in the category of sets]] (⭐) — the cardinality-plus-regularity supremum argument showing every set is $|A|^+$-small, with the role of regularity and the strict threshold ([[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness]], [[Def - Limit and Colimit]])
- [[Ex - Cofinality and the failure of smallness]] (⭐⭐⭐) — explicit escapes-to-infinity at length $\omega$ and at the singular $\aleph_\omega$, isolating regularity and cofinality as essential ([[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness]], [[Def - Limit and Colimit]])
