# Problem Statement

Show that the Quillen model structure on $\mathbf{Top}$ is [[Def - Cofibrantly Generated Model Category|cofibrantly generated]], with generating sets
$$I = \{\, S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n : n\geq 0 \,\}, \qquad J = \{\, D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1] : n\geq 0 \,\},$$
where $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ is the boundary inclusion (with $S^{-1} = \varnothing$, so the $n=0$ map is $\varnothing\hookrightarrow D^0 = *$) and $D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$ is the inclusion at one end of the cylinder. Specifically:

(a) Identify $I\text{-inj}$ (the maps with the RLP against $I$) and $J\text{-inj}$ (RLP against $J$), naming them as standard topological classes.

(b) Identify $I\text{-cof}$ and $J\text{-cof}$ as the cofibrations and trivial cofibrations.

(c) Verify the smallness hypothesis: the domains of $I$ and $J$ are small relative to the respective cell complexes.

You may quote the previous chapter's identification of the Quillen weak equivalences (weak homotopy equivalences), Serre fibrations, and cofibrations; the task is to see that these arise from $I, J$.

**Recall:**

![[Def - Cofibrantly Generated Model Category#The Definition]]

A **Serre fibration** is a map with the right lifting property against the disk-cylinder inclusions $D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$ (the homotopy lifting property against disks). A [[Def - Higher Homotopy Group|weak homotopy equivalence]] is a map inducing isomorphisms on $\pi_0$ and all homotopy groups.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is an exhibit-generators problem — the fourth recurring target — showing a known model category is cofibrantly generated by naming its two generating sets and identifying the four classes. It is the bridge from the abstract machinery to the prototypical example.

**Assumption pattern:** The decisive inputs are the *definitions* of Serre fibration and trivial fibration as lifting classes — these are already lifting properties against $I$ and $J$, so the identification is almost tautological — together with the compactness of spheres and disks, which gives smallness. The recognition that the standard topological classes are *defined* by lifting is the unlock.

**Theorem routing:** The route is: $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations *by definition* of Serre fibration; $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations (Serre fibrations that are weak equivalences) by the homotopy-lifting-plus-surjectivity characterization; then $I\text{-cof}$ and $J\text{-cof}$ are forced as the LLP-classes, matching the cofibrations and trivial cofibrations; smallness comes from compactness via [[Ex - Compact spaces are small relative to closed inclusions]].

**Key decision point:** The non-obvious point is part (a) for $I$: while $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations is immediate, $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations requires recognizing that RLP against *all* sphere-boundary inclusions $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ encodes both the lifting (fibration) and the surjectivity-on-homotopy (acyclicity) conditions — the $n=0$ generator $\varnothing\to D^0$ forces surjectivity on $\pi_0$, higher $n$ force the homotopy-group isomorphisms.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1 (part a, $J$)
> A Serre fibration is *defined* as a map with the homotopy lifting property against disks, i.e. the RLP against $\{D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]\}= J$. So $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations, by definition.

> [!note]- Hint 2 (part a, $I$)
> The RLP against $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ says: any map $S^{n-1}\to E$ that becomes nullhomotopic-compatibly downstairs lifts to $D^n$. For all $n$ together this is exactly "$p$ is a Serre fibration and a weak homotopy equivalence" — a trivial fibration. The $n=0$ case $\varnothing\to D^0 = *$ forces $p$ surjective (on $\pi_0$).

> [!note]- Hint 3 (part b)
> Once $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations and $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations, the LLP-classes are forced: $I\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{trivial fibrations}) =$ cofibrations, and $J\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{Serre fibrations}) =$ trivial cofibrations, by the lifting characterization of the classes.

> [!note]- Hint 4 (part c)
> $S^{n-1}$ and $D^n$ are compact. By [[Ex - Compact spaces are small relative to closed inclusions]], a compact space is small relative to relative cell complexes (compact image meets finitely many cells). So the domains of $I$ and of $J$ are small.

# Solution

The identification is almost definitional once you see the standard classes as lifting classes: $J\text{-inj}$ is Serre fibrations by definition (Step 1), $I\text{-inj}$ is trivial fibrations (Step 2), the LLP-classes give the cofibrations and trivial cofibrations (Step 3), and compactness gives smallness (Step 4). Assembling, $\mathbf{Top}$ meets the definition of cofibrant generation (Step 5).

**Step 1 (a): $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> The set $J = \{D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]\}$ is precisely the family of inclusions encoding the homotopy lifting property: a map $p : E\to B$ has the RLP against $D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$ exactly when, given a homotopy $D^n\times[0,1]\to B$ and a lift $D^n\to E$ of its starting end, the whole homotopy lifts. A Serre fibration is by definition a map with this property for all $n$. Hence $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations. (Equivalently one may use the horn-style inclusions $D^n\times\{0\}\cup S^{n-1}\times[0,1]\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$, which generate the same class.)

**Step 2 (a): $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> A map $p : E\to B$ has the RLP against $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ for all $n\geq 0$ iff every commuting square
> $$\begin{array}{ccc} S^{n-1} & \to & E \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p} \\ D^n & \to & B \end{array}$$
> has a diagonal lift. For $n = 0$ this is $\varnothing\to D^0 = *$, and the lift exists iff $p$ is surjective. For $n\geq 1$, the lifting condition is exactly that the relative homotopy groups $\pi_n(p)$ (the obstruction to extending a sphere-map over the disk) vanish, which by the long exact sequence of the fibration is equivalent to $p$ inducing isomorphisms on all $\pi_n$. So $I\text{-inj}$ consists of the maps that are simultaneously Serre fibrations (lifting) and weak homotopy equivalences (the isomorphisms on homotopy) — the **trivial fibrations**. This is the substantive identification: the single set $I$ encodes both lifting and acyclicity.

**Step 3 (b): $I\text{-cof} =$ cofibrations, $J\text{-cof} =$ trivial cofibrations.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> By the lifting characterization of the model structure (previous chapter, [[Thm - Closure Properties of the Model Structure]]), the cofibrations are exactly $\mathrm{LLP}(\text{trivial fibrations})$ and the trivial cofibrations are $\mathrm{LLP}(\text{Serre fibrations})$. By Steps 1–2, trivial fibrations $= I\text{-inj}$ and Serre fibrations $= J\text{-inj}$. Therefore
> $$I\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(I\text{-inj}) = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{trivial fibrations}) = \text{cofibrations},$$
> $$J\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(J\text{-inj}) = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{Serre fibrations}) = \text{trivial cofibrations}.$$
> Concretely, $I\text{-cof}$ is the retracts of relative cell complexes (CW-like inclusions), matching the known description of $\mathbf{Top}$-cofibrations.

**Step 4 (c): Smallness of the domains.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> The domains of $I$ are the spheres $S^{n-1}$ (and $\varnothing$ for $n=0$); the domains of $J$ are the disks $D^n$. All are compact. By [[Ex - Compact spaces are small relative to closed inclusions]], a compact space is small relative to the relative cell complexes (its image into a transfinite colimit of closed cell attachments is compact, meets finitely many cells, hence factors through a bounded stage). So the domains of $I$ are small relative to $I\text{-cell}$ and the domains of $J$ are small relative to $J\text{-cell}$. (The empty space $\varnothing$ is trivially small.)

**Step 5: $\mathbf{Top}$ is cofibrantly generated.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> We have exhibited sets $I, J$ with small domains (Step 4) such that $\mathrm{cof} = I\text{-cof}$, $\mathrm{triv\text{-}fib} = I\text{-inj}$ (Steps 2–3), $\mathrm{triv\text{-}cof} = J\text{-cof}$, $\mathrm{fib} = J\text{-inj}$ (Steps 1, 3). This is exactly the definition of a [[Def - Cofibrantly Generated Model Category|cofibrantly generated model category]]. Hence the Quillen model structure on $\mathbf{Top}$ is cofibrantly generated.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> $J\text{-inj} =$ Serre fibrations, because $J = \{D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]\}$ is the family encoding the homotopy lifting property. $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations, because RLP against $\{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ for all $n$ is, via the long exact sequence, "Serre fibration $+$ weak homotopy equivalence" (the $n=0$ generator $\varnothing\to *$ forcing surjectivity, higher $n$ the homotopy isomorphisms). By the lifting characterization, $I\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{trivial fibrations}) =$ cofibrations and $J\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(\text{Serre fibrations}) =$ trivial cofibrations. The domains $S^{n-1}, D^n$ are compact, hence small relative to relative cell complexes (compact image meets finitely many cells). So $I, J$ have small domains and generate the four classes: $\mathbf{Top}$ is cofibrantly generated. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**The standard topological classes are lifting classes in disguise, which is why $\mathbf{Top}$ is cofibrantly generated almost by definition.** Serre fibrations are *defined* by the homotopy lifting property, that is, the RLP against $J$; trivial fibrations are the RLP against $I$. Once you see that the classes you already know are RLP-classes against finite-data generating sets, cofibrant generation is not a deep new fact about $\mathbf{Top}$ but a re-reading of its definition. The transferable recognition is: whenever a fibration or acyclic-fibration class in any category is *defined* by lifting against standard inclusions, that category is a candidate for cofibrant generation with those inclusions as generators.

**One set $I$ encodes both lifting and acyclicity — the two conditions are bundled into the sphere-boundary inclusions.** The subtle identification is $I\text{-inj} =$ trivial fibrations: RLP against $\{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ simultaneously forces the homotopy lifting property and the vanishing of the relative homotopy groups, i.e. fibration plus weak equivalence. The $n=0$ generator $\varnothing\to *$ even handles surjectivity on $\pi_0$. This bundling is why a *single* generating set suffices for the trivial-fibration class, and it is the model for how generators in other categories (boundary inclusions in $\mathbf{sSet}$, sphere/disk complexes in $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$) encode acyclicity geometrically. Recognizing which generator enforces which piece of "trivial fibration" is the key to identifying generating sets in new examples.

**Compactness of the generator domains is the entire smallness check, and it is what makes the small object argument run in $\mathbf{Top}$.** The domains $S^{n-1}, D^n$ are compact, so they are small, so the small object argument terminates and produces the factorizations of MC5. This single observation — compact domains give smallness — is the reason the whole machine works for spaces, and it generalizes the classical compactness used in cellular approximation. When you move to a new geometric category, the smallness check is almost always "are the generator domains compact (or finitely presented, or finite)?"; if yes, the small object argument runs and the recognition theorem applies.
