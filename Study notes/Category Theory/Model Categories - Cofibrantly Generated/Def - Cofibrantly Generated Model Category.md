# Notation

Throughout, $\mathcal{M}$ is a [[Def - Model Category|model category]], in particular bicomplete (it has all small limits and colimits). The three distinguished classes are the weak equivalences $\mathcal{W}$, the cofibrations, and the fibrations; a **trivial** cofibration or fibration is one that is also a weak equivalence. For a set of maps $I$ we use the closure operations of [[Def - Relative Cell Complex]]: $I\text{-inj} = \mathrm{RLP}(I)$, $I\text{-cof} = \mathrm{LLP}(I\text{-inj})$, and $I\text{-cell}$ the relative $I$-cell complexes. "The domains of $I$ are small relative to $I\text{-cell}$" uses the [[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness|smallness]] of this chapter. The full symbol registry is on [[Model Categories — Cofibrantly Generated Model Categories and the Small Object Argument]].

The two generating sets are conventionally named $I$ (for the cofibrations, after the German *Inklusionen* or simply the first letter available) and $J$ (for the trivial cofibrations). We follow this universal convention.

# Axiom Motivation

The motivating problem is economy. A [[Def - Model Category|model category]] carries three proper classes of maps satisfying five axioms, and the two hard axioms — lifting (MC4) and factorization (MC5) — quantify over these whole classes. Checking them by hand for a concrete category is forbidding: to verify factorization you must produce, for *every* map, a factorization of each of two kinds, and to verify lifting you must solve *every* lifting problem between the relevant classes. The definition of a cofibrantly generated model category is the recognition that, in every case that arises in practice, all of this proper-class data is generated by two *sets*, and that once the generators are named the entire structure is forced and the hard axioms become a finite verification.

Why two sets, and why these two? Recall that MC5 asks for two factorizations: every map factors as (cofibration)$\circ$(trivial fibration), and as (trivial cofibration)$\circ$(fibration). Each factorization is produced by a [[Thm - The Small Object Argument|small object argument]] run against one set of generators. The set $I$ generates the first factorization: its relative cell complexes are (the building blocks of) the cofibrations, and the maps lifting against it, $I\text{-inj}$, are the trivial fibrations. The set $J$ generates the second: its relative cell complexes build the trivial cofibrations, and $J\text{-inj}$ are the fibrations. So two factorizations require two generating sets, one per factorization, and the four classes split cleanly:
$$\mathrm{cof} = I\text{-cof}, \quad \mathrm{triv\text{-}fib} = I\text{-inj}, \quad \mathrm{triv\text{-}cof} = J\text{-cof}, \quad \mathrm{fib} = J\text{-inj}.$$

Why must we demand that the **domains of $I$ and $J$ be small**? Because that is exactly the hypothesis the small object argument needs to terminate (see [[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness]]). Without it, the transfinite construction of the factorizations does not produce a map with the correct lifting property — a lifting problem could escape to infinity — and MC5 would not actually be discharged. So smallness is not a technical decoration; it is the precise condition that makes the definition *constructive* rather than merely descriptive. Drop the smallness of the domains of $I$ and you lose the (cofibration, trivial fibration) factorization; drop the smallness of the domains of $J$ and you lose the (trivial cofibration, fibration) factorization.

What goes wrong if we keep only *one* generating set, say $I$, and try to derive $J$? In general you cannot: the trivial cofibrations are not determined by the cofibrations alone — they also depend on the weak equivalences, which are extra data. The set $J$ must be chosen so that $J$-cell maps are weak equivalences (and trivial cofibrations) and so that lifting against $J$ recovers the fibrations. There is genuine content in $J$ that $I$ does not see; conflating the two would force the trivial cofibrations to equal the cofibrations, collapsing the homotopy theory. The two sets are independent inputs, tied together only through the weak equivalences and the matching conditions of the [[Thm - The Recognition Theorem for Cofibrantly Generated Model Categories|recognition theorem]].

Why phrase the cofibrations as $I\text{-cof}$ (the lifting closure) rather than $I\text{-cell}$ (the cellular closure)? Because the model-category axioms require cofibrations to be closed under retract, and $I\text{-cof}$ — being an $\mathrm{LLP}$-class — is automatically retract-closed, while $I\text{-cell}$ is not. The small object argument reconciles the two: it proves $I\text{-cof}$ is the retract-closure of $I\text{-cell}$, so the lifting definition and the cellular intuition agree. We use the lifting form in the definition because it is what makes the four classes interlock correctly with the axioms.

A reader who has absorbed the previous two pages — the small object argument needs small-domain generators to factor maps, and a set generates a saturated class via lifting and cell attachment — could invent this definition: it is the demand that *both* MC5 factorizations be producible this way, which forces exactly two small-domain generating sets and the four-fold identification above.

# The Definition

A [[Def - Model Category|model category]] $\mathcal{M}$ is **cofibrantly generated** if there exist sets of morphisms $I$ and $J$ such that:

1. The domains of the maps in $I$ are [[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness|small]] relative to $I\text{-cell}$.
2. The domains of the maps in $J$ are small relative to $J\text{-cell}$.
3. The class of cofibrations is $I\text{-cof}$, and equivalently the class of trivial fibrations is $I\text{-inj}$.
4. The class of trivial cofibrations is $J\text{-cof}$, and equivalently the class of fibrations is $J\text{-inj}$.

The set $I$ is called a set of **generating cofibrations** and $J$ a set of **generating trivial cofibrations**. Equivalently, conditions 3 and 4 say
$$\mathrm{cof} = I\text{-cof}, \qquad \mathrm{triv\text{-}fib} = I\text{-inj}, \qquad \mathrm{triv\text{-}cof} = J\text{-cof}, \qquad \mathrm{fib} = J\text{-inj}.$$

(The "equivalently" in 3 and 4 is justified by the lifting characterization of the four classes, [[Thm - Closure Properties of the Model Structure]]: cofibrations are exactly the maps with the LLP against trivial fibrations, so $\mathrm{cof} = I\text{-cof}$ if and only if $\mathrm{triv\text{-}fib} = I\text{-inj}$, and dually for $J$.)

# Categorical / Structural Definition

Structurally, a cofibrantly generated model structure is two **weak factorization systems** each presented by a generating set. A weak factorization system on $\mathcal{C}$ is a pair $(\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{R})$ of classes such that every map factors as an $\mathcal{R}$-map after an $\mathcal{L}$-map, with $\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{LLP}(\mathcal{R})$ and $\mathcal{R} = \mathrm{RLP}(\mathcal{L})$. A model structure is exactly two interlocking weak factorization systems sharing the weak equivalences:
$$(\mathrm{cof},\ \mathrm{triv\text{-}fib}) \qquad\text{and}\qquad (\mathrm{triv\text{-}cof},\ \mathrm{fib}),$$
subject to $\mathrm{cof}\cap\mathcal{W} = \mathrm{triv\text{-}cof}$ and $\mathrm{fib}\cap\mathcal{W} = \mathrm{triv\text{-}fib}$, with $\mathcal{W}$ satisfying 2-out-of-3 and retract-closure.

A weak factorization system is **cofibrantly generated** by a set $S$ if $\mathcal{L} = S\text{-cof}$ and $\mathcal{R} = S\text{-inj}$, which by the [[Thm - The Small Object Argument|small object argument]] happens whenever $S$ has small domains. Thus a cofibrantly generated model category is precisely one whose *two* weak factorization systems are *each* cofibrantly generated — by $I$ and by $J$ respectively. This is the cleanest structural statement: the model structure is a pair of generated weak factorization systems, compatible through the weak equivalences. The recognition theorem is then the precise condition on $(\mathcal{W}, I, J)$ under which the two generated weak factorization systems assemble into a model structure.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

This is the homotopy-theoretic version of **presenting a structure by generators and relations**. A group is presented by generators (the elements of a generating set) and relations; a topology is presented by a subbasis; a $\sigma$-algebra by a generating collection of sets. In each case a small amount of data determines a large structure through a closure operation. A cofibrantly generated model category presents an entire homotopy theory by the two sets $I, J$, with the "closure operation" being the small object argument's cell attachment together with lifting. The compression ratio is extreme: the model structure on $\mathbf{Top}$ — three proper classes of continuous maps — is encoded by the two countable sets $\{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ and $\{D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]\}$.

In higher category theory this compression becomes definitional: a **combinatorial model category** is a cofibrantly generated model category whose underlying category is locally presentable, and these are exactly the model categories that present **presentable ∞-categories**. The generating sets $I, J$ become the 1-categorical data from which the ∞-categorical localization is computed, and Quillen equivalences of combinatorial model categories are the comparisons that show two presentations describe the same ∞-category.

**True name:** the operational form of "cofibrantly generated" is *"the whole homotopy theory is determined by lifting against, and building from, two sets."* When you confront such a category, never reason about the full classes — reason about $I$ and $J$. To test whether a map is a fibration, lift against $J$; to build a cofibrant replacement, run the small object argument on $I$; to recognize the model structure, check Kan's conditions on $(\mathcal{W}, I, J)$.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — $\mathbf{Top}$ with the Quillen model structure.** Take $I = \{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n : n\geq 0\}$ and $J = \{D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1] : n\geq 0\}$. Then $I\text{-inj}$ is the class of trivial fibrations (maps with the RLP against sphere-boundary inclusions, which are the Serre fibrations that are also weak equivalences), $I\text{-cof}$ is the cofibrations (retracts of relative cell complexes), $J\text{-inj}$ is the Serre fibrations (RLP against the disk-cylinder inclusions = homotopy lifting property), and $J\text{-cof}$ is the trivial cofibrations. The domains $S^{n-1}$ and $D^n$ are compact, hence [[Def - Transfinite Composition and Smallness|small]]. So $\mathbf{Top}$ is cofibrantly generated.

**Is an instance — $\mathbf{sSet}$ with the Kan–Quillen model structure.** Take $I = \{\partial\Delta^n\hookrightarrow\Delta^n\}$ and $J = \{\Lambda^n_k\hookrightarrow\Delta^n : 0\leq k\leq n,\ n\geq 1\}$ (the horn inclusions). Then $I\text{-cof}$ is all monomorphisms (every object is cofibrant), $J\text{-inj}$ is the [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve|Kan fibrations]] (RLP against horns = the horn-filling/extension condition), and $J\text{-cof}$ is the anodyne maps (trivial cofibrations). The domains $\partial\Delta^n, \Lambda^n_k$ are finite simplicial sets, hence small (every object of a presheaf category is small). So $\mathbf{sSet}$ is cofibrantly generated.

**Is an instance — $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ with the projective model structure.** Take $I$ the sphere-and-disk inclusions of chain complexes ($S^{n-1}(R)\hookrightarrow D^n(R)$, where $S^{n-1}(R)$ is $R$ concentrated in degree $n-1$ and $D^n(R)$ is $R\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}} R$ in degrees $n, n-1$) and $J = \{0\hookrightarrow D^n(R)\}$. Then $I\text{-cof}$ is the cofibrations (degreewise-split monos with projective cokernel), $J\text{-inj}$ the degreewise epimorphisms (fibrations), and cofibrant replacement is projective resolution. The domains are finitely generated free complexes, hence small.

**Is NOT an instance — a model structure with proper-class cofibrations not generated by a set.** Not every model category is cofibrantly generated. There exist model structures (for instance certain ones on large categories, or the standard model structure on the category of *all* topological spaces if one uses a non-set-generated class of cofibrations, or some constructions failing smallness) where no set of generators exists; these resist the small object argument and must be built by other means. The point of the definition is that it isolates the (overwhelming) majority that *are* set-generated, where the machinery applies.

**Is NOT an instance — the trivial cofibrations are not $I$-generated.** Even in a cofibrantly generated model category, the trivial cofibrations are generated by $J$, *not* by $I$: one cannot recover $J$ from $I$ because the trivial cofibrations depend on the weak equivalences. Treating $I$ alone as generating both factorizations is the standard error — it would force $\mathrm{triv\text{-}cof} = \mathrm{cof}$ and collapse the structure. The two sets are independent data.

**Corollary — the four classes are mutually determined by $(I, J)$ and the smallness hypotheses.** Once $I, J$ are fixed with small domains, the small object argument produces both factorizations, the lifting characterization recovers all four classes, and the model structure (if it exists) is unique. So a cofibrantly generated model structure is rigidly determined by its generating sets together with the weak equivalences.

**Calibration check.** For $\mathbf{Top}$, verify that $J\text{-inj}$ = Serre fibrations is literally the homotopy lifting property (RLP against $D^n\hookrightarrow D^n\times[0,1]$). For $\mathbf{sSet}$, verify that $J\text{-inj}$ = Kan complexes when the target is the point (RLP against horns into the point = horn-filling). If you can also explain why $I$ generates the (cofibration, trivial fibration) factorization while $J$ generates the (trivial cofibration, fibration) one — i.e. why the *two* factorizations need *two* sets — you have understood the definition.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The Recognition Theorem *(from this chapter)*
> Given candidate data $(\mathcal{W}, I, J)$, Kan's [[Thm - The Recognition Theorem for Cofibrantly Generated Model Categories|recognition theorem]] gives a finite checklist that produces a cofibrantly generated model structure — turning the open-ended task of verifying five axioms into a bounded verification against the two generating sets.

> [!tip] Combinatorial Model Categories and Presentable ∞-Categories *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> A cofibrantly generated model category on a locally presentable category is a **combinatorial model category**, and these present exactly the **presentable ∞-categories** of Lurie's higher algebra. Quillen equivalences between them correspond to equivalences of the presented ∞-categories.

> [!tip] Transfer of Model Structures and Algebras over Operads *(from Homotopical Algebra)*
> Cofibrant generation is what lets a model structure be **transferred** along an adjunction $F\dashv U$: take $FI, FJ$ as generators on the target and verify the transfer (Crans) conditions. This is how model structures on monoids, on algebras over an **operad**, and on equivariant objects are built, grounding the user's interest in compositional and operadic structures in concrete homotopy theory.

> [!tip] Left Bousfield Localization *(from Homotopical Algebra)*
> Keeping the cofibrations $I\text{-cof}$ fixed and enlarging $J$ to a new set of generating trivial cofibrations produces a **left Bousfield localization** — the construction underlying the model structures for spectra, motivic homotopy theory, and the homotopy theory of local objects.
