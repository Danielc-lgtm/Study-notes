## §2.1 Initial and Terminal Objects and Universal Properties — Exercises

These exercises drill the first and simplest universal properties: an object pinned down by the cardinality of its hom-sets. The first builds fluency in spotting initial and terminal objects across the standard categories and seeing where they coincide (the zero object) and where the symmetry breaks (rings). The second rehearses the universal-arrow paradigm on its canonical instance, the free group, and reframes a universal property as initiality in a comma category. The third extracts the zero morphism from a zero object and establishes its absorbing behaviour, the gateway to additive and abelian categories. Together they install the reflex: translate "universal" into "initial or terminal", verify by a singleton-hom-set count, and read off uniqueness from [[Thm - Uniqueness of Universal Objects]].
