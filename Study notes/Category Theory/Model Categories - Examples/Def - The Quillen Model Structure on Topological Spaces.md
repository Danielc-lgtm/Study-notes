# Notation

Throughout, $\mathbf{Top}$ is the category of [[Def - Topological Space|topological spaces]] and continuous maps. We write $D^n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : |x| \leq 1\}$ for the closed $n$-disk, $S^{n-1} = \partial D^n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : |x| = 1\}$ for its boundary sphere (with $S^{-1} = \emptyset$), and $I = [0,1]$ for the unit interval; the **cylinder** on a space $X$ is $X \times I$. A space's [[Def - Higher Homotopy Group|homotopy groups]] are $\pi_n(X, x)$, and $\pi_0(X)$ is the set of path components. A continuous map $f$ is a **weak homotopy equivalence** if it induces a bijection on $\pi_0$ and isomorphisms on $\pi_n$ for every basepoint and every $n \geq 1$; we write $\xrightarrow{\sim}$. A [[Def - Homotopy|homotopy]] between maps $f, g : X \to Y$ is a continuous $H : X \times I \to Y$ with $H_0 = f$, $H_1 = g$. The full symbol registry is on [[Model Categories — Examples in Detail]].

This page defines one structure with three interlocking classes (weak equivalences, fibrations, cofibrations); each is given precisely, and the content is that they form a [[Def - Model Category|model category]].

# Axiom Motivation

The aim is to make $\mathbf{Top}$ into a [[Def - Model Category|model category]] whose homotopy category is the thing algebraic topology actually studies: the category of CW complexes and homotopy classes of maps. So the design is constrained from both ends — the weak equivalences are forced by what we want to invert, and the cofibrations and fibrations must be chosen so the cofibrant objects are the CW-like spaces and the axioms hold.

Why take the weak equivalences to be the **weak homotopy equivalences** rather than genuine [[Def - Homotopy Equivalence and Contractible Space|homotopy equivalences]]? Because the invariants of homotopy theory — homology, cohomology, [[Def - Higher Homotopy Group|homotopy groups]] — see only the weak homotopy type. Two spaces with isomorphic homotopy groups (compatibly) carry identical algebraic-topological information, even if no continuous map realises a homotopy inverse. If we took weak equivalences to be honest homotopy equivalences instead (this gives the *Strøm* model structure, a legitimate but different choice), we would invert fewer maps and get a different, larger homotopy category that distinguishes spaces the invariants cannot. Quillen's choice inverts exactly the maps the algebra cannot see past, which is why $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})$ comes out as the homotopy category of CW complexes — the cleanest possible target.

Why take fibrations to be the **Serre fibrations** — maps with the homotopy lifting property against disks — rather than against all spaces? The lifting axiom MC4 demands that fibrations be detected by lifting against the generating trivial cofibrations, which are the bottom-inclusions $D^n \hookrightarrow D^n \times I$. A map has the right lifting property against all of these precisely when it has the homotopy lifting property against disks, which is the *definition* of a Serre fibration. If we demanded lifting against *all* spaces (this is a Hurewicz fibration, and gives the Strøm structure), we would have a different, stricter class of fibrations not generated by a small set, and the small object argument would not apply. Testing against disks only is what makes the structure **cofibrantly generated**. The per-axiom analysis: weaken "fibration" to "all maps" and trivial fibrations stop being weak equivalences, collapsing $\mathrm{Ho}$; strengthen it to "Hurewicz fibration" and you leave the cofibrantly generated world and change the homotopy theory.

Why take cofibrations to be the **retracts of relative cell complexes**? A cofibrant object should be a space built by attaching cells — a CW complex, or a retract of one — because those are the spaces on which homotopy theory is well-behaved and on which Whitehead's theorem holds (a weak equivalence between CW complexes is a homotopy equivalence). The generating cofibrations are the cell attachments $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$, and the small object argument factors any map by transfinitely attaching such cells, producing a relative cell complex. Closing under retracts is forced by MC3 (the model-category axioms require all classes to be retract-closed), and it enlarges "CW complex" to "space with the homotopy type of a CW complex" in the appropriate sense. If we took cofibrations to be *all* inclusions, the lifting axiom would fail: a general subspace inclusion does not lift against trivial Serre fibrations, because there is no cell structure to push the lift across cell by cell. Projectivity of the "cells" — that maps out of a disk extend over the cylinder — is exactly what the cell structure buys, and it is the topological analogue of the projectivity that powers the chain-complex story.

Finally, could a reader invent the generators? The pair $\{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ is the most natural possible: "attach an $n$-cell along its boundary" is the defining move of CW complexes, so anyone who knows what a CW complex is would guess these. The generating trivial cofibrations $\{D^n \hookrightarrow D^n \times I\}$ encode "a space deformation-retracts onto its bottom", the elementary trivial cofibration. With these two sets the small object argument supplies both factorizations, and the entire structure is determined by a finite-looking list.

# The Definition

The **Quillen model structure** (also called the **Serre model structure**, or the **standard** model structure) on $\mathbf{Top}$ is the [[Def - Model Category|model structure]] with the following three classes of continuous maps.

> **Weak equivalences** are the **weak homotopy equivalences**: maps $f$ inducing a bijection $\pi_0(X) \to \pi_0(Y)$ and isomorphisms $\pi_n(X, x) \to \pi_n(Y, f(x))$ for all $x$ and all $n \geq 1$.
>
> **Fibrations** are the **Serre fibrations**: maps $p : E \to B$ having the homotopy lifting property against every disk $D^n$ — given $H : D^n \times I \to B$ and a lift $\tilde{h}_0 : D^n \to E$ of $H|_{D^n \times \{0\}}$, there is $\tilde{H} : D^n \times I \to E$ lifting $H$ and extending $\tilde{h}_0$.
>
> **Cofibrations** are the **retracts of relative cell complexes**: maps obtained, up to retract, by transfinitely attaching cells $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$.

A **trivial fibration** is a Serre fibration that is also a weak homotopy equivalence; these are exactly the maps with the right lifting property against all $\{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$. A **trivial cofibration** is a cofibration that is also a weak homotopy equivalence.

This model structure is **cofibrantly generated**: the generating cofibrations are $I = \{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n : n \geq 0\}$ and the generating trivial cofibrations are $J = \{D^n \hookrightarrow D^n \times I : n \geq 0\}$ (the bottom-inclusions). Serre fibrations are exactly the maps with the right lifting property against $J$; trivial fibrations are exactly the maps with the right lifting property against $I$.

Two consequences fix the (co)fibrant objects. **Every space is fibrant**, because the map $X \to *$ to the one-point space lifts every homotopy trivially. **The cofibrant objects are the retracts of CW complexes**, since $\emptyset \to X$ is a cofibration precisely when $X$ is built from $\emptyset$ by attaching cells (up to retract). Cofibrant replacement of a space $X$ is a **CW approximation**: a CW complex $\Gamma X$ with a weak homotopy equivalence $\Gamma X \xrightarrow{\sim} X$.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

As with chain complexes, the structure is determined functorially by its generators through the lifting calculus. Writing $I\text{-inj}$ for maps with the right lifting property against $I$ and $I\text{-cof} = {}^{\boxslash}(I\text{-inj})$ for the maps with the left lifting property against those, the structure is

$$\text{cofibrations} = I\text{-cof}, \qquad \text{trivial fibrations} = I\text{-inj}, \qquad \text{fibrations} = J\text{-inj}, \qquad \text{trivial cofibrations} = J\text{-cof},$$

with $I = \{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$, $J = \{D^n \hookrightarrow D^n \times I\}$, and the weak equivalences the weak homotopy equivalences.

The structural engine is again a representability statement. The disk $D^n$ corepresents the underlying-set-of-$n$-cells functor: a map $D^n \to X$ is a point of $X$ "with a chosen $n$-disk's worth of structure". A lifting problem against $D^n \hookrightarrow D^n \times I$ is, by the exponential adjunction $- \times I \dashv (-)^I$, a problem of extending a map $D^n \to E^I$ along $D^n \to B^I$ — that is, a homotopy lifting problem. This adjunction is what converts the abstract lifting axiom into the concrete homotopy lifting property, and it is the reason Serre fibrations are the right fibrations. The relationship to the model structure on [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial sets]] is also categorical: geometric realization $|{-}|$ and the singular nerve $\mathrm{Sing}$ form a [[Def - Quillen Adjunction and Quillen Equivalence|Quillen equivalence]] $\mathbf{sSet} \simeq_Q \mathbf{Top}$, so this model structure and the Kan–Quillen one present the *same* homotopy theory.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

This is the model structure that justifies the entire framework: classical algebraic topology *is* this one example. The abstract cylinder object is the honest cylinder $X \times I$; the abstract [[Def - Cylinder Object, Path Object, and Homotopy|left homotopy]] is the classical [[Def - Homotopy|homotopy]] of continuous maps; cofibrant replacement is CW approximation; and the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|homotopy category]] is the homotopy category of CW complexes. Every theorem of model-category theory specialises to a known theorem of homotopy theory here.

The structure is the topological mirror of the [[Def - Projective Model Structure on Chain Complexes|projective model structure on chain complexes]]: "space" $\leftrightarrow$ "complex", "weak homotopy equivalence" $\leftrightarrow$ "quasi-isomorphism", "Serre fibration" $\leftrightarrow$ "degreewise surjection", "CW complex" $\leftrightarrow$ "complex of projectives". The generators carry the identical names $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$ because the role is identical — "attach a cell". Singular homology, which sends a space to a chain complex, is (the derived functor of) a left Quillen functor between these two model categories, which is the precise reason homology is a homotopy invariant.

**True name:** the operational characterisation is *"cofibrant means CW, fibrant means nothing, weak equivalence means iso on all homotopy groups"*. In practice you invoke "CW-approximate, then compute homotopy classes", which is this true name applied; the formal definition of cofibration via retracts of cell complexes is what you check existence with, not what you reach for.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — a covering map is a Serre fibration.** A covering map $p : \tilde{X} \to X$ has the unique homotopy lifting property against all spaces, in particular against disks, so it is a Serre fibration (indeed a Hurewicz fibration). For example $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, is a Serre fibration with discrete fibre $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Is an instance — a fibre bundle is a Serre fibration.** Any fibre bundle, and more generally any locally trivial map over a paracompact base, satisfies the homotopy lifting property against disks. The Hopf fibration $S^3 \to S^2$ with fibre $S^1$ is the canonical example; the long exact sequence of homotopy groups it produces is the long exact sequence of the fibration, available because it is a Serre fibration.

**Is an instance — the path-space projection.** For a pointed space $X$, the endpoint projection $PX \to X$ from the path space $PX = \{\gamma : I \to X, \gamma(0) = x_0\}$ is a Serre fibration with fibre the loop space $\Omega X$. This is the universal example: it is how one *builds* fibrant-like resolutions and the loop–suspension adjunction.

**Is NOT an instance — a generic surjection is not a Serre fibration.** The map $[0,1] \to [0,1]$ that is the identity but with the topology on the source made finer, or simpler, the quotient $[0,2] \to [0,1]$ folding the interval, can fail homotopy lifting: not every continuous surjection lifts homotopies. Unlike chain complexes, where fibrations are merely the degreewise surjections, in $\mathbf{Top}$ fibration is a genuine homotopy-lifting condition strictly stronger than surjectivity.

**Is NOT an instance — a weak homotopy equivalence need not be a homotopy equivalence.** Let $W$ be the Warsaw circle (the closure of the graph of $\sin(1/x)$ on $(0,1]$ joined to an arc), which has trivial homotopy groups but is not contractible. The inclusion of a point $* \to W$ is a weak homotopy equivalence (both have trivial $\pi_n$) but not a [[Def - Homotopy Equivalence and Contractible Space|homotopy equivalence]] (no continuous map $W \to *$ has a homotopy inverse, as $W$ is not contractible). This is exactly why the homotopy category is built from cofibrant (CW) objects: Whitehead's theorem repairs the gap only there.

**Calibration check.** Verify that (i) every space is fibrant by checking $X \to *$ lifts homotopies; (ii) the inclusion $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$ is a cofibration but not a weak equivalence (compute $\pi_{n-1}$ of each); and (iii) a Serre fibration over a point is just a space, recovering that fibrancy is automatic. If you can do these three, you have understood the structure.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The Homotopy Category of Spaces and CW Approximation *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> The [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]] identifies $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})$ with CW complexes and homotopy classes of maps. **CW approximation** — replacing any space by a weakly equivalent CW complex — is cofibrant replacement, and Whitehead's theorem (a weak equivalence between CW complexes is a homotopy equivalence) is the special case of the fundamental theorem for cofibrant objects.

> [!tip] Homotopy (Co)limits, Suspension, and Loops *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> The homotopy pushout of $* \leftarrow X \to *$ is the **suspension** $\Sigma X$, computed by the double mapping cylinder; the homotopy pullback of $* \to Y \leftarrow *$ is the **loop space** $\Omega Y$. These are the derived (co)limits that respect weak equivalence, and the suspension–loop adjunction on $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top}_*)$ is the geometric origin of the Freudenthal suspension theorem and stable homotopy.

> [!tip] Spaces as ∞-Groupoids and the Homotopy Hypothesis *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> Through the [[Def - Quillen Adjunction and Quillen Equivalence|Quillen equivalence]] with [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial sets]], the fibrant simplicial sets — the [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve|Kan complexes]] — model the same homotopy types, realising the **homotopy hypothesis** that spaces *are* **∞-groupoids**. Serre fibrations correspond to Kan fibrations across this equivalence.
