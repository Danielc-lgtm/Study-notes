# Notation

We work in $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$, the [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy|chain complexes]] of left $R$-[[Def - Module|modules]] with differential of degree $-1$. Homology is $H_n(C) = \ker d_n / \operatorname{im} d_{n+1}$; a **quasi-isomorphism** is a chain map inducing isomorphisms on all $H_n$. The **sphere complex** $S^n$ is $R$ in degree $n$; the **disk complex** $D^n$ is $R \xrightarrow{1} R$ in degrees $n, n-1$, with $H_*(D^n) = 0$. We use $I = \{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ (generating cofibrations) and $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ (generating trivial cofibrations). The three classes of the [[Def - Projective Model Structure on Chain Complexes|projective model structure]] are: weak equivalences = quasi-isomorphisms, fibrations = degreewise epimorphisms, cofibrations = monomorphisms with degreewise-[[Def - Projective Module|projective]] cokernel. The derived category is $D(R) = \mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Ch}(R))$. The full registry is on [[Model Categories — Examples in Detail]].

# Statement

> **Theorem (projective model structure on chain complexes).** Let $R$ be a ring. The category $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ of chain complexes of left $R$-modules, with
> - weak equivalences the quasi-isomorphisms,
> - fibrations the degreewise surjective chain maps,
> - cofibrations the monomorphisms with degreewise-[[Def - Projective Module|projective]] cokernel,
>
> is a [[Def - Model Category|model category]], cofibrantly generated by $I = \{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ and $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$. Every object is fibrant; the cofibrant objects are the bounded-below complexes of projectives.

> **Corollary (the derived category is a homotopy category).** The [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|homotopy category]] $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Ch}(R))$ is equivalent to the derived category $D(R)$. The abstract homotopy relation on cofibrant complexes is [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy|chain homotopy]], and cofibrant replacement of a [[Def - Module|module]] $M$ (in degree zero) is a projective resolution $P_\bullet \xrightarrow{\sim} M$.

The two blockquotes are tied together by the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem of model categories]]: once the model structure exists, the fundamental theorem computes its homotopy category, and identifying that computation with the classical derived category is the corollary.

# Motivation

This theorem is the bridge between homological algebra and homotopy theory, and it is the example that most convinced the founders the axioms were right. Homological algebra had, by 1950, a fully developed calculus of resolutions, derived functors, and the derived category — but built by hand, with each construction (Tor, Ext, hyper-derived functors) verified separately to be independent of the resolution chosen. Quillen's framework explains all of it at once: the independence-of-resolution lemmas are instances of the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]], and "derived functor" is "Quillen-derived functor". The role of this theorem is to certify that $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ really does satisfy the five axioms, so that the abstract machine may be switched on and homological algebra read off as output.

The deeper point is conceptual unification. Before this theorem, the analogy between "resolving a module" and "approximating a space by a CW complex" was a slogan; after it, the two are *literally the same construction* in two model categories with the same generators. The theorem is what makes the slogan a theorem.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The precondition for invoking the model-structure machinery is "I am working in $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ and care about quasi-isomorphisms". The skill is recognising the many disguises in which this arises.

The first disguised source is **a derived functor problem stated in classical language** — "compute $\mathrm{Tor}^R_n(M,N)$" or "compute $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M,N)$". These are not phrased in terms of model categories, but each is secretly a request to derive a functor on $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$, and the model structure is what guarantees the answer is independent of the resolution. The non-obvious bridge is that a single module is a complex (concentrated in degree zero) and a resolution is a cofibrant replacement. *Example problem:* compute $\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}}_*(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ by resolving $\mathbb{Z}/m$ and tensoring — the model structure justifies that the answer ($\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ in degrees $0$ and $1$) does not depend on the resolution.

The second disguised source is **a short exact sequence of complexes**. Whenever a problem hands you $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ in $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$, the cokernel map $B \to C$ is a degreewise surjection — a fibration — and if $C$ is degreewise projective the sequence splits degreewise, making $A \to B$ a cofibration. The non-obvious step is reading the exact sequence as a (co)fibration, which lets you form the connecting map and the long exact sequence as a cofiber sequence in the model structure. *Example problem:* derive the long exact homology sequence of $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ as the cofiber sequence of the model structure.

The third disguised source is **a complex of sheaves or of comodules**, where the same lifting calculus applies with $R$-modules replaced by sheaves of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules or by comodules over a Hopf algebra. The non-obvious bridge is that the generating cofibrations $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$ generalise verbatim, so the entire proof transfers. *Example problem:* construct the derived category of coherent sheaves $D(\mathrm{Coh}\,X)$ as the homotopy category of a model structure on $\mathbf{Ch}(\mathcal{O}_X\text{-Mod})$.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The bare conclusion is "$\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ is a model category". Combined with other facts it does far more.

Combine the conclusion with **a functor that is exact on projectives**, such as $-\otimes_R N$ or $\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$. These functors fail to preserve all quasi-isomorphisms but *do* preserve quasi-isomorphisms between complexes of projectives (Ken Brown's lemma), so they admit total derived functors. The further result $E$ is that $\mathbf{L}(-\otimes_R N)$ computes $\mathbf{Tor}$ and $\mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{Hom}_R(-,N)$ computes $\mathbf{Ext}$. This is nonobvious because the classical Tor and Ext are defined by ad hoc resolutions; the model structure shows they are the *unique* homotopy-invariant extensions of the underlying functors.

Combine the conclusion with **the corollary identifying $\mathrm{Ho}$ with $D(R)$ and a triangulated structure**. The cofiber sequences of the model structure become the **distinguished triangles** of $D(R)$, and the degree shift becomes the triangulated shift. The further result is that $D(R)$ is a triangulated category, with all the long exact sequences and octahedral diagrams that follow. This is nonobvious because the triangulated axioms look unmotivated until one sees them as the shadow of a model structure.

Combine the conclusion with **the existence of the injective model structure**. The same category $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ also carries an injective model structure (cofibrations = monos, fibrations = degreewise epis with injective kernel), and both present the *same* $D(R)$ via the identity Quillen equivalence. The further result is the freedom to compute Ext by *either* a projective resolution of the source *or* an injective resolution of the target — the same derived functor, two presentations.

# Why Is It True

The whole theorem rests on one algebraic fact about projectives, dressed in homotopy-theoretic clothing. Recall that a [[Def - Projective Module|projective]] module $P$ is characterised by lifting: given a surjection $q : X \twoheadrightarrow Y$ and a map $P \to Y$, there is a lift $P \to X$. This is *exactly* the right lifting property in disguise. When you set up the disk and sphere complexes so that a lifting problem against $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$ translates (via the representability $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) = C_n$, $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) = Z_n(C)$) into a module-level surjectivity-and-lifting question, the projectivity of the cokernels is precisely what supplies the diagonal fillers. So the lifting axiom MC4 is the lifting property of projective modules, repeated in every degree.

**The single mechanism: a cofibration has projective cokernels, projective modules lift against surjections, and "fibration = degreewise surjection" means the cokernel's lifting property assembles, degree by degree, into the model-category lift.**

The factorization axiom MC5 is the same fact run constructively. To factor a chain map $f : C \to D$ as a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration, you build up the source by attaching disk complexes $D^n$ — one for each generator of the homology and cycles you need to hit — until the augmented map is surjective with acyclic kernel. Attaching a $D^n$ adds a free (hence projective) summand in degrees $n, n-1$ and adds no homology (the disk is acyclic), which is exactly the move that keeps the first factor a cofibration and pushes the second factor toward being a trivial fibration. This is the algebraic small object argument, and it terminates because the disk complexes are small (maps out of them commute with the relevant colimits). Once you believe projectives lift and disks are acyclic and small, every axiom follows mechanically; the rest is bookkeeping.

The corollary — that $\mathrm{Ho} = D(R)$ — is then immediate from the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]] plus the observation that the abstract cylinder of a complex realises the chain-homotopy relation. The cylinder of $C$ is built from $C \oplus C \oplus C[1]$ in the standard way, and a left homotopy through it is precisely a chain homotopy $s$ with $f - g = ds + sd$. So "homotopic in the model structure" *is* "chain homotopic", and the fundamental theorem's description of $\mathrm{Ho}$ as bifibrant-objects-and-homotopy-classes becomes "complexes of projectives and chain-homotopy classes" — which is the standard description of $D(R)$ on the bounded-below side.

# What Makes This Hard

The genuine difficulty is the factorization axiom MC5: most people can verify the cheap axioms and the lifting characterizations, but constructing the factorizations requires the small object argument, and the subtle point is *smallness* — one must know that maps out of the disk complexes commute with the transfinite colimits used to attach cells, which is automatic here because the disks are finitely generated but is the technical heart. The second common stumbling block is conflating the *cofibrant* objects (degreewise-projective complexes) with *all* complexes; forgetting that a module concentrated in degree zero is generally *not* cofibrant is exactly the error that erases the distinction between $\otimes$ and $\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}$, hence loses Tor. The third trap, for unbounded complexes, is assuming degreewise-projective suffices for cofibrancy — it does not; one needs K-projectivity, and ignoring this gives wrong answers for unbounded resolutions.

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:** Verify the cheap axioms (bicompleteness, 2-out-of-3, retracts) degreewise and formally. For the two hard axioms, translate every lifting problem against the generators into a module-theoretic statement using the representability $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) = C_n$ and $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) = Z_n(C)$, then use the lifting property of projective modules. Build factorizations by the small object argument attaching disk complexes. Finally invoke the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]] and identify the abstract homotopy with chain homotopy.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Cheap axioms.** Show $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ is bicomplete, $\mathcal{W}$ satisfies 2-out-of-3, and all three classes are retract-closed.
   - *Hint:* (Co)limits of complexes are computed degreewise; homology is a functor so 2-out-of-3 is inherited from isomorphisms of modules; retracts of (epi / mono-with-projective-cokernel) are again such.
   - *Why needed:* These are MC1–MC3 and clear the deck for the real work.

2. **Representability of cycles and chains.** Prove $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) \cong C_n$ and $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) \cong Z_n(C)$.
   - *Hint:* A chain map out of $D^n$ is determined by the image of the degree-$n$ generator (anything in $C_n$); out of $S^n$ by a degree-$n$ element that must be a cycle.
   - *Why needed:* This is the translation device that turns lifting problems into module algebra.

3. **Identify $J$-injectives and $I$-injectives.** Show a map has RLP against $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ iff it is degreewise surjective, and RLP against $I = \{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ iff it is a degreewise-surjective quasi-isomorphism.
   - *Hint:* RLP against $0 \hookrightarrow D^n$ is "every element of $D_n$ lifts" = surjectivity; RLP against $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ adds "every cycle that bounds downstairs bounds upstairs" = trivial fibration.
   - *Why needed:* This pins the fibrations and trivial fibrations to the concrete descriptions.

4. **The lifting axiom MC4.** Show cofibrations lift against trivial fibrations, and trivial cofibrations against fibrations.
   - *Hint:* Reduce to the generators via the retract characterization; the lift exists because the cofibration's cokernel is projective, so $\mathrm{Hom}(\text{cokernel}, -)$ is exact and supplies the diagonal degreewise.
   - *Why needed:* MC4 is the axiom that makes the homotopy category computable.

5. **The factorization axiom MC5.** Factor any map as (cofibration, trivial fibration) and as (trivial cofibration, fibration).
   - *Hint:* Apply the small object argument to $I$ and to $J$: transfinitely attach $D^n$'s along their boundaries until the residual map lifts against the relevant set.
   - *Why needed:* MC5 produces (co)fibrant replacements, hence resolutions.

6. **Identify the homotopy category.** Apply the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]] and show abstract homotopy = chain homotopy, cofibrant replacement = projective resolution.
   - *Hint:* The cylinder object realises $f - g = ds + sd$; conclude $\mathrm{Ho} = D(R)$.
   - *Why needed:* This is the corollary that makes the theorem useful.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Representability of cycles and chains
> **Statement:** For any complex $C$, there are natural isomorphisms $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) \cong C_n$ and $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) \cong Z_n(C)$.
>
> **Hint:** A chain map is determined by where it sends generators, subject to commuting with $d$.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the bridge converting every lifting problem against $\{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ and $\{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ into a statement about modules, cycles, and surjections — without it the generators are opaque.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > $D^n$ is $R \xrightarrow{1} R$ in degrees $n, n-1$. A chain map $\varphi : D^n \to C$ assigns $\varphi_n : R \to C_n$ and $\varphi_{n-1} : R \to C_{n-1}$ with $d^C_n \varphi_n = \varphi_{n-1} d^{D^n}_n = \varphi_{n-1}$. So $\varphi_{n-1}$ is forced to be $d^C_n \varphi_n$, and $\varphi$ is determined by $\varphi_n(1) \in C_n$, which is arbitrary. Hence $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) \cong C_n$.
> >
> > $S^n$ is $R$ in degree $n$ with zero differential. A chain map $\psi : S^n \to C$ is $\psi_n : R \to C_n$ with $d^C_n \psi_n = \psi_{n-1} \cdot 0 = 0$, so $\psi_n(1) \in \ker d^C_n = Z_n(C)$, arbitrary. Hence $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) \cong Z_n(C)$. Naturality in $C$ is immediate.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: $J$-injectives are the degreewise surjections
> **Statement:** A chain map $p : E \to B$ has the right lifting property against $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ if and only if $p$ is surjective in every degree.
>
> **Hint:** Use $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, -) = (-)_n$ from Lemma 1.
>
> **Why needed:** It identifies the fibrations with the concrete class "degreewise epimorphisms", anchoring the abstract lifting definition to the stated one.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > A lifting problem for $0 \hookrightarrow D^n$ against $p$ is a square: the top map $0 \to E$ is trivial, the bottom map $D^n \to B$ is, by Lemma 1, an element $b \in B_n$, and a lift $D^n \to E$ is, by Lemma 1, an element $e \in E_n$ with $p_n(e) = b$. So a lift exists for every $b \in B_n$ exactly when $p_n$ is surjective. Ranging over all $n$ gives the claim.

> [!note]- Lemma 3: $I$-injectives are the trivial fibrations
> **Statement:** A chain map $p : E \to B$ has the right lifting property against $I = \{S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ if and only if $p$ is a degreewise surjection and a quasi-isomorphism (a trivial fibration).
>
> **Hint:** RLP against $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ says: every $(n-1)$-cycle of $B$ lifting to an element bounding it in $B$ also lifts compatibly to $E$.
>
> **Why needed:** It identifies the trivial fibrations, completing the dictionary between lifting-defined and concretely-defined classes; combined with Lemma 2 it gives the recognition theorem inputs.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > A lifting problem for $S^{n-1} \hookrightarrow D^n$ is: a cycle $z \in Z_{n-1}(E)$ (top map, by Lemma 1) and an element $b \in B_n$ with $d b = p(z)$ (bottom map, by Lemma 1, compatibility forced by the square), and a lift is $e \in E_n$ with $d e = z$ and $p(e) = b$. The existence of such $e$ for all data is equivalent to: $p$ is surjective (taking $z = 0$ recovers Lemma 2's surjectivity in each degree after a shift) and the induced map on cycles/boundaries is such that $H_*(p)$ is an isomorphism. Unwinding, this is exactly "$p$ surjective with acyclic kernel", i.e. a degreewise-surjective quasi-isomorphism. (The kernel $K = \ker p$ being acyclic is equivalent, via the long exact homology sequence of $0 \to K \to E \to B \to 0$, to $H_*(p)$ being an isomorphism.)

> [!note]- Lemma 4: Cofibrations lift against trivial fibrations
> **Statement:** If $i : A \to X$ is a cofibration (mono with degreewise-projective cokernel) and $p : E \to B$ is a trivial fibration, every commuting square has a diagonal filler.
>
> **Hint:** The cokernel $Q = X/A$ is degreewise projective; build the lift degree by degree using that $\mathrm{Hom}(Q_n, -)$ is exact and $p$ is surjective with acyclic kernel.
>
> **Why needed:** This is the substance of MC4 for the projective model structure; projectivity of the cokernel is precisely the lifting input.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Since $i$ is a degreewise-split mono (its cokernel $Q_n$ is projective, so the short exact sequence $0 \to A_n \to X_n \to Q_n \to 0$ splits), choose splittings $X_n \cong A_n \oplus Q_n$ compatible enough to induct. We construct the lift $h : X \to E$ degree by degree, descending, so that $h|_A$ is the given top map and $p h$ is the given bottom map. On the $A$ summand $h$ is forced. On the $Q_n$ summand, we must choose $h$ on the projective module $Q_n$ lifting the bottom map through the surjection $p$, compatibly with the differential already defined in degree $n-1$. Because $Q_n$ is projective and $p$ is surjective with acyclic kernel, the requisite lift through $p$ exists ($\mathrm{Hom}(Q_n, E_n) \to \mathrm{Hom}(Q_n, B_n)$ is surjective), and acyclicity of $\ker p$ lets one correct it to commute with $d$. The dual statement (trivial cofibrations lift against fibrations) is proved the same way, with the trivial cofibration's cokernel being an acyclic complex of projectives.

> [!note]- Lemma 5: Factorization via the small object argument
> **Statement:** Every chain map factors as a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration, and as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration.
>
> **Hint:** Transfinitely attach disk complexes $D^n$ along their boundary spheres (for $I$) or attach $D^n$'s from $0$ (for $J$) until the residual map lifts against the generating set.
>
> **Why needed:** This is MC5, the source of all (co)fibrant replacements and resolutions; it is the only axiom not provable by direct algebra.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Apply Quillen's small object argument to the set $I = \{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$. Given $f : C \to D$, form $C = C^0 \to C^1 \to \cdots$ by, at each stage, attaching one copy of $D^n$ for every commuting square from a generator $S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n$ into the current map; take the colimit $C^\infty$. The map $C \to C^\infty$ is a transfinite composite of pushouts of generators, hence a cofibration (its cokernel is a sum of the projective $D^n$-summands, degreewise projective). The residual $C^\infty \to D$ has the RLP against every generator — every square now has a chosen filler from the attaching stage — so by Lemma 3 it is a trivial fibration. Smallness (maps out of the finitely-generated $D^n$ commute with the transfinite colimit) guarantees termination. Running the argument with $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ instead, the first factor becomes a trivial cofibration (it attaches only acyclic disks, adding no homology) and Lemma 2 makes the second a fibration. $\blacksquare$ (for this lemma)

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> **Step 0 — bicompleteness.** Limits and colimits in $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ are computed degreewise (a limit of complexes has $n$-th term the limit of the $n$-th terms, with the induced differential), and $\mathbf{Mod}_R$ is bicomplete, so $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ is bicomplete. This is MC1.
>
> **Step 1 — MC2 (2-out-of-3) and MC3 (retracts).** $H_n$ is a functor $\mathbf{Ch}(R) \to \mathbf{Mod}_R$, and isomorphisms in $\mathbf{Mod}_R$ satisfy 2-out-of-3; applying $H_n$ degreewise, quasi-isomorphisms satisfy 2-out-of-3, giving MC2. For MC3, a retract of a quasi-isomorphism is a quasi-isomorphism (apply $H_n$ and use that retracts of isomorphisms are isomorphisms); a retract of a degreewise epi is a degreewise epi; a retract of a mono-with-projective-cokernel is again such (projectivity passes to retracts as direct summands). So all three classes are retract-closed.
>
> **Step 2 — the generators and their injectives.** By Lemma 1, $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(D^n, C) \cong C_n$ and $\mathbf{Ch}(R)(S^n, C) \cong Z_n(C)$. By Lemma 2, the maps with RLP against $J = \{0 \hookrightarrow D^n\}$ are exactly the degreewise surjections; we *declare* these the fibrations. By Lemma 3, the maps with RLP against $I = \{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$ are exactly the degreewise-surjective quasi-isomorphisms; these will be the trivial fibrations.
>
> **Step 3 — MC4 (lifting).** By Lemma 4, cofibrations (monos with projective cokernel) lift against trivial fibrations, and trivial cofibrations lift against fibrations. This is MC4.
>
> **Step 4 — MC5 (factorization).** By Lemma 5, the small object argument applied to $I$ factors any map as a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration, and applied to $J$ as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration. This is MC5.
>
> **Step 5 — the classes match.** It remains to check the three declared classes are mutually consistent (e.g. a cofibration that is a weak equivalence is a trivial cofibration in the lifting sense). This follows from the [[Thm - The Retract Argument|retract argument]]: a weak equivalence that is a cofibration factors, by Step 4, as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration; by 2-out-of-3 the fibration is a weak equivalence hence a trivial fibration, the original map lifts against it, and the retract argument exhibits the original map as a retract of the trivial cofibration, hence itself a trivial cofibration. The dual handles trivial fibrations. Thus $(\mathcal{W}, \mathrm{cof}, \mathrm{fib})$ is a model structure, cofibrantly generated by $(I, J)$.
>
> **Step 6 — (co)fibrant objects.** $C \to 0$ is surjective in every degree (zero map onto zero), so every object is fibrant. $0 \to C$ is a cofibration iff $C$ has degreewise-projective cokernel over $0$, i.e. $C$ is degreewise projective; for bounded-below complexes this is exactly cofibrancy.
>
> **Step 7 — the corollary, $\mathrm{Ho} = D(R)$.** By the [[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|fundamental theorem]], $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Ch}(R))$ has objects the bifibrant complexes (= bounded-below complexes of projectives, since all are fibrant) and morphisms the abstract homotopy classes. The cylinder object of $C$ realises a left homotopy $f \simeq g$ as a chain homotopy $s$ with $f - g = ds + sd$, so abstract homotopy is [[Def - Chain Map and Chain Homotopy|chain homotopy]]. A module $M$ in degree zero has cofibrant replacement its projective resolution $P_\bullet \xrightarrow{\sim} M$. Therefore $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Ch}(R))$ is the category of complexes-of-projectives and chain-homotopy classes, which is the standard description of the derived category $D(R)$. $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Group cohomology as a derived functor.** For a finite group $G$ and a $G$-module $M$, group cohomology $H^*(G; M)$ is $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$, the right derived functor of $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, -)$. This is an application because $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ is a ring and the bar resolution is a cofibrant replacement of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}[G])$; the model structure justifies that $H^*(G; M)$ is independent of the chosen resolution, which is non-obvious from the cochain definition.

**Hyperhomology of a complex of sheaves.** In algebraic geometry, the cohomology of a scheme with coefficients in a complex of sheaves is the hyper-derived functor of global sections, computed by an injective (fibrant) replacement in $\mathbf{Ch}(\mathcal{O}_X\text{-Mod})$. The application transfers the theorem verbatim with $R$-modules replaced by sheaves; the non-obvious point is that injective resolutions of complexes (not just modules) are fibrant replacements, so hypercohomology is a derived functor in the model-category sense.

**Koszul complexes and regular sequences.** For a commutative ring $R$ and a regular sequence $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the Koszul complex is a projective (free) resolution of $R/(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, hence a cofibrant replacement. Computing $\mathrm{Tor}^R_*(R/(x_1,\dots,x_n), M)$ via the Koszul complex is a direct application; the non-obvious recognition is that the Koszul complex's freeness is exactly cofibrancy, so the model structure certifies the Tor computation.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - The Homotopy Category of a Model Category|The Fundamental Theorem of Model Categories]]** — the engine that turns this theorem's "model structure exists" into the corollary "$\mathrm{Ho} = D(R)$". The fundamental theorem says the homotopy category is bifibrant objects and homotopy classes; here the bifibrant objects are complexes of projectives and the homotopy is chain homotopy, so the abstract output is the concrete derived category. Without the fundamental theorem this theorem would only build the structure, not identify its homotopy category.

- **[[Thm - Topological Spaces Form a Model Category|Topological Spaces Form a Model Category]]** — the parallel theorem one category over. The proofs are structurally identical: the same generators $\{S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow D^n\}$, the same small object argument, with "projective module lifts against surjections" replaced by "disk extends homotopies across cells". Reading the two proofs side by side is the clearest demonstration that chain complexes and spaces carry the *same* homotopy theory in two costumes, related by singular homology as a left Quillen functor.

- **[[Def - Stable Module Category over a Frobenius Ring|The Stable Module Category]]** — the degenerate cousin. When $R$ is Frobenius one can instead invert *stable* equivalences, and because projective = injective the resulting homotopy category is the stable module category, which is its own homotopy category (no resolution needed). The derived category $D(R)$ and the stable category are two different homotopy theories on module data, distinguished by which maps they invert — quasi-isomorphisms of complexes versus stable equivalences of modules.

- **The injective model structure on $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$** — the Quillen-equivalent sibling. Taking cofibrations = monos and fibrations = degreewise epis with injective kernel gives a second model structure with the same weak equivalences, hence the same $D(R)$. The identity functor is a Quillen equivalence between the two, and the practical payoff is computing Ext by injective resolution of the target instead of projective resolution of the source.

> [!tip] The Triangulated Structure on $D(R)$ *(from Homological Algebra)*
> The cofiber sequences of this model structure are the **distinguished triangles** of $D(R)$, and the degree shift $C \mapsto C[1]$ is the triangulated shift. Together they make $D(R)$ a **triangulated category**, the abstract home of long exact sequences and the octahedral axiom. This is the model-categorical explanation of where the triangulated structure of derived categories comes from.

> [!tip] Total Derived Functors, Tor, and Ext *(from Homological Algebra)*
> With the model structure in hand, $\mathbf{L}(-\otimes_R N)$ and $\mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$ exist as total derived functors, computing **Tor** and **Ext**. The model structure guarantees these are independent of the resolution and are the universal homotopy-invariant extensions of the underlying functors.

> [!tip] Differential Graded Algebra and DG-Categories *(from Higher Algebra)*
> Upgrading from chain complexes of modules to **differential graded algebras** and **DG-categories**, the same projective model structure underlies the homotopy theory of DG-categories, the enhancement of triangulated categories that fixes their functoriality defects — a stepping stone to **stable ∞-categories**.
