# Problem Statement

Let $F : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}$ be a left adjoint, with right adjoint $U : \mathcal{N} \to \mathcal{M}$ and adjunction isomorphism $\varphi : \mathcal{N}(FA, B) \cong \mathcal{M}(A, UB)$.

(a) Prove the **adjoint lifting lemma**: for maps $i$ in $\mathcal{M}$ and $p$ in $\mathcal{N}$, the map $Fi$ has the left lifting property against $p$ in $\mathcal{N}$ **if and only if** $i$ has the left lifting property against $Up$ in $\mathcal{M}$.

(b) Deduce that $F$ preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations **if and only if** $U$ preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations — so that the two conditions defining a [[Def - Quillen Adjunction and Quillen Equivalence|Quillen adjunction]] are equivalent.

(c) Suppose the model structure on $\mathcal{M}$ is cofibrantly generated by a set $I$ of generating cofibrations, and $F$ sends each generator $i \in I$ to a cofibration of $\mathcal{N}$. Prove $F$ preserves *all* cofibrations.

**Recall:**

![[Def - Lifting Property and the Retract Argument#The Definition]]

An [[Def - Adjunction|adjunction]] $F \dashv U$ has a natural bijection $\varphi : \mathcal{N}(FA, B) \cong \mathcal{M}(A, UB)$, and left adjoints preserve colimits (in particular pushouts, coproducts, the initial object). By [[Thm - Closure Properties of the Model Structure|the closure theorem]], cofibrations are exactly the maps with LLP against all trivial fibrations, and cofibrations are closed under pushout, coproduct, transfinite composite, and retract.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is a structural lemma about adjunctions and lifting — establishing the symmetry of the [[Def - Quillen Adjunction and Quillen Equivalence|Quillen-adjunction]] definition. It supports operation 8 ("recognize a class by its lifting property") and is the technical justification for why checking either adjoint suffices.

**Assumption pattern:** The recognizable feature is an adjunction together with a lifting problem; the unlock is that the adjunction *transposes lifting squares* — a square involving $Fi$ and $p$ corresponds bijectively to a square involving $i$ and $Up$. Recognizing that $\varphi$ converts one lifting problem into the other is the whole of part (a).

**Theorem routing:** Part (a) is a direct application of the naturality of $\varphi$ to the four maps of a lifting square. Part (b) routes through [[Thm - Closure Properties of the Model Structure|the lifting characterization]]: $F$ preserves cofibrations iff $Fi$ lifts against all trivial fibrations of $\mathcal{N}$, which by (a) is iff $i$ lifts against all $Up$ for trivial fibrations $p$, which is exactly $U$ preserving trivial fibrations. Part (c) routes through closure: $F$ preserves the colimits and retracts that build all cofibrations from $I$.

**Key decision point:** The crux of part (a) is correctly transposing the *commuting square* under the adjunction. A lifting square is two maps with a commutativity relation; you must transpose all four maps (top, bottom, and the two it factors through) and verify the transposed relation still holds and that a lift transposes to a lift. Keeping the unit/counit bookkeeping straight is where the proof is won.

3. **Operation 7 from the topic page (dualize), implicitly.** The equivalence in (b) is exactly the duality between the left-adjoint/cofibration side and the right-adjoint/fibration side, mediated by the adjunction.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> For (a): a lifting square for $Fi$ against $p$ in $\mathcal{N}$ has corners $FA, X, FB, Y$ with maps $u : FA \to X$, $v : FB \to Y$. Transpose $u, v$ under the adjunction to $\tilde u : A \to UX$, $\tilde v : B \to UY$. Check these form a lifting square for $i$ against $Up$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> The commutativity of the square transposes correctly by *naturality* of $\varphi$: $\varphi$ is natural in both variables, so it converts the relation $p \circ u = v \circ Fi$ into $Up \circ \tilde u = \tilde v \circ i$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> A lift $h : FB \to X$ in the $\mathcal{N}$-square transposes to a lift $\tilde h : B \to UX$ in the $\mathcal{M}$-square, and vice versa, again by naturality. So lifts correspond bijectively — proving the iff.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For (b): "$F$ preserves cofibrations" means $Fi$ is a cofibration for every cofibration $i$, i.e. (lifting characterization) $Fi$ lifts against every trivial fibration $p$ of $\mathcal{N}$. By (a) this is "$i$ lifts against $Up$ for every trivial fibration $p$." For this to say "$U$ preserves trivial fibrations," note $i$ lifting against $Up$ for all cofibrations $i$ means $Up$ is a trivial fibration.

> [!note]- Hint 5
> For (c): every cofibration is a retract of a transfinite composite of pushouts of coproducts of generators in $I$ (cofibrant generation). $F$ is a left adjoint, so it preserves all these colimits, and it preserves retracts; if it sends generators to cofibrations, it sends the whole construction to cofibrations.

# Solution

The solution transposes lifting squares under the adjunction (a), uses the lifting characterization to convert preservation statements between the two adjoints (b), and uses colimit-preservation to extend from generators to all cofibrations (c).

**Step 1: The adjoint lifting lemma.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $i : A \to B$ in $\mathcal{M}$ and $p : X \to Y$ in $\mathcal{N}$. A lifting square for $Fi$ against $p$ is maps $u : FA \to X$, $v : FB \to Y$ with $p \circ u = v \circ Fi$. Transpose under $\varphi$: let $\tilde u = \varphi(u) : A \to UX$ and $\tilde v = \varphi(v) : B \to UY$. Naturality of $\varphi$ in the codomain gives $\varphi(p \circ u) = Up \circ \varphi(u) = Up \circ \tilde u$; naturality in the domain gives $\varphi(v \circ Fi) = \varphi(v) \circ i = \tilde v \circ i$. Since $p \circ u = v \circ Fi$, applying $\varphi$ gives $Up \circ \tilde u = \tilde v \circ i$ — a lifting square for $i$ against $Up$. This transposition is a bijection between squares (inverse via $\varphi^{-1}$). Moreover a lift $h : FB \to X$ ($h \circ Fi = u$, $p \circ h = v$) transposes to $\tilde h = \varphi(h) : B \to UX$ with $\tilde h \circ i = \tilde u$ and $Up \circ \tilde h = \tilde v$ (naturality again), i.e. a lift in the $\mathcal{M}$-square; and conversely. So lifts correspond bijectively, proving: $Fi$ has the LLP against $p$ iff $i$ has the LLP against $Up$.

**Step 2: Equivalence of the two Quillen conditions.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> ($F$ preserves cofibrations $\Rightarrow$ $U$ preserves trivial fibrations.) Suppose $F$ sends every cofibration to a cofibration. Let $p$ be a trivial fibration of $\mathcal{N}$; we show $Up$ is a trivial fibration of $\mathcal{M}$, equivalently (by [[Thm - Closure Properties of the Model Structure|lifting characterization]]) that every cofibration $i$ of $\mathcal{M}$ lifts against $Up$. By Step 1, $i$ lifts against $Up$ iff $Fi$ lifts against $p$; and $Fi$ is a cofibration (by hypothesis), so it lifts against the trivial fibration $p$ (lifting axiom). Hence $i$ lifts against $Up$ for all cofibrations $i$, so $Up$ is a trivial fibration. The converse direction and the trivial-cofibration/fibration case are identical, replacing "cofibration / trivial fibration" by "trivial cofibration / fibration." So $F$ preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations iff $U$ preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations.

**Step 3: Preservation from generators (cofibrant generation).**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Suppose the cofibrations of $\mathcal{M}$ are cofibrantly generated by a set $I$, so every cofibration is a retract of a transfinite composite of pushouts of coproducts of maps in $I$. Suppose $F i$ is a cofibration of $\mathcal{N}$ for every $i \in I$. Since $F$ is a left adjoint, it preserves all colimits: coproducts of generators map to coproducts of the $Fi$ (cofibrations, closed under coproduct), pushouts to pushouts (cofibrations closed under pushout), transfinite composites to transfinite composites (cofibrations closed under these), and retracts to retracts (cofibrations closed under retract). Therefore $F$ sends every cofibration — a retract of such a colimit — to a cofibration of $\mathcal{N}$. (To get trivial cofibrations too, apply the same argument to a generating set $J$ of trivial cofibrations.)

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **(a)** For $i : A \to B$, $p : X \to Y$, the adjunction $\varphi$ transposes a lifting square $(u, v)$ for $Fi$ against $p$ (with $pu = v \circ Fi$) to a lifting square $(\tilde u, \tilde v) = (\varphi u, \varphi v)$ for $i$ against $Up$ (with $Up \circ \tilde u = \tilde v \circ i$, by naturality), bijectively; and lifts transpose to lifts. So $Fi$ has LLP against $p$ iff $i$ has LLP against $Up$.
>
> **(b)** $F$ preserves cofibrations iff for every cofibration $i$ and trivial fibration $p$, $Fi$ lifts against $p$ (lifting characterization), iff (by (a)) $i$ lifts against $Up$ for all such $i$, iff $Up$ is a trivial fibration. So $F$ preserves cofibrations iff $U$ preserves trivial fibrations; identically $F$ preserves trivial cofibrations iff $U$ preserves fibrations. Hence the two Quillen conditions are equivalent.
>
> **(c)** If $\mathcal{M}$ is cofibrantly generated by $I$ and $F i$ is a cofibration for each $i \in I$, then since $F$ (a left adjoint) preserves coproducts, pushouts, transfinite composites, and retracts — the operations building all cofibrations from $I$ — and cofibrations of $\mathcal{N}$ are closed under all of these, $F$ preserves all cofibrations. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**An adjunction transposes lifting problems, which is why a Quillen adjunction can be checked on either adjoint.** The adjoint lifting lemma — $Fi$ lifts against $p$ iff $i$ lifts against $Up$ — is pure naturality of the adjunction isomorphism applied to the four maps of a square, and it is the reason the definition of a Quillen adjunction is symmetric. The practical payoff is that you check whichever side is easier: for $|{-}| \dashv \mathrm{Sing}$ you might verify $|{-}|$ preserves cofibrations, while for $-\otimes N \dashv \mathrm{Hom}(N,-)$ you verify $-\otimes N$ preserves cofibrations; either suffices. The trigger is "I want to know this adjunction is Quillen"; the reaction is "transpose to whichever adjoint has the cleaner preservation property." This transposition is one of the most reused tricks in the subject.

**Cofibrant generation reduces "preserves all cofibrations" to "preserves the generators," because left adjoints preserve the colimits that build everything.** Checking that a left adjoint preserves *all* cofibrations directly would be hopeless, but every cofibration is a retract of a transfinite cell complex built from a small set $I$ of generators, and a left adjoint preserves all the colimits (coproducts, pushouts, transfinite composites) and retracts involved. So the infinite class of cofibrations is controlled by a finite or small set of checks. This is the structural reason cofibrant generation is so powerful, and it is exactly the principle used in [[Ex - Geometric realization and singular nerve form a Quillen equivalence]] to verify realization is left Quillen by checking it on $\partial\Delta^n \hookrightarrow \Delta^n$. The transferable lesson: to prove a left adjoint preserves a lifting-defined class, check the generators and ride colimit-preservation.

**Left adjoints preserve colimits and cofibrations; right adjoints preserve limits and fibrations — the same duality at the homotopical level.** Ordinary category theory tells you left adjoints preserve colimits and right adjoints preserve limits ([[Thm - Right Adjoints Preserve Limits]]); the Quillen-adjunction story is the homotopical refinement, where left adjoints additionally preserve cofibrations (the "colimit-like" maps) and right adjoints preserve fibrations (the "limit-like" maps). The adjoint lifting lemma is the precise bridge: it converts a left-adjoint statement about cofibrations into a right-adjoint statement about fibrations. Recognizing this parallel — colimit/cofibration/left-adjoint on one side, limit/fibration/right-adjoint on the other — organizes the entire theory of Quillen functors and is the reason [[Thm - Quillen Adjunctions Descend to Derived Adjunctions|derived adjunctions]] pair a colimit-preserving $\mathbf{L}F$ with a limit-preserving $\mathbf{R}U$.
