# Problem Statement

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a model category, $X$ cofibrant, $Y$ fibrant, and $X^{\bullet}$ a [[Def - Cosimplicial and Simplicial Frame|cosimplicial frame]] on $X$. Prove that the [[Def - Homotopy Function Complex|homotopy function complex]] $\mathrm{map}(X, Y) = \mathcal{M}(X^{\bullet}, Y)$ satisfies
$$\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X, Y) \;\cong\; [X, Y] = \mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{M})(X, Y).$$

Specifically:

(a) Identify the $0$-simplices of $\mathrm{map}(X, Y)$ with maps $X^0 \to Y$, i.e. (since $X^0\simeq X$) with maps representing elements of $\mathcal{M}(X, Y)$.

(b) Identify the relation "joined by a $1$-simplex" with the [[Def - Cylinder Object, Path Object, and Homotopy|left-homotopy]] relation, and show it is an equivalence relation here.

(c) Conclude $\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X,Y) = \mathcal{M}(X, Y)/\!\simeq\, = [X, Y]$.

**Recall:**

For a [[Def - Cosimplicial and Simplicial Frame|cosimplicial frame]] $X^{\bullet}$ and fibrant $Y$, the homotopy function complex is the [[Def - Simplicial Set|simplicial set]] with $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_n = \mathcal{M}(X^n, Y)$; its faces are induced by the cofaces of the frame. The set of path components is $\pi_0 K = K_0 / \sim$, where $a \sim b$ iff there is a $1$-simplex $w$ with $d_1 w = a$, $d_0 w = b$.

![[Def - Cylinder Object, Path Object, and Homotopy#The Definition]]

By [[Ex - Level one of a frame is a cylinder object]], $X^1$ is a cylinder object on $X^0$ with end inclusions the two cofaces $d^0, d^1 : X^0\to X^1$.

For $X$ cofibrant and $Y$ fibrant, left homotopy $\simeq_\ell$ is an equivalence relation on $\mathcal{M}(X, Y)$ and $[X, Y] = \mathcal{M}(X,Y)/\!\simeq$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is an "unwind a definition to recover a known invariant" problem: the homotopy function complex is built precisely so that its $\pi_0$ is the homotopy classes, and the task is to verify it from the construction. The routine is to translate simplicial-set notions ($0$-simplices, $1$-simplices, path components) through the frame into model-category notions (maps, homotopies, homotopy classes).

**Assumption pattern:** The assets are cofibrancy of $X$ and fibrancy of $Y$, which are exactly the hypotheses making the left-homotopy relation an equivalence relation (from [[Def - Cylinder Object, Path Object, and Homotopy]]), and the frame structure, which makes $X^1$ a cylinder object. Recognizing that a $1$-simplex of $\mathcal{M}(X^{\bullet}, Y)$ is a map out of the cylinder $X^1$ — hence a left homotopy — is the unlock.

**Theorem routing:** This is the degree-$\le 1$ part of [[Thm - Framings Compute Homotopy Function Complexes]], routed through [[Ex - Level one of a frame is a cylinder object]] (which gives the cylinder) and the equivalence-relation theorem for left homotopy on cofibrant–fibrant pairs.

**Key decision point:** The non-obvious step is *why the relation is already an equivalence relation* at the level of $\pi_0$, without needing the full Kan-complex structure. The decision is to invoke the cofibrant-source/fibrant-target hypothesis directly (left homotopy is an equivalence relation there) rather than deriving it from $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ being a Kan complex — though both routes work.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> A $0$-simplex of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ is an element of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_0 = \mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)$. Since the frame gives $X^0 \simeq X$ (and we may take $X^0 = X$ when $X$ is cofibrant), these are maps $X\to Y$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> A $1$-simplex is an element of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_1 = \mathcal{M}(X^1, Y)$. Its two faces $d_0, d_1$ are precomposition with the two cofaces $d^0, d^1 : X^0\to X^1$. By [[Ex - Level one of a frame is a cylinder object]], $X^1$ is a cylinder object, so a $1$-simplex with faces $f, g$ is a left homotopy $f\simeq_\ell g$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> $\pi_0$ of a simplicial set is $0$-simplices modulo the relation generated by $1$-simplices. Here that relation is left homotopy, which is an equivalence relation because $X$ is cofibrant and $Y$ is fibrant. So $\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ is $\mathcal{M}(X,Y)/\!\simeq\, = [X,Y]$.

# Solution

The plan: Step 1 identifies $0$-simplices with maps $X\to Y$; Step 2 identifies $1$-simplices with left homotopies and notes the relation is an equivalence relation; Step 3 takes the quotient to get $[X,Y]$.

**Step 1: $0$-simplices are maps $X \to Y$.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> By definition $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_0 = \mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)$. The frame has $X^0 \simeq X$, and when $X$ is cofibrant we may arrange $X^0 = X$ (the degree-$0$ part of a frame is a cofibrant model of $X$, weakly equivalent to it; replacing $X$ by $X^0$ does not change $[X,Y]$ since $Y$ is fibrant). So the $0$-simplices of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ are precisely the maps $X^0 \to Y$, representing elements of $\mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)$.

**Step 2: $1$-simplices are left homotopies, and the relation is an equivalence relation.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> A $1$-simplex is an element $w \in \mathrm{map}(X,Y)_1 = \mathcal{M}(X^1, Y)$, a map $w : X^1 \to Y$. Its two faces are induced by the two cofaces $d^0, d^1 : X^0 \to X^1$:
> $$d_1 w = w\circ d^0, \qquad d_0 w = w\circ d^1 \quad (\text{as maps } X^0 \to Y).$$
> By [[Ex - Level one of a frame is a cylinder object]], $X^1$ is a cylinder object on $X^0$ with $d^0, d^1$ the two end inclusions. Therefore a $1$-simplex $w$ with faces $f = w d^0$ and $g = w d^1$ is exactly a [[Def - Cylinder Object, Path Object, and Homotopy|left homotopy]] $H = w : \mathrm{Cyl}(X^0)\to Y$ from $f$ to $g$. So "$f$ and $g$ are joined by a $1$-simplex" means "$f \simeq_\ell g$."
>
> Because $X^0$ is cofibrant and $Y$ is fibrant, the left-homotopy relation $\simeq_\ell$ on $\mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)$ is an **equivalence relation** (reflexive via the degenerate $1$-simplex $s_0 f = f\circ s^0$, the constant homotopy; symmetric and transitive by the cylinder-symmetry and cylinder-gluing arguments that hold for cofibrant sources). Hence the relation generated by $1$-simplices on $0$-simplices is already $\simeq_\ell$ itself, no further generation needed.

**Step 3: Take the quotient.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> By definition $\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X,Y) = \mathrm{map}(X,Y)_0 / (\text{relation from } 1\text{-simplices})$. By Steps 1–2 this is $\mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)/\simeq_\ell$. For cofibrant $X^0$ and fibrant $Y$, left homotopy coincides with the homotopy relation $\simeq$ and the homotopy category's hom-set is
> $$[X, Y] = \mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{M})(X, Y) = \mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)/\simeq.$$
> Therefore $\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X,Y) \cong [X, Y]$, as claimed. The bijection is natural: precomposition and postcomposition act compatibly on both sides.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **(a)** $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_0 = \mathcal{M}(X^0, Y)$ and $X^0$ is a cofibrant model of $X$ ($X^0\simeq X$), so $0$-simplices are maps representing $\mathcal{M}(X, Y)$.
>
> **(b)** $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)_1 = \mathcal{M}(X^1, Y)$, and $X^1$ is a cylinder object on $X^0$ with end inclusions $d^0, d^1$; a $1$-simplex $w$ has faces $wd^0, wd^1$, so it is a left homotopy between them. For cofibrant $X^0$ and fibrant $Y$, $\simeq_\ell$ is an equivalence relation, so the relation generated by $1$-simplices is $\simeq_\ell$.
>
> **(c)** $\pi_0\,\mathrm{map}(X,Y) = \mathcal{M}(X^0,Y)/\simeq_\ell = \mathcal{M}(X^0,Y)/\simeq = [X,Y]$, naturally. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**The homotopy function complex is designed so that $\pi_0$ recovers the homotopy classes — this is the anchor that makes it the right object.** The defining property a hom-*space* must have is that its set of components is the hom-*set* one already knew; otherwise it would not be an enrichment of $\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{M})$. This exercise verifies that anchor directly from the construction: $0$-simplices are maps, $1$-simplices are homotopies, so components are homotopy classes. Whenever you meet a "space of maps" in homotopy theory — a mapping space, a derived hom, an $\infty$-categorical mapping object — the first sanity check is $\pi_0 = [\text{source}, \text{target}]$, and it should always hold for the same reason: $1$-simplices encode homotopies.

**Cofibrancy of the source and fibrancy of the target is what makes "homotopic" an equivalence relation, and hence $\pi_0$ well-defined.** The quotient $\mathcal{M}(X,Y)/\simeq$ only makes sense if $\simeq$ is an equivalence relation, and that requires $X$ cofibrant and $Y$ fibrant — the same bifibrant hypothesis that runs through all of homotopy theory. Without it the "relation generated by $1$-simplices" would need genuine generation (it would not be transitive a priori), and one would have to invoke the full Kan-complex structure of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ to fix it. The takeaway: the bifibrant hypothesis is not a technicality to be tolerated but the precise condition under which homotopy classes form a set, and it is why one always resolves before computing $\pi_0$.

**Reading simplicial-set structure through a frame is the universal translation between combinatorics and homotopy.** The mechanical move in this exercise — "$0$-simplices = maps, $1$-simplices = homotopies, faces = precompose with cofaces" — is the template for every computation with homotopy function complexes. Higher up, $2$-simplices are homotopies between homotopies, $n$-simplices are $n$-fold homotopies, and the simplicial identities make them compose. So the entire higher structure of $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ is just "apply $\mathcal{M}(-, Y)$ to the frame and read off what each simplicial operation means." Mastering the degree-$\le 1$ translation here is the right preparation for the full statement that $\mathrm{map}(X,Y)$ is the mapping space of the underlying $\infty$-category, proved in [[Thm - Framings Compute Homotopy Function Complexes]].
