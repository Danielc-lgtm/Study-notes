## §3.2 Anodyne Extensions and Simplicial Homotopy Groups — Exercises

These exercises develop the two pillars of §3.2: the [[Def - Kan Fibration and Anodyne Extension|anodyne]] characterisation of trivial cofibrations (Gabriel–Zisman) and the combinatorial theory of [[Def - Simplicial Homotopy Group|simplicial homotopy groups]]. The unifying technique is horn-filling: it is what makes the homotopy relation transitive (hence $\pi_n$ well-defined), what supplies the group operation, and — packaged as the saturation of horn inclusions — what defines the anodyne maps. The exercises show why the [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve|Kan condition]] is exactly the fibrancy needed for homotopy groups, how the [[Thm - The Retract Argument|retract argument]] identifies anodyne maps with trivial cofibrations, and how the whole machinery computes a concrete invariant (the fundamental group of a classifying complex). The recurring trigger: "compose two homotopies / read off an operation" is always a horn-fill, and always needs the Kan condition.

- [[Ex - The simplicial homotopy relation is transitive only for Kan complexes]] (⭐⭐) — pins down exactly where the Kan condition enters the definition of $\pi_n$: it is the horn-fill that makes homotopy transitive; with a non-Kan counterexample ([[Def - Simplicial Homotopy Group]], [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve]], [[Def - Kan Fibration and Anodyne Extension]])
- [[Ex - Anodyne extensions are trivial cofibrations]] (⭐⭐) — the Gabriel–Zisman characterisation via the retract argument applied to a small-object-argument factorisation ([[Def - Kan Fibration and Anodyne Extension]], [[Thm - Simplicial Sets Form a Model Category]], [[Thm - The Retract Argument]], [[Ex - Monomorphisms are the cofibrations]], [[Ex - Trivial fibrations lift against all monomorphisms]])
- [[Ex - The fundamental group of the nerve of a group]] (⭐⭐) — computes all simplicial homotopy groups of $N(G)$, recovering $\pi_1 = G$ and $|N(G)| = BG = K(G,1)$; operations as horn fillers ([[Def - Simplicial Homotopy Group]], [[Def - Kan Complex and the Nerve]], [[Def - Groupoid]])
