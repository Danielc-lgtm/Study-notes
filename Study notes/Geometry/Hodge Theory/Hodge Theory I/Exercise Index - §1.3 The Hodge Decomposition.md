## §1.3 The Hodge Decomposition — Exercises

This section drills the Hodge decomposition theorem and its corollaries: the canonical harmonic representative of each de Rham cohomology class, the dimension count $\dim\mathcal{H}^k = b_k$, and Poincaré duality via the Hodge star. The exercises focus on computing $\mathcal{H}^k$ explicitly on a small selection of manifolds (the flat torus, Minkowski $4$D for the electromagnetic field), verifying Poincaré duality, and using the harmonic representative to compute cohomology pairings. The recurring techniques are symmetry averaging (harmonic forms on a homogeneous space are invariant forms), Fourier analysis on the flat torus, and the algebraic decomposition into self-dual / anti-self-dual components in middle dimension $4$.

- [[Ex - Harmonic 1-Forms on the Torus]] (⭐⭐) — computes $\mathcal{H}^k(T^n) = \binom{n}{k}$-dimensional space of constant-coefficient $k$-forms, verifying the Hodge isomorphism and Poincaré duality ([[Def - Harmonic Form]], [[Def - Hodge Laplacian]], [[Thm - Harmonic Forms Represent de Rham Cohomology]], [[Thm - Hodge Decomposition Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Self-Duality of the Electromagnetic Tensor in Minkowski]] (⭐⭐⭐) — analyzes the $\pm i$-eigenspace decomposition of $\star$ on $2$-forms in Lorentzian $4$D and the complex Maxwell tensor; previews the Riemannian self-dual / anti-self-dual decomposition behind Yang–Mills instantons ([[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]], [[Def - Self-Dual and Anti-Self-Dual Forms]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]])
- [[Ex - Maxwell's Equations Use the Codifferential]] (⭐⭐) — the form-language reformulation of source-free Maxwell as $dF = 0, \delta F = 0$ is the simplest example of "harmonic field" condition in the chapter, with the EM field as a coclosed-and-closed $2$-form ([[Def - The Codifferential]], [[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]])
