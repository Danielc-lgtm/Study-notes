## §1.4 Bochner Technique and Curvature Bounds — Exercises

This section drills the **Bochner technique** — the most important method connecting curvature to topology in Riemannian geometry. The exercises focus on applying Bochner's theorem to specific manifolds (the sphere, the torus, hyperbolic space forms), understanding the role of strict versus nonnegative Ricci curvature, and recognizing the sharpness of the curvature hypothesis. The recurring techniques are computing the Ricci tensor from constant sectional curvature, applying the Weitzenböck identity $\Delta = \nabla^*\nabla + \operatorname{Ric}$ on $1$-forms, and integrating over the closed manifold to extract a vanishing or rigidity conclusion. The pattern "harmonic + curvature positivity + integration → pointwise vanishing" recurs throughout the broader Bochner-Weitzenböck industry.

- [[Ex - First Betti Number Bounds via Bochner's Theorem]] (⭐⭐⭐) — applies Bochner to $S^n$ (positive Ricci, $b_1 = 0$), $T^n$ (zero Ricci, $b_1 = n$ saturating the corollary), and hyperbolic space forms (negative Ricci, $b_1$ unconstrained), demonstrating the sharpness of the positivity hypothesis ([[Thm - Bochner's Theorem]], [[Def - Riemannian Manifold]], [[Thm - Harmonic Forms Represent de Rham Cohomology]])
- [[Ex - Harmonic 1-Forms on the Torus]] (⭐⭐) — direct computation showing $\mathcal{H}^1(T^n) = \mathbb{R}^n$ via constant-coefficient $1$-forms, saturating Bochner's nonnegative-Ricci bound $b_1 \leq n$ and demonstrating that harmonic = parallel in the Ricci-flat case ([[Def - Harmonic Form]], [[Thm - Harmonic Forms Represent de Rham Cohomology]], [[Thm - Bochner's Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Computing the Hodge Star on S^2]] (⭐⭐) — sets up the analytic machinery needed for Bochner computations on $S^2$ (Hodge star, Laplace–Beltrami in spherical coordinates), with the harmonic forms on $S^2$ being just the constants and the volume form by the positive-Ricci constraint ([[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]], [[Def - Hodge Laplacian]], [[Def - Riemannian Volume Form]])
