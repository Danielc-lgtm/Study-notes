## §9.1 Orientation of Manifolds — Exercises

This section's exercises drill the central concept of orientability and its alternative characterizations. The goal is to internalize the equivalence between continuous pointwise orientations, oriented atlases, and nowhere-vanishing top-degree forms, and to recognize when each formulation is the most natural for a given problem. The exercises here practice both the constructive direction (build a nowhere-vanishing top-form to prove orientability) and the obstruction direction (show no global top-form exists for non-orientable examples). Concrete computations on $S^n$, the Möbius strip, and projective spaces tie the abstract theory to specific manifolds and prepare the reader for integration in §9.2.

- [[Ex - The Sphere is Orientable but the Möbius Strip is Not]] (⭐⭐) — Constructs an explicit nowhere-vanishing $n$-form on $S^n$ via contraction with the outward normal, and refutes orientability of the Möbius strip via the deck-transformation argument on the universal cover. ([[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold]], [[Def - Volume Form]], [[Def - Oriented Atlas]], [[Thm - A Manifold is Orientable iff it Admits a Nowhere-Vanishing Top Form]])
- [[Ex - The Sphere as a Level Set]] (⭐) — *(from [[Differential Geometry IV — Submersions, Immersions, Embeddings, and Submanifolds]])* Shows $S^n$ as the regular level set $\{|x|^2 = 1\}$, with the gradient $\nabla|x|^2 = 2x$ as the outward normal field providing orientation. ([[Def - Regular and Critical Points]], [[Thm - Regular Value Theorem on Manifolds]])
- [[Ex - The Möbius Bundle is Nontrivial]] (⭐⭐) — *(from [[Differential Geometry VI — Vector Bundles and the Cotangent Bundle]])* Establishes the Möbius bundle as a non-trivial real line bundle over $S^1$ — the bundle-theoretic perspective on the non-orientability obstruction. ([[Def - Vector Bundle]], [[Def - Local Trivialization]])
