## §3.4 The Tangent Bundle — Exercises

This section drills the structural content of the tangent bundle: identifying $TM$ as a manifold, computing tangent spaces in special cases that prefigure Lie algebras, and using the differential as a global construction. The exercises emphasize that the tangent bundle of a Lie group, or any open subset of a vector space, is naturally identified with familiar linear-algebraic data. The reusable skills are: (i) using the "open subset of a vector space" principle to identify $T_{p}M$ when $M$ has this structure, (ii) recognizing the tangent-space-at-the-identity as the Lie algebra of a matrix group, (iii) computing differentials of group operations (multiplication, inversion) via curves in the group.

- [[Ex - Tangent Space of the General Linear Group at the Identity]] (⭐⭐) — Identifying $T_{I}\mathrm{GL}(n) \cong M_{n}(\mathbb{R})$ as the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{GL}(n)$, computing the differential of matrix inversion via a curve, and previewing the commutator $[A, B] = AB - BA$ as the infinitesimal failure of commutativity ([[Def - The Tangent Space]], [[Def - Velocity of a Curve]], [[Def - The Differential of a Smooth Map]]).
- [[Ex - The Differential of a Diffeomorphism is an Isomorphism]] (⭐) — Diffeomorphisms induce isomorphisms of tangent spaces; combined with the dimension theorem, this gives a clean proof that diffeomorphic manifolds have the same dimension ([[Def - Diffeomorphism]], [[Thm - Chain Rule for the Differential]], [[Thm - Dimension of the Tangent Space]]).
- [[Ex - Tangent Vectors as Velocities of Coordinate Curves]] (⭐) — The coordinate basis at any point arises as velocities of literal curves moving in the coordinate directions; the grounding fact that every tangent vector is somebody's velocity ([[Def - Coordinate Tangent Vectors]], [[Def - Velocity of a Curve]]).
