## §11.3 The Exponential Map and Closed Subgroups — Exercises

This section drills the [[Def - Exponential Map of a Lie Group|exponential map]] $\exp : \mathfrak{g} \to G$ — its definition via one-parameter subgroups, its identification with the matrix exponential for matrix Lie groups, its naturality under Lie group homomorphisms, and its use in identifying closed subgroups via [[Thm - The Closed Subgroup Theorem|Cartan's closed subgroup theorem]]. The exercises develop the computational fluency needed to use $\exp$ as a bridge between the linear-algebraic Lie algebra and the geometric Lie group. The most reusable technique drilled is: identify the exponential map of a matrix group with the matrix exponential and use it to compute concrete one-parameter subgroups.

- [[Ex - The Exponential Map of GL(n,R) is the Matrix Exponential]] (⭐⭐) — identifies $\exp_{\mathrm{GL}}(A) = e^A$ via integral curves of left-invariant vector fields, the foundational case for all matrix Lie groups ([[Def - Exponential Map of a Lie Group]], [[Def - One-Parameter Subgroup]], [[Def - Left-Invariant Vector Field]])
- [[Ex - SU(2) is Diffeomorphic to S^3]] (⭐⭐) — quaternion parametrization, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as traceless skew-Hermitian, the adjoint double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ ([[Def - Lie Group]], [[Def - The Lie Algebra of a Lie Group]], [[Def - Adjoint Representation]])
- [[Ex - The Lie Algebra of SO(3) is Antisymmetric Matrices]] (⭐⭐) — computing $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ by differentiating $A^T A = I$ at the identity; preview of $\exp$ as the matrix exponential via Rodrigues' formula ([[Def - The Lie Algebra of a Lie Group]], [[Thm - The Closed Subgroup Theorem]])
