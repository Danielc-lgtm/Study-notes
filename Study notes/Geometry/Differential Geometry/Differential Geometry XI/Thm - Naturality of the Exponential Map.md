# Notation

$G$ and $H$ are Lie groups with Lie algebras $\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{h}$. The exponential maps are $\exp_G : \mathfrak{g} \to G$ and $\exp_H : \mathfrak{h} \to H$. A Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$ has differential $F_* = dF_e : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$. See [[Differential Geometry XI — Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and the Exponential Map]] for the full notation registry.

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (Naturality of $\exp$).** Let $F : G \to H$ be a Lie group homomorphism. The diagram
>
> $$
> \begin{array}{ccc}
> \mathfrak{g} & \xrightarrow{F_*} & \mathfrak{h} \\
> \exp_G \downarrow & & \downarrow \exp_H \\
> G & \xrightarrow{F} & H
> \end{array}
> $$
>
> commutes: $F \circ \exp_G = \exp_H \circ F_*$. Equivalently, $F(\exp_G(X)) = \exp_H(F_* X)$ for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}$.

> **Corollary.** A Lie group homomorphism $F$ from a connected $G$ is determined by its differential $F_*$ at the identity.

---

# Motivation

The exponential map is the bridge between a Lie group and its Lie algebra. The Lie functor sends a Lie group $G$ to its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ and a Lie group homomorphism $F$ to its differential $F_* = dF_e$. The natural question is: are these two functors — Lie group homomorphism $F$, and Lie algebra homomorphism $F_*$ — compatible with the exponential maps on each side? The answer, yes, is the **naturality** of $\exp$: in the language of category theory, $\exp$ is a **natural transformation** from the identity functor (regarded as taking $G \to G$ via the natural inclusion of the connected component of $e$) to itself, with $\exp$ at each $G$ landing in $G$.

In practice this means: to compute $F$ at a point of $G$ near $e$, write the point as $\exp_G(X)$ for $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ (possible because $\exp_G$ is a local diffeomorphism near $0$), apply $F_*$ to $X$, and exponentiate. The diagram square reduces the computation of $F$ to the computation of $F_*$ — a linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces.

The corollary "a connected Lie group homomorphism is determined by its differential" is the operational form: $F$ on the connected component of $e_G$ is completely captured by $F_*$, because the connected component is generated by the image of $\exp_G$ (Lee Prop 7.14), and $F$ on $\exp_G(\mathfrak{g})$ is determined by $F_*$ via the naturality square. This is the rigidity statement that makes Lie group homomorphisms tractable: they are not arbitrary smooth maps but linear data at the identity, extended by the group multiplication via exponentials.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The hypothesis is a Lie group homomorphism. The non-obvious sources are situations where one has either $F$ or $F_*$ and wants to compute the other.

The first source is **a linear map $\varphi : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$ given as a "candidate" $F_*$**. Property $B$ is "we have a Lie algebra homomorphism $\varphi$". The bridge from $\varphi$ to a Lie group homomorphism is naturality run backwards: define $F(\exp X) := \exp(\varphi(X))$ near $e_G$, and check this extends to a global Lie group homomorphism when $G$ is connected and simply connected. This is the integration direction of the Lie correspondence (Lee Thm 20.19). The simple-connectedness is essential: without it, $F$ might be multi-valued because $\exp$ is not surjective and identifications mod the fundamental group can disagree.

A second source is **a Lie group homomorphism $F$ whose value at specific elements is known**. Property $B$ is "we know $F(g_1), \ldots, F(g_k)$ for specific group elements". The bridge: if $g_i = \exp(X_i)$, then $F(g_i) = \exp(F_* X_i)$; this constrains $F_* X_i = \log(F(g_i))$ (using the local inverse of $\exp$), giving us $F_*$ on the linear span of the $X_i$. By linearity (if the span is large enough) we recover all of $F_*$.

A third source is **the conjugation action of $G$ on itself**. Property $B$ is "we want to know how $G$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}$ via $\mathrm{Ad}$". The bridge: $\mathrm{Ad}_g(X) = \log(g \exp(X) g^{-1})$ near $0$, and by naturality $g \exp(X) g^{-1} = \exp(\mathrm{Ad}_g X)$. So $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ is computed by conjugating exponentials in $G$ and reading off the result in $\mathfrak{g}$.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The conclusion is the commutativity of the naturality square. Combined with further structure, this conclusion amplifies.

The first amplification is **rigidity of Lie group homomorphisms** (the corollary): on connected $G$, $F$ is determined by $F_*$. Combined with the integration direction, this gives the bijection {Lie group homomorphisms $G \to H$} $\leftrightarrow$ {Lie algebra homomorphisms $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$} on the simply-connected side, the Lie correspondence at the level of morphisms.

A second amplification is **$\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp X} = \exp(\mathrm{ad}_X)$**: applying naturality to $\mathrm{Ad} : G \to \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$ gives this fundamental identity. The exponential of an operator (matrix exponential, for matrix Lie groups) on $\mathfrak{g}$ equals the conjugation operator $\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp X}$. This is Lee Thm 20.27 in operational form, and it is essential for the proof of the closed subgroup theorem.

A third amplification is **constraint on covering maps**. For a covering homomorphism $F : G \to H$ with $F_*$ an isomorphism, naturality gives $F \circ \exp_G = \exp_H \circ F_*$, and since $F_*$ is invertible, $F$ is determined locally — globally on connected components by extension. This is how the double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ is shown to be a covering map: $F_*$ is a Lie algebra isomorphism $\mathfrak{su}(2) \cong \mathfrak{so}(3)$, so $F$ is a local diffeomorphism by naturality + inverse function theorem.

A fourth amplification is **lifting to universal covers**. Given a Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$ with $G$ not simply connected, naturality says $F$ lifts uniquely to a homomorphism $\widetilde F : \widetilde G \to H$ from the universal cover with the same differential. This is the mechanism by which non-simply-connected obstructions are resolved.

# Why Is It True

The proof is a one-line application of the definitions. The exponential map sends $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ to $\gamma_X(1)$, where $\gamma_X : \mathbb{R} \to G$ is the unique one-parameter subgroup with $\gamma_X'(0) = X$. The composite $F \circ \gamma_X : \mathbb{R} \to H$ is then a Lie group homomorphism (composition of two), so it is itself a one-parameter subgroup of $H$. Its initial velocity is $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0) = dF_e(\gamma_X'(0)) = F_* X$. So $F \circ \gamma_X = \gamma_{F_* X}$ (one-parameter subgroups are determined by their initial velocity). Evaluating at $t = 1$:

$$F(\exp_G X) = F(\gamma_X(1)) = \gamma_{F_* X}(1) = \exp_H(F_* X).$$

**The bolded mechanism summary: the composition of a Lie group homomorphism with a one-parameter subgroup is again a one-parameter subgroup, with initial velocity transformed by the differential — and the exponential map is just the one-parameter subgroup evaluated at $t = 1$.**

The corollary follows from Lee Prop 7.14: the image of $\exp_G$ generates the connected component of $e$ in $G$. So if $F_*$ is known, $F$ is known on $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ (by naturality), hence on the connected component of $e$ (by multiplicativity).

---

# What Makes This Hard

The proof is short, but the *interpretation* is subtle. Three things to keep straight:

1. **$F_*$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism, not just a linear map.** This is the content of the previous theorem ([[Thm - Lie Group Homomorphism Induces Lie Algebra Homomorphism]]). Without it, the diagram square is not "natural" in the right sense.
2. **The corollary "F determined by $F_*$" applies to the connected component**, not all of $G$. If $G$ has multiple components, $F$ on components other than the identity component is *not* determined by $F_*$ — it can be different on each component subject to the constraint that $F$ restricted to each component must be smooth and the composition with $F$ on the identity component must be consistent.
3. **The integration direction (Lie correspondence) requires $G$ simply connected**, not just connected. The double cover $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ illustrates: $F_*$ is the same Lie algebra isomorphism for both directions, but only the $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ direction is a Lie group homomorphism — the reverse fails because $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is not simply connected.

This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.

**High-level strategy:**
Compose $F$ with the one-parameter subgroup $\gamma_X$ to get a one-parameter subgroup of $H$. Identify it via its initial velocity, which is $F_* X$. Evaluate at $t = 1$.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **$F \circ \gamma_X$ is a one-parameter subgroup of $H$.** Show the composite is a smooth homomorphism $\mathbb{R} \to H$.
   - *Hint:* Composition of two Lie group homomorphisms ($F$ and $\gamma_X$) is a Lie group homomorphism.
   - *Why needed:* It identifies the composite as a one-parameter subgroup, allowing us to use the bijection with $\mathfrak{h}$.

2. **Initial velocity of $F \circ \gamma_X$.** Compute $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0)$.
   - *Hint:* Chain rule: $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0) = dF_e \circ \gamma_X'(0) = F_*(X)$.
   - *Why needed:* It identifies the specific one-parameter subgroup of $H$, namely $\gamma_{F_* X}$.

3. **Evaluate at $t = 1$.** Conclude $F(\exp_G X) = F(\gamma_X(1)) = \gamma_{F_* X}(1) = \exp_H(F_* X)$.
   - *Why needed:* This is the statement.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Composition of Lie group homomorphisms is a Lie group homomorphism
> **Statement:** If $F : G \to H$ and $G' : K \to G$ are Lie group homomorphisms, so is $F \circ G' : K \to H$.
>
> **Hint:** Composition of smooth maps is smooth; composition of group homomorphisms is a group homomorphism.
>
> **Why needed:** It identifies $F \circ \gamma_X$ as a homomorphism $\mathbb{R} \to H$, hence a one-parameter subgroup of $H$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > $F \circ G'$ is smooth as the composition of two smooth maps. For $k_1, k_2 \in K$: $(F \circ G')(k_1 k_2) = F(G'(k_1) G'(k_2)) = F(G'(k_1)) F(G'(k_2)) = (F \circ G')(k_1)(F \circ G')(k_2)$, using the homomorphism properties of $G'$ and $F$ in turn.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: Initial velocity of $F \circ \gamma_X$
> **Statement:** For $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ and a Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$, $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0) = F_*(X)$.
>
> **Hint:** Chain rule at $t = 0$.
>
> **Why needed:** It identifies the one-parameter subgroup $F \circ \gamma_X$ of $H$ as the one with initial velocity $F_* X$, hence equal to $\gamma_{F_* X}$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0) = dF_{\gamma_X(0)} \circ \gamma_X'(0) = dF_{e_G}(X) = F_*(X)$, where $\gamma_X(0) = e_G$.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Let $F : G \to H$ be a Lie group homomorphism, $X \in \mathfrak{g}$, and let $\gamma_X : \mathbb{R} \to G$ be the unique one-parameter subgroup with $\gamma_X'(0) = X$.
>
> **Step 1.** $F \circ \gamma_X : \mathbb{R} \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism (Lemma 1), hence a one-parameter subgroup of $H$.
>
> **Step 2.** Its initial velocity is $(F \circ \gamma_X)'(0) = F_*(X)$ (Lemma 2).
>
> **Step 3.** By uniqueness of one-parameter subgroups, $F \circ \gamma_X = \gamma_{F_* X}$, the one-parameter subgroup of $H$ with initial velocity $F_*(X)$.
>
> **Step 4.** Evaluate at $t = 1$: $F(\exp_G X) = F(\gamma_X(1)) = \gamma_{F_* X}(1) = \exp_H(F_* X)$.
>
> Hence $F \circ \exp_G = \exp_H \circ F_*$. $\qquad\blacksquare$
>
> **Proof of corollary.** Let $G$ be connected. The image of $\exp_G$ generates the connected component of $e$ (which is all of $G$, by connectedness) — see Lee Prop 7.14. By the main theorem, $F$ on $\exp_G(\mathfrak{g})$ is determined by $F_*$. By multiplicativity of $F$ ($F(g_1 g_2) = F(g_1) F(g_2)$), $F$ on finite products of exponentials is determined. Hence $F$ on all of $G$ is determined.

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Quantum mechanics — time evolution determined by the Hamiltonian.** In QM, the time-evolution operator $U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ is a one-parameter subgroup of the unitary group on Hilbert space, generated by the Hamiltonian $H \in i\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{H})$. The naturality of $\exp$ says that to compute the time evolution of an observable $A$, we differentiate: $\dot A = (i/\hbar)[H, A]$, the Heisenberg equation, which is exactly the action of $\mathrm{ad}_{iH/\hbar}$ on $A$, related to $U(t) A U(t)^{-1}$ via $\mathrm{Ad}_{U(t)} A = e^{\mathrm{ad}_{iHt/\hbar}}(A)$. Naturality of $\exp$ applied to the adjoint representation gives the bridge.

**Algebraic topology — covering maps via $F_*$ isomorphism.** For a Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$ with $G$ connected and $F_*$ a Lie algebra isomorphism: naturality says $F$ is locally a diffeomorphism (since $\exp$ is a local diffeomorphism on both sides and $F_*$ is an isomorphism). So $F$ is an open map; combined with constant rank, $F$ is a covering map onto its image. The canonical example: $\mathrm{SU}(2) \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ has $F_*$ an isomorphism, hence is a covering, with discrete kernel $\{\pm I\}$.

**Differential geometry — geodesics on compact Lie groups.** On a compact Lie group with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric, the Riemannian exponential map coincides with the Lie-group exponential map. Naturality of $\exp$ then says: an isometry $F : G \to G$ that fixes $e$ acts on geodesics through $e$ via its differential at $e$, exactly the way it should for an isometry. This unifies the differential-geometric and Lie-group-theoretic versions of $\exp$.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Lie Group Homomorphism Induces Lie Algebra Homomorphism|Lie group homomorphism ⟹ Lie algebra homomorphism]]** — that theorem says $F_*$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism (it preserves the bracket). Naturality says additionally that $F_*$ and $F$ are compatible with the exponential maps on both sides. Together they make $G \mapsto \mathfrak{g}$ a functor $\mathbf{LieGrp} \to \mathbf{LieAlg}$ and $\exp$ a natural transformation between identity functors on the connected-component subcategory.

- **The Lie correspondence on the simply-connected side** (Lee Thm 20.19) — the converse to naturality: every Lie algebra homomorphism $\varphi : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$ with $G$ simply connected integrates uniquely to a Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$ with $F_* = \varphi$. The proof uses the fundamental theorem on Lie algebra actions (Lee Thm 20.16) and the formula $F(g) = \text{(orbit of $e_H$ under the $G$-action generated by $\varphi$)}$. So integration is the inverse of "differentiation at $e$".

- **The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula** — naturality of $\exp$ extends to a "second-order" statement: $\log(\exp X \exp Y) = X + Y + \tfrac{1}{2}[X, Y] + \cdots$. The first-order naturality says $F(\exp X) = \exp(F_* X)$; the BCH formula encodes how the bracket on $\mathfrak{g}$ relates to non-commutativity of products of exponentials. Together they give: $G$ is fully reconstructed from $\mathfrak{g}$ (with bracket) via the exponential on small neighbourhoods, with BCH supplying the multiplication law in algebra coordinates.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] $\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp X} = \exp(\mathrm{ad}_X)$ *(from this chapter)*
> Applying naturality to the adjoint representation $\mathrm{Ad} : G \to \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$ with differential $\mathrm{ad} : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})$ gives the fundamental identity $\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp X} = \exp(\mathrm{ad}_X)$. This is Lee Thm 20.27 and is the operational form connecting the Lie group adjoint to the Lie algebra adjoint via the matrix exponential.

> [!tip] Rigidity of Lie Group Homomorphisms *(from this chapter)*
> A Lie group homomorphism from a connected Lie group is determined by its differential at the identity. This is the key rigidity statement of Lie theory: smooth maps respecting the group structure are determined by linear data at one point.

> [!tip] Lie Correspondence (integration direction) *(from Lie Groups, Advanced)*
> Every Lie algebra homomorphism $\varphi : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$ with $G$ simply connected lifts uniquely to a Lie group homomorphism $F : G \to H$ with $F_* = \varphi$ (Lee Thm 20.19). Naturality of $\exp$ is what gives the construction: define $F$ on $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ by $F(\exp X) = \exp(\varphi(X))$, and extend by simply-connectedness.

> [!tip] Universal Covering of a Lie Group *(from Lie Groups, Advanced)*
> Every connected Lie group $G$ has a simply connected universal covering Lie group $\widetilde G$ with a covering homomorphism $\pi : \widetilde G \to G$, $\mathrm{Lie}(\widetilde G) = \mathfrak{g}$. Lie group homomorphisms out of $\widetilde G$ are classified by Lie algebra homomorphisms out of $\mathfrak{g}$ (by the Lie correspondence + naturality). Connected Lie groups with the same Lie algebra are quotients of $\widetilde G$ by discrete central subgroups.
