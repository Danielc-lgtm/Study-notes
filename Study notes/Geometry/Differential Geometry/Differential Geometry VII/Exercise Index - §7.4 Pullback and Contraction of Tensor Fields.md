## §7.4 Pullback and Contraction of Tensor Fields — Exercises

This section drills the two operations on tensor fields that are used most often in the rest of the curriculum: **pullback** (transporting covariant tensor fields back along a smooth map) and **contraction** (collapsing one upper index against one lower index). Pullback is the engine of every coordinate calculation and of integration on manifolds; contraction is the engine of every scalar invariant (trace, scalar curvature, divergence). The exercises here focus on the *substitute-and-expand* recipe for pullback in coordinates and on the basis-invariance of contraction.

- [[Ex - Computing the Pullback of a Tensor Field in Coordinates]] (⭐⭐) — apply the naturality identities and chain rule to compute the pullback of a $(0, 2)$-tensor under the polar-to-Cartesian map; the recipe scales to any rank and any smooth map ([[Def - Pullback of a Covariant Tensor Field]], [[Thm - Pullback Commutes with Tensor Product]]).

- [[Ex - The Metric Tensor in Polar Coordinates]] (⭐⭐) — the canonical worked pullback computation, producing the line element $ds^2 = dr^2 + r^2 d\theta^2$; demonstrates the orthogonality of polar coordinates via cancellation of cross-terms ([[Def - Pullback of a Covariant Tensor Field]], [[Thm - Pullback Commutes with Tensor Product]], [[Thm - Transformation Rule for Tensor Components]]).

- [[Ex - The Kronecker Delta as a Mixed Tensor]] (⭐) — relevant for contraction: the trace $\delta^i_i = n$ is the simplest nontrivial scalar contraction, and the basis-invariance of the components of $\delta$ is the prototype for tensor-invariance ([[Def - Contraction of a Tensor]], [[Def - Mixed Tensor]]).
