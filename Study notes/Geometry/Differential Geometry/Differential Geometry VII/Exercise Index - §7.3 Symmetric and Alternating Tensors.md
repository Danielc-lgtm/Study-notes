## §7.3 Symmetric and Alternating Tensors — Exercises

This section drills the two symmetry classes that bifurcate the rest of differential geometry: symmetric tensor fields (the home of metrics and quadratic forms) and alternating tensor fields (the home of differential forms and integration). The exercises focus on **recognizing the symmetry class** of a tensor field, **decomposing a tensor into symmetric and antisymmetric parts** (when the decomposition exists), and **using physical conservation laws to derive symmetry properties** (the stress tensor's symmetry from angular momentum conservation). The failure of the symmetric-plus-antisymmetric decomposition for $k \geq 3$ is exhibited via a concrete counterexample, demonstrating that the $k = 2$ case is special and does not generalize.

- [[Ex - Decomposing a 2-Tensor into Symmetric and Antisymmetric Parts]] (⭐) — apply the symmetrization and alternation projectors to decompose any covariant 2-tensor; verify the projector identities; exhibit the counterexample for $k = 3$ ([[Def - Symmetric Tensor Field]], [[Def - Alternating Tensor Field]], [[Thm - Symmetrization and Alternation Projectors]]).

- [[Ex - The Stress Tensor as a Symmetric 2-Tensor]] (⭐⭐) — recognize the Cauchy stress tensor as a $(1, 1)$-tensor; derive its symmetry from angular momentum conservation; apply the spectral theorem for principal stresses and orthogonal principal axes ([[Def - Symmetric Tensor Field]], [[Def - Mixed Tensor]]).

- [[Ex - The Kronecker Delta as a Mixed Tensor]] (⭐) — also relevant: the Kronecker delta is symmetric in a trivial sense ($\delta^i_j = \delta_{ij}$ is a symmetric matrix), illustrating how the symmetry class of a tensor can be assessed from its components ([[Def - Mixed Tensor]], [[Def - Symmetric Tensor Field]]).
