## §1.3 The Dirac Equation in Minkowski Space — Exercises

This section drills the algebraic and analytic mechanics of the Dirac equation on flat Minkowski space. The unifying technique is the **squaring identity** $\not\partial^2 = \Box$ derived from the Clifford relation $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}I$: this converts the first-order Dirac equation into the second-order Klein-Gordon equation, automatically giving the relativistic dispersion $E^2 = \vec p^2 + m^2$. The signature pattern: every computation in flat-space relativistic quantum mechanics reduces to manipulations of $4 \times 4$ gamma matrices and the algebraic structure of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C}) = \mathrm{Spin}^+(1, 3)$ acting on Dirac spinors.

- [[Ex - Dirac Matrices in the Weyl Representation]] (⭐) — explicit verification of the Clifford relation $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}I$ for the Weyl-representation gamma matrices, with block-matrix computations of $\gamma^5$ as the chirality projector ([[Def - Dirac Gamma Matrices]], [[Def - The Pauli Matrices]]).
- [[Ex - The Square of a Dirac Spinor Gives a 4-Current]] (⭐⭐) — derivation of the conserved Dirac probability current $j^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$, with verification of Lorentz covariance via the spinor representation $\rho(A) = \mathrm{diag}(A, (A^\dagger)^{-1})$ and conservation via the conjugate Dirac equation ([[Def - The Dirac Equation]], [[Def - Dirac Gamma Matrices]]).
- [[Ex - Plane-Wave Solutions of the Free Dirac Equation]] (⭐⭐) — construction of plane-wave solutions, derivation of the on-shell condition $p^2 = -m^2$, and counting the two spin states $\times$ (particle/antiparticle) decomposition $4 = 2 \times 2$ of the Dirac spinor module ([[Def - The Dirac Equation]], [[Thm - Dirac Equation Squares to Klein-Gordon]], [[Def - Weyl Spinor]]).
