# Problem Statement

Let $\Sigma_g$ be a closed orientable surface of genus $g \geq 0$ (a sphere with $g$ handles). Compute the Betti numbers $b_0, b_1, b_2$ of $\Sigma_g$ and verify the formula $\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$ in two independent ways:

(i) From the Betti numbers, via $\chi = b_0 - b_1 + b_2$.

(ii) From the standard polygon-triangulation: present $\Sigma_g$ as a $4g$-gon with edge identifications $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1}$ (the "canonical" word for $\Sigma_g$), triangulate, and compute $V - E + F$ from the resulting cell counts.

**Recall:**

A closed orientable surface of genus $g$ is the connected sum of $g$ tori: $\Sigma_g = T^2 \# T^2 \# \cdots \# T^2$ ($g$ copies); equivalently, the unique closed orientable $2$-manifold whose fundamental polygon is the $4g$-gon with the identification word above. For $g = 0$, $\Sigma_0 = S^2$.

Singular homology of $\Sigma_g$: $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ (connected), $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$ (generated by $2g$ independent loops $a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g$), $H_2 = \mathbb{Z}$ (orientable closed surface, generated by the fundamental class $[\Sigma_g]$).

[[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|The alternating-Betti identity]]: $\chi(\Sigma_g) = \sum (-1)^p \dim H_p(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{R}) = b_0 - b_1 + b_2$. Also $\chi = \sum (-1)^p c_p$ for any triangulation with $c_p$ cells in dimension $p$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** This is a verification exercise: confirm a numerical formula ($\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$) by two independent methods. The class is "cross-check via two computations of a topological invariant" — a standard technique for catching arithmetic errors or misidentifications, and a sanity check on the [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|topological invariance of the alternating cell count]].

**Assumption pattern:** We have a closed orientable surface $\Sigma_g$, presented either by its homology (with known $H_*$) or by its $4g$-gon fundamental polygon (with explicit identification pattern). Both presentations give natural ways to compute $\chi$, and the two must agree by topological invariance.

**Theorem routing:** (i) From homology: directly substitute $b_0 = 1$, $b_1 = 2g$, $b_2 = 1$ into $\chi = b_0 - b_1 + b_2 = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$. (ii) From triangulation: triangulate the $4g$-gon (the simplest triangulation has $2$ vertices, $4g + 1$ edges, and $4g - 1$ triangles — no wait, recheck this; alternative: use the polygon's standard triangulation by drawing diagonals from a single vertex). Then count $V$, $E$, $F$ after the edge identifications and verify $V - E + F = 2 - 2g$.

**Key decision point:** The non-obvious step is the cell count for the $4g$-gon triangulation. The naïve count gives $4g$ boundary edges + diagonals, $4g$ + corresponding vertices. After identifications, multiple boundary vertices may collapse to a single point, and identified edges become single edges. Careful counting (or alternative use of cellular homology with the $4g$-gon as a $2$-cell) gives the right answer $V = 1, E = 2g, F = 1$ (in the minimal CW structure), confirming $\chi = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Read off Betti numbers from the known homology** (operation 9 from the topic page, in the form "use known answers"). $b_0 = 1$ (connected), $b_1 = 2g$ ($2g$ loops in the canonical generators), $b_2 = 1$ (orientable closed surface).

2. **Compute $\chi$ as alternating Betti sum** (analogous to operation 10 from the topic page). $\chi = b_0 - b_1 + b_2 = 2 - 2g$.

3. **Triangulate and count cells** (operation 11). Use the polygon presentation to count vertices, edges, faces; verify $V - E + F = 2 - 2g$ by the [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|alternating-cell-count identity]].

4. **Use the CW structure for efficient counting** (operation 11 again, in CW form). The minimal CW structure of $\Sigma_g$ has $1$ vertex, $2g$ edges (the loop generators), and $1$ $2$-cell (the polygon interior), giving $\chi = 1 - 2g + 1$.

> Method (i) is direct. The Betti numbers of $\Sigma_g$ are $(b_0, b_1, b_2) = (1, 2g, 1)$. The first comes from connectedness, the second from the $2g$ generators of the fundamental group (and hence of $H_1$ by abelianisation), and the third from the existence of a fundamental class $[\Sigma_g]$ (orientability). Compute $\chi = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Method (ii) uses the CW structure. $\Sigma_g$ has a minimal CW structure with:
> - $1$ vertex $v$ (a single point, the common starting/ending point of all the loops)
> - $2g$ $1$-cells (the loops $a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g$)
> - $1$ $2$-cell (the interior of the $4g$-gon, attached via the canonical word $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots$)
>
> Count $\chi = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$. ✓

> [!note]- Hint 3
> Refine to a simplicial triangulation: subdivide the $4g$-gon into triangles by drawing $4g - 3$ diagonals from a single vertex (giving $4g - 2$ triangles). Add the interior triangulation vertices and edges. After accounting for identifications on the boundary, the resulting simplicial complex has more vertices, edges, and faces, but the alternating sum $V - E + F$ is still $2 - 2g$ by [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|topological invariance]].

# Solution

The proof has three steps. Step 1 records the Betti numbers of $\Sigma_g$ and computes $\chi$ from them. Step 2 sets up the minimal CW structure of $\Sigma_g$ and computes $V - E + F$. Step 3 verifies the agreement and discusses simplicial refinement.

**Step 1: Betti numbers and $\chi$ from homology.**

The Betti numbers of $\Sigma_g$ are:
$$
b_0(\Sigma_g) = 1, \qquad b_1(\Sigma_g) = 2g, \qquad b_2(\Sigma_g) = 1.
$$

Compute:
$$
\chi(\Sigma_g) = b_0 - b_1 + b_2 = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g.
$$

> [!note]- Derivation
> *$b_0 = 1$:* $\Sigma_g$ is path-connected.
>
> *$b_1 = 2g$:* The fundamental group of $\Sigma_g$ has the presentation $\pi_1(\Sigma_g) = \langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g \mid a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1} \rangle$ (the canonical relation comes from the polygon identification). Abelianising this: the relation $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots = 1$ becomes $a_1 - a_1 + b_1 - b_1 + \cdots = 0$ in the abelianisation, which is trivially satisfied. So $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the free abelian group on $a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g$, i.e. $\mathbb{Z}^{2g}$. By Hurewicz, $H_1(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$, so $b_1 = 2g$.
>
> *$b_2 = 1$:* $\Sigma_g$ is a closed orientable $2$-manifold, so $H_2(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the fundamental class $[\Sigma_g]$. Hence $b_2 = 1$.
>
> *$\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$:* Direct substitution.

**Step 2: Cell count from the minimal CW structure.**

The minimal CW structure of $\Sigma_g$ has:
- **$V = 1$:** the single vertex $v$ where all loops start and end.
- **$E = 2g$:** the $2g$ edges $a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g$, each a loop starting and ending at $v$.
- **$F = 1$:** the single $2$-cell, attached via the canonical word $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1}$.

Count: $V - E + F = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Vertex count:* in the polygon presentation, all $4g$ corners of the $4g$-gon are identified to a single point — because each pair of adjacent identified edges meets at a corner, and the identification forces the corners to coincide. So after identification there is one vertex $v$.
>
> *Edge count:* the $4g$ sides of the polygon are identified in pairs (each $a_i$ identified with the corresponding $a_i^{-1}$, and each $b_i$ with $b_i^{-1}$). So there are $4g/2 = 2g$ distinct edges after identification.
>
> *Face count:* there is a single $2$-cell — the polygon's interior — attached to the $1$-skeleton via the boundary word.
>
> $V - E + F = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g$. ✓
>
> This is the minimal CW structure of $\Sigma_g$; any other (CW or simplicial) structure has more cells but the same alternating sum.

**Step 3: Verify agreement and discuss simplicial refinement.**

Both methods give $\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$, agreeing as required by [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|the topological invariance of the alternating cell count]].

For a simplicial refinement: triangulate the $4g$-gon by drawing diagonals from one corner to all non-adjacent corners. This produces $4g - 2$ triangles. After accounting for identifications:
- Vertices: $1$ (the single identification point) + interior vertices (none with this triangulation since the diagonals all meet at the corner). So $V_{\mathrm{simp}} = 1$ ... actually we need to be careful: the diagonals meet only at the chosen corner, but they end at the other corners (which are also the single identification point), so they are all loops at the same vertex. Total $V_{\mathrm{simp}} = 1$.
- Edges: $2g$ (boundary edges, after identification) + $4g - 3$ (diagonals, not identified with each other since they are interior). Total $E_{\mathrm{simp}} = 2g + 4g - 3 = 6g - 3$.
- Faces: $4g - 2$ triangles, all distinct after identification.

Check: $V_{\mathrm{simp}} - E_{\mathrm{simp}} + F_{\mathrm{simp}} = 1 - (6g - 3) + (4g - 2) = 1 - 6g + 3 + 4g - 2 = 2 - 2g$. ✓

> [!note]- Derivation
> The simplicial refinement increases the cell count: from $1 + 2g + 1 = 2g + 2$ cells to $1 + (6g - 3) + (4g - 2) = 10g - 4$ cells. But the alternating sum is unchanged: $2 - 2g$ either way. This is the topological invariance of $\chi$ in action — different cell structures, same $\chi$.
>
> More general triangulations (with additional interior vertices and edges) also give $\chi = 2 - 2g$. For instance, the standard $T^2$ triangulation (Frankel Figure 13.12, $18$ triangles, $9$ vertices, $27$ edges after identifications) gives $9 - 27 + 18 = 0 = 2 - 2 \cdot 1$, the genus-$1$ case.
>
> The key insight: the alternating cell count $\sum (-1)^p c_p$ depends only on the topological space, not the triangulation. This is the content of [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers]], which equates the alternating cell count to the alternating Betti sum.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Theorem.** For the closed orientable surface $\Sigma_g$ of genus $g \geq 0$, $\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$.
>
> *Proof.*
>
> *Method (i) — from homology:* $H_0(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}$ ($\Sigma_g$ connected), $H_1(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}^{2g}$ (canonical generators), $H_2(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}$ (closed orientable surface, fundamental class). Compute
> $$
> \chi(\Sigma_g) = b_0 - b_1 + b_2 = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g.
> $$
>
> *Method (ii) — from triangulation:* present $\Sigma_g$ as a $4g$-gon with edge identifications $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1}$. The minimal CW structure has $V = 1$ vertex (all corners identified), $E = 2g$ edges (the $2g$ loop generators), $F = 1$ face (the polygon interior). Compute
> $$
> \chi(\Sigma_g) = V - E + F = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g.
> $$
>
> Both methods agree, as required by [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers]]. $\qquad\blacksquare$
>
> *Corollary:* For $g = 0$ ($\Sigma_0 = S^2$), $\chi = 2$. For $g = 1$ ($\Sigma_1 = T^2$), $\chi = 0$. For $g \geq 2$, $\chi$ is a negative even integer decreasing as $-2(g - 1)$.

# Key Takeaways

**The Euler characteristic is a topological invariant captured by alternating cell counts.** The formula $\chi(\Sigma_g) = V - E + F$ for any triangulation of $\Sigma_g$ is independent of the triangulation chosen — this is the content of [[Thm - Euler Characteristic via Alternating Betti Numbers|the alternating-cell-count identity]]. Different triangulations have different individual counts $V, E, F$ but the same alternating sum. For $\Sigma_g$ specifically, the alternating sum is always $2 - 2g$, recovering Euler's $V - E + F = 2$ for the sphere ($g = 0$). This is the deepest content of Euler's polyhedral formula: not just a numerical fact about specific polyhedra, but a topological invariant of the underlying $2$-sphere.

**The genus $g$ counts handles, and Euler characteristic measures handle count.** $\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$ says: adding one handle (going from genus $g$ to genus $g + 1$) decreases $\chi$ by $2$. Geometrically, attaching a handle to a surface replaces two disks with a cylinder: this removes two $2$-cells, adds two $1$-cells, and identifies four vertices, with the net effect on $\chi$ being $-2 + 0 + 0 = -2$ (approximately — exact bookkeeping depends on the triangulation, but the alternating-sum effect is $-2$). So Euler characteristic provides a complete topological classification of closed orientable surfaces: two are homeomorphic iff they have the same $\chi$, equivalently iff they have the same genus.

**Hurewicz theorem connects $H_1$ to $\pi_1$.** $H_1(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$ comes from the abelianisation of $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$, which has the presentation $\langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g \mid a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots \rangle$. The relation, written multiplicatively, abelianises to $a_1 + b_1 - a_1 - b_1 + \cdots = 0$, which is trivially satisfied. So the abelianisation is the free abelian group on $2g$ generators, giving $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$. The Hurewicz theorem $H_1(X; \mathbb{Z}) = \pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the bridge between topology (homotopy groups) and algebra (homology groups) in degree $1$. See [[Algebraic Topology II — Fundamental Group and Covering Spaces]] for the full story.

**Multiple equivalent descriptions yield identical $\chi$.** The minimal CW structure ($1$-vertex, $2g$-edges, $1$-face) and any simplicial refinement give the same $\chi$, by the topological invariance of the alternating cell count. This is a robustness property: $\chi$ is the right quantity to compute because it does not depend on the specific representation. The same principle underlies:
- Cohomological computations: any chain complex whose homology is $H_*(M)$ gives the same alternating-sum Euler characteristic.
- Numerical sanity checks: if you triangulate $\Sigma_g$ and get $V - E + F \neq 2 - 2g$, you have miscounted.
- Multi-method verification: computing $\chi$ from homology, from CW cells, from a triangulation, from Gauss–Bonnet ($\chi = (1/2\pi) \int K\, dA$ for a Riemannian metric), or from Morse theory (number of critical points with alternating sign) all give the same answer.

The Gauss–Bonnet route is particularly striking: $\chi(\Sigma_g) = (1/2\pi) \int_{\Sigma_g} K\, dA$ for any Riemannian metric on $\Sigma_g$. So the genus is computable from any choice of metric, by integrating the Gaussian curvature. This is the geometric realisation of the topological invariant — see [[Riemannian Geometry IV — Classical Geometry of Surfaces in R^3]].
