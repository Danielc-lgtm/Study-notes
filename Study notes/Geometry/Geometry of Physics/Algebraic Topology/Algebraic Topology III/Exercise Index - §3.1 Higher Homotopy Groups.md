## §3.1 Higher Homotopy Groups — Exercises

This section drills the foundational results of higher homotopy theory: the definition and basic computation of $\pi_k(X)$, the abelianness for $k \geq 2$ (via Eckmann–Hilton), and the use of the Hurewicz theorem to reduce the first non-trivial homotopy computation to a homology problem. The recurring techniques are the **identification of $\pi_n(S^n)$ with Brouwer degree**, the **Eckmann–Hilton slide for commutativity**, and the **bootstrap from connectivity to Hurewicz isomorphism**. Master these and you can compute the first non-trivial homotopy of any sufficiently connected CW complex.

- [[Ex - Pi_n of S^n is Z]] (⭐⭐) — Compute $\pi_n(S^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ via Hurewicz applied to the $(n-1)$-connected sphere; identify the integer with the Brouwer degree. ([[Def - Higher Homotopy Group]], [[Thm - Hurewicz Theorem (Statement)]], [[Def - Singular Homology]])

The other exercises in this section appear in §3.2 (Hopf-fibration computation of $\pi_3(S^2)$ uses the long exact sequence and is more naturally indexed there) — see [[Exercise Index - §3.2 Fibrations and the Long Exact Sequence]].
