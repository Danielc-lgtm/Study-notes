# The Organizing Question

Gauge theory studies connections modulo changes of frame and extracts geometry and topology from their curvature and solution spaces. The series follows the dependency chain
$$\text{bundles}\to\text{connections}\to\text{curvature}\to\text{field equations}\to\text{elliptic moduli problems}\to\text{four-manifold invariants}.$$

# Geometric Language

1. [[Gauge Theory I — Connections on Vector Bundles and the Electromagnetic Connection]] begins with covariant differentiation, curvature, electromagnetic holonomy, monopoles, and quantization.
2. [[Gauge Theory II — Principal Bundles, Representations, and Bundle Classification]] separates the abstract symmetry bundle from each representation and associated field bundle.
3. [[Gauge Theory III — Principal Connections, Curvature, Holonomy, and Gauge Symmetry]] develops horizontal distributions, connection forms, curvature, transport, and gauge action.
4. [[Gauge Theory IV — Chern–Weil Theory, Characteristic Classes, Chern–Simons, and Flat Moduli]] turns curvature into topology and introduces transgression and flat moduli.

# Field Theory and Analysis

5. [[Gauge Theory V — Hodge Theory, Maxwell, Yang–Mills, and Instantons]] derives field equations variationally and studies self-duality and the BPST solution.
6. [[Gauge Theory VI — Clifford Algebras, Spin Geometry, and Dirac Operators]] constructs spinors and the Dirac–Weitzenböck package.
7. [[Gauge Theory VII — Sobolev Spaces, Elliptic Operators, and Gauge-Fixing Complexes]] supplies completions, estimates, regularity, and Fredholm theory.
8. [[Gauge Theory VIII — Fredholm Maps, Transversality, Determinant Lines, and Degree]] explains finite-dimensional reduction, generic regularity, orientations, and invariant counts.

# Four-Manifold Gauge Theory

9. [[Gauge Theory IX — Seiberg–Witten Equations, Compactness, and Moduli Spaces]] constructs and analyzes monopole moduli spaces.
10. [[Gauge Theory X — Seiberg–Witten Invariants and Four-Manifold Applications]] extracts characteristic numbers and geometric obstructions.
11. [[Gauge Theory XI — Topology, Intersection Forms, and Donaldson Theory]] relates algebraic topology and intersection lattices to nonabelian instanton constraints.

# Re-entry Routes

For physical gauge fields, enter at I and V. For bundle geometry, enter at II and III. For characteristic classes and holonomy, enter at IV. For nonlinear PDE and moduli spaces, begin with VI–VIII before IX. For smooth four-manifold topology, read XI after V, VII, and VIII, then compare its Donaldson route with IX–X.
