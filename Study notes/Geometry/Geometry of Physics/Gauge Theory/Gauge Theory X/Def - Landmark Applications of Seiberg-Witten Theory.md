# Purpose

Haydys closes with a map of what the invariant can detect. The statements below are landmarks rather than consequences of the introductory analytic package alone; each requires substantial additional geometry. Recording the hypotheses and mechanisms prevents their use as black-box slogans.

# Landmark Results

**Connected-sum vanishing.** If $X_1$ and $X_2$ are closed oriented four-manifolds with $b_2^+(X_i)\ge1$, then all Seiberg–Witten invariants of $X_1\#X_2$ vanish. The proof stretches the neck and analyzes limiting monopoles; the positive directions on both summands make the relevant gluing contribution vanish.

**Taubes nonvanishing.** If $(X,\omega)$ is a closed symplectic four-manifold with $b_2^+(X)\ge2$, then the spin-c structure canonically determined by an $\omega$-compatible almost-complex structure has nonzero invariant (with a standard homology orientation, its value is $\pm1$). The proof studies the large-perturbation limit and relates monopoles to pseudoholomorphic curves.

Together with [[Thm - Positive Scalar Curvature Forces Seiberg-Witten Vanishing]], Taubes's theorem obstructs positive scalar curvature on many symplectic four-manifolds.

**Smooth-structure detection.** Seiberg–Witten invariants can distinguish smooth manifolds that are homeomorphic. Combined with four-manifold topology and surgery constructions, this contributes to examples of topological four-manifolds with no smooth structure and families with infinitely many pairwise nondiffeomorphic smooth structures.

# Scope warning

The neck-stretching, pseudoholomorphic-curve correspondence, and surgery calculations are not proved in this introductory series. They are not legal dependencies for later proofs unless their full analytic packages are added as separate topics. Here they serve as an explicit source-coverage roadmap and delimit what has, and has not, been derived.
