# Notation

For a principal $G$-bundle $\pi_P : P \to M$ and a smooth left $G$-action on $F$, the associated bundle is $E = P \times_G F$, with projection $\pi : E \to M$ sending $[u, y] \mapsto \pi_P(u)$. See [[Gauge Theory II — Fibre Bundles, Principal Bundles, and Gauss–Bonnet]] for the registry.

---

# Statement

> **Theorem.** Let $\pi_P : P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle and let $G$ act smoothly on the left of a smooth manifold $F$. Then the quotient
> $$E = P \times_G F = (P \times F)/G, \qquad (u, y) \sim (u\cdot g, \, g^{-1}y) \text{ for all } g \in G,$$
> carries a unique smooth-manifold structure such that the quotient map $P \times F \to E$ is a smooth submersion, and the projection $\pi : E \to M$, $[u, y] \mapsto \pi_P(u)$, makes $E$ into a smooth fibre bundle over $M$ with:
>
> - **typical fibre** $F$;
> - **structure group** $G$ acting on $F$ as given;
> - **transition functions** $c^E_{\alpha\beta}(p) = c^P_{\alpha\beta}(p)$ (acting on $F$ via the chosen $G$-action), where $\{c^P_{\alpha\beta}\}$ is the cocycle for $P$.
>
> The fibres are $E_p \cong F$. Sections of $E$ correspond bijectively to $G$-equivariant smooth maps $\tilde s : P \to F$ (with $\tilde s(u \cdot g) = g^{-1}\tilde s(u)$).

> **Corollary (universality).** Every fibre bundle over $M$ with structure group $G$ is isomorphic to $P \times_G F$ for some principal $G$-bundle $P$ and $G$-action on $F$. The principal $G$-bundle $P = \mathrm{Fr}_G(E)$ of "frames" is the universal source.

---

# Motivation

This theorem is the **construction theorem for the entire zoo of bundles with structure group $G$**: from a single principal $G$-bundle $P$, choose any $G$-manifold $F$ and out pops a fibre bundle $P \times_G F$. The recipe is functorial in $F$: tensor products of $F$ give tensor products of bundles, dual representations give dual bundles, restrictions give subbundles. So the entire category of bundles with structure group $G$ over a fixed $M$ is generated by a single $P$ together with the representation theory of $G$.

The theorem also justifies the "principal bundle is universal" perspective: every $G$-bundle is an associated bundle of *some* principal bundle (namely its frame bundle), and so the principal-bundle viewpoint losses nothing while gaining the intrinsic right $G$-action, the gauge group, and the natural home for connection forms.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (input broadening).**

*Source 1: A principal $G$-bundle and a representation of $G$.* The standard setup. A representation $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ on a vector space $V$ gives the associated vector bundle $P \times_\rho V$. The B → A bridge is automatic.

*Source 2: A principal $G$-bundle and a homogeneous space $G/H$ as fibre.* The associated bundle $P \times_G (G/H)$ has fibre $G/H$. The B → A bridge: $G$ acts on the homogeneous space by left translation in $G$. Example: $P \times_{\mathrm{SO}(n)} S^{n-1}$ is the unit sphere bundle of the rank-$n$ vector bundle associated to $P$.

*Source 3: A principal $G$-bundle and any smooth $G$-action on any manifold.* The most general source. Adjoint representation on $\mathfrak{g}$ gives the **adjoint bundle** $\mathrm{Ad}(P) = P \times_G \mathfrak{g}$, sections of which are infinitesimal gauge transformations. Conjugation action of $G$ on itself gives the gauge group bundle $P \times_G G$.

*Source 4: A principal bundle of frames $\mathrm{Fr}(E)$ from a vector bundle $E$.* The principal $\mathrm{GL}(k)$-bundle $\mathrm{Fr}(E)$ associated to a rank-$k$ vector bundle, plus the standard representation $\rho = \mathrm{id}$ on $\mathbb{R}^k$, recovers $E = \mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} \mathbb{R}^k$. This is the **inverse** of the frame-bundle construction. The B → A bridge: frame bundle is a principal bundle (by definition), standard representation is a $\mathrm{GL}(k)$-action on $\mathbb{R}^k$, associated bundle is automatic.

**Targets (output amplification).**

*Target 1: Construct dual / tensor / symmetric / exterior power bundles automatically.* Given a vector bundle $E$ from $\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} \mathbb{R}^k$, the dual is $\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} (\mathbb{R}^k)^*$ (contragredient action), the symmetric square is $\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} \mathrm{Sym}^2 \mathbb{R}^k$, etc. All tensor constructions are built this way.

*Target 2: Induce connections automatically.* A principal connection on $P$ — a single $\mathfrak{g}$-valued 1-form — induces covariant derivatives on *every* associated bundle simultaneously, via the equivariant-map correspondence. This is the gauge-theoretic principle: gauge fields live on the principal bundle, matter fields are sections of associated bundles, and the same gauge field couples to all matter automatically.

*Target 3: Compute sections via equivariant maps.* The theorem's claim "sections of $P \times_G F$ = $G$-equivariant maps $P \to F$" is operationally extremely useful: instead of working with sections (which require local trivializations), work with $G$-equivariant maps $P \to F$ (which are globally defined). All gauge-theoretic computations exploit this.

*Target 4: Match representations to characteristic classes.* The Chern classes of an associated vector bundle $P \times_G V$ are pulled back from the characteristic classes of the universal bundle on $BG$ via the representation $G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$. Different representations give different combinations of characteristic classes — e.g., the standard representation of $U(n)$ gives the usual Chern classes; the adjoint representation gives certain combinations of them.

# Why Is It True

The theorem is true because of two structural facts.

First, **the diagonal $G$-action on $P \times F$ is free and proper**: it is free because the right action on $P$ is free (a principal-bundle fact), and proper because $P \to M$ is proper as a fibre bundle. The quotient manifold theorem then gives a smooth manifold structure on $E = (P \times F)/G$ with the quotient map a submersion.

Second, **the local triviality of $P$ pushes down to local triviality of $E$**. In a trivialization $\Phi : P|_U \cong U \times G$, the associated quotient is $E|_U = (U \times G \times F)/G$, where $G$ acts on $(g, y)$ by $(g, y) \cdot h = (gh, h^{-1}y)$. The orbit of $(g, y)$ contains $(e, gy)$ (choose $h = g^{-1}$), so each orbit has a unique representative with first coordinate $e$, giving $E|_U \cong U \times F$ via $[(g, y)] \leftrightarrow gy$. This is the local trivialization. The transition functions are computed by tracking how this identification changes under a change of trivialization of $P$, which gives $c^E = c^P$ acting on $F$.

**Mechanism summary: the associated bundle is constructed by quotienting out the diagonal $G$-action, and the quotient is automatically locally trivial because $P$ is — the local trivialization of $E$ comes for free from the local trivialization of $P$ combined with the unique-representative trick in each $G$-orbit.**

---

# What Makes This Hard

The hardest step is **identifying the local trivialization of $E$ correctly from that of $P$**. The diagonal-quotient construction is easy to write down, but the resulting fibre over $p$ — abstractly $\pi^{-1}(p) = (G \times F)/G$ — must be identified with $F$ via the right (canonical) choice. The correct identification is "use the unique orbit representative with first coordinate $e$"; the resulting fibre map $[g, y] \mapsto gy$ involves the left $G$-action on $F$, which is why the *left* $G$-action on $F$ enters the construction even though only the *right* $G$-action on $P$ was used to define the quotient. Confusing left and right at this step is the common error.

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:** Show the diagonal $G$-action on $P \times F$ is free and proper, apply the quotient manifold theorem to get the smooth structure on $E$. Then construct local trivializations by working in local trivializations of $P$.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Subgoal 1: The diagonal $G$-action on $P \times F$ is free.** Use freeness of the right $G$-action on $P$.

2. **Subgoal 2: The diagonal action is proper.** Combine properness of $P \to M$ (from local triviality) with smoothness of the $G$-action on $F$.

3. **Subgoal 3: Apply the quotient manifold theorem.** $E = (P \times F)/G$ inherits a smooth structure with smooth submersion $P \times F \to E$.

4. **Subgoal 4: Define $\pi : E \to M$ and show it is smooth.** $\pi([u, y]) = \pi_P(u)$, well-defined because the diagonal action preserves $\pi_P$ on $P$.

5. **Subgoal 5: Construct local trivializations of $E$.** Over a trivializing patch $U$ of $P$ with $\Phi : P|_U \cong U \times G$, identify $E|_U = (U \times G \times F)/G \cong U \times F$ via the "first coordinate $= e$" representative, giving $[g, y] \mapsto gy$ for the fibre map.

6. **Subgoal 6: Compute transition functions.** On overlaps, change of trivialization of $P$ by $c^P_{\alpha\beta}(p)$ becomes change of trivialization of $E$ by $y \mapsto c^P_{\alpha\beta}(p) y$ in the $G$-action.

7. **Subgoal 7: Bijection sections ↔ equivariant maps.** A section $s : M \to E$ lifts (after a choice of $u_0 \in P$ over each point — i.e., a local section of $P$) to an equivariant map $\tilde s : P \to F$.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: The diagonal $G$-action on $P \times F$ is free
> **Statement:** If $(u \cdot g, g^{-1}y) = (u, y)$ for some $(u, y) \in P \times F$ and $g \in G$, then $g = e$.
>
> **Hint:** Use freeness of the right $G$-action on $P$ alone.
>
> **Why needed:** Required for the quotient manifold theorem to give $E$ a smooth manifold structure.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > If $u \cdot g = u$, then $g = e$ by freeness of the right $G$-action on the principal bundle $P$. (The $F$-component condition $g^{-1}y = y$ is then automatically satisfied with $g = e$.)

> [!note]- Lemma 2: The diagonal $G$-action on $P \times F$ is proper
> **Statement:** The map $(P \times F) \times G \to (P \times F) \times (P \times F)$, $((u, y), g) \mapsto ((u, y), (u \cdot g, g^{-1}y))$, is proper (preimages of compact sets are compact).
>
> **Hint:** Use properness of the action on $P$ (which is built into the principal-bundle structure) combined with smoothness of the $F$-action.
>
> **Why needed:** Required for the smoothness of the quotient $E = (P \times F)/G$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > The right $G$-action on $P$ is proper (as $P$ is a fibre bundle: locally $P \cong M \times G$ and the action on the $G$-factor is by right multiplication, proper). The diagonal action inherits properness from the $P$-action: a compact set in $(P \times F) \times (P \times F)$ projects to a compact set in $P \times P$, whose preimage under the $P$-action is compact; on the $F$-factor, smoothness of the action handles the rest.

> [!note]- Lemma 3: Local trivializations of $E$ from local trivializations of $P$
> **Statement:** Over a trivializing patch $U$ of $P$ with $\Phi_U : \pi_P^{-1}(U) \cong U \times G$, the associated bundle has $E|_U \cong U \times F$, with the identification $[u, y] \leftrightarrow (\pi_P(u), \mathrm{pr}_2(\Phi_U(u)) \cdot y)$.
>
> **Hint:** Each orbit $[(g, y)]$ of the $G$-action on $\{p\} \times G \times F$ has a unique representative with first coordinate $e$ — namely $(e, gy)$, obtained by acting by $h = g^{-1}$.
>
> **Why needed:** Confirms $E$ is a fibre bundle with fibre $F$ and provides the local trivializations.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > In $\pi^{-1}(U)$, every point is $[u, y]$ with $u \in \pi_P^{-1}(U)$, $y \in F$. Write $u = \Phi_U^{-1}(p, g)$ for $p \in U$, $g \in G$. Then $[u, y] = [\Phi_U^{-1}(p, g), y]$, and by the $G$-action on the equivalence class (with $h = g^{-1}$) this equals $[\Phi_U^{-1}(p, e), gy] = [s_U(p), gy]$, where $s_U(p) = \Phi_U^{-1}(p, e)$ is the canonical local section of $P$. So each fibre point has a unique representative of the form $[s_U(p), y]$, and the fibre map $[s_U(p), y] \mapsto y$ is a diffeomorphism $E|_U \cong U \times F$. Combining with the trivialization of $P$, the formula above gives the identification.

> [!note]- Lemma 4: Sections of $E$ are $G$-equivariant maps $P \to F$
> **Statement:** $\Gamma(E) = \mathrm{Map}_G(P, F) := \{\tilde s : P \to F \text{ smooth} : \tilde s(u \cdot g) = g^{-1}\tilde s(u) \text{ for all } g, u\}$.
>
> **Hint:** Given a section $s : M \to E$ and a point $u \in P$, define $\tilde s(u)$ as the second coordinate of any representative $[u, y]$ with first coordinate $u$. Verify equivariance.
>
> **Why needed:** The most important computational tool — converts section computations to equivariant-map computations on the principal bundle.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Given a section $s : M \to E$, $s(\pi_P(u)) = [u, \tilde s(u)]$ for some unique $\tilde s(u) \in F$ (uniqueness because $[u, y] = [u, y']$ iff $y = y'$, by Lemma 1). The map $\tilde s : P \to F$ is smooth (locally; use Lemma 3 to express in coordinates). Equivariance: $s(\pi_P(u \cdot g)) = s(\pi_P(u)) = [u, \tilde s(u)] = [u \cdot g, g^{-1}\tilde s(u)]$, so $\tilde s(u \cdot g) = g^{-1}\tilde s(u)$. Conversely, given equivariant $\tilde s$, define $s(p) = [u, \tilde s(u)]$ for any $u \in \pi_P^{-1}(p)$ — well-defined because of equivariance.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> **Step 0 — Well-definedness of the diagonal action.** The map $(u, y) \cdot g := (u \cdot g, g^{-1}y)$ is a smooth right $G$-action on $P \times F$: associativity follows from the right-action axiom on $P$ and the left-action axiom on $F$, identity follows from $u \cdot e = u$ and $e^{-1}y = y$.
>
> **Step 1 — Smooth manifold structure on $E$.** By Lemmas 1 and 2, the diagonal action is free and proper. The quotient manifold theorem yields a unique smooth-manifold structure on $E = (P \times F)/G$ such that the quotient map $\rho : P \times F \to E$ is a smooth surjective submersion. The dimension is $\dim(P \times F) - \dim G = (\dim M + \dim G + \dim F) - \dim G = \dim M + \dim F$, as expected for a fibre bundle.
>
> **Step 2 — Projection $\pi : E \to M$ is well-defined and smooth.** Set $\pi([u, y]) := \pi_P(u)$. Well-definedness: $\pi_P(u \cdot g) = \pi_P(u)$ by fibre-preservation of the right $G$-action on $P$. Smoothness: the composition $\pi \circ \rho : P \times F \to M$ is $\pi_P \circ \mathrm{pr}_1$, smooth; since $\rho$ is a submersion, $\pi$ is smooth by the universal property of submersive quotients.
>
> **Step 3 — Local triviality of $E$.** By Lemma 3, over any trivializing patch $U$ of $P$, $E|_U \cong U \times F$ with the identification $\Phi^E_U : [\Phi_U^{-1}(p, g), y] \mapsto (p, gy)$. This is a smooth diffeomorphism commuting with $\pi$.
>
> **Step 4 — Transition functions.** Over $U \cap V$, the change of trivialization is computed: $\Phi^E_V \circ (\Phi^E_U)^{-1}(p, y) = ?$. The inverse $(\Phi^E_U)^{-1}(p, y) = [s_U(p), y]$, and applying $\Phi^E_V$: $s_U(p) = s_V(p) \cdot c^P_{VU}(p)$ in $V$-coordinates, so $[s_U(p), y] = [s_V(p) \cdot c^P_{VU}(p), y] = [s_V(p), c^P_{VU}(p)y]$. Therefore $\Phi^E_V([s_U(p), y]) = (p, c^P_{VU}(p) y)$. So the transition function of $E$ over $U \cap V$ is $y \mapsto c^P_{VU}(p) y$, i.e., the same cocycle as $P$ acting on $F$.
>
> **Step 5 — Fibre $E_p \cong F$.** Direct from Step 3 / Lemma 3.
>
> **Step 6 — Sections ↔ equivariant maps.** Lemma 4.

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

1. **Linear algebra / tensor bundles.** Verify the abstract construction by exhibiting the cotangent bundle $T^*M = \mathrm{Fr}(TM) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(n)} (\mathbb{R}^n)^*$ (with $\mathrm{GL}(n)$ acting on $(\mathbb{R}^n)^*$ by contragredient) and comparing with the direct construction. Confirm transition functions on $T^*M$ are inverse transposes of those on $TM$ — i.e., the contragredient of the $TM$ cocycle.

2. **Lie theory / induced representations.** For the homogeneous bundle $H \to G \to G/H$, identify the induced representation $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G V = \Gamma(G \times_H V)$. Apply the theorem: sections of $G \times_H V$ are $H$-equivariant maps $G \to V$, which is the standard definition of induced representation. This unifies the geometric and representation-theoretic definitions.

3. **Quantum mechanics / spinor bundles.** Given a spin structure on a Riemannian manifold (a principal $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$-bundle $P_{\mathrm{Spin}}$ double-covering the orthonormal frame bundle), the **spinor bundle** is $P_{\mathrm{Spin}} \times_{\mathrm{Spin}} \Delta$, where $\Delta$ is the spinor representation. Apply the theorem to confirm this is a vector bundle of fibre $\Delta \cong \mathbb{C}^{2^{[n/2]}}$.

# Bridges

- **[[Def - Associated Bundle]]** — The construction whose validity this theorem establishes. The theorem upgrades "associated bundle" from a definition to a *construction*: given the principal bundle $P$ and the $G$-action on $F$, the associated bundle exists and is a fibre bundle with the expected properties.

- **[[Def - Principal G-Bundle]]** — Provides the input to the construction. The intrinsic right $G$-action on $P$ is what makes the diagonal-quotient construction work — without the free right action, the quotient would not be smooth.

- **[[Thm - Principal Bundles are Locally Trivial via G-Action]]** — Provides the freeness, properness, and local triviality of the right $G$-action used in this proof. The two theorems are companions: one establishes that principal bundles have intrinsic right actions, the other uses those right actions to build associated bundles.

- **[[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle]]** — The frame bundle and the standard representation together demonstrate the universality of the associated-bundle construction: every vector bundle is recovered as the associated bundle of its frame bundle with the standard representation. The corollary of universality is what makes this theorem one of the central organizational tools of bundle geometry.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Functorial Constructions on Vector Bundles *(from Geometric Functional Calculus)*
> Every functorial construction on vector spaces (tensor product, symmetric power, exterior power, dual, etc.) extends to a functorial construction on vector bundles via the associated-bundle construction: $\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} F(\mathbb{R}^k)$ for any functor $F$ on the category of vector spaces. This is the categorical reason the entire algebra of vector spaces transfers verbatim to vector bundles.

> [!tip] Induced Connections on All Associated Bundles *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> A single principal connection on $P$ induces a covariant derivative on *every* associated bundle $P \times_G F$ simultaneously, compatible with the $G$-action. This is why gauge theorists work with principal bundles: one connection on the principal bundle gives connections on all matter fields automatically. See [[Gauge Theory III — Connections in Principal and Associated Bundles]].

> [!tip] Twisted Equivariant Cohomology *(from Equivariant Topology)*
> The bijection sections($P \times_G F$) = equivariant maps $P \to F$ generalizes to higher cohomology: $H^*(M; P \times_G F)$ in twisted coefficients equals $G$-equivariant cohomology $H^*_G(P; F)$. This is the bridge from bundle cohomology to equivariant cohomology, central to **localization formulas** in Atiyah-Bott style.
