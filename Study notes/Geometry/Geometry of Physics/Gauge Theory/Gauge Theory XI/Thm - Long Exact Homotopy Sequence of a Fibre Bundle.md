# Statement
> [!theorem] Long exact sequence
> For a fibre bundle $F\hookrightarrow E\xrightarrow p B$ with compatible base points, there are connecting maps making
> $$\cdots\to\pi_n(F)\xrightarrow{i_*}\pi_n(E)\xrightarrow{p_*}\pi_n(B)\xrightarrow\partial\pi_{n-1}(F)\to\cdots$$
> exact.

# Why this should be true
A sphere in the base lifts over a hemisphere. Its failure to close along the equator is a sphere one dimension lower in the fibre; that failure is $\partial$.

> [!proof]- Formal Proof
> Represent $[u]\in\pi_n(B)$ by $u:(I^n,\partial I^n)\to(B,b_0)$. The homotopy-lifting property lifts $u$ after prescribing the lift to be $e_0$ on all boundary faces except the terminal face. Restriction to that face lies in $F$ and defines $\partial[u]$. Two choices are homotopic by lifting a homotopy, so $\partial$ is well-defined. If a class comes from $E$, choose its lift itself; its terminal face is constant, proving $\operatorname{im}p_*\subseteq\ker\partial$. Conversely, if the terminal face is null-homotopic in $F$, attach that null-homotopy and use homotopy lifting to alter the lift so that every boundary face is constant; it then represents a class in $\pi_n(E)$ mapping to $[u]$. Thus $\ker\partial\subseteq\operatorname{im}p_*$. The same lifting-and-extension argument at the adjacent terms proves $\ker p_*=\operatorname{im}i_*$ and $\ker i_*=\operatorname{im}\partial$. Hence the sequence is exact.

# Gauge-theory example
For $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$, vanishing of $\pi_2(S^1)$ and $\pi_3(S^1)$ yields $\pi_3(S^2)\cong\pi_3(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z$, generated by the Hopf map.
