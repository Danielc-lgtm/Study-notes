# Notation

$(M, \omega)$ is a symplectic manifold. $H : M \to \mathbb{R}$ is a smooth Hamiltonian. $X_H$ is the [[Def - Hamiltonian Vector Field|Hamiltonian vector field]] defined by $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$. $\phi^H_t : M \to M$ is the flow of $X_H$, the time-$t$ map. $\mathcal{L}_X$ denotes the Lie derivative along $X$.

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (Hamiltonian flows preserve the symplectic form).** Let $(M, \omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and let $H : M \to \mathbb{R}$ be a smooth (possibly time-dependent) Hamiltonian function. The flow $\phi^H_t$ of the Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ preserves $\omega$:
> $$\big(\phi^H_t\big)^* \omega = \omega \quad \text{for all } t \text{ for which } \phi^H_t \text{ is defined}.$$
> Equivalently, $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$. Therefore $\phi^H_t \in \mathrm{Symp}(M, \omega)$ — every Hamiltonian flow is a 1-parameter family of [[Def - Symplectomorphism (Canonical Transformation)|symplectomorphisms]].

> **Corollary (preservation of $\omega^k$).** $\phi^H_t$ preserves every power $\omega^k$ for $k = 1, \dots, n$. In particular, $(\phi^H_t)^*\omega^n = \omega^n$, the **Liouville volume** is preserved — see [[Thm - Liouville's Theorem on Phase Space Volume]].

---

# Motivation

This theorem is the structural reason classical mechanics has the rich symplectic-geometric character that it does. The Hamiltonian flow — the dynamical evolution of any mechanical system — preserves the symplectic form. This is not a constraint we impose; it is forced by the definition of $X_H$ via $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ combined with the closedness $d\omega = 0$. So **every Hamiltonian dynamics is automatically a 1-parameter family of canonical transformations**, with no extra work.

The role of the theorem is to elevate the symplectic structure from "a formal definition" to "the conserved geometric quantity of classical dynamics". In Riemannian geometry, the metric $g$ is *not* preserved by gradient flow (gradient flow generally distorts distances). In symplectic geometry, $\omega$ *is* preserved by Hamiltonian flow. This is the structural reason symplectic geometry is the right setting for classical mechanics: the geometry is preserved by the dynamics, so the dynamics is well-behaved.

The most important consequence is **Liouville's theorem**: phase-space volume is preserved by Hamiltonian flow. This is the foundation of statistical mechanics (the microcanonical ensemble), of dynamical systems (Hamiltonian systems are volume-preserving, ruling out attractors), and of integrable systems (action-angle coordinates exist precisely because of the volume-preservation property).

Beyond Liouville, the theorem says that **Hamiltonian flows are 1-parameter subgroups of the infinite-dimensional symplectomorphism group $\mathrm{Symp}(M, \omega)$**. This identifies Hamiltonian functions with one-parameter subgroups, providing the Lie-algebraic structure: the bracket of two Hamiltonian flows is again Hamiltonian, with Hamiltonian (up to sign) the Poisson bracket of the original ones.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening).**

The hypothesis is "you have a Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ defined by $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ for some smooth $H$". The interesting question is what kinds of dynamics turn out to be Hamiltonian — sometimes non-obviously.

**Source: a vector field $X$ with $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$ on a simply connected manifold.** Such vector fields are called **symplectic**. By Cartan's formula, $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = d\iota_X\omega + \iota_X d\omega = d\iota_X\omega$ (closed $\omega$), so $\iota_X\omega$ is closed. If $M$ is simply connected (or more generally $H^1_{dR}(M; \mathbb{R}) = 0$), then $\iota_X\omega$ is exact: $\iota_X\omega = dH$ for some $H$. Hence $X = X_H$ is Hamiltonian. So **on simply connected symplectic manifolds, every symplectic vector field is Hamiltonian** — and the theorem applies. *Example use:* on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ (which is simply connected), every symplectic vector field is Hamiltonian.

**Source: a vector field generating a 1-parameter group of symplectomorphisms.** If you can verify (by direct calculation or by other means) that the time-$t$ flow $\phi_t$ satisfies $\phi_t^*\omega = \omega$ for all $t$, then differentiation at $t = 0$ gives $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$, so $X$ is symplectic, hence (on simply connected $M$) Hamiltonian. *Example use:* a rotation of the harmonic oscillator's phase space is volume-preserving and angle-preserving — it preserves the symplectic form — so it must be generated by a Hamiltonian, which turns out to be the angular momentum.

**Source: a divergence-free vector field on $\mathbb{R}^2$.** On $(\mathbb{R}^2, dp \wedge dq)$, every divergence-free vector field $X = u(q, p)\partial_q + v(q, p)\partial_p$ with $\partial_q u + \partial_p v = 0$ is symplectic ($\mathcal{L}_X\omega = (\partial_q u + \partial_p v)dq \wedge dp = 0$). Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, $X = X_H$ for some $H$, which is the **stream function** of the vector field (familiar from incompressible 2D fluid dynamics). *Example use:* incompressible 2D fluid flow is Hamiltonian, with the stream function as the Hamiltonian.

**Source: a smooth function $H$ on $(M, \omega)$ without further structure.** This is the direct hypothesis: any smooth function generates a Hamiltonian vector field, and the theorem applies. *Example use:* take $H = \tfrac{1}{2}(q^2 + p^2)$; the resulting flow is the rotation of phase space, automatically symplectic.

**Targets (Output Amplification).**

The conclusion is "$\phi^H_t$ preserves $\omega$". Combined with other facts, this is enormously powerful.

**Target + $\omega$ nondegenerate = phase-space volume conserved.** $\phi^H_t$ preserves $\omega^n/n!$, the Liouville volume. This is **Liouville's theorem** in its modern symplectic form, and it is the foundation of statistical mechanics. *Combination use:* given any region $R \subset M$, the volume $\mathrm{Vol}(\phi^H_t(R)) = \mathrm{Vol}(R)$ for all $t$.

**Target + $H$ in involution with $f$ = $f$ is preserved by flow of $H$.** If $\{f, H\} = 0$, then $f$ is constant along $\phi^H_t$ (since $X_H(f) = \{f, H\} = 0$). So conserved quantities of the Hamiltonian dynamics are exactly the Poisson-commuting functions with $H$. *Combination use:* identifying conserved quantities — momenta from spatial translation symmetry, angular momentum from rotational, etc. — is the prerequisite to integrating the system.

**Target + $\phi^H_t$ preserves $\omega$ = $\phi^H_t$ takes Lagrangians to Lagrangians.** A submanifold $L \subset M$ on which $\omega$ restricts to zero (a Lagrangian) is sent by any symplectomorphism to another such submanifold. *Combination use:* Lagrangian intersection theory studies the dynamics of Lagrangian submanifolds under Hamiltonian flow — the Arnold conjecture, Floer homology.

**Target + Cartan magic formula = simple proof of Liouville.** The proof of "Hamiltonian flows preserve $\omega$" via $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d\iota_{X_H}\omega + \iota_{X_H}d\omega = d(dH) + 0 = 0$ is so short (two applications of Cartan's formula combined with $d^2 = 0$ and $d\omega = 0$) that it functions as a unifying example for the whole calculus of forms. *Combination use:* in introductory courses, this theorem is often the first non-trivial application of Cartan's magic formula and showcases the power of the form-based formalism.

# Why Is It True

**The mechanism in one sentence:** *Hamiltonian flows preserve $\omega$ because the Lie derivative $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega$ vanishes by a one-line Cartan calculation, using $d^2 = 0$ and $d\omega = 0$.*

Here is the calculation, which is the entire substance of the theorem:

$$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d\iota_{X_H}\omega + \iota_{X_H}d\omega = d(dH) + \iota_{X_H}(0) = 0 + 0 = 0.$$

The first equality is **Cartan's magic formula** $\mathcal{L}_X = d\iota_X + \iota_X d$ specialized to forms. The second equality uses the **defining property** of the Hamiltonian vector field $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$, and the **closedness** $d\omega = 0$ of the symplectic form. The third equality uses **$d^2 = 0$** (every exact form is closed). 

That's it. The conclusion $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$ follows in three lines from the most basic identities of the calculus of forms.

**What if we drop closedness?** Then $\iota_{X_H}d\omega$ is nonzero, and $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d(dH) + \iota_{X_H}d\omega = \iota_{X_H}d\omega \neq 0$. So **closedness is essential** — without $d\omega = 0$, Hamiltonian flows do *not* preserve $\omega$, and Liouville's theorem fails. This is the structural reason **closedness is one of the two axioms of a symplectic manifold**: it's the algebraic enabler of dynamical structure preservation.

**What if $H$ depends on time?** The formula $\mathcal{L}_{X_{H_t}}\omega = 0$ still holds at each $t$, and the time-dependent flow $\phi^H_t$ still preserves $\omega$. The argument is identical: $\mathcal{L}_{X_{H_t}}\omega = d(dH_t) + \iota_{X_{H_t}}(0) = 0$. The time-dependence of $H$ does not affect the symplectic preservation — only the autonomous-Hamiltonian conclusion ($H$ is conserved along flow) is lost.

**From flow preservation of $\omega$ to flow preservation of $\omega^n$:** apply the Leibniz rule for Lie derivative. $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}(\omega \wedge \omega) = \mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega \wedge \omega + \omega \wedge \mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0 \wedge \omega + \omega \wedge 0 = 0$. Iterating: $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega^n = 0$ for all $n$. So $\phi^H_t$ preserves every power $\omega^k$, and in particular preserves $\omega^n$ — the symplectic volume.

# What Makes This Hard

The theorem itself is easy once you know Cartan's magic formula — the proof is three lines. The conceptual difficulty is recognizing that this short calculation has such powerful consequences (Liouville's theorem, no attractors in Hamiltonian systems, the structure of integrable systems, etc.). Most students see the theorem as an exercise in applying Cartan's formula but underappreciate how much follows. The other common stumbling block is the *generalization to time-dependent Hamiltonians*: the formula $\mathcal{L}_{X_{H_t}}\omega = 0$ still holds at each $t$, but the flow is now a path of symplectomorphisms rather than a one-parameter group, and many students initially expect the time-dependence to spoil the preservation. The non-obvious step is realizing the time-dependence affects energy conservation but not symplectic structure preservation.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Cartan's Magic Formula
> **Statement:** For any vector field $X$ and any differential form $\omega$ on a smooth manifold,
> $$\mathcal{L}_X \omega = d\iota_X\omega + \iota_X d\omega.$$
>
> **Hint:** Proved by induction on the degree of $\omega$, starting from functions (where $\mathcal{L}_X f = X(f) = \iota_X df$) and extending via Leibniz.
>
> **Why needed:** This is the central identity that makes the entire proof of the theorem one line. See [[Thm - Cartan's Magic Formula]] for the proof.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > See [[Thm - Cartan's Magic Formula]] for the complete proof. The key idea is: both sides are derivations of degree $0$ on $\Omega^*(M)$ that agree on functions and exact 1-forms (the generators of the exterior algebra), hence agree everywhere by induction on degree.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: The Lie derivative vanishes iff the flow preserves the form
> **Statement:** For a vector field $X$ with flow $\phi_t$, the following are equivalent:
> (a) $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$.
> (b) $\phi_t^*\omega = \omega$ for all $t$ for which the flow is defined.
>
> **Hint:** $(d/dt)\phi_t^*\omega = \phi_t^*(\mathcal{L}_X\omega)$ is the definition of $\mathcal{L}_X$ on forms.
>
> **Why needed:** Converts the differential conclusion $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$ to the integral conclusion that the flow preserves $\omega$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > $\mathcal{L}_X\omega := (d/dt)|_{t=0}\phi_t^*\omega$ by definition. So (a) means $(d/dt)\phi_t^*\omega|_{t=0} = 0$. For (a) $\Rightarrow$ (b): by the chain rule and the 1-parameter group structure $\phi_{t+s} = \phi_t \circ \phi_s$,
> > $$\frac{d}{dt}\phi_t^*\omega = \frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}\phi_{t+s}^*\omega = \phi_t^* \frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}\phi_s^*\omega = \phi_t^*\mathcal{L}_X\omega = \phi_t^*(0) = 0.$$
> > So $\phi_t^*\omega$ is constant in $t$, equal to $\phi_0^*\omega = \omega$. For (b) $\Rightarrow$ (a): differentiate $\phi_t^*\omega = \omega$ at $t = 0$ to get $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Let $(M, \omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and let $H : M \to \mathbb{R}$ be a smooth function with associated Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$, defined by $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$.
>
> **Step 1 — Compute $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega$ via Cartan's magic formula.** By Lemma 1,
> $$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d(\iota_{X_H}\omega) + \iota_{X_H}(d\omega).$$
>
> **Step 2 — Substitute defining properties.** Using $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ (definition of $X_H$) and $d\omega = 0$ (closedness of $\omega$),
> $$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d(dH) + \iota_{X_H}(0).$$
>
> **Step 3 — Apply $d^2 = 0$.** The exterior derivative satisfies $d^2 = 0$ on any form, so $d(dH) = 0$. Also $\iota_{X_H}(0) = 0$ trivially.
> $$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0 + 0 = 0.$$
>
> **Step 4 — Translate to flow preservation.** By Lemma 2, $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$ implies $(\phi^H_t)^*\omega = \omega$ for all $t$ for which the flow is defined.
>
> **Step 5 — Time-dependent generalization.** If $H = H_t$ depends explicitly on time, the same Steps 1–3 give $\mathcal{L}_{X_{H_t}}\omega = 0$ at each $t$. For a time-dependent vector field $X_t = X_{H_t}$ with flow $\phi_{t, s}$ (from time $s$ to time $t$), the analogous formula
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t, s}^*\omega = \phi_{t, s}^*(\mathcal{L}_{X_t}\omega) = 0$$
> gives $\phi_{t, s}^*\omega = \phi_{s, s}^*\omega = \omega$, the preservation result.
>
> **Step 6 — Corollary on $\omega^n$.** By the Leibniz rule for $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}$,
> $$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega^n = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \omega^i \wedge \mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega \wedge \omega^{n-i-1} = 0,$$
> using $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$. So $(\phi^H_t)^*\omega^n = \omega^n$, the Liouville volume is preserved.
>
> $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Fluid dynamics: vorticity conservation.** The 2D Euler equations for an incompressible inviscid fluid can be reformulated as a Hamiltonian system on the dual of the Lie algebra of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms. The vorticity 2-form is conserved (in the sense of being convected with the flow), and this is the fluid-dynamic incarnation of "Hamiltonian flows preserve $\omega$". More generally, the **Kelvin circulation theorem** and **Helmholtz vortex theorems** are consequences of the symplectic preservation property applied to the appropriate phase space.

**Quantum mechanics: unitarity of time evolution.** The quantum-mechanical analogue of "Hamiltonian flows are symplectomorphisms" is **"time evolution is unitary"**: the time-evolution operator $U(t) = e^{-i\hat H t/\hbar}$ generated by a self-adjoint Hamiltonian $\hat H$ preserves the Hermitian inner product on Hilbert space, hence preserves probabilities. The structural analogue: symplectomorphism $\leftrightarrow$ unitary; symplectic form $\omega$ $\leftrightarrow$ Hermitian inner product; Hamiltonian function $H$ $\leftrightarrow$ self-adjoint operator $\hat H$. Both statements have the same algebraic content: the dynamics preserves the geometric structure of the phase space (classical) or state space (quantum).

**Optics and symplectic ray-tracing.** Geometrical optics in inhomogeneous media is governed by the **eikonal equation**, which is a Hamilton–Jacobi equation. The corresponding Hamiltonian dynamics — light rays as integral curves of $X_H$ for $H = n(x)|p|$ (with $n$ the refractive index) — preserves the optical symplectic form. Practical consequence: **ray-tracing algorithms in lens design** that respect the symplectic structure (symplectic integrators) produce accurate ray patterns over long propagation distances, while non-symplectic methods accumulate spurious geometric aberrations.

**Numerical analysis: symplectic integrators.** As discussed in [[Thm - Darboux's Theorem]]'s cross-field section, symplectic integrators numerically preserve a *discrete* version of $\omega$, with superior long-time stability. The reason is precisely the theorem: the continuous Hamiltonian flow preserves $\omega$, and the symplectic integrator approximates this by preserving the discrete analogue at each step. Non-symplectic methods accumulate phase-space distortion exponentially with time.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Liouville's Theorem on Phase Space Volume]]**: this is the immediate corollary obtained by taking the top wedge power $\omega^n$ — the symplectic volume is preserved by Hamiltonian flow. The bridge is via the Leibniz rule for Lie derivative: $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0 \implies \mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega^n = 0$. Liouville's theorem is what the symplectic preservation looks like at the top level of forms, and it is the fact most directly used in physics (statistical mechanics, ergodic theory).

- **[[Thm - Cartan's Magic Formula]]**: this is the technical lemma without which the proof of the theorem would not be a one-liner. Cartan's formula $\mathcal{L}_X = d\iota_X + \iota_X d$ converts the analytic Lie derivative into an algebraic combination of $d$ and $\iota_X$, eliminating the need to compute flows explicitly. The proof of "Hamiltonian flows are symplectomorphisms" is essentially a single application of Cartan's formula combined with the algebraic defining property $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ and the integrability $d\omega = 0$.

- **The relationship between $\mathfrak{symp}$ and $\mathfrak{ham}$**: this theorem says that every Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ is symplectic (i.e., $\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = 0$). The converse — every symplectic vector field is Hamiltonian — holds only when $H^1_{dR}(M; \mathbb{R}) = 0$ (e.g., simply connected $M$). The obstruction $\mathfrak{symp}/\mathfrak{ham} \cong H^1_{dR}(M; \mathbb{R})$ is the **Calabi invariant**, and it measures the difference between "preserves $\omega$ infinitesimally" and "preserves $\omega$ by a flow of an energy function".

- **Noether's theorem in symplectic form**: a symmetry of the system, encoded as a 1-parameter group of symplectomorphisms $\phi_t$, has an infinitesimal generator $X$ that is symplectic ($\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$). On simply connected $M$, this gives $X = X_f$ for some Hamiltonian $f$, and the Hamiltonian $f$ is a conserved quantity: $\{f, H\} = 0$ when $\phi_t$ commutes with $\phi^H_t$. This is the precise symplectic statement of Noether's theorem, and the moment map is its multi-dimensional generalization.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] No Attractors in Hamiltonian Dynamics *(from Dynamical Systems)*
> A direct consequence of the volume-preserving property is that **Hamiltonian systems have no attractors**. An attractor would be a region of phase space that absorbs nearby trajectories, with the region itself having smaller volume than its basin — but volume preservation rules this out. So all the rich asymptotic behavior of Hamiltonian dynamics (KAM tori, stable and unstable manifolds, ergodic regions) coexists in a fundamentally volume-preserving way. This is in stark contrast to dissipative systems (gradient flows, Lorenz-type chaos) where attractors of various dimensions and complexity arise. The absence of attractors makes Hamiltonian dynamics simultaneously simpler (volume-preserving) and harder (no asymptotic simplification) than dissipative dynamics.

> [!tip] Poincaré Recurrence Theorem *(from Ergodic Theory)*
> Because Hamiltonian flows preserve a finite measure (on regions where the dynamics is confined to a compact subset of phase space), the **Poincaré recurrence theorem** applies: almost every initial condition returns arbitrarily close to itself infinitely often in time. This is a deep dynamical-systems consequence of Liouville's theorem (volume preservation on a compact region forces the orbit to return near itself). It implies that an isolated Hamiltonian system, after evolving for a sufficiently long time, will spontaneously return arbitrarily close to its initial state — including configurations that look extremely "unlikely". This is the source of various paradoxes (Loschmidt's reversibility paradox, Zermelo's recurrence paradox) in the foundations of statistical mechanics, resolved by noting that the recurrence times are typically astronomical (e.g., $\sim e^{N}$ for an $N$-particle system).

> [!tip] Symplectic Camel — Gromov Non-Squeezing *(from Symplectic Topology)*
> Hamiltonian flows preserve $\omega$, hence preserve $\omega^n/n!$ — the Liouville volume. But they preserve **more** than volume: they preserve all the "symplectic capacities", a hierarchy of invariants that includes the **Gromov width**. **Gromov's non-squeezing theorem** says that a ball $B^{2n}(r) \subset \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ cannot be symplectically embedded into a "cylinder" $B^2(R) \times \mathbb{R}^{2n - 2}$ unless $r \leq R$ — a much stronger statement than volume preservation, which would allow squeezing through arbitrarily thin cylinders. The "symplectic camel" then **cannot pass through the eye of a needle** in symplectic geometry, despite the volumes being compatible. This is one of the founding results of modern symplectic topology and shows that symplectic structures carry much more rigid information than volume preservation alone suggests.
