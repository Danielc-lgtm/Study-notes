## §3.1 The Riemann Curvature Tensor — Exercises

This section drills the algebraic machinery of the Riemann curvature tensor — its definition as the obstruction to commuting covariant derivatives, its computation via Cartan's structural equations in an orthonormal frame, and the algebraic symmetries that cut the apparent $n^4$ components down to $\tfrac{1}{12}n^2(n^2 - 1)$ independent ones. The principal technique is **Cartan's method**: orthonormal coframe → solve first structural equation for connection 1-form → compute curvature 2-form via second structural equation → read off Riemann components. This route is dramatically faster than computing Christoffel symbols, and it makes the algebraic-symmetry structure transparent throughout.

- [[Ex - Computing the Riemann Tensor of S^2 from Cartan's Equations]] (⭐⭐) — Apply Cartan's method to the round $S^2$ in spherical coordinates; verify $K = 1$ pointwise ([[Def - Riemann Curvature Tensor]], [[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - Levi-Civita Connection]]).
- [[Ex - Sectional Curvature of the Round Sphere is 1]] (⭐⭐) — Alternative route via the Gauss equation for a hypersurface in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$; verify $K \equiv 1$ on all of $S^n$ ([[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - Riemann Curvature Tensor]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
- [[Ex - Sectional Curvature of the Hyperbolic Plane is -1]] (⭐⭐) — Apply Cartan's method to the Poincaré upper half-plane; verify $K \equiv -1$ ([[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - The Hyperbolic Space H^n]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
