## §2.1 The Geodesic Equation — Exercises

This section drills the foundational machinery of [[Def - Geodesic|geodesics]]: identifying geodesics on specific manifolds, deriving the geodesic equation in coordinates, and exploiting symmetry and uniqueness to characterise geodesics without brute computation. The unifying technique is the **uniqueness trick**: by the existence-and-uniqueness theorem, the geodesic with given initial position and velocity is unique, so any curve with the right initial data that is "obviously" a geodesic (e.g., the fixed set of an [[Def - Isometry|isometry]] fixing the initial data, or the integral curve of a parallel vector field) must be the geodesic. This trick converts the verification of geodesic-ness from a Christoffel-symbol computation into a symmetry argument.

- [[Ex - Great Circles are the Geodesics of the Sphere]] (⭐) — symmetry/uniqueness identification of geodesics on $S^n$, via reflection across the 2-plane through $p$ and $v$ ([[Def - Geodesic]], [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of Geodesics]], [[Def - Riemannian Metric]])

- [[Ex - Geodesics of the Hyperbolic Plane]] (⭐⭐) — direct integration of the geodesic ODE on the Poincaré upper half-plane, using Killing-field conservation laws to reduce to quadrature ([[Def - Geodesic]], [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of Geodesics]], [[Def - Vector Field on a Manifold]])

- [[Ex - The Exponential Map on a Sphere is a Local Diffeomorphism]] (⭐⭐) — computing $\exp_p$ explicitly from the great-circle parametrisation, identifying the injective range and the singular sphere of conjugate points ([[Def - The Riemannian Exponential Map]], [[Def - Geodesic]], [[Thm - The Inverse Function Theorem]])
