## §2.4 Hamilton's Principle in Mechanics — Exercises

This section drills the bridge between classical mechanics and Riemannian geometry: **every conservative mechanical system on a configuration manifold $(M, g)$ with potential $V$ at fixed total energy $E$ is, after the conformal rescaling $\tilde g = 2(E - V) g$, the geodesic problem in the Jacobi metric**. So mechanical trajectories are [[Def - Geodesic|geodesics]], periodic motions are closed geodesics, and the entire variational and geometric apparatus transfers wholesale. The unifying principle is **Jacobi's principle of least action** (Maupertuis–Euler–Jacobi), and the exercise here drills the pendulum as the simplest non-trivial case, together with the Lagrangian-to-Hamiltonian Legendre transform deriving the cotangent-bundle picture. Because the topic is variational, only one rich exercise is included here, but it covers (a) Jacobi's principle abstractly, (b) the pendulum application, and (c) the Legendre transform separately — three sub-exercises that together fulfil the role of multiple short exercises.

This index references the cross-listed sphere exercises ([[Ex - Great Circles are the Geodesics of the Sphere]], [[Ex - The Exponential Map on a Sphere is a Local Diffeomorphism]]) which also illustrate Hamilton's principle implicitly: great circles on $S^n$ are the trajectories of a free particle constrained to the sphere, and the variational characterisation in [[Thm - First Variation of Arc Length]] confirms they extremise length.
