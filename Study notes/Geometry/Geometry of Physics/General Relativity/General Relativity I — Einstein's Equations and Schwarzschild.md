# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole topic: **we work in geometrised units, $c = 1$ and (often) $G = 1$.** With $c = 1$ time and length are measured in the same units, and the speed of light disappears from every formula; this makes the symmetry between space and time manifest, exactly as in [[Special Relativity I — Lorentz Transformations and Minkowski Space]]. With $G = 1$ as well, mass, length, and time are all measured in the same units (the mass of the Sun, $M_\odot \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{km}$ in these units), and the Einstein equations read $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ instead of $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$. To restore units, the rule is: insert factors of $c$ and $G$ by dimensional analysis until both sides have units of inverse length squared.

A second convention: **we use the "mostly minus" Lorentzian signature $(+,-,-,-)$**, matching Frankel and the special-relativity notes — a timelike vector has $g(v,v) > 0$, a spacelike vector has $g(v,v) < 0$. Wald, MTW, and Carroll use the opposite "mostly plus" signature $(-,+,+,+)$; to convert a formula, flip the sign of $g_{\mu\nu}$ everywhere and flip the sign of timelike/spacelike inequalities.

> [!warning] Convention: signature and dimensional units
> Sign conventions in GR diverge widely. Frankel uses $(+,-,-,-)$; MTW–Wald–Carroll use $(-,+,+,+)$. Under a signature flip $g \to -g$, the Riemann tensor $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ and the Ricci tensor $R_{\mu\nu}$ change sign (because two factors of $g$ are involved in their computation in mixed form), but the scalar $R$ stays the same in the $R^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu}$ convention. The Einstein equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ are preserved if and only if one flips $T_{\mu\nu}$ as well — and the energy density $T_{00}$ is positive in one convention, negative in the other. Always check the conventions of a textbook before importing a formula.

- $(M, g)$ — a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold; $M$ the spacetime, $g$ the spacetime metric of signature $(+,-,-,-)$; see [[Def - Lorentzian Manifold]] and [[Def - Spacetime Manifold]]
- $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ — components of the metric tensor in a coordinate chart; Greek indices $\mu, \nu, \rho, \sigma \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$
- $g^{\mu\nu}$ — inverse metric, $g^{\mu\rho} g_{\rho\nu} = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$
- $g = \det(g_{\mu\nu})$ — the metric determinant; in Lorentzian signature $g < 0$, so $\sqrt{-g}$ is the natural volume measure
- $\sqrt{-g}\, d^4x$ — the **Lorentzian volume form**; covariant under coordinate change
- $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$ — the Minkowski metric, the local-frame model
- $\nabla_\mu$ — covariant derivative of the [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation|Levi-Civita connection]] of $g$; sometimes written $T^{\mu}{}_{\nu/\rho}$ in Frankel's notation
- $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ — **Christoffel symbols** (connection coefficients), $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} g^{\rho\sigma}(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu})$
- $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ — components of the **Riemann curvature tensor**; see [[Riemannian Geometry III — Riemann Curvature and Topology]]
- $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ — **Ricci tensor** (one trace of the Riemann tensor)
- $R = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu}$ — **scalar curvature** (Ricci scalar)
- $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ — **Einstein tensor**; see [[Def - Einstein Tensor]]
- $T_{\mu\nu}$ — **stress-energy tensor** of matter (and non-gravitational fields); $T^{00}$ is energy density, $T^{0i}$ momentum density, $T^{ij}$ spatial stress; see [[Def - Stress-Energy Tensor]]
- $\Lambda$ — **cosmological constant** (units of inverse length squared); see [[Def - Cosmological Constant]]
- $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4$ — Einstein's coupling constant; with $G = c = 1$ this is $8\pi$
- $u^\mu$ — four-velocity of a particle or fluid element, normalised $g_{\mu\nu} u^\mu u^\nu = 1$ (or $c^2$ with units)
- $\tau$ — proper time along a timelike worldline, $d\tau^2 = ds^2$
- $\rho$ — rest energy density of matter; $p$ — pressure (for perfect fluids)
- $A_\mu$ — electromagnetic vector potential (one-form); $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ — field-strength tensor
- $S$ — an action functional; $S_\text{grav}$ the gravitational (Hilbert) action, $S_\text{matter}$ the matter action
- $V^3(t)$ — a spatial slice (constant-$t$ hypersurface) of spacetime; $h_{\alpha\beta}$ its induced Riemannian metric; $\alpha, \beta \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ for spatial indices
- $b_{\alpha\beta}$ — the second fundamental form of a spatial slice; $\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \kappa_3$ its principal (extrinsic) curvatures
- $N$ — unit timelike normal to a spatial slice
- $M$ — in the Schwarzschild metric, the **mass parameter** (in geometrised units, the mass of the central body); $r_s = 2M$ — the **Schwarzschild radius**
- $d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2$ — the round metric on $S^2$
- Summation convention: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0, 1, 2, 3$.

# Motivation

Here is the entire topic in one sentence: **gravity is the curvature of spacetime, and the matter living in spacetime tells spacetime how to curve.** The first half is the equivalence principle, the second half is Einstein's field equations, and everything in this chapter is a consequence of these two statements.

Newton's theory of gravity served physics for two and a half centuries, but it has two problems that special relativity makes intolerable. The first is *instantaneity*: a gravitational disturbance — moving a mass — propagates infinitely fast in Newton's theory, so an observer at the other end of the galaxy feels the force at the same instant. After special relativity this is impossible: no signal travels faster than light, and every interaction must respect the light cone. The second is *deeper*: Newton's theory has no clean answer to "which inertial frame?" A free-falling observer (an astronaut, a person in a falling lift) feels no gravity at all — they float weightless — and a uniformly accelerating observer feels exactly the same "force" as one in a uniform gravitational field. Newton calls the first an inertial frame and the second non-inertial; Einstein notices that locally they are indistinguishable. The equivalence between gravitational and inertial mass (Galileo: all bodies fall with the same acceleration) is not an accident but a fundamental fact that demands a new theory.

Einstein's resolution is to take the second observation seriously and demote gravity. A free-falling observer feels no gravity because *they are not falling* — they are following the straight line (the geodesic) in a curved spacetime. There is no gravitational force; there is only the geometry that all bodies, regardless of mass or composition, follow. The "force" of gravity that we feel standing on the Earth is the *normal* force the Earth exerts to prevent us from following our natural free-fall worldline. This is the equivalence principle, and it forces the conclusion that *spacetime is curved* in the presence of matter, and the curvature *is* gravity.

To make this quantitative we need three ingredients. First, a spacetime — a [[Def - Lorentzian Manifold|Lorentzian manifold]] $(M, g)$, generalising the flat [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric|Minkowski space]] of special relativity. The metric $g$ now varies from event to event, $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$; the constant Minkowski $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is the *tangent-space* form, valid in a locally inertial frame at each event. The ten independent components of $g_{\mu\nu}$ are the "gravitational potentials" — they play the role Newton's single scalar potential $\phi$ played, and at first sight it is mysterious that gravity needs ten potentials instead of one. Section §1.1 explains this: the equivalence principle tells us that $g_{00} \sim -(1 - 2\phi)$ in the Newtonian limit, so $g_{00}$ alone recovers Newton — but the other components carry genuinely new physics (frame-dragging, gravitational waves) that Newton's theory cannot describe.

Second, the equation of motion. A free particle (one acted on by no non-gravitational forces) follows a [[Riemannian Geometry II — Geodesics, the Exponential Map, and Variational Principles|geodesic]] of $g$: the timelike [[Def - Geodesic|geodesics]] for massive particles, the null geodesics for light. This is one of the two great content-statements of GR. It contains Newton's law of motion in the appropriate limit — the geodesic equation reduces to $\ddot x^\alpha = -\partial^\alpha \phi$ for slow motion in a weak field, with $g_{00} = -(1 - 2\phi)$ identifying $\phi$ as the Newtonian potential — and at the same time it predicts that *light is bent by gravity* (light follows null geodesics, which are not straight in coordinates), the prediction confirmed by Eddington's 1919 eclipse.

Third, the field equation: a rule connecting the metric to its sources. The source is no longer Newton's mass density $\rho$, which is not a scalar (it transforms under Lorentz boosts) but the $(0,0)$ component of the [[Def - Stress-Energy Tensor|stress-energy tensor]] $T_{\mu\nu}$. The curvature must be built from $g$ and its first two derivatives, must be a symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor matching $T_{\mu\nu}$, and must have automatic divergence-zero (matching local conservation of energy-momentum). These three constraints, plus the requirement that the Newtonian limit hold, single out the **Einstein tensor** $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ — uniquely, by Lovelock's theorem — and the field equations are

$$G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}.$$

This is the central display of the chapter, the equation Einstein arrived at in November 1915 after almost a decade of search. Its right-hand side is the matter content; its left-hand side is the gravitational response; the coupling constant $8\pi G/c^4$ is fixed by matching the Newtonian limit. The Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is automatic from the geometry, and forces $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — local conservation of energy-momentum is not imposed, it is a consequence of the field equations themselves. This is the structural payoff of having the $-\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ term: without it, Einstein's first guess $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (which he held for two years) is inconsistent with conservation.

Section §1.2 develops the tensor analysis needed for all of this — covariant differentiation of general tensors, the Bianchi identities, the Ricci identities for commutators of covariant derivatives. Section §1.3 derives the Einstein equations from a variational principle: Hilbert's action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \int R \sqrt{-g}\, d^4 x + S_\text{matter}$, varied with respect to the metric, yields the field equations. This is the cleanest, most powerful derivation, and it generalises immediately to other contexts (with cosmological constant, with higher-curvature corrections, with matter coupled to gravity). Section §1.4 is the geometry of a spatial slice in spacetime — its second fundamental form, the Gauss-Codazzi equations — which expose the Einstein equation as a *constraint* equation on initial data plus an evolution equation, the **3+1 decomposition** that underlies numerical relativity. Section §1.5 specialises to the Schwarzschild solution: the unique spherically symmetric vacuum solution to Einstein's equations, the geometry outside a non-rotating spherical mass, the first and still most-studied exact solution, the geometry of the **Sun, Earth, and non-rotating black hole**.

The reader is assumed to know [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation|connections and covariant differentiation]] and [[Riemannian Geometry III — Riemann Curvature and Topology|the Riemann curvature tensor]] in the Lorentzian setting; [[Special Relativity I — Lorentz Transformations and Minkowski Space|special relativity]] is the local model the entire theory reduces to in a freely-falling frame; [[Differential Geometry XII — Riemannian and Semi-Riemannian Manifolds|Lorentzian manifolds]] is where the setting is defined. The genuinely new ingredient is not the differential geometry — that is borrowed wholesale from the Riemannian batch — but the *physical interpretation*: that the geometry is what gravity *is*, that the metric is a dynamical field with its own equations of motion, that matter sources curvature, and that the global structure of spacetime (light cones, event horizons, singularities) is the object of physics.

# Concept Map

## §1.1 The Equivalence Principle and Metric Potentials

- **[[Def - Spacetime Manifold]]**
	- A spacetime is a four-dimensional, smooth, connected, Hausdorff, second-countable Lorentzian manifold $(M, g)$ of signature $(+, -, -, -)$, equipped with a continuous choice of time orientation (a smooth global future-pointing timelike vector field, when one exists). Events are points; the worldline of a particle is a smooth curve $\gamma(\tau)$; the four-velocity is $\dot\gamma$. Minkowski space is the flat example; the **Schwarzschild spacetime**, **Kerr black hole**, and **FLRW cosmology** are curved examples. The assumption of global hyperbolicity (existence of a Cauchy surface) is usually added in mathematical GR to ensure a well-posed initial value problem.

- **[[Def - The Metric Tensor as Gravitational Potential]]**
	- In a local coordinate chart the metric is $ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu}(x)\, dx^\mu dx^\nu$ with $g_{\mu\nu}$ symmetric, non-degenerate, and of Lorentzian signature; its 10 independent components are the "gravitational potentials". The component $g_{00}$ recovers the Newtonian potential via $g_{00} \approx -(1 - 2\phi)$ in the weak-field slow-motion limit (with $c = 1$). The off-diagonal $g_{0i}$ describe *frame dragging* (rotation of inertial frames by moving matter), and the spatial $g_{ij}$ describe purely-spatial curvature. The full ten potentials are what enables gravitational waves, gravitomagnetism, and all the post-Newtonian effects beyond pure attraction.

- **[[Def - Equivalence Principle]]**
	- The **weak equivalence principle** asserts that the trajectory of a freely-falling test body depends only on its initial position and velocity, not on its mass or composition (Galileo's law of falling bodies). The **Einstein equivalence principle** strengthens this to: locally — in a small enough region of spacetime — the laws of physics in a freely-falling frame are exactly those of special relativity. The **strong equivalence principle** further requires this for *all* physics, including gravity itself. The principle forces the spacetime to be a Lorentzian manifold with the geodesic equation as the law of motion, with gravity encoded as curvature rather than as a force.

- **[[Thm - Newtonian Limit of Einstein's Equations]]**
	- In the slow-motion, weak-field limit — where $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$ and particle velocities are much less than $c$ — the geodesic equation reduces to $\ddot x^\alpha = \frac{1}{2} \partial^\alpha g_{00} = -\partial^\alpha \phi$ with the identification $g_{00} = -(1 - 2\phi)$. The $(0,0)$ component of the Einstein equation reduces to Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 \phi = 4\pi G \rho$. Thus general relativity contains Newton's gravity exactly as a low-velocity weak-field limit, with the gravitational potential $\phi$ identified as half the deviation of $g_{00}$ from $-1$. The fact that GR *correctly* reduces to Newton in this regime — with the right coupling constant $8\pi G$ — is what fixes the numerical factor in the field equations.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Gravitational Redshift *(from Observational General Relativity)*
> Once $g_{00}$ encodes the Newtonian potential, a clock at potential $\phi$ ticks at a rate $\sqrt{-g_{00}} \approx 1 + \phi$ relative to a clock at infinity. Light climbing out of a gravitational well loses energy: the observed frequency at infinity is $\nu_\infty = \nu_\text{emit}\sqrt{-g_{00}}$, smaller than the emitted frequency. This is the **gravitational redshift**, confirmed by Pound–Rebka (1959) measuring the $\gamma$-ray redshift over $22.5$ m in a Harvard tower, and routinely corrected for in GPS satellites (which would be off by $\sim 10$ km per day without GR corrections).

> [!tip] Unlocked: [[Def - Equivalence Principle|Equivalence Principle]] Tests *(from Experimental Gravitation)*
> The weak equivalence principle is the best-tested principle in physics. Eötvös (1908) compared falling rates of different materials to one part in $10^9$; modern lunar-laser-ranging tests compare the falling rates of the Earth and Moon toward the Sun to one part in $10^{13}$; the MICROSCOPE satellite (2017) achieved one part in $10^{15}$. **Composition-dependent violations** of the equivalence principle would signal **fifth forces** or non-universal coupling of dark matter to ordinary matter — major hunts in modern experimental gravitation.

## §1.2 Stress-Energy and Einstein Equations

- **[[Def - Stress-Energy Tensor]]**
	- The stress-energy tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ of matter (and non-gravitational fields) is the symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor whose components in an orthonormal frame are: $T_{00} =$ energy density, $T_{0i} =$ momentum density (also energy flux), $T_{ij} =$ spatial stress (pressure on the diagonal, shear off-diagonal). It is conserved in special relativity: $\partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, expressing local conservation of energy and momentum. In GR the conservation law is upgraded to $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Examples: **dust** $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho u^\mu u^\nu$, **perfect fluid** $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p) u^\mu u^\nu - p g^{\mu\nu}$, **electromagnetic field** $T^{\mu\nu} = (1/4\pi)[F^{\mu\rho} F^\nu{}_\rho - \frac{1}{4} g^{\mu\nu} F^{\rho\sigma} F_{\rho\sigma}]$.

- **[[Thm - Stress-Energy of a Perfect Fluid]]**
	- A perfect fluid is a continuous matter distribution with rest-frame energy density $\rho$, isotropic pressure $p$, and four-velocity $u^\mu$ (timelike, normalised $g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 1$). Its stress-energy tensor is $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p) u^\mu u^\nu - p g^{\mu\nu}$ (with signature $(+,-,-,-)$). The local conservation law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ decomposes into the **relativistic continuity equation** $\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu) = -p \nabla_\mu u^\mu$ and the **relativistic Euler equation** $(\rho + p) u^\mu \nabla_\mu u^\nu = -(g^{\nu\mu} - u^\nu u^\mu) \nabla_\mu p$. In the non-relativistic limit these reduce to ordinary continuity and Euler. Perfect fluids model stars, the early universe, cosmological dust ($p = 0$), and radiation ($p = \rho/3$).

- **[[Def - Einstein Tensor]]**
	- The Einstein tensor is $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$, a symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor built from the Ricci tensor and scalar of the [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation|Levi-Civita connection]]. Its key property is the **contracted Bianchi identity**: $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$, identically and automatically — it is a consequence of the second Bianchi identity for the Riemann tensor. This divergence-free property is what selects $G_{\mu\nu}$ as the unique tensor (modulo $g_{\mu\nu}$ itself and the cosmological constant) suitable as the left-hand side of the field equations: Einstein's first guess $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ fails because $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} \neq 0$ in general, while $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ matches $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ automatically.

- **[[Def - The Einstein Field Equations]]**
	- The Einstein field equations are $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}$ (or $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ in geometrised units, $G = c = 1$); they connect spacetime curvature ($G_{\mu\nu}$, built from $g$ and its first two derivatives) to matter content ($T_{\mu\nu}$). They are a coupled system of ten nonlinear second-order PDEs for the ten components of $g_{\mu\nu}$, but the contracted Bianchi identity imposes four constraints, reducing the dynamical degrees of freedom to six — and gauge invariance under [[Def - Diffeomorphism|diffeomorphisms]] removes another four, leaving two: the two polarisations of gravitational waves. The vacuum equations $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ govern regions with no matter (outside stars, in gravitational wave spacetimes, in pure black hole geometries).

- **[[Thm - Contracted Bianchi Implies Stress-Energy Conservation]]**
	- The second Bianchi identity for the Riemann tensor, $\nabla_\rho R_{\mu\nu\sigma\tau} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\mu\nu\tau\rho} + \nabla_\tau R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = 0$, when contracted twice gives $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$, identically (no equation of motion needed). The Einstein field equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ then *force* $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — local conservation of energy and momentum is a consequence, not an additional postulate. This is the structural reason for the $-\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ term: it makes the field equations automatically consistent with conservation, and without it the theory would be over-determined. It is the same logic as Maxwell adding the displacement current to ensure charge conservation.

- **[[Def - Cosmological Constant]]**
	- A modification of the Einstein equations consistent with all the structural requirements (symmetric, divergence-free, built from the metric) is to add a term $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, where $\Lambda$ is a constant — the cosmological constant. The modified equations $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ admit a static universe solution (Einstein's original motivation, abandoned after Hubble's discovery of expansion). Re-introduced in the 1990s after **Type Ia supernova** observations indicated accelerating cosmic expansion. The current best estimate $\Lambda \sim 10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ corresponds to a vacuum energy density of $\sim 10^{-29}\,\mathrm{g/cm}^3$ — the **dark energy** of contemporary cosmology, comprising roughly $70\%$ of the universe's energy budget. The discrepancy between this observed value and quantum-field-theoretic expectations ($\sim 10^{120}$ times larger) is the **cosmological constant problem**.

> [!tip] Unlocked: ADM Mass and Positive Energy Theorem *(from Mathematical General Relativity)*
> For an asymptotically flat spacetime — one approaching Minkowski space at spatial infinity — the **ADM (Arnowitt–Deser–Misner) mass** is the total energy seen by an observer at infinity, computed from a surface integral over a 2-sphere at infinity of the spatial metric and its derivatives. The **positive energy theorem** (Schoen–Yau 1979, Witten 1981) states that for any asymptotically flat spacetime satisfying the dominant energy condition, the ADM mass is non-negative, and zero iff the spacetime is Minkowski. This is a deep result in mathematical GR, tying together global geometry, dominant energy condition, and topology.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Energy Conditions *(from Causal Structure and Singularity Theorems)*
> Physical reasonableness of matter is encoded in **energy conditions** on $T_{\mu\nu}$. The **weak energy condition** $T_{\mu\nu} t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$ for every timelike $t$ (energy density non-negative in every frame); the **null energy condition** $T_{\mu\nu} k^\mu k^\nu \geq 0$ for null $k$; the **strong energy condition** $(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} T) t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$ (gravity is attractive); the **dominant energy condition** (energy flux is causal). The Penrose and Hawking **singularity theorems** show that any spacetime satisfying these conditions, with appropriate global structure, must contain incomplete causal geodesics — singularities.

- **[[Ex - Stress-Energy Tensor of Dust]]** (⭐)
	- Verify directly that the dust stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho u^\mu u^\nu$ has the right components in the rest frame ($T^{00} = \rho$, all others zero) and that $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ reproduces the relativistic continuity equation and the geodesic equation for the dust four-velocity.

- **[[Ex - Stress-Energy Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Derive the Maxwell stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu} = (1/4\pi)[F^{\mu\rho} F^\nu{}_\rho - \frac{1}{4} g^{\mu\nu} F^{\rho\sigma} F_{\rho\sigma}]$ from the Hilbert action $S_\text{em} = -\frac{1}{16\pi}\int F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x$, verify it is symmetric, traceless, and conserved when Maxwell's equations hold, and recover the classical Poynting energy density $T^{00} = (1/8\pi)(E^2 + B^2)$.

- **[[Ex - Conservation of Energy-Momentum for the Klein-Gordon Field]]** (⭐⭐)
	- For a free massive scalar field with action $S = \int [\frac{1}{2} g^{\mu\nu} \partial_\mu\phi \partial_\nu\phi - \frac{1}{2} m^2 \phi^2] \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x$, derive the stress-energy tensor and verify that $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds as a consequence of the Klein–Gordon equation $(\Box + m^2)\phi = 0$.

## §1.3 Hilbert's Variational Approach

- **[[Def - Hilbert Action]]**
	- The Hilbert (Einstein–Hilbert) action for the gravitational field on a spacetime region $M \subseteq \mathcal{M}$ is $S_\text{grav}[g] = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \int_M R\, \sqrt{-g}\, d^4 x$, a functional of the metric $g$, where $R$ is the scalar curvature. The total action is $S = S_\text{grav} + S_\text{matter}$, where $S_\text{matter}$ is built from the matter fields and the metric. The matter stress-energy tensor is *defined* by the variational formula $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta S_\text{matter}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$. The action is the simplest diffeomorphism-invariant scalar functional of $g$ that yields second-order field equations — uniqueness up to boundary terms is **Lovelock's theorem**.

- **[[Thm - Hilbert's Variational Principle Yields Einstein Equations]]**
	- **(Flagship theorem.)** Variation of the total action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int R \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x + S_\text{matter}$ with respect to the inverse metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ yields the Einstein field equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}$. The proof requires computing three pieces: the variation of $\sqrt{-g}$ (gives $-\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g}$), the variation of $R$ as $R_{\mu\nu} \delta g^{\mu\nu}$ plus a boundary term (from the variation of the Ricci tensor, which is a total divergence — the Palatini identity), and the variation of $S_\text{matter}$ as $-\frac{1}{2} T_{\mu\nu} \sqrt{-g}\, \delta g^{\mu\nu}$. The boundary term from the Ricci tensor is normally discarded for compactly-supported variations; for spacetimes with boundary, the **Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term** is added to the action to make the variational principle well-posed.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Modified Gravity and Lovelock's Theorem *(from Beyond Einstein Gravity)*
> Lovelock's theorem (1971) states that in four [[Def - Dimension|dimensions]], the Einstein tensor (plus a possible cosmological constant) is the *unique* divergence-free symmetric tensor built from $g_{\mu\nu}$ and its first two derivatives that is linear in second derivatives. So any modification of gravity in 4D — $f(R)$ gravity, scalar-tensor theories, higher-derivative gravity — must either introduce new fields, drop the linearity in second derivatives, or change the dimension. **Lovelock gravity** in higher dimensions admits richer Lagrangians (Gauss–Bonnet term in 5D, etc.), studied in **string theory** and **brane-world cosmology**.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Quantum Gravity and the Path Integral *(from Quantum Gravity)*
> Hilbert's action is the starting point for **canonical quantisation** (where it generates the **Wheeler–DeWitt equation**) and for **path-integral quantisation** ($Z = \int \mathcal{D}g\, e^{iS[g]/\hbar}$, the Euclidean version giving black hole thermodynamics). The fact that this path integral is non-renormalisable in 4D (each loop introduces new divergences requiring new counter-terms) is the deep technical obstruction to a quantum theory of gravity, motivating **string theory**, **loop quantum gravity**, and **asymptotic safety**.

## §1.4 Schwarzschild and Its Tests

- **[[Def - The Schwarzschild Metric]]**
	- The Schwarzschild metric is the unique spherically symmetric vacuum solution of the Einstein field equations, describing the geometry outside a non-rotating spherical mass $M$ (e.g., the Sun, the Earth, or a non-rotating black hole). In **Schwarzschild coordinates** $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$: $$ds^2 = \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right) dt^2 - \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1} dr^2 - r^2 (d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2).$$ Here $M$ is the gravitational mass parameter (in geometrised units, the literal mass); $r$ is *not* the radial distance from the centre but the "areal radius" — the sphere $r = a$ has area $4\pi a^2$. The coordinate $t$ is the time of an asymptotic observer at rest at infinity. The surface $r = 2M$ is the **Schwarzschild radius** or **event horizon** of a black hole; the metric is coordinate-singular there but the spacetime is regular (the singularity is genuinely at $r = 0$).

- **[[Thm - Schwarzschild Solution]]**
	- **(Flagship theorem.)** Starting from the most general spherically symmetric static ansatz $ds^2 = e^{2\Phi(r)} dt^2 - e^{2\Lambda(r)} dr^2 - r^2 d\Omega^2$, the vacuum Einstein equations $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ reduce to a system of ODEs in $r$. Combining them gives $\Phi(r) + \Lambda(r) =$ constant (chosen zero by rescaling $t$) and $e^{-2\Lambda} = 1 - 2M/r$ where $M$ is an integration constant. This yields the Schwarzschild metric, and identifying $M$ with the Newtonian mass parameter via the weak-field limit ($g_{00} = -(1 - 2M/r) \approx -(1 + 2\phi)$ with $\phi = -M/r$) fixes the interpretation. **Birkhoff's theorem** ([[Thm - Birkhoff's Theorem]]) strengthens this: spherical symmetry alone (without assuming staticity) forces the exterior solution to be Schwarzschild — radial pulsations of a star do *not* generate gravitational waves.

- **[[Thm - Birkhoff's Theorem]]**
	- The unique spherically symmetric solution of the vacuum Einstein equations in a region is the Schwarzschild metric; in particular, any spherically symmetric vacuum solution is necessarily static, even if the source is dynamically pulsating or collapsing (as long as it remains spherical). This is the GR analogue of Newton's shell theorem (the field outside a spherical mass equals that of a point mass at the centre), but stronger: it also forbids monopole gravitational radiation, since a pulsating spherical star has constant exterior metric. Gravitational radiation requires at least quadrupole asymmetry, which is why **binary inspirals** are the textbook sources of gravitational waves.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Kerr Black Hole *(from Black Hole Physics)*
> Removing the assumption of spherical symmetry and allowing axial symmetry plus stationarity gives the **Kerr black hole** family — solutions parametrised by mass $M$ and angular momentum $J$. The Kerr metric has a more complex structure: an **ergosphere** (region where no observer can remain stationary, but can still escape), an **inner Cauchy horizon**, and a *[[Def - Ring|ring]] singularity* at the equatorial plane. By the **no-hair theorem** (Israel, Carter, Robinson), the most general stationary asymptotically-flat black hole solution to vacuum Einstein with electromagnetic field is the **Kerr–Newman** family, parametrised by mass, angular momentum, and charge.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Reissner-Nordström and Charged Black Holes *(from Black Hole Physics)*
> Adding an electromagnetic field to the Schwarzschild ansatz — spherical, static, with charge $Q$ — gives the **Reissner–Nordström** metric $ds^2 = (1 - 2M/r + Q^2/r^2)dt^2 - (\ldots)^{-1} dr^2 - r^2 d\Omega^2$. The spacetime has two horizons (outer and inner) when $|Q| < M$, and the inner horizon hosts a Cauchy horizon whose stability is one of the central questions of **strong cosmic censorship**. Real astrophysical black holes are believed to be nearly neutral (the universe is charge-neutral on large scales), so Reissner–Nordström is mainly of theoretical interest as the simplest exact solution with internal structure.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Penrose Diagrams *(from Conformal Geometry of Spacetimes)*
> The global causal structure of the Schwarzschild and Reissner–Nordström spacetimes is revealed by **Penrose diagrams**: conformal compactifications that map the entire spacetime onto a finite picture, with null rays at $45^\circ$ and infinity drawn as boundary lines. The maximally extended Schwarzschild solution (Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates) reveals two asymptotically flat regions connected by a "wormhole" (an Einstein–Rosen bridge), with two black-hole regions, in a diagram with the symmetry of an hourglass. Penrose diagrams are the working tool of mathematical GR for analysing causal structure.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Singularity Theorems *(from Mathematical General Relativity)*
> The Schwarzschild singularity at $r = 0$ is a genuine spacetime singularity (the curvature scalar $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ diverges), not a coordinate artefact. **Hawking–Penrose singularity theorems** generalise: under mild assumptions (energy conditions, global hyperbolicity, trapped surface or sufficient symmetry), every spacetime contains incomplete causal geodesics — physical singularities are unavoidable. The proofs use **Raychaudhuri's equation** for the focusing of null geodesic congruences, not the Einstein equations directly; they apply to any reasonable theory of gravity matching GR's causal structure.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Black Hole Thermodynamics *(from Quantum Gravity)*
> The event horizon of a Schwarzschild black hole has area $A = 16\pi M^2$. Hawking (1974) showed that quantum effects cause the black hole to radiate at the **Hawking temperature** $T_H = \hbar/(8\pi M)$ — a quantum-gravitational result that ties black-hole physics to thermodynamics. The **Bekenstein–Hawking entropy** $S_\text{BH} = A/(4 \hbar G)$ — area divided by four Planck areas — is the entropy of a black hole, and the **four laws of black hole mechanics** are formally identical to the laws of thermodynamics. This is a central clue to **quantum gravity**, suggesting that gravitational degrees of freedom live on lower-dimensional surfaces — the **holographic principle**.

> [!tip] Unlocked: AdS/CFT Correspondence *(from String Theory)*
> Spacetimes with negative cosmological constant ($\Lambda < 0$) are **anti-de Sitter** (AdS), the maximally-symmetric vacuum solution. Maldacena's **AdS/CFT correspondence** (1997) conjectures that quantum gravity on $\text{AdS}_{d+1}$ is dual to a conformal field theory living on its $d$-dimensional boundary — a precise realisation of the holographic principle. The correspondence has become a major tool in **strongly-coupled QFT** (computing properties of quark-gluon plasmas, high-temperature superconductors, etc.) by translating to weakly-coupled gravity calculations on the AdS side.

- **[[Ex - Computing the Ricci Tensor of the Schwarzschild Metric]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Compute the Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor, and Ricci tensor of the Schwarzschild metric using the orthonormal frame (Cartan) method or by direct coordinate computation. Verify that $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, confirming the vacuum equations are satisfied. This is the cleanest way to *prove* (rather than guess) that the Schwarzschild metric is a solution.

- **[[Ex - The Schwarzschild Radius and the Event Horizon]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show that the Schwarzschild radius $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ is a *coordinate* singularity, not a curvature singularity: the curvature scalars are finite there, but the metric components blow up. Construct **Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates** $v = t + r + 2M \ln|r/(2M) - 1|$ (advanced) and show the metric extends regularly across $r = 2M$. Compute the proper time for a radially infalling observer to cross the horizon (finite) versus the coordinate time $t$ (infinite) — the observer falls in, but appears to an outside observer to slow asymptotically and freeze.

- **[[Ex - Newtonian Limit Recovers Poisson Equation]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Linearise the Einstein equations around Minkowski, $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$ and slow matter. Identify $h_{00} = -2\phi$ (with $\phi$ the Newtonian potential) and show the $(0,0)$ component of $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$ reduces to $\nabla^2 \phi = 4\pi G \rho$, Poisson's equation. This is the fixed coupling constant in the field equations.

- **[[Ex - Light Bending Around the Sun (Order of Magnitude)]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Compute the deflection angle of a light ray grazing the Sun, using the Schwarzschild metric. The deflection is $\Delta\phi = 4GM_\odot/(b c^2)$, where $b$ is the impact parameter; for $b = R_\odot$ (grazing) this gives $\approx 1.75$ arcseconds — exactly twice the value a naive Newtonian computation (treating light as a particle moving at $c$) would give. Eddington's 1919 eclipse observation confirmed the GR value, securing GR's place as the correct theory of gravity.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The exercises of a general relativity chapter chase a small, recognisable set of goals. The most common is to **derive or verify an exact solution of the Einstein equations**: given symmetry assumptions (spherical, axial, stationary, homogeneous-isotropic) and matter content (vacuum, perfect fluid, electromagnetic), write the most general metric consistent with the symmetry, substitute into the field equations, and solve the resulting ODEs. This is how one obtains Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström, Kerr, FLRW, de Sitter, anti-de Sitter, and the various exact black-hole and cosmological solutions. A second target is to **compute a geometric or physical observable in a known spacetime**: the trajectory of a freely-falling particle (geodesic), the redshift of a light signal, the time of flight, the precession of an orbit, the bending of a light ray — all are integrals along geodesics in a metric one already knows. A third is to **establish a conservation law or constraint**: $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ from the field equations, conservation laws from Killing vectors (Noether), the ADM constraints on a spatial slice from the Gauss–Codazzi equations. A fourth is to **classify the global structure** of a known solution: identify horizons, singularities, asymptotic regions, causal structure, and produce a Penrose diagram. A fifth, more analytic, is to **prove a theorem of the geometry**: positivity of mass, uniqueness of stationary black holes (no-hair), singularity theorems, area theorems. Across all five the unifying remark is that the field equations are *both* a PDE system for $g$ (most exercises are about solving them in special cases) *and* a geometric condition encoding the equivalence principle (most theoretical results are about consequences of this structure). You understand a GR problem when you can flip between the two viewpoints: a metric is a PDE solution and a geometric object simultaneously.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A symmetry is given**: spherical, axial, time-translation, spatial homogeneity, isotropy — and the symmetry restricts the metric to an ansatz with few free functions, converting the field PDEs into ODEs. Spherical symmetry alone, combined with vacuum, forces the Schwarzschild solution (**Birkhoff's theorem**); time-translation invariance plus axial symmetry plus asymptotic flatness leads to the Kerr family. **A matter content is given**: vacuum ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, giving $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$), perfect fluid (Schwarzschild interior, FLRW), electromagnetic field (Reissner–Nordström), scalar field. Each matter type contributes a specific $T_{\mu\nu}$ that the curvature must match. **An asymptotic structure is given**: asymptotically flat (approaches Minkowski at spatial infinity), asymptotically de Sitter, asymptotically anti-de Sitter — these constrain the integration constants in solving the field equations and define total mass, charge, and angular momentum at infinity. **An initial-value problem is given**: an initial spatial slice $V^3$ with induced metric $h_{\alpha\beta}$ and second fundamental form $b_{\alpha\beta}$ satisfying the **Gauss–Codazzi constraints** (the $G_{0\mu}$ components of Einstein's equations), and the evolution equations (the $G_{\alpha\beta}$ components) propagate forward. This is the **3+1 decomposition** and the basis of **numerical relativity**. **An energy condition is given**: $T_{\mu\nu} t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$ for every timelike $t$ (weak), $T_{\mu\nu} k^\mu k^\nu \geq 0$ for null $k$, etc. — these are the inputs to **focusing theorems** (Raychaudhuri's equation) and **singularity theorems** (Penrose, Hawking). The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a given symmetry routes through the ansatz to ODEs, which integrate to an exact solution; a given matter content routes through $T_{\mu\nu}$ to a specific right-hand side of Einstein's equations; an energy condition routes through Raychaudhuri to focusing and through focusing to singularity theorems.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves almost every problem in the topic is assembled from. The bookkeeping is *which tensor index lives in which frame* (coordinate vs. orthonormal), and what is the local geometric meaning of each quantity. When stuck, scan the list and try each one.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Lift Newtonian intuitions to GR by replacing the metric with a curved one and the partial derivative with a covariant one.** A Newtonian gravitational potential $\phi(x)$ becomes the metric component $g_{00}(x) \approx -(1 - 2\phi)$; Newton's $\ddot x = -\nabla \phi$ becomes the [[Riemannian Geometry II — Geodesics, the Exponential Map, and Variational Principles|geodesic equation]] $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho} \dot x^\nu \dot x^\rho = 0$; the Newtonian density $\rho$ becomes the $T_{00}$ component of [[Def - Stress-Energy Tensor|the stress-energy tensor]]; Poisson's $\nabla^2 \phi = 4\pi G \rho$ becomes Einstein's $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$. *Trigger:* you have a Newtonian formula and want to know its GR form. *Pattern:* identify the Newtonian piece in $g_{00}$, then upgrade scalar densities to $T_{\mu\nu}$ and partial derivatives to covariant ones.

2. **Use the equivalence principle to replace gravity with acceleration locally.** At any single event $p$ one can find coordinates in which $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ — the **locally inertial frame** at $p$. In this frame, the laws of physics at $p$ are exactly those of special relativity, and gravity *disappears*. *Trigger:* you want to evaluate a tensor equation at a single point, or to understand a local physical phenomenon (free fall, equivalence of acceleration and gravity). *Pattern:* introduce normal coordinates at $p$, in which all $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}(p) = 0$, perform the calculation in flat space, then use covariance to lift to a general frame. This is a powerful labour-saving device.

3. **Impose symmetry to reduce the field equations to ODEs.** If the metric admits a continuous symmetry — a **Killing vector field** $K$ satisfying $\nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu = 0$ — then one can choose coordinates in which the metric components are independent of the corresponding coordinate. Time-translation symmetry (stationarity) makes $g_{\mu\nu}$ independent of $t$; spherical symmetry makes them depend only on $r$. The Einstein PDEs reduce to ODEs in the remaining variable. *Trigger:* a problem mentions "spherically symmetric", "stationary", "homogeneous isotropic" — these signpost a symmetry-restricted ansatz. *Pattern:* write the most general metric of that symmetry, substitute into $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} T)$, integrate the ODE system.

4. **Compute the Riemann/Ricci tensor in an orthonormal frame using Cartan's structural equations.** Rather than computing Christoffel symbols directly in a coordinate basis (often a long calculation), choose an orthonormal coframe $\{e^a\}$ for the metric, compute the connection 1-forms $\omega^a{}_b$ from $de^a + \omega^a{}_b \wedge e^b = 0$ and $\omega_{ab} = -\omega_{ba}$, then compute curvature 2-forms $\Omega^a{}_b = d\omega^a{}_b + \omega^a{}_c \wedge \omega^c{}_b$. The Riemann components $R^a{}_{bcd}$ read off from $\Omega^a{}_b = \frac{1}{2} R^a{}_{bcd} e^c \wedge e^d$. *Trigger:* an explicit metric with diagonal or near-diagonal form. *Pattern:* read off the coframe from $ds^2 = \eta_{ab} e^a \otimes e^b$, then mechanical computation via Cartan's equations.

5. **Use the contracted Bianchi identity to deduce conservation.** The geometric identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is automatic from the second Bianchi identity; combined with the Einstein equation $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, it forces $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$. *Trigger:* you want to derive a conservation law for matter without separately postulating it. *Pattern:* the four equations $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ are the relativistic version of energy conservation ($\nu = 0$) and momentum conservation ($\nu = i$); they unpack into the relativistic continuity and Euler equations for a perfect fluid, the Lorentz force law for a charged fluid in an EM field, and the geodesic equation for dust.

6. **Vary the Hilbert action to obtain field equations.** For any new matter or modified gravity theory, write the action $S = S_\text{grav}[g] + S_\text{matter}[g, \psi]$ and demand $\delta S = 0$ under variations $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (gives the gravitational field equation, with $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \delta S_\text{matter}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$) and $\delta \psi$ (gives the matter equations of motion). *Trigger:* you want to derive the stress-energy tensor of some matter, or to introduce a new matter coupling. *Pattern:* the variational route is the *only* systematic way to ensure the stress-energy tensor is automatically conserved (by diffeomorphism invariance of $S_\text{matter}$) and that the field equations are consistent.

7. **Decompose into 3+1 form for an initial-value problem.** Foliate spacetime by spacelike hypersurfaces $V^3(t)$ with induced metric $h_{\alpha\beta}$, unit normal $N$, and second fundamental form $b_{\alpha\beta}$. The Einstein equations split into **constraint equations** (on each $V^3$: the Gauss–Codazzi equations $G_{0\nu} = 8\pi T_{0\nu}$) and **evolution equations** (governing the time-development of $h_{\alpha\beta}$ and $b_{\alpha\beta}$). *Trigger:* you want to study time evolution, numerical simulation, or the structure of initial data. *Pattern:* this is the **ADM (Arnowitt–Deser–Misner) formulation**, the basis of numerical relativity and canonical quantum gravity.

8. **Pass to a coordinate-singularity-free chart.** Schwarzschild's $g_{rr} = -(1 - 2M/r)^{-1}$ blows up at $r = 2M$, but this is a coordinate artefact: the curvature is finite there, and **Eddington–Finkelstein** or **Kruskal–Szekeres** coordinates extend the metric smoothly across the horizon. *Trigger:* a metric component blows up (or vanishes pathologically) at some surface, but you suspect the geometry is regular there. *Pattern:* compute curvature scalars to check — finite scalars at a coordinate singularity, infinite scalars at a curvature singularity — then construct adapted null coordinates that extend across.

9. **Identify Killing vectors and the conserved quantities they generate.** Each Killing vector $K$ of a metric $g$ yields a conserved quantity along geodesics: if $\gamma$ is a geodesic with tangent $\dot\gamma$, then $g(K, \dot\gamma) =$ constant along $\gamma$ (Noether's theorem for spacetime symmetries). In Schwarzschild, the Killing vector $\partial_t$ gives conserved **energy**, and $\partial_\phi$ gives conserved **angular momentum**. *Trigger:* you want to integrate geodesics in a symmetric spacetime — instead of solving the second-order geodesic equation, use the conserved quantities to reduce to a first-order ODE in $r$.

10. **Use the geodesic equation as the law of motion.** A free (test) particle follows a timelike geodesic of $g$; light follows a null geodesic. In coordinates: $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho} \dot x^\nu \dot x^\rho = 0$, where the dot is differentiation with respect to **proper time** for timelike geodesics or an **affine parameter** for null. *Trigger:* you want to compute the trajectory of a particle or light ray in a known metric. *Pattern:* combine with Killing-vector conservation laws (legal operation 9) to reduce to a first-order ODE in the radial coordinate.

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Treating the metric as a "background" and matter as living on it independently
> The metric is a *dynamical field* in GR: it has its own equation of motion (the Einstein equations) and responds to matter. One cannot, except in **test-particle approximation** (small mass that does not back-react), specify both the metric and the matter independently — they must be a consistent solution of the coupled Einstein-matter system. The illusion is fostered by special relativity, where the Minkowski metric is genuinely fixed background. In GR this fails: a large mass distorts the spacetime around it, and the test-particle approximation breaks down for binaries, gravitational wave sources, and the early universe. The "becomes legal exactly when..." condition: the test-particle limit is legal when the test body's stress-energy is negligible compared to the background sources — i.e., when its mass is small and its size irrelevant.

> [!warning] 2. Coordinate-dependent statements masquerading as physics
> The Schwarzschild metric component $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r)$ vanishes at $r = 2M$, but this *coordinate* fact does not mean a horizon-crosser experiences something singular: in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, the metric is regular there. Conversely, a metric can look perfectly nice in some coordinates while hiding genuine singularities (rotating $\mathbb{R}^2$ in cylindrical coordinates is regular except at $r = 0$, but the cylindrical chart shows no overt pathology). The repair: phrase physical statements in terms of *scalars* — proper time, geodesic completeness, curvature invariants ($R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$) — not coordinate components. A claim about $g_{tt}$ or $\Gamma^\mu$ at a particular point is, by itself, not a physical statement.

> [!warning] 3. Using the Newtonian formula $F = GMm/r^2$ in strong-field regimes
> Newton's gravitational force, with $r$ taken as the radial Schwarzschild coordinate, gives wrong answers near a black hole or for relativistic objects. Newtonian gravity is the $r \gg 2M$ weak-field limit; near $r = 2M$ the orbits deviate substantially, with the **innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO)** at $r = 6M$ (no stable circular orbits below this), light bending of $4GM/(bc^2)$ rather than $2GM/(bc^2)$, and perihelion precession at $43''/$century for Mercury. The repair: use the full geodesic equation in the Schwarzschild metric, not the Newtonian inverse-square law, whenever $v/c$ or $GM/(rc^2)$ is not small.

> [!warning] 4. Choosing $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ as the field equation
> This was Einstein's first guess (October 1915), and it is *wrong*. The Ricci tensor is *not* divergence-free in general: $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} \nabla_\nu R$ (from the contracted Bianchi identity), so $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ would force $R$ constant — an unphysical constraint. With $T_{\mu\nu}$ on the right-hand side, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (local conservation) is then violated. The repair is exactly the structural fix Einstein arrived at in November 1915: subtract $\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ to form the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$, which is identically divergence-free by the contracted Bianchi identity. The illegal-but-tempting form becomes legal only in the **vacuum** case ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$), where $R = 0$ and the two formulations coincide.

> [!warning] 5. Confusing the radial coordinate $r$ with proper distance
> In Schwarzschild, $r$ is the **areal radius** — a sphere $r = a$ has area $4\pi a^2$ — but the proper *distance* between two spheres $r = r_1$ and $r = r_2$ along a radial line in a spatial slice is $\int_{r_1}^{r_2} (1 - 2M/r)^{-1/2}\, dr > r_2 - r_1$. So in Schwarzschild, going inward you cross more proper distance than the change in $r$ suggests; the spatial geometry of constant-$t$ slices is curved (a "Flamm paraboloid"). The repair: distinguish coordinate radius from proper radial distance, integrate $\sqrt{g_{rr}}\, dr$ for proper distance, and never assume $r$ is what it would be in flat space.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this topic split cleanly by the **kind of question being asked**, and the decisive first move is always to classify which one.

If the problem **asks to derive or verify an exact solution**, the strategy is: identify the symmetries (spherical, axial, stationary, homogeneous-isotropic), write the most general metric ansatz consistent with the symmetry, identify the matter ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for vacuum, perfect fluid, EM field), substitute into the Einstein equations to get ODEs in the surviving coordinates, integrate. The Schwarzschild derivation ([[Thm - Schwarzschild Solution]]) is the canonical example. The art is in choosing the right symmetry-adapted coordinates: spherical means $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$ with $g$ depending only on $r$; axial-stationary means $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$ with $g$ depending only on $r, \theta$. Once the ansatz is written, the Einstein PDE system reduces to a small number of ODEs (Schwarzschild: two ODEs, one variable, one integration constant); for vacuum, the system $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is decoupled enough to integrate by hand.

If the problem **asks to compute a geometric or physical observable in a known spacetime**, you are integrating the geodesic equation. The strategy is: use the [[Riemannian Geometry II — Geodesics, the Exponential Map, and Variational Principles|geodesic Lagrangian]] $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu$ and the Euler–Lagrange equations, identify the Killing vectors (legal operation 9), use them to extract conserved energy $E = -g_{0\mu} \dot x^\mu$ and angular momentum $L = g_{\phi\mu} \dot x^\mu$, reduce the geodesic equation to a first-order radial equation $(\dot r)^2 = E^2 - V_\text{eff}(r)$ with an effective potential, and analyse. This is how one computes light bending (use null geodesics with $E$ and $L$ conserved), perihelion precession (integrate the timelike radial equation), and the innermost stable circular orbit (minimum of $V_\text{eff}$).

If the problem **asks to establish a conservation law or constraint**, the route is via the contracted Bianchi identity. The structural fact $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is automatic; combined with $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ it gives $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically — and this *is* the conservation of energy and momentum for matter. Specific conservation laws for specific matter types (continuity equation for dust, Euler equation for perfect fluids, Lorentz force law for charged fluids) are unpacked components of this. For constraint equations on a spatial slice, the source is the Gauss–Codazzi equations: project the Einstein equation along the normal $N$ to get the **Hamiltonian constraint** $R^{(3)} + (\mathrm{tr}\, b)^2 - b_{\alpha\beta} b^{\alpha\beta} = 16\pi \rho$, and along $V^3$ to get the **momentum constraint**.

If the problem **asks to classify the global structure** of a solution, the strategy is: identify horizons (where $g_{00}$ vanishes for stationary metrics, or more generally where a one-way membrane forms), check whether singularities are coordinate or curvature (compute curvature scalars), construct alternative coordinates that extend across coordinate singularities, identify the maximal extension, then draw the **Penrose diagram** — a conformal compactification that maps the entire spacetime to a finite picture preserving causal relationships. This is how Kruskal–Szekeres globally extended the Schwarzschild solution, revealing the wormhole and the second asymptotic region.

If the problem **asks to prove a theorem of the geometry** — uniqueness of black holes, positivity of mass, singularity formation — the routes are more varied and depend on the theorem. The toolkit includes: energy conditions on $T_{\mu\nu}$, focusing theorems (**Raychaudhuri's equation** for geodesic congruences), variational principles, conformal techniques, spinor methods (Witten's proof of positive mass uses spinors). These are advanced topics that take entire textbooks; what one carries from this chapter is the Einstein equation as the starting point and the contracted Bianchi identity as the structural cement.

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **the metric is the central object**. Every question becomes a question about $g_{\mu\nu}$ — its symmetries, its curvature, its initial data, its global structure — and every answer comes from solving or analysing the field equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. The metric is to GR what the wave function is to quantum mechanics: the complete description of the system, the thing that all observables are computed from, the variable in the equation of motion. Once this is internalised, the entire subject organises around computing, classifying, and exploiting metrics.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **The Einstein equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$**: ten coupled nonlinear second-order PDEs for the metric. The reusable content is that they are the *only* way (modulo cosmological constant) to consistently couple matter (via $T_{\mu\nu}$) to spacetime geometry (via $G_{\mu\nu}$) in a diffeomorphism-invariant theory with second-order field equations. Whenever a problem mentions matter, gravitational sources, or curved spacetime dynamics, this is what to write down. The contracted Bianchi identity makes them automatically consistent with local energy-momentum conservation, which is why they are *the* field equations and not some nearby variant.

- **The Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$**: identically true from the geometry of the [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation|Levi-Civita connection]]. Combined with the field equations, it gives $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — conservation of energy-momentum without separate postulate. The reusable skill is to recognise this as a "free" consistency check: any candidate matter coupling must yield a $T_{\mu\nu}$ that is automatically conserved when matter equations of motion hold, and this is guaranteed if $T_{\mu\nu}$ is derived from a diffeomorphism-invariant matter action (legal operation 6).

- **The geodesic equation $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho} \dot x^\nu \dot x^\rho = 0$**: the law of motion of free test bodies (timelike for matter, null for light). Reach for it whenever you need a trajectory in a known metric — its first integrals from Killing vectors reduce it to ODEs, and its careful analysis gives orbital precession, light bending, infall times, and the existence of innermost stable orbits. It is the *one* equation of motion in GR; everything else (the Lorentz force in EM, the relativistic Euler equation in fluids) is a generalisation built on top.

- **Coordinate freedom (diffeomorphism invariance)**: physical statements must be coordinate-independent. The reusable move is to phrase results in terms of scalars — proper time intervals, curvature invariants, geodesic completeness, redshifts — and to use coordinate choices (locally inertial, Schwarzschild, Kruskal, Eddington–Finkelstein) as labour-saving devices, not physical postulates. A surprising fact (like "$g_{tt} = 0$ at $r = 2M$") that depends on the coordinate is a coordinate fact, not a physical one; one must check whether scalars also blow up to identify a genuine singularity. This discipline is what saves you from claiming, with Schwarzschild, that the horizon is singular when it is not.

- **The contracted-twice second Bianchi identity / Lovelock's theorem**: in four dimensions, the *only* symmetric, divergence-free $(0,2)$-tensor built from $g$ and at most its second derivatives, linear in those derivatives, is $aG_{\mu\nu} + b g_{\mu\nu}$ — Einstein plus cosmological constant. The reusable content is that the form of the field equations is essentially forced; any modification of gravity in 4D requires either adding new fields (scalar-tensor), going to higher derivatives (with attendant instabilities), or higher dimensions (Lovelock gravity). When evaluating a proposed alternative gravity theory, Lovelock's theorem says: "what new ingredient does it introduce?"

# Bridges

1. **Riemannian Geometry — GR is pseudo-Riemannian geometry in physical garb.** Every mathematical object in GR — the metric $g$, the Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}$, the Riemann tensor $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$, the Ricci tensor and scalar — is exactly the object studied in Riemannian geometry, except that the metric is now of **Lorentzian** signature $(+, -, -, -)$ rather than Euclidean. The Levi-Civita connection of a Lorentzian metric (covered in [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation]]) is defined by exactly the same formula as the Riemannian one, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} g^{\rho\sigma}(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu})$, with the metric simply allowed to have indefinite signature. The geodesic equation $\ddot\gamma^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho} \dot\gamma^\nu \dot\gamma^\rho = 0$ from [[Riemannian Geometry II — Geodesics, the Exponential Map, and Variational Principles]] is the GR equation of motion — only now its solutions split into timelike, null, and spacelike classes, with timelike geodesics representing massive particle worldlines and null geodesics representing light rays. The Riemann tensor of [[Riemannian Geometry III — Riemann Curvature and Topology]] is the obstruction to flatness, and its first Bianchi identity is what gives Ricci symmetry while the second Bianchi identity, *contracted twice*, gives $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — the geometric identity that powers all of GR's consistency. The single physical-content statement of GR is that the Ricci-Einstein side of these geometric structures equals the matter side $8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. The mathematical content is entirely inherited from Riemannian geometry; the physical content is the identification.

2. **Special Relativity — GR reduces to SR in every locally inertial frame, and to SR globally when curvature vanishes.** The flat Minkowski metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ of [[Special Relativity I — Lorentz Transformations and Minkowski Space]] is the *tangent-space* model for the curved metric $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ of GR. At every event $p$ of spacetime, one can introduce **locally inertial coordinates** in which $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$, so the laws of physics at $p$ are *exactly* those of special relativity — this is the Einstein equivalence principle made precise. Globally, when the Riemann tensor vanishes everywhere, the metric is Minkowski everywhere and GR reduces to SR. The four-vectors, four-velocity, four-momentum, and energy-momentum tensor of SR all generalise: a [[Def - Four-Vector|four-vector]] is now a vector in a tangent space (different at each event), parallel transport (via $\nabla$) is the GR replacement for moving SR objects between distant events, and the [[Def - Stress-Energy Tensor|stress-energy tensor]] of SR becomes the right-hand side of the Einstein equation. The principle "spacetime is locally Minkowskian" is what makes SR the linearisation of GR around any single event, and what justifies importing SR particle physics (relativistic kinematics of collisions, scattering, etc.) into GR with only minor modifications.

3. **Gauge Theory — gravity is a "gauge theory of the Lorentz group", but a peculiar one.** In **Yang-Mills theory** ([[Gauge Theory V — Hodge Theory, Maxwell, Yang–Mills, and Instantons]]) the gauge field is a connection on a principal bundle with structure group $G$, and the field strength $F_{\mu\nu}$ is its curvature. In GR, the analogue gauge group is the Lorentz group $SO(1,3)$ acting on the tangent space at each event, and the analogue gauge field is the spin connection $\omega^a{}_b$ on the **frame bundle**. The Riemann tensor is the curvature of this connection — exactly as $F_{\mu\nu}$ is the curvature of the Yang-Mills connection. The peculiarity is that gravity has an *additional* structure not present in Yang-Mills: the soldering form (the tetrad $e^a$) that identifies the abstract gauge bundle with the tangent bundle of the manifold. This extra structure is what makes the Einstein action $\int R \sqrt{-g}$ *linear* in the Riemann tensor (after contraction) — Yang-Mills is *quadratic*, $\int F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}$ — and is the origin of the difficulty of quantising gravity. The electromagnetic stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_\text{EM}$ that sources GR is, on the gauge-theory side, the energy-momentum carried by the $U(1)$ Yang-Mills field, and the coupling of GR to EM is the coupling of curvature of two different connections — Riemannian and $U(1)$ — through the matter action.

4. **PDE theory — Einstein's equations are a hyperbolic PDE system once gauge-fixed.** The Einstein equations, viewed as PDEs for $g_{\mu\nu}$, are *not* hyperbolic in their raw form (because of the diffeomorphism gauge freedom — any solution can be transformed by a coordinate change, giving infinitely many equivalent descriptions). In **harmonic coordinates** (where $g^{\mu\nu} \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = 0$), the field equations become a quasi-linear hyperbolic system, the **reduced Einstein equations**, with characteristic speed equal to the speed of light. The Cauchy problem — given initial data on a spacelike slice satisfying the constraint equations, find a solution to the evolution equations — is *well-posed* in this gauge (Choquet-Bruhat, 1952). This is the basis of **numerical relativity** and of the global existence and uniqueness theorems for asymptotically flat data (Christodoulou–Klainerman: small initial data give geodesically complete solutions). The structural fact is that the constraint equations are preserved by the evolution: if they hold on the initial slice, they hold on every later slice — exactly because $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$.

5. **Functional analysis and variational principles — Hilbert's action exhibits gravity as a geometric Lagrangian field theory.** The Hilbert action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int R \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x + S_\text{matter}$ is a functional on the space of Lorentzian metrics, and the Einstein equations are its **Euler–Lagrange equations** — exactly as the Yang-Mills equations are the EL equations of the Yang-Mills action, and Maxwell's equations are the EL equations of the EM action $-\frac{1}{4}\int F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}$. This places GR in the broader framework of **classical field theory** as a Lagrangian system, where every continuous symmetry of the action gives a conserved current via **Noether's theorem**. Diffeomorphism invariance of $S_\text{matter}$ implies $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — local energy-momentum conservation is a Noether identity, not an independent postulate. The variational framework also generalises immediately: with cosmological constant ($S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int (R - 2\Lambda) \sqrt{-g}\, d^4x$), with higher-curvature terms (Gauss-Bonnet, $f(R)$ gravity), or with new matter couplings (scalar fields, gauge fields, fermions).

6. **Number theory / arithmetic — the rationality of the cosmological constant problem.** The observed cosmological constant $\Lambda \sim 10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ corresponds to an energy density $\sim 10^{-29}\,\mathrm{g/cm}^3$, but naive quantum field theory estimates from vacuum fluctuations of all known fields give a value about $10^{120}$ times larger — the largest discrepancy in all of physics. The **cosmological constant problem** is the question of why the actual value is so small compared to its "natural" QFT estimate. This is not a bridge to a different *field* of mathematics in the usual sense, but it is a bridge to a different *type of question*: whether the small value of $\Lambda$ is fine-tuned, anthropically selected (only universes with small $\Lambda$ permit galaxies and observers), or determined by some unknown mechanism (a **quintessence** field that dynamically relaxes $\Lambda$, a symmetry that forbids large $\Lambda$, an unknown cancellation principle). Modern approaches via **string theory** populate a **landscape** of $10^{500}$ vacua, of which a small fraction have $\Lambda$ as small as observed — turning the problem into a question of statistical distribution rather than a single fine-tuning.

7. **Causal structure and topology — the global geometry of spacetime is a topological-geometric subject in its own right.** Once the local Einstein equations are accepted, the global question is: what are the topological possibilities for a spacetime, and how do the equations constrain them? This is the subject of **mathematical general relativity** and includes the **singularity theorems** of Penrose and Hawking (every "physically reasonable" spacetime contains incomplete causal geodesics — singularities are unavoidable), the **topology censorship** theorems (the topology of a black hole horizon must be spherical in 4D), the **positive mass theorem** of Schoen–Yau and Witten (asymptotically flat spacetimes have non-negative total mass), and the **Penrose inequality** (the total mass exceeds the area of any apparent horizon, divided by $\sqrt{16\pi}$). The tools are conformal compactification (Penrose diagrams), focusing theorems for null and timelike geodesics (Raychaudhuri's equation), and the analysis of causal structure on Lorentzian manifolds (Cauchy surfaces, global hyperbolicity, achronal sets). These results are *consequences* of the Einstein equation combined with reasonable matter conditions, and they form the structural backbone of contemporary relativistic geometry.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: gravity is curvature, not force.** The single conceptual overturning of GR is that *gravity is not a force in the Newtonian sense*. A free-falling particle is following a straight line in curved spacetime, and the apparent "force" we feel standing on the Earth is the normal force the Earth exerts to prevent us from following our natural geodesic toward the centre. This is not a metaphor — it is the precise content of the equivalence principle, and it is what justifies replacing $F = ma$ with the geodesic equation as the law of motion. Once internalised, every problem becomes a problem about geometry: "what is the geometry of spacetime around this mass?", "what geodesics does this geometry support?", "what curvature invariants are physically measurable?". The Einstein equations are then *not* an equation of force or energy balance; they are the rule for how matter curves spacetime, the geometric analogue of how charge sources the EM field.

**The true name of the Einstein equations is "spacetime tells matter how to move, matter tells spacetime how to curve."** John Wheeler's aphorism captures the structural content. The matter side ($T_{\mu\nu}$) sources the geometry side ($G_{\mu\nu}$): given matter, you solve Einstein's equations for the metric. The geometry side then dictates the motion of further matter via the geodesic equation: given the metric, geodesics are determined. The two halves are coupled — a moving body affects the metric, which affects how the body moves — and the coupling is nonlinear, which is why exact solutions are rare and most realistic problems require either approximation methods (post-Newtonian expansion, weak-field) or numerical simulation. This nonlinearity is why GR is so much harder than electromagnetism: in EM, charges source the field linearly, and the field acts on charges without feedback that changes the source. In GR, matter sources gravity, which moves matter, which changes the source.

**A trigger-reaction pattern: when you see "spherically symmetric and static" think "Schwarzschild".** This is the single most reusable specific recognition in the topic. Outside any spherically symmetric, non-rotating mass distribution — a star, a planet, a non-rotating black hole — the metric is necessarily Schwarzschild, by **Birkhoff's theorem**. Even when the source is dynamically pulsating (spherically), the *exterior* is Schwarzschild. So in any astrophysical problem involving the gravity outside a roughly spherical body, the first move is to write down the Schwarzschild metric and compute geodesics in it. This includes light bending around the Sun, perihelion precession of Mercury, gravitational redshift, Shapiro time delay, and the orbits around a non-rotating black hole. The companion pattern: when you see "spherical *and rotating*", think Kerr; when you see "homogeneous and isotropic", think FLRW; when you see "asymptotically flat and stationary", think the no-hair theorem (Kerr–Newman is the most general).

**The contracted Bianchi identity is the structural cement of GR.** Without it, the field equations would have to be supplemented by an *additional* postulate of energy-momentum conservation, and there would be no guarantee that the two were consistent. With it — $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically — conservation is forced by the geometry itself. This is the deep reason why the $-\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ term is in the Einstein tensor: it converts the not-divergence-free Ricci tensor into the identically-divergence-free Einstein tensor. Einstein's first guess $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ failed because of this very issue, and the November 1915 correction was exactly to add the $-\frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$ term. The pattern recurs: any divergence-free tensor identity in a geometric theory is enforcing a conservation law via the field equations, and identifying these identities is the structural skeleton of any field theory.

**Inheritance from special relativity: where does the form of $T_{\mu\nu}$ come from?** The stress-energy tensor of a perfect fluid is $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p) u^\mu u^\nu - p g^{\mu\nu}$ (signature $+---$), and the form looks somewhat arbitrary. It is not: it is the unique symmetric $(2,0)$-tensor depending only on rest-frame energy density $\rho$, isotropic pressure $p$, and four-velocity $u^\mu$, which in the rest frame ($u^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 0)$) reduces to $\mathrm{diag}(\rho, p, p, p)$. The $g^{\mu\nu}$ in the second term ensures the spatial isotropy of the pressure (it would have to be $-\delta^{\mu\nu}$ in the rest frame if we wrote spatial components, and $-g^{\mu\nu} - u^\mu u^\nu / c^2$ extracts the spatial projection). The whole structure is inherited from the rest-frame analysis — the same trick that made special relativity workable: work in the rest frame, where the physics is simple, then transform out by Lorentz invariance. In GR, the "rest frame" is replaced by a locally inertial frame, and the tensor structure ensures the result is the same in every frame.

**The geometric meaning of the $00$ Einstein equation: a sum of sectional curvatures equals energy density.** Frankel's exposition (his §11.5) makes a beautiful geometric point: the $G_{00}$ component of the Einstein equation, in an orthonormal frame with $e_0$ timelike, equals $K(e_1 \wedge e_2) + K(e_1 \wedge e_3) + K(e_2 \wedge e_3)$ — the sum of sectional curvatures of the three spatial 2-planes through the point. Combined with the Gauss equation for a spatial slice ($K_M(e_\alpha \wedge e_\beta) = K_V(e_\alpha \wedge e_\beta) + \kappa_\alpha \kappa_\beta$), the Einstein equation $G_{00} = 8\pi \rho$ becomes $\frac{1}{2} R_V + (\kappa_1 \kappa_2 + \kappa_1 \kappa_3 + \kappa_2 \kappa_3) = 8\pi \rho$ — Wheeler's slogan that *the intrinsic scalar curvature plus the extrinsic curvature combination of a spatial slice equals the energy density*. This generalises Gauss's *Theorema Egregium* (in flat $\mathbb{R}^3$, $K = \kappa_1 \kappa_2$ for a surface; the analogue in flat $\mathbb{R}^4$ for a hypersurface is $0 = \frac{1}{2} R_V - (\kappa_1 \kappa_2 + \kappa_1 \kappa_3 + \kappa_2 \kappa_3)$). The Einstein equation is the *physical* deviation from this "Theorema Egregium of spacetime", measured by the matter content.
