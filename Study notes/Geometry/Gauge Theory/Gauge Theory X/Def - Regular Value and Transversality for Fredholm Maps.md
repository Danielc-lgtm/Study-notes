# Notation

Throughout, $X$ and $Y$ are [[Def - Banach Manifold and Smooth Maps between Banach Spaces|Banach manifolds]] and $F\colon X\to Y$ is a smooth map, most often a [[Def - Fredholm Map and Its Index|Fredholm map]]. For $x\in X$ we write $T_xX$ for the [[Def - The Tangent Space|tangent space]] at $x$ (a Banach space) and
$$d_xF\colon T_xX\longrightarrow T_{F(x)}Y$$
for the differential of $F$ at $x$, the bounded linear map read in charts as the Fréchet derivative. We write $\operatorname{im}d_xF=\{d_xF(v):v\in T_xX\}\subseteq T_{F(x)}Y$ for its range and $\ker d_xF=\{v\in T_xX:d_xF(v)=0\}$ for its kernel. The linear map $d_xF$ is **surjective** if $\operatorname{im}d_xF=T_{F(x)}Y$; for a Fredholm map, where the range is closed of finite codimension, surjectivity is equivalent to the vanishing of the cokernel $\operatorname{coker}d_xF=T_{F(x)}Y/\operatorname{im}d_xF$.

Points of $X$ are denoted $x,p$; points of $Y$ are denoted $y,z$. When $Z\subseteq Y$ is a [[Def - Embedded Submanifold|smoothly embedded submanifold]] of finite dimension we write $T_zZ\subseteq T_zY$ for its tangent space at $z\in Z$ and $F^{-1}(Z)=\{x\in X:F(x)\in Z\}$ for the preimage. The transversality symbol is $\pitchfork$; we write $F\pitchfork Z$ for "$F$ is transverse to $Z$" and reserve $\varnothing$ for the empty set. The full symbol registry for the chapter is on [[Gauge Theory X — Fredholm Maps, Transversality, and Degree]].

The single source for this page is Haydys, *Introduction to Gauge Theory*, Definition 161's surrounding text and Corollary 163 (the regular-value notion, p. 52) together with the unlabelled definition of transversality and its remark preceding Theorem 165 (p. 53). There is no competing Bär convention here.

> [!warning] Convention: source phrasings unified
> Haydys introduces "$0$ is a regular value" only inside the statement of Corollary 163, for the special value $0$ of a map $F\colon X\to Y$ between Banach *spaces*. We state the notion for an arbitrary value $y\in Y$ of a map between Banach *manifolds*, which is the form every later page uses; specialising to $Y$ a Banach space and $y=0$ recovers Haydys verbatim. Haydys' source sentence "$F$ is transverse to $Z=\{z\}$ is and only if $z$ is a regular value" contains an evident slip for "if and only if"; we use the corrected reading, which is proved as the compatibility clause below.

# Axiom Motivation

The one construction that organises all of differential topology is the passage from a map to the smooth structure of its fibres: if $F\colon X\to Y$ is smooth and $y\in Y$ is well chosen, then $F^{-1}(y)$ is a smooth manifold whose dimension is fixed in advance by $F$ alone. In finite dimensions "well chosen" is made precise by the [[Thm - Regular Value Theorem on Manifolds|regular value theorem]]: it suffices that $d_xF$ be *surjective* at every solution $x\in F^{-1}(y)$, and then $\dim F^{-1}(y)=\dim X-\dim Y$. This page isolates the two hypotheses under which the same mechanism runs for a [[Def - Fredholm Map and Its Index|Fredholm map]] between Banach manifolds — first the regular-value condition, which produces the fibre $F^{-1}(y)$, and then its strict generalisation, transversality to a submanifold $Z$, which produces the preimage $F^{-1}(Z)$. The theorems that these definitions make possible are stated and proved on [[Thm - Regular Value and Transversality Theorems for Fredholm Maps|the regular value and transversality theorem page]]; here we motivate and pin down the definitions themselves.

Begin with the desideratum for a *regular value*. We want a property of a point $y\in Y$ that guarantees $F^{-1}(y)$ is a smooth manifold and, moreover, that we can *find* such $y$ in abundance (that abundance is the content of the [[Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem|Sard–Smale theorem]], not of this definition, but the definition has to be the kind of thing that can be generic). The naive attempt "$y$ is in the image and the fibre is nicely cut out" is circular. The right condition is linear and pointwise: the differential must be *onto* at every preimage. The reason surjectivity is exactly what is needed becomes visible in the [[Thm - Kuranishi Model for a Fredholm Map|Kuranishi model]], which writes $F$ near a zero $p$ as $F\circ\phi(x_0,x_1)=Tx_1+f(x_0,x_1)$ with $T\colon X_1\to\operatorname{im}d_pF$ a linear isomorphism and $f_0\colon\ker d_pF\to\operatorname{coker}d_pF$ the finite-dimensional correction; surjectivity of $d_pF$ is precisely the statement $\operatorname{coker}d_pF=0$, which kills the correction $f_0$ and leaves the fibre locally diffeomorphic to the linear space $\ker d_pF$. Drop surjectivity at even one preimage point and the correction $f_0$ can be a genuinely singular map — for $x\mapsto x^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$ at $y=0$ it is $x_0\mapsto x_0^2$, whose zero set is a point of the "wrong" dimension, a cusp of the theory — and the fibre need not be a manifold at all. So surjectivity is not a convenience; it is the minimal linear condition that trivialises the obstruction space.

Why phrase the definition as a condition on *values* rather than on points, and why the vacuous clause for $y\notin\operatorname{im}F$? Because it is values we get to choose and perturb, and the theory of degree and moduli spaces needs to say "almost every $y$ is regular". A value $y$ with empty preimage satisfies "$d_xF$ surjective for all $x\in F^{-1}(y)$" vacuously, and it *should* count as regular: its fibre is the empty manifold, which is a manifold of every dimension, and in particular of dimension $\operatorname{index}F$. This is not a technicality to be tolerated but the correct convention, and it carries real information for a Fredholm map of negative index: there such a regular value forces $F^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$, because a non-empty manifold cannot have negative dimension, so "generic surjectivity" becomes "generic non-solvability" (this is the calibration check below). Excluding empty fibres from the definition would break exactly this reading. The complementary notion, the set $\operatorname{Crit}(F)$ of **critical points** — the $x$ where $d_xF$ fails to be surjective — and its image $F(\operatorname{Crit}(F))$ of **critical values**, is what "almost every value is regular" is measuring the smallness of; the definition must name both sides of the dichotomy so that Sard–Smale has something to be about.

Now the desideratum for *transversality*. Regular values cut out fibres $F^{-1}(y)$ over single points; but very often the object we want is the preimage $F^{-1}(Z)$ of a whole submanifold $Z\subseteq Y$ — the locus of parameters at which a solution meets a wall, the cobordism between two fibres $F^{-1}(y_0)$ and $F^{-1}(y_1)$ inside $F^{-1}(\gamma)$ for a path $\gamma$, the intersection theory of moduli spaces. We want a condition on $F$ and $Z$ guaranteeing that $F^{-1}(Z)$ is again a smooth submanifold of $X$, of a predictable dimension. The condition cannot be "$d_xF$ surjective", because that would force $Z=Y$; the correct relaxation is that $\operatorname{im}d_xF$ needs to be onto only *modulo the directions already tangent to $Z$*. That is exactly
$$\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zZ=T_zY\qquad(z=F(x)),$$
the sum of two subspaces of $T_zY$ filling the whole tangent space. What breaks if this clause is dropped? Then $F$ can meet $Z$ tangentially, and the preimage develops a singularity: for the curve $t\mapsto(t,t^2)$ in the plane meeting the line $Z=\{(s,0)\}$ at the origin, the image of the differential is the horizontal line, which coincides with $T_0Z$, so their sum is a line, not the plane; the two curves are tangent and the "intersection" is not transverse — a perturbation of $F$ changes the number of intersection points, so no stable count exists. What if we tried to *strengthen* the clause to "$\operatorname{im}d_xF\supseteq T_zZ$" or to plain surjectivity? Then we would exclude the very intersections the theory is built to handle (a generic line meets a generic line in the plane transversally without either differential being onto the plane by itself), and we would lose the dimension count $\dim F^{-1}(Z)=\operatorname{index}F+\dim Z$ that reduces to the regular-value count when $Z$ shrinks to a point. The sum condition is the Goldilocks clause: weaker and the preimage is singular, stronger and the theory has almost no instances.

Two structural remarks justify the fine print. First, because $F$ is Fredholm, $\operatorname{im}d_xF$ is closed of finite codimension $\dim\operatorname{coker}d_xF$; and because $Z$ is finite-dimensional, $T_zZ$ is finite-dimensional. The transversality equation therefore asks a finite-dimensional space $T_zZ$ to surject onto the finite-dimensional cokernel $T_zY/\operatorname{im}d_xF$ — a condition that *can* hold, and whose failure is measured by a finite integer. This is why $Z$ is required to be finite-dimensional and smoothly embedded: only then are $T_zZ$ and the quotient normal space finite-dimensional, and only then is $F^{-1}(Z)$ of finite codimension in $X$. Second, taking $Z=\{z\}$ a single point makes $T_zZ=0$, and the sum condition collapses to $\operatorname{im}d_xF=T_zY$, i.e. surjectivity at every $x\in F^{-1}(z)$ — the regular-value condition. So transversality is not a separate axiom bolted on; it is the one notion of which "regular value" is the point-sized case, and a reader who has understood why surjectivity trivialises the obstruction, why the sum condition is the right relaxation over a submanifold, and why $Z$ must be finite-dimensional could write both definitions unaided.

# The Definition

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [[Def - Banach Manifold and Smooth Maps between Banach Spaces|Banach manifolds]] and $F\in C^\infty(X;Y)$ a smooth map.

**Critical points, critical values, regular values.** The **critical set** of $F$ is
$$\operatorname{Crit}(F):=\{x\in X:d_xF\colon T_xX\to T_{F(x)}Y\ \text{is not surjective}\},$$
its elements are the **critical points** of $F$, and the image $F(\operatorname{Crit}(F))\subseteq Y$ is the set of **critical values**. A point $y\in Y$ is a **regular value** of $F$ if $d_xF$ is surjective for every $x\in F^{-1}(y)$:
$$\operatorname{im}d_xF=T_yY\qquad\text{for all }x\in F^{-1}(y).$$
If $F^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$ the condition holds vacuously, so **every point not in the image of $F$ is a regular value**. Equivalently, $y$ is a regular value if and only if $y$ is not a critical value, that is $y\notin F(\operatorname{Crit}(F))$.

**Transversality to a submanifold.** Let $Z\subseteq Y$ be a [[Def - Embedded Submanifold|smoothly embedded submanifold]] of finite dimension. The map $F$ is **transverse to $Z$**, written $F\pitchfork Z$, if for every $z\in Z$ and every $x\in F^{-1}(Z)$ with $F(x)=z$,
$$\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zZ=T_zY.$$
Here $\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zZ=\{u+w:u\in\operatorname{im}d_xF,\ w\in T_zZ\}$ is the sum of the two subspaces of $T_zY$; the condition demands that this sum be the whole tangent space $T_zY$. As with regular values, if $F^{-1}(Z)=\varnothing$ the condition holds vacuously and $F\pitchfork Z$ trivially.

**Compatibility: regular value is transversality to a point.** For a single point $z\in Y$, regarded as the zero-dimensional submanifold $Z=\{z\}$, one has $T_zZ=\{0\}$, and the transversality condition reads
$$\operatorname{im}d_xF+\{0\}=T_zY\iff\operatorname{im}d_xF=T_zY\qquad\text{for all }x\in F^{-1}(z),$$
which is exactly the statement that $z$ is a regular value of $F$. Thus
$$F\pitchfork\{z\}\iff z\ \text{is a regular value of }F,$$
and transversality to a finite-dimensional submanifold is the honest generalisation of the regular-value notion (Haydys, Remark following Theorem 165; see also Guillemin–Pollack for the finite-dimensional theory). This compatibility is what licenses reusing the same symbol $\pitchfork$ and the same dimension bookkeeping throughout: the regular-value theorem is the $\dim Z=0$ case of the transversal-preimage theorem, both proved on [[Thm - Regular Value and Transversality Theorems for Fredholm Maps|the regular value and transversality theorem page]].

The consequences these definitions are designed to produce, and which are *not* part of the definition, are: if $F$ is Fredholm and $y$ is a regular value then $F^{-1}(y)$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $\operatorname{index}F$; and if $F$ is Fredholm and $F\pitchfork Z$ then $F^{-1}(Z)$ is a smooth submanifold of $X$ with $\dim F^{-1}(Z)=\operatorname{index}F+\dim Z$. Both are theorems, proved from the [[Thm - Kuranishi Model for a Fredholm Map|Kuranishi model]] on the theorem page.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

Both notions have a cleaner structural form obtained by dividing out the directions along $Z$. Fix $z\in Z$ and let
$$\nu_z\colon T_zY\longrightarrow N_zZ:=T_zY/T_zZ$$
be the quotient projection onto the **normal space** $N_zZ$ of $Z$ at $z$; since $Z$ is finite-dimensional and embedded, $N_zZ$ is a well-defined Banach space (a quotient of $T_zY$ by the closed finite-dimensional subspace $T_zZ$). Then, for $x\in F^{-1}(Z)$ with $F(x)=z$,
$$\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zZ=T_zY\iff\nu_z\circ d_xF\colon T_xX\longrightarrow N_zZ\ \text{is surjective.}$$
The equivalence is the elementary fact that, for subspaces $A,B$ of a vector space $V$ with quotient map $\nu\colon V\to V/B$, one has $A+B=V$ if and only if $\nu(A)=V/B$ (indeed $\nu(A)=(A+B)/B$, which is all of $V/B$ exactly when $A+B=V$). Under this reading:

- **$F$ is transverse to $Z$** if and only if the *normal component of the differential*, $\nu_z\circ d_xF$, is surjective at every $x\in F^{-1}(Z)$. In words, $F$ is transverse to $Z$ precisely when it is a submersion *in the directions normal to $Z$*. The regular-value case $Z=\{z\}$ is the extreme in which the normal space is all of $T_zY$ (nothing is tangent to a point), so "submersion in the normal directions" becomes plain surjectivity.

- **The preimage as a fibre product.** Transversality is exactly the condition under which the set-theoretic fibre product
$$X\times_Y Z=\{(x,z)\in X\times Z:F(x)=z\}\cong F^{-1}(Z)$$
carries a smooth manifold structure making it the categorical pullback of $F\colon X\to Y$ along the inclusion $Z\hookrightarrow Y$: the diagram commutes, and for $F\pitchfork Z$ the pullback is a manifold with tangent space $T_x F^{-1}(Z)=(\nu_z\circ d_xF)^{-1}(0)=(d_xF)^{-1}(T_zZ)$ at each point, of finite codimension $\dim N_zZ-\dim\operatorname{coker}d_xF$. This is the universal-property face of the transversal-preimage theorem; the manifold structure itself is proved on [[Thm - Regular Value and Transversality Theorems for Fredholm Maps|the theorem page]].

The structural form is the one to hold in mind when generalising: it says transversality is *relative surjectivity*, surjectivity measured against whatever is already supplied by $Z$, and it makes transparent why only the finite-dimensional cokernel and the finite-dimensional normal space enter — the whole condition lives in the finite-dimensional quotient $N_zZ$.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

Transversality is the geometric name, from differential topology, for a single algebraic idea: *two subspaces are in general position when their sum is everything*. In [[Def - Immersion, Submersion, and Embedding|submersion]] theory it is surjectivity of one differential; in intersection theory it is the general-position condition $T_pA+T_pB=T_pM$ that makes $A\cap B$ a submanifold of the expected dimension $\dim A+\dim B-\dim M$; in the deformation theory of moduli spaces it is the vanishing of an obstruction cokernel; in homological algebra the same "sum is everything, so the quotient is exact" pattern is the surjectivity in a right-exact sequence. What the Fredholm setting adds is that the ambient space $T_zY$ is infinite-dimensional, so plain surjectivity of $d_xF$ would be too much to ask of a single map; but because $F$ is Fredholm the *deficiency* $\operatorname{coker}d_xF$ is finite-dimensional, and transversality only asks the finite-dimensional $T_zZ$ to cover that finite deficiency. The infinite-dimensional problem has been reduced, by the Fredholm hypothesis, to a finite-dimensional general-position question — which is exactly why Sard's theorem, a finite-dimensional statement about measure-zero critical values, can be upgraded to the [[Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem|Sard–Smale theorem]] in this setting.

**True name.** Officially a regular value is a point whose every preimage has surjective differential, and $F\pitchfork Z$ is the subspace-sum condition $\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zZ=T_zY$. Operationally, as the structural section makes literal, both say the same thing: *$F$ is a submersion into the normal directions of the target locus*. A regular value $y$ is a point at which $F$ is normally surjective onto all of $T_yY$; $F\pitchfork Z$ is normal surjectivity onto $N_zZ$. The number this normal surjectivity controls is the codimension of the preimage: $\operatorname{codim}F^{-1}(Z)=\dim Z-\operatorname{index}F$ rearranges to $\dim F^{-1}(Z)=\operatorname{index}F+\dim Z$, the dimension formula that specialises to $\dim F^{-1}(y)=\operatorname{index}F$ when $Z$ is a point. When one needs to *use* transversality, this operational form — "normal surjectivity, of net effect the index plus the dimension of $Z$" — is the working picture.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — regular and critical values of $x\mapsto x^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$.** Let $F\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $F(x)=x^2$, a smooth (hence Fredholm, of index $\dim\mathbb{R}-\dim\mathbb{R}=0$) map. Its differential is $d_xF=2x$, the multiplication map $v\mapsto 2xv$ on $\mathbb{R}$, which is surjective onto $\mathbb{R}$ if and only if $2x\neq0$, i.e. $x\neq0$. Hence $\operatorname{Crit}(F)=\{0\}$ and the set of critical values is $F(\operatorname{Crit}(F))=\{0\}$. We check each type of value clause by clause.
- **$y=0$ is a critical value (not regular).** Its preimage is $F^{-1}(0)=\{0\}$, and at $x=0$ the differential $d_0F=0$ is not surjective (its image is $\{0\}\neq\mathbb{R}$). So the regular-value condition "surjective at every preimage point" fails at $x=0$, and $0$ is a critical value. Consistently, $F^{-1}(0)=\{0\}$ is a single point — dimension $0=\operatorname{index}F$ here by accident, but the fibre is not cut out transversally, and the Kuranishi correction is the singular map $x_0\mapsto x_0^2$.
- **$y>0$ is a regular value.** Its preimage is $F^{-1}(y)=\{+\sqrt{y},-\sqrt{y}\}$, two points, and at each the differential is $d_{\pm\sqrt y}F=\pm2\sqrt{y}\neq0$, hence surjective onto $\mathbb{R}$. Every preimage point has surjective differential, so $y$ is a regular value; the fibre is a $0$-manifold (two points), matching $\dim F^{-1}(y)=\operatorname{index}F=0$.
- **$y<0$ is a regular value, vacuously.** Its preimage is $F^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$ (no real square is negative), so the condition "surjective at every $x\in F^{-1}(y)$" holds with nothing to check. Thus $y$ is regular, the empty fibre is the empty $0$-manifold, and $y\notin\operatorname{im}F$ as the definition predicts.

**Is an instance — transversality of a curve and a line in the plane.** Take $Y=\mathbb{R}^2$, the smoothly embedded $1$-dimensional submanifold $Z=\{(s,0):s\in\mathbb{R}\}$ (the horizontal axis, with $T_zZ=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$ at every $z\in Z$), and the curve $F\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^2$, $F(t)=(t,t)$. The preimage is $F^{-1}(Z)=\{t:t=0\}=\{0\}$, meeting $Z$ only at the origin $z=(0,0)$. There $d_0F(v)=(v,v)$, so $\operatorname{im}d_0F=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}$. Compute the sum:
$$\operatorname{im}d_0F+T_zZ=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}+\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}=\mathbb{R}^2,$$
since $(1,1)$ and $(1,0)$ are linearly independent and span the plane. The transversality equation $\operatorname{im}d_0F+T_zZ=T_zY$ holds at the only intersection point, so $F\pitchfork Z$. The transversal-preimage theorem then predicts $\dim F^{-1}(Z)=\operatorname{index}F+\dim Z=(1-2)+1=0$, and indeed $F^{-1}(Z)=\{0\}$ is a single point, a $0$-manifold.

**Is NOT an instance — a tangential intersection fails transversality.** Keep $Y=\mathbb{R}^2$ and $Z=\{(s,0)\}$ as above, but take the curve $G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^2$, $G(t)=(t,t^2)$. Again $G^{-1}(Z)=\{t:t^2=0\}=\{0\}$, meeting $Z$ at $z=(0,0)$. Now $d_0G(v)=(v,0)$, so $\operatorname{im}d_0G=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$, which *coincides* with $T_zZ=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$. Their sum is
$$\operatorname{im}d_0G+T_zZ=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}\neq\mathbb{R}^2,$$
a line, not the plane. The transversality condition fails at the origin, so $G\not\pitchfork Z$: the parabola is tangent to the axis. The pathology is exactly what the definition is built to exclude — a small vertical translate of $Z$ meets the parabola in two points, a translate the other way in none, so the intersection number is unstable, and $G^{-1}(Z_{\text{translated}})$ jumps in size; there is no manifold of the expected dimension varying continuously.

**Is NOT an instance — a value in the image with non-trivial cokernel.** Let $H\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^2$, $H(t)=(t,0)$, a smooth embedding of the line as the horizontal axis. Its differential is $d_tH(v)=(v,0)$ at every $t$, with $\operatorname{im}d_tH=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$ and cokernel
$$\operatorname{coker}d_tH=\mathbb{R}^2/\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}\cong\mathbb{R}\neq\{0\}.$$
Take the value $y=(0,0)$, which *is* in the image ($H(0)=(0,0)$). Its preimage is $H^{-1}(y)=\{0\}$, and at $x=0$ the differential $d_0H$ is not surjective, since its cokernel is one-dimensional. Therefore $y=(0,0)$ is a **critical value**, not a regular value, even though it is a perfectly ordinary point of the image. In fact every point of the image (the whole axis) is a critical value here, because $H$ can never be a submersion from a $1$-dimensional domain onto a $2$-dimensional target; the *only* regular values of $H$ are the points off the axis, whose preimage is empty. This is the phenomenon the spec singles out: a point in the image where $d_xF$ has cokernel $\neq0$ is exactly a critical value, and it exhibits why "regular value" and "value in the image" are different notions — for a map of negative index, no value in the image is ever regular.

**Corollary — the critical values are what Sard–Smale bounds.** The set of regular values is the complement of the set of critical values, $Y\setminus F(\operatorname{Crit}(F))$. For a smooth Fredholm map between paracompact Banach manifolds, the [[Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem|Sard–Smale theorem]] states that this complement is residual (a countable intersection of open dense sets), hence dense: *almost every value is regular*. The definition on this page is thus exactly the property that Sard–Smale asserts to be generic, and it is the finite-dimensionality of $\operatorname{coker}d_xF$, built into the Fredholm hypothesis, that lets the classical Sard theorem be applied to the local Kuranishi model $f_0\colon\ker d_pF\to\operatorname{coker}d_pF$ between finite-dimensional spaces.

**Calibration check.** Three verifications the reader can run from the page. (1) For a Fredholm map $F$ with $\operatorname{index}F<0$, a regular value $y$ must have $F^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$: if some $x\in F^{-1}(y)$ existed with $d_xF$ surjective, then $\operatorname{coker}d_xF=0$ and $\dim\ker d_xF=\operatorname{index}F<0$ is impossible for a dimension, so no such $x$ exists — "regularity forces non-solvability" in negative index. (2) Transversality to $Z=Y$ is automatic: $T_zZ=T_zY$, so $\operatorname{im}d_xF+T_zY=T_zY$ for any $F$, and indeed $F^{-1}(Y)=X$ is a manifold of dimension $\operatorname{index}F+\dim Y$ — but this is only sensible when $\dim Y<\infty$, matching the finite-dimensionality requirement on $Z$. (3) If $\operatorname{im}d_xF$ already equals $T_zY$ at some $x\in F^{-1}(Z)$ (i.e. $d_xF$ is surjective there), the transversality clause at that point holds regardless of $T_zZ$; so a regular value of $F$ that happens to lie on $Z$ never obstructs $F\pitchfork Z$, which is why transversality is a condition only where $F$ is *not* already a submersion.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Regular value and transversality theorems for Fredholm maps *(from Gauge Theory X)*
> With these definitions in hand, [[Thm - Regular Value and Transversality Theorems for Fredholm Maps|the regular value and transversality theorem]] states that a regular value's fibre $F^{-1}(y)$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $\operatorname{index}F$, and a transverse preimage $F^{-1}(Z)$ is a smooth submanifold with $\dim F^{-1}(Z)=\operatorname{index}F+\dim Z$ — the infinite-dimensional regular value and preimage theorems.

> [!tip] The Sard–Smale theorem *(from Gauge Theory X)*
> The [[Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem|Sard–Smale theorem]] promotes these from conditional to generic: for a Fredholm map the set of regular values is residual and dense, so regularity — and, applied to an auxiliary map, transversality — can always be achieved by an arbitrarily small perturbation.

> [!tip] Parametric transversality and generic families *(from Gauge Theory X)*
> The [[Thm - Parametric Transversality|parametric transversality theorem]] uses this definition applied to a family $F\colon X\times P\to Y$: if the total map is transverse to $Z$, then $F_p\pitchfork Z$ for almost every parameter $p$, the engine behind generic-metric and generic-perturbation arguments in gauge theory.

> [!tip] Degree theory for proper Fredholm maps *(from Gauge Theory X)*
> Counting the finite preimage $F^{-1}(y)$ at a regular value $y$ of a proper index-zero Fredholm map defines the [[Def - Mod-2 Degree of a Proper Fredholm Map|mod-2 degree]] and, with orientations, the [[Def - Integer Degree of an Oriented Proper Fredholm Map|integer degree]]; the regular-value definition on this page is what makes the count finite and the degree well defined.
