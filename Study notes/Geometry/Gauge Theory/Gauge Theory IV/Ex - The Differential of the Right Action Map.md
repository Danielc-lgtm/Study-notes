# Problem Statement

Let $\pi\colon P\to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle, so that the Lie group $G$ acts on the total space $P$ on the **right**, $R_g(p)=p\cdot g$, in accordance with the standing convention of the series. Assemble the action into a single smooth map
$$\hat R\colon P\times G\longrightarrow P,\qquad \hat R(p,g)=p\cdot g,$$
which differs from the individual right translations $R_g$ only in that the group element is now allowed to vary as well. Fix a point $(p,g)\in P\times G$; the tangent space of the product there splits canonically as $T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)=T_pP\oplus T_gG$, so a tangent vector at $(p,g)$ is a pair $(v,w)$ with $v\in T_pP$ and $w\in T_gG$.

**Prove that the differential of $\hat R$ at $(p,g)$ is**
$$\hat R_{*}\,(v,w)=(R_g)_{*}\,v+\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g),\qquad v\in T_pP,\ w\in T_gG,$$
where $(R_g)_{*}\colon T_pP\to T_{pg}P$ is the differential of the right translation $R_g$, where $\theta_g(w)=d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)\in\mathfrak g$ is the value at $w$ of the left **Maurer–Cartan form** $\theta$ of $G$, and where $\xi_P$ denotes the **fundamental vector field** on $P$ generated by $\xi\in\mathfrak g$. The intended route is to exploit the associativity identity
$$\hat R\big(p(t),g(t)\big)=\hat R\Big(\hat R\big(p(t),g\big),\,g^{-1}g(t)\Big),$$
valid for any curves $p(t)$ in $P$ and $g(t)$ in $G$, and differentiate both sides at $t=0$.

This is Step 1 of Haydys's proof that a connection on a vector bundle corresponds to a connection on its frame bundle (his Theorem 46), where it is stated and "left as an exercise". Haydys writes the fundamental vector field of $\xi$ as $K_\xi$ and writes the left-translated velocity as $(L_{g^{-1}})_{*}w$; in the notation of this series these are $\xi_P$ and $\theta_g(w)$ respectively, and the two forms of the statement are identical.

**Recall:**

The three objects in play are the right action of $G$ on the principal bundle, the fundamental vector field of a Lie-algebra element, and the Maurer–Cartan form that converts a tangent vector on $G$ into a Lie-algebra element.

![[Def - Principal G-Bundle#The Definition]]

The single feature of the definition that this exercise uses is that $G$ acts smoothly on $P$ on the right, and that the action is a genuine group action: $p\cdot(g_1g_2)=(p\cdot g_1)\cdot g_2$ and $p\cdot e=p$ for the identity $e\in G$. The right translation by a fixed group element is written $R_g\colon P\to P$, $R_g(p)=p\cdot g$; it is a diffeomorphism with inverse $R_{g^{-1}}$. For each fixed $p\in P$ the **orbit map** is
$$\sigma_p\colon G\longrightarrow P,\qquad \sigma_p(g)=p\cdot g,$$
a smooth map from the group into the total space. The two maps $R_g$ and $\sigma_p$ are the two partial maps of $\hat R$: freezing the second slot of $\hat R$ gives $R_g$, and freezing the first slot gives $\sigma_p$.

![[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action#The Definition]]

For the right action of $G$ on $P$ and a Lie-algebra element $\xi\in\mathfrak g=T_eG$, the [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action|fundamental vector field]] $\xi_P\in\mathfrak X(P)$ is
$$\xi_P(q)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\,q\cdot\exp(t\xi),\qquad q\in P.$$
Equivalently, and this is the form we shall use, $\xi_P(q)$ is the image of $\xi$ under the differential of the orbit map at the identity,
$$\xi_P(q)=(\sigma_q)_{*}\big|_{e}(\xi),$$
because the curve $t\mapsto\exp(t\xi)$ starts at $e$ with velocity $\xi$ and $\sigma_q(\exp(t\xi))=q\cdot\exp(t\xi)$; since the differential of a smooth map at a point depends only on the base point and the velocity vector, any curve $h(t)$ in $G$ with $h(0)=e$ and $\dot h(0)=\xi$ gives the same value $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{0}\,q\cdot h(t)=(\sigma_q)_{*}|_e(\xi)=\xi_P(q)$.

![[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form#The Definition]]

The left [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|Maurer–Cartan form]] $\theta\in\Omega^1(G;\mathfrak g)$ is defined by
$$\theta_g\colon T_gG\longrightarrow T_eG=\mathfrak g,\qquad \theta_g(w)=d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w),$$
where $L_{g^{-1}}\colon G\to G$, $h\mapsto g^{-1}h$, is left translation by $g^{-1}$. In words, $\theta_g$ carries a tangent vector $w$ sitting at $g$ back to the identity by left translation, producing a Lie-algebra element. For a matrix group $\theta_g(w)=g^{-1}w$, whence the familiar shorthand $\theta=g^{-1}\,dg$. The only property we invoke here is the defining formula $\theta_g(w)=d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)$ itself.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *compute-a-differential* problem: an explicit smooth map $\hat R$ is given, and its total differential at an arbitrary point is required in closed form. The distinguishing feature is that $\hat R$ is built from a group action, so its two partial differentials are objects with names — the differential of a right translation, and, at the identity, the fundamental vector field. The task is to assemble the total differential out of these two partial pieces and to see that the second piece is a fundamental vector field only after the group variable has been transported to the identity.

**Assumption pattern.** The recognisable trigger is that the second argument of $\hat R$ lives in the Lie group $G$, and that the fundamental vector field is defined by differentiating the orbit map *at the identity element* $e$, not at a general $g$. A naïve differentiation of $t\mapsto p\cdot g(t)$ at a general $g$ produces $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g(w)$, which is not literally a fundamental vector field, because $w$ sits at $g$ rather than at $e$. The pattern that resolves this is: whenever a construction is anchored at the identity of a group but you meet it at a general group element, translate back to the identity. The Maurer–Cartan form is exactly the operator that performs this translation, and its appearance in the answer is forced by this consideration alone.

**Theorem routing.** The route has three moves. First, split the differential over the direct sum $T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)=T_pP\oplus T_gG$: a differential is linear, so $\hat R_{*}(v,w)=\hat R_{*}(v,0)+\hat R_{*}(0,w)$. Second, evaluate each summand by freezing the other coordinate, which turns $\hat R$ into $R_g$ (for the first summand) and into the orbit map $\sigma_p$ (for the second). Third, factor the orbit map as $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$ — precisely the associativity identity in the hint — so that the chain rule expresses $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g$ through the Maurer–Cartan form $\theta_g=d_gL_{g^{-1}}$ followed by the differential of $\sigma_{p\cdot g}$ at $e$, which *is* the fundamental vector field by [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action|its definition]].

**Key decision point.** The single non-obvious step is the factorisation $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$. It is what the hint's identity $\hat R(p(t),g(t))=\hat R(\hat R(p(t),g),g^{-1}g(t))$ encodes at the level of maps. Without it, the second summand $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g(w)$ is a bare tangent vector at $p\cdot g$ with no name; with it, the derivative of $\sigma_p$ at $g$ is routed through the identity of $G$, where the fundamental vector field lives. The decision to insert $g^{-1}g=e$ and regroup the action — algebraically trivial, geometrically the whole content — is the crux.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations, named descriptively; the topic page for Gauge Theory IV lists them under its Legal Operations, and the numbering there will be reconciled with these descriptions.

1. **Split a differential over a product tangent space.** The differential of a map defined on a product manifold $P\times G$ is a linear map on $T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)=T_pP\oplus T_gG$; it may therefore be evaluated on a pair $(v,w)$ by summing its values on $(v,0)$ and $(0,w)$. It is applied once, to $\hat R$ at $(p,g)$, to reduce the total differential to its two partial differentials.

2. **Recover a partial map by freezing a coordinate.** Restricting $\hat R$ to a slice $P\times\{g\}$ recovers the right translation $R_g$; restricting to a slice $\{p\}\times G$ recovers the orbit map $\sigma_p$. The differential of $\hat R$ on a vector tangent to a slice equals the differential of the corresponding partial map.

3. **Factor the orbit map through the identity of the group.** Using associativity of the action, $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$, so that the orbit map at $p$ evaluated near $g$ is the orbit map at $p\cdot g$ evaluated near $e$, precomposed with left translation by $g^{-1}$.

4. **Apply the chain rule and read off the Maurer–Cartan form.** Differentiating the factorisation of operation 3 gives $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g=(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}|_e\circ d_gL_{g^{-1}}$; the second factor is by definition $\theta_g$, and the first factor applied to a Lie-algebra element is by definition the fundamental vector field at $p\cdot g$.

5. **Reassemble by linearity and check the codomain.** Add the two partial differentials recovered by operations 2–4 back together through the splitting of operation 1, and verify that both summands are tangent vectors at the common point $p\cdot g=\hat R(p,g)$, so that their sum is a legitimate value of $\hat R_{*}|_{(p,g)}$.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> A differential is a linear map, and the tangent space of a product is the direct sum of the tangent spaces. Write $(v,w)=(v,0)+(0,w)$ and treat the two pieces separately. On each piece, one of the two coordinates of $\hat R$ is being held constant — so on each piece $\hat R$ reduces to a map you already have a name for.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Freezing the group element $g$ turns $\hat R(\cdot,g)$ into the right translation $R_g$, so the first piece is just $(R_g)_{*}v$. Freezing the point $p$ turns $\hat R(p,\cdot)$ into the orbit map $\sigma_p(g)=p\cdot g$; the second piece is $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g(w)$. The difficulty is that this is a derivative of the orbit map at a *general* $g$, whereas the fundamental vector field is defined by differentiating the orbit map at the *identity* $e$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> Move the derivative back to the identity. Because the action is associative, $p\cdot h=(p\cdot g)\cdot(g^{-1}h)$ for every $h$, that is, $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$ as maps $G\to P$. This is the hint's identity read at the level of maps. Differentiate this composition at $g$ with the chain rule.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> The chain rule gives $(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g(w)=(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}|_e\big(d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)\big)$. The inner factor $d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)$ is precisely $\theta_g(w)\in\mathfrak g$, the Maurer–Cartan form. The outer factor is the differential of the orbit map at the identity, which by the definition of the fundamental vector field sends a Lie-algebra element $\xi$ to $\xi_P(p\cdot g)$. Put the pieces together.

# Solution

The map $\hat R$ has two arguments, one in the total space and one in the group, so its total differential is the sum of two partial differentials. The first partial differential is the differential of a right translation and needs no work. The second is the differential of an orbit map, and the only subtlety is that it must be transported from a general group element $g$ back to the identity $e$ — where the fundamental vector field is defined — by left translation; this transport is exactly the Maurer–Cartan form, and its appearance is the entire content of the exercise.

**Step 1: Split the differential over the two factors.**

By linearity of the differential on the product tangent space, $\hat R_{*}(v,w)$ is the sum of its value on the $P$-direction $(v,0)$ and its value on the $G$-direction $(0,w)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> The tangent space of the product at $(p,g)$ decomposes canonically as
> $$T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)=T_pP\oplus T_gG,$$
> where $T_pP$ is identified with $T_pP\oplus\{0\}$ (velocities of curves with constant second coordinate) and $T_gG$ with $\{0\}\oplus T_gG$ (velocities of curves with constant first coordinate). The differential
> $$\hat R_{*}\big|_{(p,g)}\colon T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)\longrightarrow T_{p\cdot g}P$$
> is a linear map (the differential of a smooth map at a point is linear). Applying linearity to the decomposition $(v,w)=(v,0)+(0,w)$,
> $$\hat R_{*}(v,w)=\hat R_{*}(v,0)+\hat R_{*}(0,w)\qquad\text{(linearity of the differential)}.$$
> We evaluate the two summands in Steps 2 and 3.

**Step 2: The $P$-direction is the differential of the right translation $R_g$.**

Freezing the group element recovers $R_g$, so $\hat R_{*}(v,0)=(R_g)_{*}v$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Choose a smooth curve $p(t)$ in $P$ with $p(0)=p$ and $\dot p(0)=v$, and consider the curve $t\mapsto(p(t),g)$ in $P\times G$; it starts at $(p,g)$ with velocity $(v,0)$. Along it,
> $$\hat R\big(p(t),g\big)=p(t)\cdot g=R_g\big(p(t)\big)\qquad\text{(definition of }\hat R\text{ and of }R_g\text{)}.$$
> Differentiating at $t=0$, and using that the differential computes the velocity of the image curve,
> $$\hat R_{*}(v,0)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}R_g\big(p(t)\big)=(R_g)_{*}\big|_p\,\dot p(0)=(R_g)_{*}\,v\qquad\text{(chain rule; }\dot p(0)=v\text{)}.$$
> Here $(R_g)_{*}\colon T_pP\to T_{p\cdot g}P$ is the differential of the right translation $R_g$, which is a diffeomorphism of $P$ by the principal-bundle axioms.

**Step 3: The $G$-direction, transported to the identity, is a fundamental vector field.**

Freezing the point recovers the orbit map $\sigma_p$; factoring $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$ and applying the chain rule gives $\hat R_{*}(0,w)=\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Choose a smooth curve $g(t)$ in $G$ with $g(0)=g$ and $\dot g(0)=w$, and consider the curve $t\mapsto(p,g(t))$ in $P\times G$; it starts at $(p,g)$ with velocity $(0,w)$. Along it,
> $$\hat R\big(p,g(t)\big)=p\cdot g(t)=\sigma_p\big(g(t)\big)\qquad\text{(definition of }\hat R\text{ and of the orbit map }\sigma_p\text{)},$$
> so that, differentiating at $t=0$,
> $$\hat R_{*}(0,w)=(\sigma_p)_{*}\big|_g\,w\qquad\text{(chain rule; }\dot g(0)=w\text{)}. \tag{3.1}$$
> This is the differential of the orbit map at the *general* element $g$, which is not yet a fundamental vector field. We transport it to the identity. Associativity of the right action gives, for every $h\in G$,
> $$\sigma_p(h)=p\cdot h=p\cdot\big(g\,g^{-1}h\big)=(p\cdot g)\cdot\big(g^{-1}h\big)=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\big(g^{-1}h\big)=\big(\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}\big)(h),$$
> where $L_{g^{-1}}(h)=g^{-1}h$; this is the hint's identity $\hat R(p(t),g(t))=\hat R(\hat R(p(t),g),g^{-1}g(t))$ read as an equality of maps $G\to P$, with $h=g(t)$. Hence, as maps,
> $$\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}\qquad\text{(associativity of the action, }g\,g^{-1}=e\text{)}. \tag{3.2}$$
> Differentiating $(3.2)$ at $g$ by the chain rule, and noting $L_{g^{-1}}(g)=e$ so that the outer differential is taken at $e$,
> $$(\sigma_p)_{*}\big|_g=(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}\big|_{e}\circ\, d_gL_{g^{-1}}\qquad\text{(chain rule applied to }(3.2)\text{)}. \tag{3.3}$$
> The inner factor is the Maurer–Cartan form by [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|its definition]]: $d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)=\theta_g(w)\in\mathfrak g$. Writing $\xi:=\theta_g(w)$, the outer factor evaluated on $\xi$ is the differential of the orbit map $\sigma_{p\cdot g}$ at the identity, which by [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action|the definition of the fundamental vector field]] is
> $$(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}\big|_{e}(\xi)=\xi_P(p\cdot g)\qquad\text{(definition of the fundamental vector field }\xi_P\text{)}.$$
> Combining $(3.1)$, $(3.3)$ and this last identity,
> $$\hat R_{*}(0,w)=(\sigma_p)_{*}\big|_g\,w=(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}\big|_{e}\big(\theta_g(w)\big)=\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g).$$

**Step 4: Assemble the two summands.**

Adding the results of Steps 2 and 3 through the splitting of Step 1 yields the claimed formula.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Substituting Step 2 and Step 3 into the splitting of Step 1,
> $$\hat R_{*}(v,w)=\hat R_{*}(v,0)+\hat R_{*}(0,w)=(R_g)_{*}\,v+\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g)\qquad\text{(Steps 1, 2, 3)}.$$
> Both terms are tangent vectors at $p\cdot g$: the first is $(R_g)_{*}v\in T_{p\cdot g}P$, and the second is the value at $p\cdot g$ of the fundamental vector field of $\theta_g(w)$, again in $T_{p\cdot g}P$. Their sum therefore lies in $T_{p\cdot g}P=T_{\hat R(p,g)}P$, as a value of $\hat R_{*}$ must. This is exactly the asserted identity.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** For the smooth map $\hat R\colon P\times G\to P$, $\hat R(p,g)=p\cdot g$, associated to the right action of $G$ on a principal bundle $P$, the differential at $(p,g)$ satisfies
> $$\hat R_{*}(v,w)=(R_g)_{*}\,v+\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g),\qquad v\in T_pP,\ w\in T_gG.$$
>
> *Proof.* We must compute the linear map $\hat R_{*}|_{(p,g)}\colon T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)\to T_{p\cdot g}P$ on an arbitrary pair $(v,w)$.
>
> **Splitting.** The product tangent space decomposes as $T_{(p,g)}(P\times G)=T_pP\oplus T_gG$, with $(v,0)$ the velocities of curves constant in $G$ and $(0,w)$ those constant in $P$. Since the differential is linear,
> $$\hat R_{*}(v,w)=\hat R_{*}(v,0)+\hat R_{*}(0,w).$$
>
> **The $P$-direction.** Let $p(t)$ satisfy $p(0)=p$, $\dot p(0)=v$. Then $\hat R(p(t),g)=R_g(p(t))$ by the definitions of $\hat R$ and $R_g$, so $\hat R_{*}(v,0)=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0R_g(p(t))=(R_g)_{*}v$ by the chain rule, where $(R_g)_{*}\colon T_pP\to T_{p\cdot g}P$ is the differential of the diffeomorphism $R_g$.
>
> **The $G$-direction.** Let $g(t)$ satisfy $g(0)=g$, $\dot g(0)=w$. Then $\hat R(p,g(t))=p\cdot g(t)=\sigma_p(g(t))$ with $\sigma_p(h)=p\cdot h$ the orbit map, so $\hat R_{*}(0,w)=(\sigma_p)_{*}|_g\,w$ by the chain rule. Associativity of the action gives $\sigma_p(h)=p\cdot h=(p\cdot g)\cdot(g^{-1}h)=\sigma_{p\cdot g}(L_{g^{-1}}(h))$ for all $h$, that is $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$. The chain rule, together with $L_{g^{-1}}(g)=e$, yields
> $$(\sigma_p)_{*}\big|_g=(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}\big|_{e}\circ\,d_gL_{g^{-1}}.$$
> By the definition of the Maurer–Cartan form, $d_gL_{g^{-1}}(w)=\theta_g(w)=:\xi\in\mathfrak g$; by the definition of the fundamental vector field, $(\sigma_{p\cdot g})_{*}|_e(\xi)=\xi_P(p\cdot g)$. Hence $\hat R_{*}(0,w)=\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g)$.
>
> **Conclusion.** Adding the two directions, $\hat R_{*}(v,w)=(R_g)_{*}v+\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(p\cdot g)$, both terms lying in $T_{p\cdot g}P$. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting shortcut
> One is tempted to write "$\hat R_{*}(0,w)=w_P(p\cdot g)$", treating $w\in T_gG$ as though it were already a Lie-algebra element. This is a type error: $w_P$ is only defined for $w\in\mathfrak g=T_eG$, whereas here $w\in T_gG$. The fundamental-vector-field construction is anchored at the identity, and a tangent vector at a general $g$ must first be left-translated to the identity — which is exactly the Maurer–Cartan form $\theta_g$ — before it can generate a fundamental field. The step becomes legal precisely when $g=e$, for then $\theta_e=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak g}$ and the two expressions coincide.

**Independent sanity check (matrix group).** For $G\subset GL_k(\mathbb R)$ a matrix group, $\theta_g(w)=g^{-1}w$ and $\xi_P(q)=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0q\cdot\exp(t\xi)$. Take the trivial bundle $P=M\times G$ with $(m,a)\cdot g=(m,ag)$, so $\hat R((m,a),g)=(m,ag)$; a tangent vector at $((m,a),g)$ is $((u,\alpha),w)$ with $u\in T_mM$, $\alpha\in T_aG\subset\mathfrak{gl}_k$, $w\in T_gG\subset\mathfrak{gl}_k$. Direct differentiation of the matrix product gives $\hat R_{*}((u,\alpha),w)=(u,\alpha g+aw)$. The formula predicts $(R_g)_{*}(u,\alpha)+\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P(m,ag)=(u,\alpha g)+\big(g^{-1}w\big)_P(m,ag)$. The fundamental vector field of $\xi=g^{-1}w$ at $(m,ag)$ is $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0(m,ag)\cdot\exp(t\xi)=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0(m,ag\exp(t\xi))=(0,ag\,\xi)=(0,ag\,g^{-1}w)=(0,aw)$. Adding, $(u,\alpha g)+(0,aw)=(u,\alpha g+aw)$, which matches the direct computation exactly, confirming the identity in the matrix case.

# Key Takeaways

**The total differential of a group action splits into "move the point" plus "move by the group", and the second piece is a fundamental vector field only after transport to the identity.** The map $\hat R$ encodes the entire action, and its differential decomposes along the product $T_pP\oplus T_gG$ into the differential of a right translation and the differential of an orbit map. The right-translation piece is inert — it is just $(R_g)_{*}$ — but the orbit-map piece carries all the geometry, because differentiating an orbit map at the identity is the definition of a fundamental vector field. The reusable principle: whenever you differentiate an expression $p\cdot g(t)$ with $g(t)$ a curve in the group, expect the Maurer–Cartan form to appear, converting the velocity $\dot g$ into a Lie-algebra element that then generates a fundamental field. The trigger is a product of a base object with a *varying* group element; the reaction is to left-translate the group velocity to the identity.

**Associativity of the action is the engine that routes a general-point derivative back to the identity.** The one algebraically trivial, geometrically decisive move is $p\cdot h=(p\cdot g)\cdot(g^{-1}h)$, i.e. $\sigma_p=\sigma_{p\cdot g}\circ L_{g^{-1}}$. Inserting $g^{-1}g=e$ and regrouping does nothing to the values of the map but everything to how its differential factors: it exhibits the orbit map at $p$ as the orbit map at $p\cdot g$ read near the identity, precomposed with a left translation. The transferable diagnostic: when a construction is defined at a distinguished point of a group (here the identity) but you need it at a general point, look for a translation that carries the general point to the distinguished one, and factor the map through that translation before differentiating. This same manoeuvre underlies the transformation law $A_{s'}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}A_s+g^{*}\theta$ for local connection forms and, in [[Thm - Transformation of Local Connection and Curvature Forms|the transformation theorem]], is invoked in exactly this form as the differential of the action map.

**This identity is the local backbone of the correspondence between vector-bundle and principal connections.** The whole reason the exercise appears in Haydys is that it is Step 1 of the proof that a covariant derivative on a vector bundle $E$ is the same datum as a connection on the frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$: when two local frames $e,e'=e\cdot g$ are compared, the change of section is a change in both the point and the group element, so its differential is governed by exactly this formula. The inhomogeneous Maurer–Cartan term $g^{*}\theta$ in every gauge-transformation law is the residue of the $\big(\theta_g(w)\big)_P$ term computed here. In spaced review, the fact to reconstruct is that the pure-gauge term in connection transformation laws is *not* an extra postulate but a direct consequence of how the differential of the right action behaves — and that its appearance is forced the moment one differentiates a section that moves along the fibre. Companion exercises are [[Ex - The Pull-Back of the Maurer-Cartan Form along a Product of Maps]], which packages the same computation as an identity for $g^{*}\theta$, and [[Ex - Induced Connections on the Dual and Endomorphism Bundles Agree with the Direct Ones]], which uses the resulting correspondence to identify induced connections.
