# Notation

Standing conventions of the series: manifolds are smooth ($C^\infty$), Hausdorff and second countable; "vector bundle" means a smooth real vector bundle of finite rank unless the complex case is named; $\Gamma(E)$ denotes the smooth sections of $E$. Throughout, $M$ and $M'$ are smooth manifolds of dimensions $n$ and $n'$, and $\pi_E : E \to M$ and $\pi_F : F \to M$ are smooth [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundles]] over the *same* base $M$, of ranks $k$ and $l$ respectively; when only one bundle is in play we write $\pi : E \to M$. The fibre over $m \in M$ is $E_m := \pi^{-1}(m)$, a real vector space of dimension $k$.

A [[Def - Local Trivialization|local trivialization]] of $E$ over an open set $U \subseteq M$ is a diffeomorphism $\Phi : \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times \mathbb{R}^k$ with $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \Phi = \pi$ that is linear on each fibre. We write $\phi_m := \operatorname{pr}_2 \circ \Phi|_{E_m} : E_m \to \mathbb{R}^k$ for the linear isomorphism it induces on the fibre over $m \in U$, so that $\Phi(e) = (\pi(e), \phi_{\pi(e)}(e))$. A trivializing atlas $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in A}$ has [[Def - Transition Function of a Vector Bundle|transition functions]] $\tau_{\alpha\beta} : U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(k, \mathbb{R})$, defined — in the direction fixed on that page, from $\Phi_\beta$ to $\Phi_\alpha$ — by
$$(\Phi_\alpha \circ \Phi_\beta^{-1})(m, v) = (m, \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m)\, v), \qquad \text{equivalently } \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) = \phi_{\alpha, m} \circ \phi_{\beta, m}^{-1} .$$
For the second bundle $F$ we write $\Phi^F_\alpha$, $\phi^F_{\alpha,m}$ and $\tau^F_{\alpha\beta}$; where both bundles appear we assume (after refining) that they are trivialized over a common cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ — a refinement is harmless because the restriction of a local trivialization to a smaller open set is again a local trivialization.

Linear algebra. For a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$, $V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{R})$ is the [[Def - Dual Space|dual space]]; $\Lambda^p V$ is the $p$-th exterior power (defined in Step 0 below in terms of [[Def - Alternating Tensor and Lambda k V Dual|alternating tensors]]); $V \oplus W$ is the direct sum; $V \otimes W$ is the [[Def - Tensor Product of Vector Spaces|tensor product]]; $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$ is the space of linear maps and $\operatorname{End} V := \operatorname{Hom}(V, V)$. The standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^k$ is $(e_1, \dots, e_k)$ and its dual basis in $(\mathbb{R}^k)^*$ is $(\varepsilon^1, \dots, \varepsilon^k)$, $\varepsilon^i(e_j) = \delta^i_j$. $\operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$ is the space of real $l \times k$ matrices, identified with $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^k, \mathbb{R}^l)$ by letting a matrix act on column vectors; $E_{ji} \in \operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$ is the elementary matrix with a single $1$ in row $j$, column $i$. For a matrix $A$, $A^t$ is the transpose and $A^{-t} := (A^{-1})^t = (A^t)^{-1}$.

Maps. $f : M' \to M$ is a smooth map; $\iota : M' \hookrightarrow M$ is the inclusion of an open subset. The symbols $f^*E$ (pull-back) and $E|_{M'}$ (restriction) are defined on this page. For the fibre-product description we write $E' := \{(m', e) \in M' \times E : f(m') = \pi(e)\}$ and $\operatorname{pr}_1, \operatorname{pr}_2$ for the two projections of $M' \times E$.

This is a compound page: it defines the six algebraic operations $E^*$, $\Lambda^p E$, $E \oplus F$, $E \otimes F$, $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$, $\operatorname{End} E$ and the two pull-back operations $f^*E$, $E|_{M'}$ together, because they are all instances of one construction — apply a linear-algebra operation fibre by fibre, transport the local trivializations through it, and invoke the [[Thm - Vector Bundle Construction Lemma|vector bundle construction lemma]] — and because the transition-function bookkeeping that makes each of them a smooth bundle (Haydys's "the reader should check", item R2.1.2) is one uniform argument.

> [!warning] Convention: sources and the vault
> Haydys (§2.1.2, p. 5) defines every operation fibrewise, $(E^*)_m := (E_m)^*$, $(f^*E)_{m'} := E_{f(m')}$, and leaves local triviality to the reader; his Definition 1 asks $\pi$ to be a smooth submersion, which is implied by the vault's [[Def - Vector Bundle|definition]] (with a local trivialization $\Phi$, $\pi = \operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \Phi$ is the composite of a diffeomorphism and a submersion). Bär (Def. 2.1.11, pp. 39–40) defines the pull-back as the fibre product $\{(b', p) \in B' \times E : \lambda(b') = \pi(p)\}$ with projection $\operatorname{pr}_1$, for a general fibre bundle, and writes $\lambda : B' \to B$ where Haydys and this series write $f : M' \to M$. The two definitions of $f^*E$ produce isomorphic bundles — this is proved below (Proposition 2, Part III). Bär also allows complex vector bundles (Def. 2.1.7, typical fibre $\mathbb{K}^n$); every construction on this page goes through verbatim with $\mathbb{C}$ in place of $\mathbb{R}$ and $GL(k, \mathbb{C})$ in place of $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$, see the remark after Proposition 1.
>
> Transition functions versus frame changes. The vault's $\tau_{\alpha\beta}$ is the matrix of $\Phi_\alpha \circ \Phi_\beta^{-1}$. Haydys works with local frames: a frame $e = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ over $U$ corresponds to the trivialization $\Phi^{-1}(m, v) = \sum_i v^i e_i(m)$ ([[Ex - Local Frames Correspond to Local Trivialisations]]), and a frame change $e' = e\,g$ with $g : U \to GL(k, \mathbb{R})$ gives $\Phi' \circ \Phi^{-1}(m, v) = (m, g(m)^{-1} v)$, because $\sum_i v^i e_i = \sum_j (g^{-1} v)^j e'_j$. So *frame* change matrices are the inverses of *trivialization* transition functions; converting a formula from one language to the other replaces $\tau$ by $g^{-1}$.

# Axiom Motivation

We begin with what we have and what we want. A vector bundle, in Haydys's informal description (R2.1.1), is a family of vector spaces $\{E_m\}_{m \in M}$ parametrised smoothly by the points of a manifold; the smoothness is carried entirely by the local trivializations, which say that near every point the family looks like the constant family $U \times \mathbb{R}^k$. Linear algebra gives us a large repertoire of ways to make new vector spaces out of old ones — the dual, the exterior powers, direct sums, tensor products, spaces of linear maps — and the question this page answers is whether these operations can be performed on a *family* of vector spaces so as to produce a new *smooth* family. Nothing in gauge theory can be written without this: the connection matrix of a covariant derivative lives in $\Omega^1(U; \mathfrak{gl}_k)$, the curvature in $\Omega^2(M; \operatorname{End} E)$, a bundle-valued form in $\Gamma(\Lambda^p T^*M \otimes E)$, and a gauge transformation is a section of $\operatorname{End} E$; every one of these is a section of a bundle built by one of the operations defined here. The pull-back answers a second question of the same kind: given a bundle over $M$ and a map $f : M' \to M$, is there a bundle over $M'$ whose fibre over $m'$ is the fibre of $E$ over $f(m')$? Restricting a bundle to an open set, taking the bundle along a curve, and comparing two bundles through a map all require it.

The first desideratum is that the construction be *fibrewise*: the fibre of $E^*$ over $m$ must be the dual of the fibre of $E$ over $m$, and the fibre of $f^*E$ over $m'$ must be $E_{f(m')}$, with nothing added and nothing identified. The reason is that every later use is pointwise — a covector field is evaluated on a vector field point by point, an endomorphism field acts on a section point by point — and any construction whose fibres were not literally the vector spaces $(E_m)^*$ or $E_{f(m')}$ would force us to carry an identification along with every computation. This clause is not optional, but it is also the easy one: the underlying *set* $\bigsqcup_m (E_m)^*$ is determined, and the only question is which topology and smooth structure it carries.

The second desideratum is that the smooth structure be *canonical* — determined by the bundle $E$ alone — and here is where a genuine choice would otherwise enter. The construction lemma says that a family of vector spaces becomes a smooth bundle once we prescribe local bijections $\Phi_\alpha : \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$, linear on fibres, whose overlaps are given by smooth matrix-valued functions. So to make $\bigsqcup_m (E_m)^*$ a bundle we must *choose* such bijections, and the natural choice is to take the given trivializations of $E$ and push them through the linear-algebra operation: the isomorphism $\phi_{\alpha,m} : E_m \to \mathbb{R}^k$ induces an isomorphism $(E_m)^* \to (\mathbb{R}^k)^* \cong \mathbb{R}^k$, namely $\xi \mapsto \xi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}$. For this to be an *allowed* choice, two things must hold. The operation must be functorial on isomorphisms, so that the induced map is again a linear isomorphism, and the operation must act *smoothly* on $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$, so that the derived transition function $m \mapsto (\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m)^{-1})^t$ is again smooth. If we drop the second requirement the definition collapses. Consider the non-example worked in full in the Examples section below: on the trivial line bundle $E = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ over $M = \mathbb{R}$, one may declare two "identifications" of the fibres of $E \otimes E$ with $\mathbb{R}$ over the two open sets $U_1 = (-\infty, 1)$ and $U_2 = (-1, \infty)$ that differ on the overlap by the non-smooth factor $h(m) = 1 + |m|$. Each is a perfectly good fibrewise linear bijection, but no smooth structure on $\bigsqcup_m E_m \otimes E_m$ makes both of them local trivializations, because if it did then $\Phi_2 \circ \Phi_1^{-1}(m, t) = (m, h(m) t)$ would be smooth and so would $h$. The vector spaces alone do not determine the bundle; the identifications do, and they must be smooth. This is exactly why Haydys's R2.1.2 has content and why the [[Thm - Vector Bundle Construction Lemma|construction lemma]] is the right tool: it isolates precisely the data (smooth $GL$-valued transition functions satisfying the cocycle identities) that a family of vector spaces needs in order to be a bundle, and it guarantees the resulting smooth structure is unique.

The third desideratum, specific to the pull-back, is that the smooth structure on $f^*E$ be the one for which the tautological map $f^*E \to E$, $(m', e) \mapsto e$, is a smooth bundle homomorphism covering $f$ ([[Def - Bundle Homomorphism]], the version with a base map). Why insist on this? Because the whole purpose of $f^*E$ is to let $E$ be used *along* $f$: a vector field along a curve $\gamma$ is a section of $\gamma^*TM$ precisely so that it can be differentiated, compared, and transported using the geometry of $TM$, and that requires the map $\gamma^*TM \to TM$ to be smooth. The clause "$f$ smooth" is what makes this possible: with $f$ merely continuous, the sets $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ are still open and the transition functions $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$ are still continuous, so one obtains a *topological* bundle, but $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$ need not be smooth and the construction lemma does not apply. With $f$ discontinuous, even $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ fails to be open and there is no local triviality at all. Bär's fibre-product description $\{(m', e) : f(m') = \pi(e)\}$ makes the third desideratum visible: $f^*E$ is carved out of $M' \times E$ as a closed embedded submanifold, and the map to $E$ is the restriction of a projection.

A reader who accepts these three desiderata can reconstruct every definition on this page. Given an operation on vector spaces that is functorial and smooth on $GL$, define the new bundle fibrewise, transport the trivializations through the operation, verify that the derived transition functions are smooth and satisfy the cocycle identities, and invoke the construction lemma. Given a smooth map $f$, define $(f^*E)_{m'} := E_{f(m')}$, transport the trivializations by precomposition with $f$, and do the same. What would go wrong without the operations is not that some theorem becomes false but that the objects of the theory cannot be named: the statement "the curvature of a connection is a $2$-form with values in $\operatorname{End} E$" ([[Def - Curvature of a Vector-Bundle Connection]]) presupposes that $\operatorname{End} E$ and $\Lambda^2 T^*M \otimes \operatorname{End} E$ are vector bundles whose sections can be differentiated, and the [[Thm - Tensoriality Lemma for C-Infinity-Linear Maps|tensoriality lemma]], which identifies $C^\infty(M)$-linear operators $\Gamma(E) \to \Omega^p(M; F)$ with sections of $\Lambda^p T^*M \otimes \operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$, is meaningless until $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$ is a bundle.

# The Definition

**Step 0 — the linear-algebra operations, with the matrices they induce.** Let $V$, $W$ be real vector spaces of dimensions $k$, $l$, with bases $(v_1, \dots, v_k)$, $(w_1, \dots, w_l)$, and let $A : V \to V'$, $B : W \to W'$ be linear maps to spaces of the same dimensions with bases $(v'_1, \dots, v'_k)$, $(w'_1, \dots, w'_l)$; write $A$, $B$ also for the matrices $A v_j = \sum_i A_{ij} v'_i$, $B w_j = \sum_i B_{ij} w'_i$. We record the six operations, the map each induces on $A$ and $B$, and the matrix of that induced map in the induced bases. Every claim carries its verification.

1. **Dual.** $V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, \mathbb{R})$ with the dual basis $(v^1, \dots, v^k)$, $v^i(v_j) = \delta^i_j$; $\dim V^* = k$ ([[Thm - Dimension of Dual Space]]). The induced map is the transpose $A^* : V'^* \to V^*$, $A^*(\xi) := \xi \circ A$; it runs *backwards*, so an isomorphism $A$ induces the isomorphism $(A^{-1})^* : V^* \to V'^*$, $\xi \mapsto \xi \circ A^{-1}$. Its matrix is $A^{-t}$: writing $(A^{-1})^* v^i = \sum_j c_{ji} v'^j$ and evaluating on $v'_j$ gives $c_{ji} = v^i(A^{-1} v'_j) = (A^{-1})_{ij}$, so the matrix $(c_{ji})$ is $(A^{-1})^t$ (by the definition of the matrix of a linear map in a basis, and the definition of the dual basis).

2. **Exterior power.** For $p \geq 0$ define $\Lambda^p V$ to be the space of alternating $p$-linear maps $V^* \times \cdots \times V^* \to \mathbb{R}$, that is, $\Lambda^p V := \Lambda^p((V^*)^*)$ in the notation of [[Def - Alternating Tensor and Lambda k V Dual]] applied to the space $V^*$; the canonical isomorphism $V \to (V^*)^*$, $v \mapsto (\xi \mapsto \xi(v))$, which is injective since a nonzero $v$ can be completed to a basis and then its dual basis vector does not vanish on it, and hence bijective by equality of dimensions, lets us regard the elements of $V$ as functionals on $V^*$. For $u_1, \dots, u_p \in V$ put $(u_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge u_p)(\xi^1, \dots, \xi^p) := \det\big(\xi^i(u_j)\big)_{i,j}$; this is alternating and multilinear in the $\xi^i$ because the determinant is alternating and multilinear in its rows, and it is multilinear and alternating in the $u_j$ because the determinant is so in its columns. By the basis theorem on the cited page (applied to the space $V^*$, whose dual is $V$), the elements $v_I := v_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge v_{i_p}$ for increasing multi-indices $I = (i_1 < \cdots < i_p)$ form a basis of $\Lambda^p V$, so $\dim \Lambda^p V = \binom{k}{p}$, with $\Lambda^0 V = \mathbb{R}$, $\Lambda^1 V = V$ and $\Lambda^p V = 0$ for $p > k$. The induced map is $\Lambda^p A : \Lambda^p V \to \Lambda^p V'$, $(\Lambda^p A)(\omega)(\eta^1, \dots, \eta^p) := \omega(\eta^1 \circ A, \dots, \eta^p \circ A)$, which is linear in $\omega$ and satisfies $(\Lambda^p A)(u_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge u_p) = A u_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A u_p$ (evaluate both sides on $(\eta^1, \dots, \eta^p)$: both give $\det(\eta^i(A u_j))$) and $\Lambda^p(A' \circ A) = \Lambda^p A' \circ \Lambda^p A$, $\Lambda^p(\mathrm{id}) = \mathrm{id}$ (evaluate: $\Lambda^p A' (\Lambda^p A\, \omega)(\eta) = (\Lambda^p A\, \omega)(\eta \circ A') = \omega(\eta \circ A' \circ A)$). Consequently $\Lambda^p$ of an isomorphism is an isomorphism, with inverse $\Lambda^p(A^{-1})$. Its matrix in the bases $(v_I)$, $(v'_J)$ has entries the $p \times p$ minors $\det(A_{J,I})$ (rows $J$, columns $I$): indeed
$$(\Lambda^p A)(v_I) = A v_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge A v_{i_p} = \sum_{j_1, \dots, j_p} A_{j_1 i_1} \cdots A_{j_p i_p}\; v'_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge v'_{j_p} \qquad \text{(multilinearity of } \wedge \text{ in each slot)},$$
the terms with a repeated index vanish (alternation), and collecting the terms whose index set is a given increasing $J$ and reordering each by a permutation $\sigma \in S_p$ (a sign $\operatorname{sgn} \sigma$, by alternation) gives $\sum_J \big(\sum_{\sigma \in S_p} \operatorname{sgn}\sigma \prod_{r} A_{j_{\sigma(r)} i_r}\big) v'_J = \sum_J \det(A_{J,I})\, v'_J$ (Leibniz formula for the determinant). We write $\Lambda^p A$ also for this $\binom{k}{p} \times \binom{k}{p}$ matrix; its entries are polynomials in the entries of $A$.

3. **Direct sum.** $V \oplus W$ with basis $(v_1, \dots, v_k, w_1, \dots, w_l)$, $\dim = k + l$; induced map $A \oplus B$, $(v, w) \mapsto (Av, Bw)$, with block-diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(A, B)$ (read off from the images of the basis vectors). An isomorphism pair induces an isomorphism with inverse $A^{-1} \oplus B^{-1}$.

4. **Tensor product.** $V \otimes W$ as on [[Def - Tensor Product of Vector Spaces]] — the bilinear forms on $V^* \times W^*$, with $(v \otimes w)(\xi, \eta) := \xi(v) \eta(w)$ — has basis $(v_i \otimes w_j)$ and dimension $kl$. Induced map $A \otimes B$, $(A \otimes B)(\theta)(\xi', \eta') := \theta(\xi' \circ A, \eta' \circ B)$, linear in $\theta$, with $(A \otimes B)(v \otimes w) = Av \otimes Bw$ (evaluate: both sides give $\xi'(Av)\eta'(Bw)$) and $(A' \otimes B') \circ (A \otimes B) = (A'A) \otimes (B'B)$ (evaluate as in item 2). Hence isomorphisms induce isomorphisms. Its matrix in the bases $(v_i \otimes w_j)$, $(v'_a \otimes w'_b)$, ordered lexicographically, is the Kronecker product $A \otimes B$ with entries $(A \otimes B)_{(a,b),(i,j)} = A_{ai} B_{bj}$, since $(A \otimes B)(v_i \otimes w_j) = Av_i \otimes Bw_j = \sum_{a,b} A_{ai} B_{bj}\, v'_a \otimes w'_b$ (bilinearity of $\otimes$). Its entries are polynomials in the entries of $A$ and $B$. We fix the identification $\mathbb{R}^k \otimes \mathbb{R}^l \cong \mathbb{R}^{kl}$ by this lexicographic basis $(e_i \otimes e_j)$.

5. **Hom.** $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$ with basis the maps $\epsilon_{ji}$, $\epsilon_{ji}(v_a) := \delta_{ia} w_j$, so $\dim = lk$; for $V = \mathbb{R}^k$, $W = \mathbb{R}^l$ this is $\operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$ with basis $(E_{ji})$, and we fix the identification $\operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^{lk}$ by listing entries lexicographically. For isomorphisms $A : V \to V'$, $B : W \to W'$ the induced map is $\operatorname{Hom}(A, B) : \operatorname{Hom}(V, W) \to \operatorname{Hom}(V', W')$, $\varphi \mapsto B \circ \varphi \circ A^{-1}$; it is linear in $\varphi$, has inverse $\psi \mapsto B^{-1} \circ \psi \circ A$, and satisfies $\operatorname{Hom}(A'A, B'B) = \operatorname{Hom}(A', B') \circ \operatorname{Hom}(A, B)$ (since $B'B \varphi A^{-1} A'^{-1} = B'(B \varphi A^{-1})A'^{-1}$). In matrix terms it is $X \mapsto B X A^{-1}$ on $\operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$, whose entries $(BXA^{-1})_{ba} = \sum_{j,i} B_{bj} X_{ji} (A^{-1})_{ia}$ are polynomials in the entries of $B$ and of $A^{-1}$; as a $lk \times lk$ matrix acting on the lexicographic coordinates it is the Kronecker product $B \otimes A^{-t}$, but we shall not need this form.

6. **End.** $\operatorname{End} V := \operatorname{Hom}(V, V)$, the case $W = V$ of item 5, with induced map $\varphi \mapsto A \varphi A^{-1}$ (conjugation).

**Step 0′ — smoothness of the induced matrix maps.** Each of the maps $A \mapsto A^{-t}$, $A \mapsto \Lambda^p A$, $(A, B) \mapsto \operatorname{diag}(A, B)$, $(A, B) \mapsto A \otimes B$, $(A, B) \mapsto (X \mapsto BXA^{-1})$ is a smooth map from $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$ (or $GL(k, \mathbb{R}) \times GL(l, \mathbb{R})$) into the appropriate $GL(k', \mathbb{R})$. Reason: matrix inversion $GL(k, \mathbb{R}) \to GL(k, \mathbb{R})$ is smooth, because by Cramer's rule $(A^{-1})_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} \det(A^{(ji)}) / \det A$ with $A^{(ji)}$ the minor matrix, a rational function of the entries whose denominator does not vanish on $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$; transposition is linear; and every other entry is a polynomial in the entries of $A$, $B$, $A^{-1}$ (items 2, 4, 5 of Step 0). A composite of smooth maps is smooth, and the image lies in $GL$ because each operation sends isomorphisms to isomorphisms (Step 0). This is the only place where the smoothness of the linear-algebra operations enters, and it is what item R2.1.2 asks the reader to check.

## Definition 1 (algebraic operations on vector bundles)

Let $\pi_E : E \to M$ and $\pi_F : F \to M$ be vector bundles of ranks $k$ and $l$ over the same base. Define families of vector spaces over $M$ by
$$(E^*)_m := (E_m)^*, \qquad (\Lambda^p E)_m := \Lambda^p(E_m), \qquad (E \oplus F)_m := E_m \oplus F_m,$$
$$(E \otimes F)_m := E_m \otimes F_m, \qquad \operatorname{Hom}(E, F)_m := \operatorname{Hom}(E_m, F_m), \qquad (\operatorname{End} E)_m := \operatorname{End}(E_m),$$
and let $E^*$, $\Lambda^p E$, $E \oplus F$, $E \otimes F$, $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$, $\operatorname{End} E$ denote the corresponding disjoint unions over $m \in M$, each with the projection sending the fibre over $m$ to $m$. For a trivializing atlas $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}$ of $E$ (and $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi^F_\alpha)\}$ of $F$ over the same cover), the **induced local trivializations** are the bijections, over $U_\alpha$,
$$\Phi^{*}_\alpha(\xi) := \big(m,\ \xi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}\big), \qquad \Phi^{\Lambda^p}_\alpha(\omega) := \big(m,\ \Lambda^p(\phi_{\alpha,m})\, \omega\big), \qquad \Phi^{\oplus}_\alpha(e, f) := \big(m,\ (\phi_{\alpha,m} e,\ \phi^F_{\alpha,m} f)\big),$$
$$\Phi^{\otimes}_\alpha(\theta) := \big(m,\ (\phi_{\alpha,m} \otimes \phi^F_{\alpha,m})\, \theta\big), \qquad \Phi^{\operatorname{Hom}}_\alpha(\varphi) := \big(m,\ \phi^F_{\alpha,m} \circ \varphi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}\big), \qquad \Phi^{\operatorname{End}}_\alpha(\varphi) := \big(m,\ \phi_{\alpha,m} \circ \varphi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}\big),$$
for $\xi \in (E_m)^*$, $\omega \in \Lambda^p E_m$, and so on, where $m$ is the base point of the argument and the second components are read in $\mathbb{R}^{k'}$ through the identifications of Step 0 ($(\mathbb{R}^k)^* \cong \mathbb{R}^k$ via the dual basis, $\Lambda^p \mathbb{R}^k \cong \mathbb{R}^{\binom{k}{p}}$ via the $e_I$, $\mathbb{R}^k \oplus \mathbb{R}^l = \mathbb{R}^{k+l}$, $\mathbb{R}^k \otimes \mathbb{R}^l \cong \mathbb{R}^{kl}$, $\operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^{lk}$). The **induced transition functions** are

| bundle | rank | transition function from $\Phi^{\bullet}_\beta$ to $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha$ |
|---|---|---|
| $E^*$ | $k$ | $\tau^{*}_{\alpha\beta} = (\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})^t = \tau_{\beta\alpha}^t$ |
| $\Lambda^p E$ | $\binom{k}{p}$ | $\tau^{\Lambda^p}_{\alpha\beta} = \Lambda^p \tau_{\alpha\beta}$ (matrix of $p \times p$ minors) |
| $E \oplus F$ | $k + l$ | $\tau^{\oplus}_{\alpha\beta} = \operatorname{diag}(\tau_{\alpha\beta}, \tau^F_{\alpha\beta})$ |
| $E \otimes F$ | $kl$ | $\tau^{\otimes}_{\alpha\beta} = \tau_{\alpha\beta} \otimes \tau^F_{\alpha\beta}$ (Kronecker product) |
| $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$ | $lk$ | $\tau^{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\alpha\beta} : X \mapsto \tau^F_{\alpha\beta}\, X\, \tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}$ |
| $\operatorname{End} E$ | $k^2$ | $\tau^{\operatorname{End}}_{\alpha\beta} : X \mapsto \tau_{\alpha\beta}\, X\, \tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}$ |

and the **smooth vector bundle structure** on each of the six total spaces is the unique one, supplied by Proposition 1 below, for which the induced local trivializations are smooth local trivializations.

**The smallest case.** Let $L \to M$ be a line bundle, $k = 1$, so that the transition functions are nowhere-vanishing smooth functions $\tau_{\alpha\beta} : U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(1, \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}^\times$. The table becomes: $L^*$ has transition functions $\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}$; $L \otimes L$ has $\tau_{\alpha\beta}^2$; $\operatorname{End} L = \operatorname{Hom}(L, L)$ has $X \mapsto \tau_{\alpha\beta} X \tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1} = X$, that is, the constant $1$; $\Lambda^0 L$ and $\Lambda^1 L = L$ have $1$ and $\tau_{\alpha\beta}$, and $\Lambda^p L = 0$ for $p \geq 2$. Two conclusions follow at once from Lemma C below. First, $\operatorname{End} L$ is trivial for every line bundle $L$: its transition functions are identically $1$. Second, for the Möbius bundle $L \to S^1$ ([[Ex - The Möbius Bundle is Nontrivial]]) with its two-set trivializing atlas whose transition function takes the values $\pm 1$, we have $\tau^{-1} = \tau$ and $\tau^2 = 1$, so $L^* \cong L$ and $L \otimes L$ is trivial — the square of the Möbius bundle is the cylinder. The transition functions detect exactly what the operations do.

## Proposition 1 (each operation produces a vector bundle)

> **Proposition 1.** With the notation of Definition 1, each of $E^*$, $\Lambda^p E$, $E \oplus F$, $E \otimes F$, $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$, $\operatorname{End} E$ carries a unique topology and smooth structure making it a smooth vector bundle over $M$ of the rank in the table, with the induced local trivializations as smooth local trivializations and the induced transition functions as its transition functions. The structure does not depend on the trivializing atlas chosen: two atlases of $E$ (and $F$) induce the same smooth structure.

The proof is by the [[Thm - Vector Bundle Construction Lemma|vector bundle construction lemma]], which we restate: *if $E_p$ ($p \in M$) are real vector spaces of dimension $k'$, $\{U_\alpha\}$ is an open cover of $M$, $\Phi_\alpha : \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ are bijections restricting to linear isomorphisms on each fibre, and for each $\alpha, \beta$ the composite $\Phi_\alpha \circ \Phi_\beta^{-1}$ has the form $(p, v) \mapsto (p, \tau_{\alpha\beta}(p) v)$ with $\tau_{\alpha\beta} : U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(k', \mathbb{R})$ smooth and satisfying $\tau_{\alpha\alpha} = \mathrm{id}$, $\tau_{\alpha\gamma} = \tau_{\alpha\beta}\tau_{\beta\gamma}$, then $\bigsqcup_p E_p$ has a unique topology and smooth structure making it a smooth rank-$k'$ vector bundle with the $\Phi_\alpha$ as smooth local trivializations.* We first isolate two lemmas.

> [!note]- Lemma A: the cocycle identities are automatic
> **Statement.** Let $\Psi_\alpha : \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ be bijections, linear isomorphisms on each fibre, indexed by an open cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ of $M$, and suppose that for all $\alpha, \beta$ there is a map $\sigma_{\alpha\beta} : U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(k', \mathbb{R})$ with $(\Psi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\beta^{-1})(m, v) = (m, \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m) v)$ for all $(m, v)$. Then $\sigma_{\alpha\alpha}(m) = \mathrm{id}$, $\sigma_{\beta\alpha}(m) = \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)^{-1}$ and $\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}(m) = \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)\sigma_{\beta\gamma}(m)$ wherever the maps are defined.
>
> **Full proof.** We assume the displayed form and must show the three identities. **Identity.** $\Psi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\alpha^{-1}$ is the identity of $U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$, so $(m, \sigma_{\alpha\alpha}(m) v) = (m, v)$ for every $v \in \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ (by hypothesis, with $\beta = \alpha$); a matrix that fixes every vector is the identity, hence $\sigma_{\alpha\alpha}(m) = \mathrm{id}$. **Cocycle.** For $m \in U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \cap U_\gamma$ and $v \in \mathbb{R}^{k'}$,
> $$(m, \sigma_{\alpha\gamma}(m) v) = (\Psi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\gamma^{-1})(m, v) = (\Psi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\beta^{-1}) \circ (\Psi_\beta \circ \Psi_\gamma^{-1})(m, v) = (\Psi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\beta^{-1})(m, \sigma_{\beta\gamma}(m) v) = (m, \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)\sigma_{\beta\gamma}(m) v)$$
> (first and last equalities: the hypothesis; middle: $\Psi_\beta^{-1} \circ \Psi_\beta = \mathrm{id}$ on $\pi^{-1}(U_\beta)$, which contains $\Psi_\gamma^{-1}(m, v)$ because $\pi(\Psi_\gamma^{-1}(m, v)) = m \in U_\beta$). Two matrices agreeing on every vector are equal, so $\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}(m) = \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)\sigma_{\beta\gamma}(m)$. **Inverse.** Taking $\gamma = \alpha$ in the cocycle identity and using the identity property, $\mathrm{id} = \sigma_{\alpha\alpha}(m) = \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)\sigma_{\beta\alpha}(m)$, so $\sigma_{\beta\alpha}(m) = \sigma_{\alpha\beta}(m)^{-1}$. Therefore all three identities hold, and they hold for the transition functions of *any* system of fibrewise-linear bijections of the displayed form, before any smoothness is known. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Lemma B: the induced trivializations are fibrewise linear isomorphisms and their overlaps have the displayed form
> **Statement.** Each induced trivialization $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha$ of Definition 1 is a bijection $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ restricting to a linear isomorphism on every fibre, and $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha \circ (\Phi^{\bullet}_\beta)^{-1}(m, v) = (m, \tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}(m) v)$ with $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}$ as in the table.
>
> **Full proof.** We must show, for each of the six operations, that the fibre map is a linear isomorphism and that the composite has the stated matrix. In every case the fibre map is the linear-algebra operation of Step 0 applied to the isomorphism $\phi_{\alpha,m} : E_m \to \mathbb{R}^k$ (and $\phi^F_{\alpha,m}$), and Step 0 established that each operation carries isomorphisms to isomorphisms; the map on the whole of $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ is a bijection onto $U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ because it is a bijection fibre by fibre and preserves the base point. It remains to compute the overlaps. Fix $m \in U_\alpha \cap U_\beta$ and recall $\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) = \phi_{\alpha,m} \circ \phi_{\beta,m}^{-1}$ (Notation).
>
> **Dual.** For $w \in \mathbb{R}^k$, read as the functional $\sum_i w_i \varepsilon^i$, $(\Phi^*_\beta)^{-1}(m, w) = (\sum_i w_i \varepsilon^i) \circ \phi_{\beta,m} \in (E_m)^*$ (inverting $\xi \mapsto \xi \circ \phi_{\beta,m}^{-1}$), and then $\Phi^*_\alpha$ of this is $(\sum_i w_i \varepsilon^i) \circ \phi_{\beta,m} \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1} = (\sum_i w_i \varepsilon^i) \circ \tau_{\beta\alpha}(m)$ (definition of $\tau_{\beta\alpha}$). Its $j$-th component is $\sum_i w_i \varepsilon^i(\tau_{\beta\alpha}(m) e_j) = \sum_i w_i (\tau_{\beta\alpha}(m))_{ij} = (\tau_{\beta\alpha}(m)^t w)_j$ (definition of the matrix entries). So $\tau^*_{\alpha\beta} = \tau_{\beta\alpha}^t = (\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})^t$, the last step by Lemma A applied to the atlas of $E$.
>
> **Exterior power.** $\Phi^{\Lambda^p}_\alpha \circ (\Phi^{\Lambda^p}_\beta)^{-1}(m, \cdot) = \Lambda^p(\phi_{\alpha,m}) \circ \Lambda^p(\phi_{\beta,m})^{-1} = \Lambda^p(\phi_{\alpha,m}) \circ \Lambda^p(\phi_{\beta,m}^{-1}) = \Lambda^p(\phi_{\alpha,m} \circ \phi_{\beta,m}^{-1}) = \Lambda^p(\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m))$ (functoriality of $\Lambda^p$, Step 0 item 2), whose matrix in the basis $(e_I)$ is the matrix of minors.
>
> **Direct sum, tensor product.** The same functoriality computation with $\oplus$ and $\otimes$ (Step 0 items 3, 4) gives $\phi_{\alpha,m}\phi_{\beta,m}^{-1} \oplus \phi^F_{\alpha,m}(\phi^F_{\beta,m})^{-1} = \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) \oplus \tau^F_{\alpha\beta}(m)$, matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\tau_{\alpha\beta}, \tau^F_{\alpha\beta})$, and $\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) \otimes \tau^F_{\alpha\beta}(m)$, the Kronecker product.
>
> **Hom, End.** For $X \in \operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$, $(\Phi^{\operatorname{Hom}}_\beta)^{-1}(m, X) = (\phi^F_{\beta,m})^{-1} \circ X \circ \phi_{\beta,m}$ (inverting $\varphi \mapsto \phi^F_{\beta,m}\varphi\phi_{\beta,m}^{-1}$), and applying $\Phi^{\operatorname{Hom}}_\alpha$ gives $\phi^F_{\alpha,m}(\phi^F_{\beta,m})^{-1} \circ X \circ \phi_{\beta,m}\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1} = \tau^F_{\alpha\beta}(m)\, X\, \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m)^{-1}$ (definition of the transition functions, and $\phi_{\beta,m}\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1} = \tau_{\beta\alpha}(m) = \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m)^{-1}$ by Lemma A). $\operatorname{End}$ is the case $F = E$.
>
> Therefore every induced trivialization is fibrewise a linear isomorphism and its overlaps are given by the matrices in the table. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Complete proof of Proposition 1
> **What is assumed and what must be shown.** We are given the bundles $E$, $F$ with trivializing atlases over a common cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ and must show that each of the six families of vector spaces satisfies the hypotheses (i)–(iii) of the construction lemma, then that the resulting structure is independent of the atlas.
>
> **Step 1 — hypotheses (i) and (ii).** The cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ is an open cover of $M$ (hypothesis (i)). Each fibre is a real vector space of the dimension listed in the table (Step 0: $\dim V^* = k$, $\dim \Lambda^p V = \binom{k}{p}$, $\dim V \oplus W = k + l$, $\dim V \otimes W = kl$, $\dim \operatorname{Hom}(V, W) = lk$, $\dim \operatorname{End} V = k^2$), and by Lemma B each induced trivialization is a bijection $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ that is a linear isomorphism on every fibre (hypothesis (ii)).
>
> **Step 2 — hypothesis (iii): form, smoothness, cocycle.** By Lemma B the composite $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha \circ (\Phi^{\bullet}_\beta)^{-1}$ has the form $(m, v) \mapsto (m, \tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}(m) v)$ with $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}$ given by the table. Each $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}$ is the composite of the smooth map $\tau_{\alpha\beta}$ (or $(\tau_{\alpha\beta}, \tau^F_{\alpha\beta})$, smooth into the product $GL(k, \mathbb{R}) \times GL(l, \mathbb{R})$ because each component is smooth) with one of the smooth matrix maps of Step 0′, hence is a smooth map $U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(k', \mathbb{R})$ (composition of smooth maps). The cocycle identities $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\alpha} = \mathrm{id}$ and $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\gamma} = \tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}\tau^{\bullet}_{\beta\gamma}$ hold by Lemma A applied to the system $\{\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha\}$, whose overlaps have the required form by Lemma B. (They can also be checked directly from the table: for the dual, $(\tau_{\alpha\gamma}^{-1})^t = ((\tau_{\alpha\beta}\tau_{\beta\gamma})^{-1})^t = (\tau_{\beta\gamma}^{-1}\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})^t = (\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})^t(\tau_{\beta\gamma}^{-1})^t$ by the cocycle identity for $E$ and the rules $(XY)^{-1} = Y^{-1}X^{-1}$, $(XY)^t = Y^tX^t$; for the others it is the functoriality of Step 0.) Hypothesis (iii) holds.
>
> **Step 3 — conclusion of the construction lemma.** All hypotheses of the [[Thm - Vector Bundle Construction Lemma|construction lemma]] hold, so each total space has a unique topology and smooth structure making it a smooth vector bundle of rank $k'$ over $M$ with the $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha$ as smooth local trivializations; its transition functions are then the $\tau^{\bullet}_{\alpha\beta}$ by definition of transition functions ([[Def - Transition Function of a Vector Bundle]]).
>
> **Step 4 — independence of the atlas.** Let $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in A}$ and $\{(V_\gamma, \Psi_\gamma)\}_{\gamma \in C}$ be two trivializing atlases of $E$ (and likewise for $F$). Their union $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\} \cup \{(V_\gamma, \Psi_\gamma)\}$ is again a trivializing atlas of $E$, whose transition functions $\Phi_\alpha \circ \Psi_\gamma^{-1}(m, v) = (m, \rho_{\alpha\gamma}(m) v)$ are smooth $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$-valued, because both $\Phi_\alpha$ and $\Psi_\gamma$ are smooth local trivializations of the *same* bundle $E$ ([[Def - Local Trivialization]]: the composite of two smooth trivializations has this form with $\rho$ smooth). Applying Steps 1–3 to the union atlas yields a smooth structure on $E^{\bullet}$ for which *all* of $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha$, $\Psi^{\bullet}_\gamma$ are smooth local trivializations; by the uniqueness clause of the construction lemma this structure coincides with the one obtained from $\{\Phi_\alpha\}$ alone (it makes the $\Phi^{\bullet}_\alpha$ smooth trivializations) and with the one obtained from $\{\Psi_\gamma\}$ alone. Hence the two structures agree.
>
> Therefore each of the six operations produces a well-defined smooth vector bundle, canonically attached to $E$ and $F$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (complex bundles).** If $E$ and $F$ are complex vector bundles in Bär's sense (Def. 2.1.7: fibres are complex vector spaces, trivializations complex-linear on fibres, transition functions in $GL(k, \mathbb{C})$), the same argument applies with $\mathbb{C}$ in place of $\mathbb{R}$ throughout Step 0 and Step 0′, giving complex bundles $E^*$, $\Lambda^p E$, and so on. To see that the construction lemma, stated on the vault's page for real bundles, still applies, regard $\mathbb{C}^{k'}$ as $\mathbb{R}^{2k'}$ and $GL(k', \mathbb{C})$ as the subgroup of $GL(2k', \mathbb{R})$ of real matrices commuting with the standard complex structure $J$; the inclusion $GL(k', \mathbb{C}) \hookrightarrow GL(2k', \mathbb{R})$ is the restriction of the real-linear map $\operatorname{Mat}(k' \times k', \mathbb{C}) \to \operatorname{Mat}(2k' \times 2k', \mathbb{R})$ that writes $a + ib$ as the block $\begin{pmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$, hence is smooth. The lemma then produces a real bundle of rank $2k'$ whose trivializations are complex-linear on fibres, which is exactly a complex vector bundle of rank $k'$.

> [!note]- Lemma C: equal transition functions give isomorphic bundles; identity transition functions give a trivial bundle
> **Statement.** Let $\pi : E \to M$ and $\pi' : E' \to M$ be vector bundles of rank $k'$ with trivializing atlases $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}$ and $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi'_\alpha)\}$ over the same cover and with the same transition functions, $\tau'_{\alpha\beta} = \tau_{\alpha\beta}$ for all $\alpha, \beta$. Then $E \cong E'$ as vector bundles over $M$. In particular, a bundle admitting a trivializing atlas all of whose transition functions are identically $\mathrm{id}$ is trivial.
>
> **Full proof.** We assume equal transition functions and must construct a bundle isomorphism $\Theta : E \to E'$ over $M$. **Define locally.** On $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ put $\Theta_\alpha := (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1} \circ \Phi_\alpha : \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha) \to \pi'^{-1}(U_\alpha)$. This is a diffeomorphism (composite of two diffeomorphisms), covers the identity ($\pi' \circ (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1} = \operatorname{pr}_1$ and $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \Phi_\alpha = \pi$), and is linear on each fibre (composite of fibrewise linear maps). **Check agreement on overlaps.** For $e \in \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta)$ with $\Phi_\beta(e) = (m, v)$,
> $$\Theta_\alpha(e) = (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}\big(\Phi_\alpha \Phi_\beta^{-1}(m, v)\big) = (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}(m, \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) v) = (\Phi'_\beta)^{-1}\big(\Phi'_\beta (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}(m, \tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) v)\big) = (\Phi'_\beta)^{-1}(m, \tau'_{\beta\alpha}(m)\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) v) = (\Phi'_\beta)^{-1}(m, v) = \Theta_\beta(e)$$
> (second equality: definition of $\tau_{\alpha\beta}$; fourth: definition of $\tau'_{\beta\alpha}$; fifth: $\tau'_{\beta\alpha} = \tau_{\beta\alpha} = \tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}$ by hypothesis and Lemma A). **Glue.** Hence the $\Theta_\alpha$ agree on overlaps and define a single map $\Theta : E \to E'$, which is smooth (smoothness is local, and $\Theta$ restricts to the smooth $\Theta_\alpha$ on the open sets $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ covering $E$), bijective with smooth inverse glued from the $\Theta_\alpha^{-1} = \Phi_\alpha^{-1}\Phi'_\alpha$ (which agree on overlaps by the same computation with the roles of $E$ and $E'$ exchanged), covers the identity and is fibrewise a linear isomorphism. So $\Theta$ is a bundle isomorphism ([[Def - Bundle Homomorphism]]). **The particular case.** If all $\tau_{\alpha\beta} = \mathrm{id}$, apply the above with $E' := M \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$ and $\Phi'_\alpha := \mathrm{id}$ on $U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$, whose transition functions are $\mathrm{id}$; then $E \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^{k'}$, that is, $E$ is trivial. Therefore bundles with the same transition functions are isomorphic, and identity transition functions force triviality. $\blacksquare$

## Definition 2 (pull-back and restriction)

Let $\pi : E \to M$ be a vector bundle of rank $k$ and $f : M' \to M$ a smooth map. Define the family of vector spaces over $M'$
$$(f^*E)_{m'} := E_{f(m')} \qquad (m' \in M'),$$
with total space the disjoint union $f^*E := \bigsqcup_{m' \in M'} E_{f(m')}$, whose elements we write as pairs $(m', e)$ with $e \in E_{f(m')}$ to keep the base point visible, and projection $\pi'(m', e) := m'$. For a trivializing atlas $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}$ of $E$, the **induced local trivializations** of $f^*E$ are, over the open sets $U'_\alpha := f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$,
$$\Phi'_\alpha : \pi'^{-1}(U'_\alpha) \to U'_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^k, \qquad \Phi'_\alpha(m', e) := \big(m',\ \phi_{\alpha, f(m')}(e)\big),$$
and the **induced transition functions** are $\tau'_{\alpha\beta} := \tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f : U'_\alpha \cap U'_\beta \to GL(k, \mathbb{R})$. The **pull-back bundle** $f^*E \to M'$ is $f^*E$ with the unique smooth vector bundle structure (Proposition 2) for which the $\Phi'_\alpha$ are smooth local trivializations. The map
$$\hat f : f^*E \to E, \qquad \hat f(m', e) := e,$$
is the **tautological bundle map**; it covers $f$, that is, $\pi \circ \hat f = f \circ \pi'$.

If $M' \subseteq M$ is an open subset and $\iota : M' \hookrightarrow M$ the inclusion (a smooth map, [[Ex - The Inclusion of an Open Submanifold is Smooth]]), the **restriction** of $E$ to $M'$ is $E|_{M'} := \iota^* E$; its fibre over $m' \in M'$ is $E_{m'}$ and its trivializations are the restrictions of those of $E$ to the open sets $U_\alpha \cap M'$. Proposition 2, Part IV, identifies $E|_{M'}$ with the open subset $\pi^{-1}(M') \subseteq E$ with the restricted projection — the object usually meant by "the restriction".

**The smallest case.** Let $f : M' \to M$ be the constant map with value $m_0 \in M$. Then $(f^*E)_{m'} = E_{m_0}$ for every $m'$, and for a trivialization $\Phi_\alpha$ with $m_0 \in U_\alpha$ we have $U'_\alpha = f^{-1}(U_\alpha) = M'$ and $\Phi'_\alpha(m', e) = (m', \phi_{\alpha, m_0}(e))$, a *global* trivialization; so $f^*E \cong M' \times E_{m_0} \cong M' \times \mathbb{R}^k$ is trivial. Pulling back along a constant map forgets everything about $E$ except one fibre. At the other extreme, $f = \mathrm{id}_M$ gives $\mathrm{id}^* E = E$ with the same trivializations, and the composite rule $(g \circ f)^* E = f^*(g^* E)$ holds on the nose for the fibrewise definition ($E_{g(f(m''))}$ both times) with transition functions $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ g \circ f$ both times.

## Proposition 2 (the pull-back is a vector bundle, and it is Bär's fibre product)

> **Proposition 2.** Let $\pi : E \to M$ be a smooth vector bundle of rank $k$ and $f : M' \to M$ a smooth map, $n' := \dim M'$.
>
> **Part I (pull-back bundle).** $f^*E$ carries a unique topology and smooth structure making it a smooth vector bundle of rank $k$ over $M'$ with the $\Phi'_\alpha$ as smooth local trivializations and the $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$ as transition functions, independent of the atlas of $E$.
>
> **Part II (the tautological map).** $\hat f : f^*E \to E$, $(m', e) \mapsto e$, is a smooth bundle homomorphism covering $f$ which restricts to the linear isomorphism $(f^*E)_{m'} = E_{f(m')} \to E_{f(m')}$, the identity, on each fibre.
>
> **Part III (fibre-product description, Bär Def. 2.1.11 and pp. 39–40).** The subset $E' := \{(m', e) \in M' \times E : f(m') = \pi(e)\}$ is a closed embedded submanifold of $M' \times E$ of dimension $n' + k$; with $\pi' := \operatorname{pr}_1|_{E'}$ and the vector space structure on $E'_{m'} = \{m'\} \times E_{f(m')}$ transported from $E_{f(m')}$, $(E', \pi', M')$ is a smooth vector bundle of rank $k$; the bijection $j : f^*E \to E'$, $(m', e) \mapsto (m', e)$, is a bundle isomorphism over $M'$; and $\operatorname{pr}_2|_{E'} : E' \to E$ is a smooth bundle map covering $f$ which is a linear isomorphism $E'_{m'} \to E_{f(m')}$ on every fibre. The square $\pi \circ \operatorname{pr}_2 = f \circ \pi'$ commutes.
>
> **Part IV (restriction).** If $M' \subseteq M$ is open and $\iota$ the inclusion, then $\hat\iota : \iota^*E \to \pi^{-1}(M')$ is a bundle isomorphism onto the open subset $\pi^{-1}(M') \subseteq E$ regarded as a vector bundle over $M'$ with projection $\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(M')}$.

The general fibre-bundle version of Part III (Bär's Theorem on pp. 39–40, for an arbitrary typical fibre) is [[Thm - The Pull-Back of a Fibre Bundle is a Fibre Bundle]] in chapter III; here we give the complete proof in the vector-bundle case, which is the one this chapter uses.

> [!note]- Complete proof of Proposition 2
> **Part I.** We must verify hypotheses (i)–(iii) of the [[Thm - Vector Bundle Construction Lemma|construction lemma]] (restated above, after Proposition 1) for the family $(f^*E)_{m'} = E_{f(m')}$ and the bijections $\Phi'_\alpha$.
>
> **Hypothesis (i).** The sets $U'_\alpha = f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ are open in $M'$ because $f$ is continuous, and they cover $M'$ because the $U_\alpha$ cover $M$: for $m' \in M'$ pick $\alpha$ with $f(m') \in U_\alpha$; then $m' \in U'_\alpha$.
>
> **Hypothesis (ii).** Each fibre $E_{f(m')}$ is a real vector space of dimension $k$ (as a fibre of $E$). $\Phi'_\alpha$ maps $\pi'^{-1}(U'_\alpha) = \{(m', e) : m' \in U'_\alpha, e \in E_{f(m')}\}$ to $U'_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^k$; on the fibre over $m'$ it is $e \mapsto \phi_{\alpha, f(m')}(e)$, a linear isomorphism $E_{f(m')} \to \mathbb{R}^k$ because $\phi_{\alpha, f(m')}$ is one ($f(m') \in U_\alpha$, [[Def - Local Trivialization]]). Being a fibrewise bijection preserving the base point, $\Phi'_\alpha$ is a bijection.
>
> **Hypothesis (iii).** For $m' \in U'_\alpha \cap U'_\beta$ and $v \in \mathbb{R}^k$, $(\Phi'_\beta)^{-1}(m', v) = (m', \phi_{\beta, f(m')}^{-1}(v))$ and so
> $$\Phi'_\alpha \circ (\Phi'_\beta)^{-1}(m', v) = \big(m',\ \phi_{\alpha, f(m')} \phi_{\beta, f(m')}^{-1}(v)\big) = \big(m',\ \tau_{\alpha\beta}(f(m'))\, v\big) \qquad \text{(definition of } \tau_{\alpha\beta} \text{ at the point } f(m') \in U_\alpha \cap U_\beta\text{)}.$$
> So the overlap has the required form with $\tau'_{\alpha\beta} = \tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$, which is smooth as the composite of the smooth maps $f|_{U'_\alpha \cap U'_\beta} : U'_\alpha \cap U'_\beta \to U_\alpha \cap U_\beta$ and $\tau_{\alpha\beta}$, and takes values in $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$. The cocycle identities hold by Lemma A (or directly: $\tau_{\alpha\gamma} \circ f = (\tau_{\alpha\beta}\tau_{\beta\gamma}) \circ f = (\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f)(\tau_{\beta\gamma} \circ f)$ and $\tau_{\alpha\alpha} \circ f = \mathrm{id}$, from the cocycle identities of $E$ evaluated at $f(m')$).
>
> **Conclusion of Part I.** By the construction lemma, $f^*E$ has a unique topology and smooth structure making it a smooth rank-$k$ vector bundle over $M'$ with the $\Phi'_\alpha$ as smooth trivializations; its transition functions are the $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$. Independence of the atlas follows exactly as in Step 4 of the proof of Proposition 1 (a union of two atlases of $E$ is an atlas, the structure it induces makes all induced trivializations smooth, and uniqueness identifies it with each of the two).
>
> **Part II.** We must show $\hat f$ is smooth, covers $f$, and is fibrewise the identity. Covering: $\pi(\hat f(m', e)) = \pi(e) = f(m') = f(\pi'(m', e))$ since $e \in E_{f(m')}$. Fibrewise: on $(f^*E)_{m'} = E_{f(m')}$ the map is $e \mapsto e$, the identity, which is a linear isomorphism. Smoothness is local, so fix $\alpha$ and compute in the trivializations $\Phi'_\alpha$ of $f^*E$ over $U'_\alpha$ and $\Phi_\alpha$ of $E$ over $U_\alpha$ (note $\hat f(\pi'^{-1}(U'_\alpha)) \subseteq \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ because $\pi \circ \hat f = f \circ \pi'$): for $(m', v) \in U'_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^k$,
> $$\Phi_\alpha \circ \hat f \circ (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}(m', v) = \Phi_\alpha\big(\phi_{\alpha, f(m')}^{-1}(v)\big) = \big(f(m'),\ v\big) \qquad \text{(definition of } \Phi'_\alpha \text{, of } \hat f \text{, and } \Phi_\alpha(e) = (\pi(e), \phi_{\alpha,\pi(e)}(e))\text{)},$$
> which is the smooth map $f \times \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^k}$. Since $\Phi_\alpha$ and $\Phi'_\alpha$ are diffeomorphisms, $\hat f$ is smooth on $\pi'^{-1}(U'_\alpha)$, hence on all of $f^*E$. So $\hat f$ is a smooth bundle homomorphism covering $f$ in the sense of [[Def - Bundle Homomorphism]].
>
> **Part III.** We proceed in four steps.
>
> **Step III.1 — $E'$ is closed.** The map $F : M' \times E \to M \times M$, $F(m', e) := (f(m'), \pi(e))$, is continuous (each component is), and $E' = F^{-1}(\Delta_M)$ where $\Delta_M := \{(m, m) : m \in M\}$ is the diagonal. $\Delta_M$ is closed in $M \times M$ because $M$ is Hausdorff (if $(a, b) \notin \Delta_M$, disjoint neighbourhoods $A \ni a$, $B \ni b$ give the neighbourhood $A \times B$ of $(a, b)$ missing $\Delta_M$). Hence $E'$ is closed.
>
> **Step III.2 — slice charts: $E'$ is an embedded submanifold of dimension $n' + k$.** Fix $(m'_0, e_0) \in E'$, so $\pi(e_0) = f(m'_0) =: m_0$. Choose a trivialization $\Phi$ of $E$ over an open $U \ni m_0$, a chart $(V, \psi)$ of $M$ with $m_0 \in V \subseteq U$ (restricting $\Phi$ to $\pi^{-1}(V)$ keeps it a trivialization), and a chart $(W, \varphi)$ of $M'$ with $m'_0 \in W \subseteq f^{-1}(V)$ (possible because $f^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighbourhood of $m'_0$). Then
> $$\Theta : W \times \pi^{-1}(V) \to \varphi(W) \times \psi(V) \times \mathbb{R}^k, \qquad \Theta(m', e) := \big(\varphi(m'),\ \psi(\pi(e)),\ \phi_{\pi(e)}(e)\big),$$
> is a chart of the product manifold $M' \times E$ around $(m'_0, e_0)$: it is the product of the chart $\varphi$ with the chart $(\psi \times \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^k}) \circ \Phi|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}$ of $E$ (a diffeomorphism from the open set $\pi^{-1}(V) \subseteq E$ onto the open set $\psi(V) \times \mathbb{R}^k \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^k$, hence a chart of $E$), and a product of charts is a chart of the product. Let $G := \psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1} : \varphi(W) \to \psi(V)$, smooth as a composite. In the chart $\Theta$,
> $$\Theta\big(E' \cap (W \times \pi^{-1}(V))\big) = \{(x, y, v) \in \varphi(W) \times \psi(V) \times \mathbb{R}^k : y = G(x)\},$$
> because $(m', e) \in E'$ if and only if $\pi(e) = f(m')$, if and only if $\psi(\pi(e)) = \psi(f(m'))$ ($\psi$ injective on $V$, and both points lie in $V$), if and only if $y = G(x)$ with $x = \varphi(m')$. Now define, on the open set $D := \varphi(W) \times \psi(V) \times \mathbb{R}^k$,
> $$\Xi(x, y, v) := \big(x,\ y - G(x),\ v\big).$$
> $\Xi$ is smooth, injective, and its image $\Xi(D) = \{(x, z, v) : x \in \varphi(W),\ z + G(x) \in \psi(V)\}$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^{n'} \times \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^k$ (it is the intersection of the open set $\varphi(W) \times \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^k$ with the preimage of the open set $\psi(V)$ under the continuous map $(x, z, v) \mapsto z + G(x)$); its inverse $(x, z, v) \mapsto (x, z + G(x), v)$ is smooth. Hence $\Xi$ is a diffeomorphism of $D$ onto $\Xi(D)$, and $\Xi \circ \Theta$ is a chart of $M' \times E$ around $(m'_0, e_0)$ in which
> $$(\Xi \circ \Theta)\big(E' \cap (W \times \pi^{-1}(V))\big) = \{(x, 0, v)\} \cap \Xi(D) = \Xi(D) \cap \big(\mathbb{R}^{n'} \times \{0\}^{n} \times \mathbb{R}^k\big)$$
> (a point $(x, y, v)$ of $D$ has $y = G(x)$ if and only if its $\Xi$-image has middle block $0$). After permuting the coordinates so that the $\mathbb{R}^{n'}$ and $\mathbb{R}^k$ blocks come first, this is precisely the local $(n' + k)$-slice condition of [[Def - Embedded Submanifold]]. Since $(m'_0, e_0) \in E'$ was arbitrary, $E'$ is an embedded submanifold of $M' \times E$ of dimension $n' + k$, with the subspace topology (Hausdorff and second countable, as a subspace of the manifold $M' \times E$) and with smooth structure given by the slice charts $\chi := (x, v) \circ (\Xi \circ \Theta)|_{E'}$, that is, $\chi(m', e) = (\varphi(m'), \phi_{\pi(e)}(e))$ on $E' \cap (W \times \pi^{-1}(V))$.
>
> **Step III.3 — $(E', \pi', M')$ is a vector bundle and $j$ is an isomorphism.** *Projection.* $\pi' = \operatorname{pr}_1|_{E'}$ is smooth, being the restriction to an embedded submanifold of the smooth map $\operatorname{pr}_1$ (equivalently, $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \operatorname{incl}$ with $\operatorname{incl} : E' \hookrightarrow M' \times E$ smooth); it is surjective because for every $m'$ the fibre $E_{f(m')}$ is nonempty (it contains $0$). *Fibres.* $\pi'^{-1}(m') = \{(m', e) : \pi(e) = f(m')\} = \{m'\} \times E_{f(m')}$, on which we put the vector space structure making $\operatorname{pr}_2 : \{m'\} \times E_{f(m')} \to E_{f(m')}$ a linear isomorphism; this is Bär's identification of the fibre. *Local trivializations.* With $\Phi$, $U$ as in Step III.2 and $U' := f^{-1}(U)$, define $\Psi : \pi'^{-1}(U') \to U' \times \mathbb{R}^k$, $\Psi(m', e) := (m', \phi_{\pi(e)}(e))$. It satisfies $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \Psi = \pi'$ and is linear on each fibre (there it is $\phi_{f(m')} \circ \operatorname{pr}_2$). It is smooth, as the restriction to $E'$ of the smooth map $(m', e) \mapsto (m', \phi_{\pi(e)}(e)) = (\mathrm{id} \times (\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ \Phi))$ on $M' \times \pi^{-1}(U)$. Its inverse is $\Psi^{-1}(m', v) = (m', \Phi^{-1}(f(m'), v))$: this lands in $E'$ because $\pi(\Phi^{-1}(f(m'), v)) = f(m')$, and it inverts $\Psi$ because $\Phi^{-1}(f(m'), \phi_{f(m')}(e)) = e$ for $e \in E_{f(m')}$. It is smooth as a map into $E'$: in the slice chart $\chi$ of Step III.2 around any point of its image, $\chi \circ \Psi^{-1}(m', v) = (\varphi(m'), v)$, which is smooth; and smoothness into an embedded submanifold is checked in slice charts. So $\Psi$ is a diffeomorphism and a smooth local trivialization; the sets $U'$ cover $M'$ (Part I, hypothesis (i)). Hence $(E', \pi', M')$ is a smooth vector bundle of rank $k$ ([[Def - Vector Bundle]]). *The isomorphism $j$.* $j : f^*E \to E'$, $(m', e) \mapsto (m', e)$, is a bijection covering $\mathrm{id}_{M'}$, fibrewise the identity $E_{f(m')} \to \{m'\} \times E_{f(m')} \cong E_{f(m')}$, and in the trivializations $\Phi'_\alpha$ of $f^*E$ and $\Psi_\alpha$ of $E'$ (built from the same $\Phi_\alpha$) we have $\Psi_\alpha \circ j \circ (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}(m', v) = \Psi_\alpha(m', \phi_{\alpha, f(m')}^{-1}(v)) = (m', v)$: the identity of $U'_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^k$. So $j$ and $j^{-1}$ are smooth on every $\pi'^{-1}(U'_\alpha)$, hence everywhere, and $j$ is a bundle isomorphism over $M'$. In particular, by the uniqueness clause of the construction lemma, transporting the structure of $E'$ along $j$ gives back the structure of Part I: Haydys's fibrewise definition and Bär's fibre product are the same bundle.
>
> **Step III.4 — $\operatorname{pr}_2|_{E'}$.** It is smooth (restriction of the smooth $\operatorname{pr}_2$ to an embedded submanifold), satisfies $\pi \circ \operatorname{pr}_2(m', e) = \pi(e) = f(m') = f \circ \pi'(m', e)$ on $E'$ — the commuting square — and on the fibre $\{m'\} \times E_{f(m')}$ it is the linear isomorphism onto $E_{f(m')}$ by the choice of vector space structure in Step III.3. Note also $\operatorname{pr}_2|_{E'} \circ j = \hat f$, so Part II is recovered.
>
> **Part IV.** Let $M' \subseteq M$ be open and $\iota$ the inclusion. $\pi^{-1}(M')$ is open in $E$ ($\pi$ continuous), hence a smooth manifold with the restricted smooth structure; the restriction $\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(M')} : \pi^{-1}(M') \to M'$ is smooth and surjective, its fibres are the $E_{m'}$, and the restrictions $\Phi_\alpha|_{\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha \cap M')} : \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha \cap M') \to (U_\alpha \cap M') \times \mathbb{R}^k$ are smooth local trivializations (restrictions of diffeomorphisms to open sets, still projection-compatible and fibrewise linear), so $\pi^{-1}(M') \to M'$ is a smooth vector bundle of rank $k$. By Part II, $\hat\iota : \iota^*E \to E$, $(m', e) \mapsto e$, is smooth, and it maps $\iota^*E$ bijectively onto $\pi^{-1}(M')$ (its fibre over $m'$ is $E_{\iota(m')} = E_{m'}$, and every $e \in \pi^{-1}(M')$ arises from $(\pi(e), e)$). In the trivializations $\Phi'_\alpha$ of $\iota^*E$ over $U'_\alpha = U_\alpha \cap M'$ and $\Phi_\alpha|$ of $\pi^{-1}(M')$, the computation of Part II gives $\Phi_\alpha \circ \hat\iota \circ (\Phi'_\alpha)^{-1}(m', v) = (\iota(m'), v) = (m', v)$, the identity; so $\hat\iota$ is a diffeomorphism onto $\pi^{-1}(M')$, covers $\mathrm{id}_{M'}$, and is fibrewise the identity — a bundle isomorphism.
>
> Therefore the pull-back $f^*E$ is a smooth vector bundle over $M'$, canonically isomorphic to Bär's fibre product $\{(m', e) : f(m') = \pi(e)\}$ with $\operatorname{pr}_2$ identifying fibres, and the restriction to an open subset is the open subset $\pi^{-1}(M')$ of the total space. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (sections of a pull-back).** A smooth section of $f^*E$ is, by Part III, a smooth map $s : M' \to M' \times E$ of the form $s(m') = (m', V(m'))$ with $V(m') \in E_{f(m')}$; and $s$ is smooth if and only if $V = \operatorname{pr}_2 \circ s : M' \to E$ is smooth, because in the trivializations $\Phi'_\alpha$ (or $\Psi_\alpha$) the section reads $m' \mapsto (m', \phi_{\alpha, f(m')}(V(m')))$, whose second component is $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ \Phi_\alpha \circ V$, and $\Phi_\alpha$ is a diffeomorphism. So $\Gamma(f^*E)$ is the space of *smooth sections of $E$ along $f$*: smooth maps $V : M' \to E$ with $\pi \circ V = f$. This is the form in which pull-backs are used in chapter II onwards (vector fields along curves, connections pulled back to a curve for parallel transport in [[Def - Parallel Transport in a Principal Bundle]]), and it is the verified content of Bär's Example 2.1.12 below.

# Categorical Definition

The six algebraic operations are the values on objects of *functors* $\mathbf{Vect}_M \to \mathbf{Vect}_M$ (or $\mathbf{Vect}_M \times \mathbf{Vect}_M \to \mathbf{Vect}_M$) on the category of smooth vector bundles over $M$ with bundle homomorphisms over $M$ as morphisms. On a morphism $\varphi : E \to E'$ over $M$ (fibrewise linear, [[Def - Bundle Homomorphism]]) they act fibrewise by the linear-algebra functor: $\Lambda^p \varphi$ on $\Lambda^p E \to \Lambda^p E'$, $\varphi \oplus \psi$, $\varphi \otimes \psi$, and — contravariantly in the first slot — $\operatorname{Hom}(\varphi, \psi) : \operatorname{Hom}(E', F) \to \operatorname{Hom}(E, F')$, $\theta \mapsto \psi \circ \theta \circ \varphi$, and $\varphi^* : E'^* \to E^*$, $\xi \mapsto \xi \circ \varphi$. That these fibrewise maps are smooth follows from the same computation as in Lemma B: in the induced trivializations the map $\Lambda^p \varphi$ reads $(m, w) \mapsto (m, \Lambda^p(\varphi_\alpha(m)) w)$ where $\varphi_\alpha(m) := \phi'_{\alpha,m} \circ \varphi|_{E_m} \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}$ is the smooth matrix-valued local representative of $\varphi$, and $\Lambda^p$ is smooth on all of $\operatorname{Mat}(k \times k, \mathbb{R})$ (polynomial entries, Step 0), not only on $GL$; the same holds for the other operations (for $\operatorname{Hom}$ and the dual, the induced map is $X \mapsto \psi_\alpha X \varphi_\alpha$, respectively $w \mapsto \varphi_\alpha^t w$, with no inverse required because the variance has been reversed). The general principle is that any functor on finite-dimensional vector spaces whose action on $\operatorname{Hom}$-spaces is smooth defines a functor on vector bundles; the proof is Proposition 1 with the functor in place of the six named operations.

The pull-back is the *categorical pullback* (fibre product) of the diagram $M' \xrightarrow{f} M \xleftarrow{\pi} E$ in the category of smooth manifolds, and it has the following universal property in the category of vector bundles with bundle homomorphisms covering arbitrary maps.

> **Universal property of $f^*E$.** Let $\pi'' : E'' \to N$ be a vector bundle, $g : N \to M'$ a smooth map, and $G : E'' \to E$ a bundle homomorphism covering $f \circ g$ (so $\pi \circ G = f \circ g \circ \pi''$, and $G$ is linear on fibres). Then there is a unique bundle homomorphism $\tilde G : E'' \to f^*E$ covering $g$ with $\hat f \circ \tilde G = G$.

*Proof.* **Uniqueness.** If $\tilde G$ covers $g$ and $\hat f \circ \tilde G = G$, then in the pair notation $\tilde G(x) = (m', e)$ forces $m' = \pi'(\tilde G(x)) = g(\pi''(x))$ (covering) and $e = \hat f(\tilde G(x)) = G(x)$; so $\tilde G(x) = (g(\pi''(x)), G(x))$ is determined. **Existence.** Define $\tilde G(x) := (g(\pi''(x)), G(x))$. This lies in $f^*E$: $G(x) \in E_{\pi(G(x))} = E_{f(g(\pi''(x)))}$ by the covering hypothesis on $G$, which is the fibre of $f^*E$ over $g(\pi''(x))$. It covers $g$ and satisfies $\hat f \circ \tilde G = G$ by construction, and it is linear on each fibre $E''_x \to E_{f(g(x))}$ because $G$ is. Smoothness: fix $\alpha$, let $N_\alpha := (g \circ \pi'')^{-1}(U'_\alpha) \cap$ (a trivializing set of $E''$ with trivialization $\Phi''$), an open set; on it,
$$\Phi'_\alpha \circ \tilde G \circ (\Phi'')^{-1}(y, w) = \Big(g(y),\ \phi_{\alpha, f(g(y))}\big(G((\Phi'')^{-1}(y, w))\big)\Big) = \Big(g(y),\ \operatorname{pr}_2 \circ \Phi_\alpha \circ G \circ (\Phi'')^{-1}(y, w)\Big)$$
(definition of $\Phi'_\alpha$; and $\phi_{\alpha, \pi(e)}(e) = \operatorname{pr}_2 \Phi_\alpha(e)$), a composite of smooth maps. Hence $\tilde G$ is smooth. $\blacksquare$

In words: a bundle map into $E$ that covers a map factoring through $f$ factors uniquely through $f^*E$. The fibre-product description of Part III makes this literally the universal property of a pullback square in $\mathbf{Man}$: a pair of maps $(g, G)$ into $M'$ and $E$ agreeing in $M$ is the same as a map into $E' \subseteq M' \times E$. The categorical pullback in general is defined on [[Def - Pullback and Pushout]]; here we have verified the universal property directly rather than invoking that page.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**Transition functions as the true carrier of the operations.** Every one of the constructions on this page is, once the fibres are fixed, a statement about the transition functions: pass from the cocycle $\{\tau_{\alpha\beta}\}$ with values in $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$ to the cocycle $\{\rho(\tau_{\alpha\beta})\}$, where $\rho : GL(k, \mathbb{R}) \to GL(k', \mathbb{R})$ is one of the smooth homomorphisms of Step 0′ ($\rho(A) = A^{-t}$, $\Lambda^p A$, $A \otimes B$, and so on — each of these is a group homomorphism, by the functoriality identities of Step 0). This is the vector-bundle shadow of the associated-bundle construction of chapter III: a representation $\rho : G \to GL(V)$ of the structure group turns a principal $G$-bundle into a vector bundle with fibre $V$, and $E^*$, $\Lambda^p E$, $\operatorname{End} E$ are the bundles associated to the frame bundle of $E$ through the dual, exterior-power and adjoint representations of $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$ ([[Def - Associated Bundle]], [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle]]). Seen this way, Lemma B is the computation of a representation on a cocycle, and the pull-back is the cocycle $\tau_{\alpha\beta} \circ f$ — the pull-back of a principal bundle is the same operation ([[Thm - Principal Bundles are Classified by Cocycles]]).

**Modules over the ring of functions.** Taking sections converts each operation into the corresponding operation on $C^\infty(M)$-modules, at least locally: $\Gamma(U, E \oplus F) = \Gamma(U, E) \oplus \Gamma(U, F)$, and over a trivializing $U$ with frames, $\Gamma(U, E \otimes F)$ is the tensor product of the free modules $\Gamma(U, E)$ and $\Gamma(U, F)$ over $C^\infty(U)$, $\Gamma(U, \operatorname{Hom}(E, F))$ is the module of $C^\infty(U)$-linear maps $\Gamma(U, E) \to \Gamma(U, F)$, and $\Gamma(U, E^*)$ is the dual module ([[Thm - Local Frames Span Sections]]: sections over a trivializing set form a free module of rank $k$). Globally, the statement "$C^\infty(M)$-linear maps on sections are sections of $\operatorname{Hom}$" is the [[Thm - Tensoriality Lemma for C-Infinity-Linear Maps|tensoriality lemma]], the module-theoretic true name of $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$.

**Pull-back as base change.** In algebraic geometry the pull-back of a vector bundle along $f$ is the base change of a locally free sheaf, $f^* \mathcal{E} = f^{-1}\mathcal{E} \otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_M} \mathcal{O}_{M'}$; the fibre-product description of Part III is the geometric form of the same operation. The two extreme cases computed above — the constant map (which forgets all but one fibre) and the identity — are the two ends of the spectrum of what base change can do, and the intermediate cases (restriction to an open set, restriction to a curve) are the ones used in this series.

**True name.** The definition is already operational: $E^{\bullet}$ *is* the family of vector spaces obtained fibrewise, with the smooth structure determined by transporting the trivializations of $E$; $f^*E$ *is* the family $\{E_{f(m')}\}$, equivalently the closed submanifold $\{(m', e) : f(m') = \pi(e)\}$ of $M' \times E$. The one thing the official definition hides — and this page makes explicit in Step 0′ — is that the operations are well defined only because dualising, taking exterior powers, tensoring and conjugating are *smooth* maps on $GL(k, \mathbb{R})$.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Example 1 — the product bundle and the tangent bundle (Haydys Example 1).** The product bundle $M \times \mathbb{R}^k \to M$ with $\Phi = \mathrm{id}$ has a one-set trivializing atlas with transition function $\mathrm{id}$; hence every operation applied to it produces a trivial bundle: $(M \times \mathbb{R}^k)^* \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^k$ (transition $\mathrm{id}^{-t} = \mathrm{id}$), $\Lambda^p(M \times \mathbb{R}^k) \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^{\binom{k}{p}}$, $\operatorname{End}(M \times \mathbb{R}^k) \cong M \times \operatorname{Mat}(k \times k, \mathbb{R})$, each by Lemma C (identity transition functions). The tangent bundle $TM$ (a vector bundle of rank $n$, with trivializations induced by charts, $\Phi_\alpha(v) = (m, (dx^1_\alpha(v), \dots, dx^n_\alpha(v)))$ and transition functions the Jacobian matrices $\tau_{\alpha\beta}(m) = \big(\partial x^i_\alpha / \partial x^j_\beta\big)(m)$, [[Def - Vector Bundle]]) is the standing example from which the next three are built.

**Example 2 — $T^*M = (TM)^*$ (verified).** The vault's [[Def - Cotangent Space and Cotangent Bundle|cotangent bundle]] has fibre $T^*_mM = (T_mM)^*$ by definition and local trivializations $\xi \mapsto (m, (\xi(\partial/\partial x^1_\alpha), \dots, \xi(\partial/\partial x^n_\alpha)))$, the components of $\xi$ in the coordinate coframe $(dx^i_\alpha)$. We check that this is exactly the induced trivialization $\Phi^*_\alpha$ of Definition 1 applied to $E = TM$: $\Phi^*_\alpha(\xi) = (m, \xi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1})$, and the $i$-th component of the functional $\xi \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is its value on $e_i$, namely $\xi(\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}(e_i)) = \xi(\partial/\partial x^i_\alpha)$ (since $\phi_{\alpha,m}(\partial/\partial x^i_\alpha) = e_i$ by the definition of the chart-induced trivialization). The two trivializations agree, so by the uniqueness clause of the construction lemma the two smooth structures agree: $T^*M = (TM)^*$ as smooth vector bundles. The transition functions are, by the table, $\tau^*_{\alpha\beta} = \tau_{\beta\alpha}^t = \big(\partial x^j_\beta / \partial x^i_\alpha\big)_{ij}$, which is the classical transformation rule for the components of a covector, $\xi_{\alpha, i} = \sum_j (\partial x^j_\beta / \partial x^i_\alpha)\, \xi_{\beta, j}$ — as it must be, since $dx^j_\beta = \sum_i (\partial x^j_\beta/\partial x^i_\alpha)\, dx^i_\alpha$ by the chain rule ([[Thm - Chain Rule for the Differential]]).

**Example 3 — $\Lambda^p T^*M$ and differential forms (verified; Bär Examples 2.1.8 and the table on p. 39).** Applying $\Lambda^p$ to $E = T^*M$ gives the bundle with fibre $\Lambda^p(T^*_mM)$, the alternating $p$-linear maps on $(T^*_mM)^* = T_mM$ (Step 0 item 2, with the canonical double-dual identification), which is the fibre of the bundle of [[Def - Differential k-Form on a Manifold|differential $p$-forms]]; the induced frame over a chart is $(dx^I_\alpha)_{I \text{ increasing}}$, $dx^I = dx^{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{i_p}$, and the rank is $\binom{n}{p}$. The induced transition functions are the matrices of $p \times p$ minors of the covector transition matrices, which is the classical rule $dx^J_\beta = \sum_I \det\big(\partial x^{j}_\beta / \partial x^{i}_\alpha\big)_{j \in J, i \in I}\, dx^I_\alpha$ obtained by expanding the wedge product multilinearly — the same computation as in Step 0 item 2. Sections of these bundles are, in order (Bär's table): sections of $TM$ are vector fields; sections of $T^*M$ are $1$-forms; sections of $\Lambda^p T^*M$ are $p$-forms; sections of $TM^{\otimes k} \otimes (T^*M)^{\otimes l}$ — built by iterating the tensor product of Definition 1 — are $(k, l)$-tensor fields ([[Def - Tensor Bundle]], [[Def - Tensor Field on a Manifold]]). The same fibrewise linear algebra produces Bär's symmetric powers $\odot^k T^*B$ (the symmetric $k$-tensors, with transition functions the induced maps on symmetric tensors, polynomial in the Jacobian entries); we do not use them in this series and record them only to cover the source's list.

**Example 4 — $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \cong E^* \otimes F$ (Haydys Exercise 2; verified here fibrewise and on transition functions).** Fibrewise, define $\kappa_m : (E_m)^* \otimes F_m \to \operatorname{Hom}(E_m, F_m)$ on elementary tensors by $\kappa_m(\xi \otimes w) := (u \mapsto \xi(u) w)$. This is well defined and linear by the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product|universal property of the tensor product]] — *a bilinear map $V \times W \to U$ factors uniquely through a linear map $V \otimes W \to U$* — applied to the bilinear map $(\xi, w) \mapsto (u \mapsto \xi(u)w)$ (bilinear because $\xi(u)w$ is linear in $\xi$ and in $w$). It is an isomorphism: with a basis $(u_i)$ of $E_m$, dual basis $(u^i)$, and a basis $(w_j)$ of $F_m$, $\kappa_m(u^i \otimes w_j) = \epsilon_{ji}$ (the map sending $u_a$ to $\delta_{ia} w_j$), and the $\epsilon_{ji}$ form a basis of $\operatorname{Hom}(E_m, F_m)$ (Step 0 item 5) while the $u^i \otimes w_j$ form a basis of $(E_m)^* \otimes F_m$ ([[Def - Tensor Product of Vector Spaces]]); a linear map carrying a basis to a basis is an isomorphism. Now the bundle statement. In the induced trivializations $\Phi^{\otimes}_\alpha$ of $E^* \otimes F$ and $\Phi^{\operatorname{Hom}}_\alpha$ of $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$, the map $\kappa := \bigsqcup_m \kappa_m$ reads $\Phi^{\operatorname{Hom}}_\alpha \circ \kappa \circ (\Phi^{\otimes}_\alpha)^{-1}(m, \varepsilon^i \otimes e_j) = (m, E_{ji})$: indeed $(\Phi^{\otimes}_\alpha)^{-1}(m, \varepsilon^i \otimes e_j) = (\varepsilon^i \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}) \otimes (\phi^F_{\alpha,m})^{-1}(e_j)$, whose $\kappa_m$-image is $u \mapsto \varepsilon^i(\phi_{\alpha,m} u)\, (\phi^F_{\alpha,m})^{-1}(e_j)$, and conjugating by the trivializations gives $v \mapsto \varepsilon^i(v)\, e_j$, the matrix $E_{ji}$. So in local trivializations $\kappa$ is the *constant* linear isomorphism $L : (\mathbb{R}^k)^* \otimes \mathbb{R}^l \to \operatorname{Mat}(l \times k, \mathbb{R})$, $\varepsilon^i \otimes e_j \mapsto E_{ji}$, independent of $m$; hence $\kappa$ is smooth with smooth inverse, covers $\mathrm{id}_M$, and is fibrewise an isomorphism — a bundle isomorphism. As a check that the transition functions of the two bundles correspond under $L$ (they must, since $L$ is the local form of a global map), compute both sides on $\varepsilon^i \otimes e_j$: on the tensor side, $(\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-t} \otimes \tau^F_{\alpha\beta})(\varepsilon^i \otimes e_j) = \sum_{a, b} (\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})_{ia}(\tau^F_{\alpha\beta})_{bj}\; \varepsilon^a \otimes e_b$ (since $\tau^{-t}\varepsilon^i = \varepsilon^i \circ \tau^{-1} = \sum_a (\tau^{-1})_{ia}\varepsilon^a$ and $\tau^F e_j = \sum_b (\tau^F)_{bj} e_b$), whose $L$-image is $\sum_{a,b} (\tau^F_{\alpha\beta})_{bj}(\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})_{ia} E_{ba}$; on the Hom side, $\tau^F_{\alpha\beta}\, E_{ji}\, \tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}$ has $(b, a)$-entry $(\tau^F_{\alpha\beta})_{bj}(\tau_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})_{ia}$ — the same matrix. The exercise page [[Ex - Hom(E,F) is Isomorphic to E-Dual Tensor F]] carries this as a worked problem. Consequently $\operatorname{End} E \cong E^* \otimes E$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$ need not be introduced as a separate operation at all — it is included in Definition 1 because the series writes $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{End} E)$ and $\Omega^p(M; \operatorname{Hom}(E, F))$ constantly and the direct description of its trivializations (conjugation) is the one used in every local computation.

**Example 5 — vector fields along a curve are sections of $\gamma^* TM$ (Bär Example 2.1.12; verified).** Let $\gamma : (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to M$ be a smooth curve and $E = TM$. By the remark after Proposition 2, a smooth section of $\gamma^* TM$ is the same as a smooth map $V : (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to TM$ with $\pi \circ V = \gamma$, that is, $V(t) \in T_{\gamma(t)} M$ for every $t$ — a *smooth vector field along $\gamma$*. We verify the smoothness equivalence once more in this case, concretely: in a chart $(U, x)$ of $M$, $V(t) = \sum_i V^i(t)\, \partial/\partial x^i|_{\gamma(t)}$ for $t \in \gamma^{-1}(U)$, and the trivialization $\Phi'_U$ of $\gamma^*TM$ sends the section $t \mapsto (t, V(t))$ to $t \mapsto (t, (V^1(t), \dots, V^n(t)))$; so the section is smooth if and only if the component functions $V^i$ are smooth on $\gamma^{-1}(U)$ for every chart, if and only if $V : (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to TM$ is smooth (smoothness of a map into $TM$ is checked in the chart-induced trivializations). The velocity field $\dot\gamma(t) := d\gamma_t(d/dt) \in T_{\gamma(t)} M$ ([[Def - Velocity of a Curve]]) is such a section: in the chart, $\dot\gamma(t) = \sum_i (x^i \circ \gamma)'(t)\, \partial/\partial x^i|_{\gamma(t)}$ by the definition of the differential, and the components $(x^i \circ \gamma)'$ are smooth because $x^i \circ \gamma$ is. Note that $\dot\gamma$ is *not* in general a section of $TM$ over the image of $\gamma$ — if $\gamma$ crosses itself, $\gamma(t_1) = \gamma(t_2)$ with $\dot\gamma(t_1) \neq \dot\gamma(t_2)$, no vector field on $M$ has both values at that point — which is exactly why $\gamma^*TM$, whose fibres over $t_1$ and $t_2$ are two separate copies of $T_{\gamma(t_1)}M$, is the right home. Other vector fields along curves arise as variational fields of one-parameter families of curves and, for geodesic variations, as [[Def - Jacobi Field|Jacobi fields]]; see [[Ex - Vector Fields along a Curve are Sections of the Pull-Back of the Tangent Bundle]].

**Example 6 — the zero section, and bundles without sections (Bär Remark 2.1.10).** Every vector bundle $E \to M$ has a smooth global section, the zero section $s_0(m) := 0 \in E_m$: in any local trivialization it reads $m \mapsto (m, 0)$, which is smooth, and smoothness is local ([[Def - Section of a Vector Bundle]]). Likewise every $\operatorname{End} E$ has the *identity section* $\mathrm{id}_E(m) := \mathrm{id}_{E_m}$, which in the induced trivialization $\Phi^{\operatorname{End}}_\alpha$ reads $m \mapsto (m, \phi_{\alpha,m} \circ \mathrm{id} \circ \phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}) = (m, I_k)$, constant, hence smooth; and every $E \oplus F$ has the sections $(s, 0)$ and $(0, t)$. A general fibre bundle need not have any continuous section: the standard example is the Hopf bundle $S^3 \to S^2$ ([[Def - The Hopf Bundle]]), a principal $U(1)$-bundle, and a principal bundle with a section is trivial ([[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]]) while the Hopf bundle is not — both facts are proved in chapter III and are recorded here only as the forward reference Bär's remark points to. The contrast is the reason vector bundles are the more tractable class: the linear structure of the fibres supplies a distinguished point in each, and with it the partitions-of-unity arguments ([[Thm - Existence of Smooth Partitions of Unity]]) that build global sections, metrics and connections.

**Non-example — a family of vector spaces with non-smooth identifications is not a bundle.** Let $M = \mathbb{R}$, $E = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ the trivial line bundle, and consider the family $(E \otimes E)_m = E_m \otimes E_m \cong \mathbb{R}$ (each fibre is one-dimensional, spanned by $1 \otimes 1$). Cover $M$ by $U_1 = (-\infty, 1)$ and $U_2 = (-1, \infty)$ and define fibrewise-linear bijections $\Psi_1 : \pi^{-1}(U_1) \to U_1 \times \mathbb{R}$, $\Psi_1(m, t\, (1 \otimes 1)) := (m, t)$, and $\Psi_2 : \pi^{-1}(U_2) \to U_2 \times \mathbb{R}$, $\Psi_2(m, t\,(1 \otimes 1)) := (m, h(m) t)$ with $h(m) := 1 + |m|$, which is continuous and nowhere zero but not smooth at $m = 0$. Each $\Psi_i$ is a bijection restricting to a linear isomorphism on every fibre, so hypotheses (i) and (ii) of the construction lemma hold, and $\Psi_2 \circ \Psi_1^{-1}(m, t) = (m, h(m) t)$ has the form required in (iii) with the *continuous* transition function $h : (-1, 1) \to GL(1, \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}^\times$; the cocycle identities hold by Lemma A. Only the smoothness of $h$ fails. We claim that no topology and smooth structure on $\bigsqcup_m E_m \otimes E_m$ makes both $\Psi_1$ and $\Psi_2$ smooth local trivializations. Suppose one did. Then $\Psi_2 \circ \Psi_1^{-1} : (-1, 1) \times \mathbb{R} \to (-1, 1) \times \mathbb{R}$ would be smooth (a composite of diffeomorphisms), so its second component evaluated at $t = 1$, namely $m \mapsto h(m) = 1 + |m|$, would be a smooth function on $(-1, 1)$; but $|m|$ is not differentiable at $0$ (its difference quotients from the left and right are $-1$ and $+1$). This is the named contradiction. So this family, with these identifications, is not a vector bundle: the fibres are right, the bijections are fibrewise linear, and still there is no bundle. The canonical structure of Definition 1, by contrast, uses $\Phi^{\otimes} = \Phi \otimes \Phi$ with transition function $\tau \otimes \tau = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$, smooth, and yields the trivial line bundle — the same underlying family, now with the identifications that Step 0′ guarantees are smooth.

**Corollary — the natural pairings are smooth bundle maps.** The evaluation $E^* \oplus E \to M \times \mathbb{R}$, $(\xi, e) \mapsto \xi(e)$, the evaluation $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \oplus E \to F$, $(\varphi, e) \mapsto \varphi(e)$, and the composition $\operatorname{Hom}(F, G) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \to \operatorname{Hom}(E, G)$ are smooth maps of total spaces (fibrewise bilinear). Proof for the second, the others being the same computation: in induced trivializations over $U_\alpha$, the map reads $(m, (X, v)) \mapsto (m, X v)$, because $\Phi^F_\alpha(\varphi(e)) = (m, \phi^F_{\alpha,m}\varphi(e)) = (m, (\phi^F_{\alpha,m}\varphi\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1})(\phi_{\alpha,m} e)) = (m, X v)$ with $X := \phi^F_{\alpha,m}\varphi\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}$ and $v := \phi_{\alpha,m} e$ (inserting $\phi_{\alpha,m}^{-1}\phi_{\alpha,m} = \mathrm{id}$); and $(X, v) \mapsto Xv$ is polynomial, hence smooth. Consequently, for sections $\varphi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{Hom}(E, F))$ and $s \in \Gamma(E)$ the pointwise image $\varphi(s) : m \mapsto \varphi(m)(s(m))$ is a smooth section of $F$ (composite of the smooth map $m \mapsto (\varphi(m), s(m))$ into $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \oplus E$ with the smooth evaluation). This is what makes $\Gamma(\operatorname{End} E)$ act on $\Gamma(E)$, and hence what makes the gauge group [[Def - Gauge Group of a Vector Bundle]] act on sections and on connections.

**Calibration check.** First, the rank of $\Lambda^p E$ is $\binom{k}{p}$, so $\Lambda^k E$ is a line bundle (the *determinant line* of $E$, with transition functions $\det \tau_{\alpha\beta}$ — the $k \times k$ minor is the determinant) and $\Lambda^p E = 0$ for $p > k$; for $E = T^*M$ this recovers that top-degree forms are sections of a line bundle and there are no forms of degree above $n$. Second, $f^*E$ is trivial whenever $f$ is constant, by the smallest-case computation after Definition 2 — and more generally whenever $f(M')$ lies in a single trivializing set $U_\alpha$, since then $U'_\alpha = M'$; the reader should check that this shows every bundle pulled back to a contractible-looking piece of the base, such as a coordinate ball, is trivial, without any homotopy argument. Third, $\operatorname{End} E$ always has the identity section, computed in Example 6, so $\Gamma(\operatorname{End} E)$ is a unital algebra under pointwise composition (the corollary above gives smoothness of the product); the reader should verify from the table that $\operatorname{End} L$ is trivial for every line bundle $L$ but $\operatorname{End} E$ is in general not trivial for $k \geq 2$, because $X \mapsto \tau X \tau^{-1}$ is the identity for all $X$ only when $\tau$ is scalar.
