# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $n$ (Hausdorff, second countable, $C^\infty$), and $E \to M$ is a smooth real [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundle]] of rank $k$; the complex case, with $\mathbb{R}$ replaced by $\mathbb{C}$ and $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ by $GL_k(\mathbb{C})$, is identical and is noted where it differs. We write $\mathbb{K}$ for either $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ when a statement holds for both. The fibre of $E$ over $m \in M$ is the $k$-dimensional $\mathbb{K}$-vector space $E_m = \pi_E^{-1}(m)$, where $\pi_E : E \to M$ is the bundle projection.

A **frame** of $E_m$ is an ordered basis $(p_1, \dots, p_k)$ of $E_m$; we identify it throughout with the linear isomorphism
$$p : \mathbb{R}^k \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } E_m, \qquad p(\hat{e}_j) = p_j \quad (j = 1, \dots, k),$$
where $\hat{e}_1, \dots, \hat{e}_k$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^k$. The two descriptions carry the same information: an isomorphism $p$ recovers the basis $(p(\hat{e}_1), \dots, p(\hat{e}_k))$, and a basis recovers the isomorphism $x = (x_1, \dots, x_k) \mapsto \sum_{j=1}^k x_j p_j$. We use whichever is clearer and pass between them without comment.

The group $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ is the group of invertible real $k \times k$ matrices, regarded as the open subset $\{h \in \mathbb{R}^{k^2} : \det h \neq 0\}$ of $\mathbb{R}^{k^2}$; it is a Lie group of dimension $k^2$, with smooth multiplication and inversion. It acts on the frames of $E_m$ **on the right** by precomposition,
$$p \cdot h := p \circ h \qquad (p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m),\ h \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})),$$
which on ordered bases reads $(p_1, \dots, p_k) \cdot h = \big(\sum_i h_{i1} p_i, \dots, \sum_i h_{ik} p_i\big)$, matching the matrix action used in both sources. This is a right action: $p \cdot (h_1 h_2) = p \circ (h_1 h_2) = (p \circ h_1) \circ h_2 = (p \cdot h_1) \cdot h_2$, and $p \cdot \mathrm{id} = p$.

A **local frame** of $E$ over an open set $U \subseteq M$ is a $k$-tuple $e = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ of smooth sections $e_i \in \Gamma(U; E)$ that is a basis of $E_m$ at every $m \in U$ (see [[Def - Local Frame]]); equivalently it is a smooth map $e : U \to \operatorname{Fr}(E)$, $m \mapsto e(m)$, where $e(m) : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$ is the isomorphism $\hat{e}_j \mapsto e_j(m)$. Such a local frame exists over $U$ if and only if $E|_U$ is trivial, i.e. if and only if there is a local trivialisation $\psi : E|_U \to U \times \mathbb{R}^k$ of $E$; the two are related by $e_j(m) = \psi^{-1}(m, \hat{e}_j)$.

If $e$ over $U$ and $e'$ over $U'$ are two local frames, then over $U \cap U'$ there is a unique smooth **change-of-frame map** $g : U \cap U' \to GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ with
$$e = e' \cdot g, \qquad \text{that is} \qquad e(m) = e'(m) \circ g(m) \quad (m \in U \cap U'),$$
which is Haydys's equation (6); it is smooth because it is the transition function of $E$ read off the two trivialisations. On ordered bases this says $e_j(m) = \sum_i g_{ij}(m)\, e'_i(m)$.

> [!warning] Convention: rank index and the source's typo
> Haydys writes the change-of-frame group as $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ at equation (6); this is a typo for $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, the rank of $E$ being $k$ and $n$ being reserved for $\dim M$. We use $k$ for the fibre rank throughout, and $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ for the structure group of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

> [!warning] Convention: "the frame bundle" of a manifold versus of a bundle
> Haydys builds $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ for an arbitrary vector bundle $E$; Bär reserves the phrase *the frame bundle* for the special case $E = TM$ of the tangent bundle (Definition 2.2.7), constructing the general case as Example 2.2.5. We follow Haydys, defining $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ for any $E$, and write $\operatorname{Fr}(M) := \operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ for the tangent case that Bär names; the constructions and the verification below are identical.

> [!warning] Convention: right action, not left
> A [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal bundle]] in this series carries a **right** action of its structure group (series conventions). The natural composition action $p \cdot h = p \circ h$ is a right action; the temptation to write a *left* action $h \cdot p = p \circ h$ is analysed and rejected in the non-example below, and is the reason the inverse appears when frames are turned into equivariant functions on associated bundles.

This is a compound page: it defines four interlocking objects — the frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ of a vector bundle, its orthonormal reduction $O(E)$ of a Euclidean bundle, its unitary reduction $U(E)$ of a Hermitian bundle, and the table of structure groups obtained by taking bases that respect extra fibre structure — because all four are the same construction (collect the frames, act on the right, chart by local frames) applied to a bundle with successively more structure, and separating them would hide that they are one idea.

# Axiom Motivation

The computations of the previous chapter — the connection matrix $A = A(\nabla, e)$, its transformation $A' = g^{-1} A g + g^{-1} dg$ under a change of local frame $e' = e g$, the curvature $F = dA + A \wedge A$ and its conjugation $F' = g^{-1} F g$ — all share a defect: every object is defined only after a local frame has been *chosen*, and every statement about it must be accompanied by a bookkeeping rule for what happens when the choice changes. The choices proliferate, the transformation laws accumulate, and the geometry is buried under the coordinate changes. The frame bundle is the device that removes the choice. Its guiding idea is to consider **all frames at once**: instead of picking one frame over each patch and tracking the transitions, we assemble the set of every frame at every point into a single smooth manifold on which the change-of-frame group acts, and read the whole family of local pictures off that one space. The question this section must settle is whether that assembly is possible — whether $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a genuine smooth manifold and a genuine principal bundle — and the answer forces the definition.

What must the object capture? First, at each point $m$ it must contain exactly the frames of $E_m$ and nothing else, so that a point of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ over $m$ *is* a frame of $E_m$; this is what makes the frame bundle the universal home for local frames. Second, the change-of-frame group $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ must act on it, moving one frame to another over the same point, and this action must be free and transitive on each fibre so that the fibre is a copy of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ carrying no basepoint — a torsor, not a group. Third, the whole thing must be locally a product $U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, so that a local frame of $E$ over $U$ becomes a trivialisation of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ over $U$; without this the "manifold of all frames" would be a set with no differential structure and the construction would be useless.

The definition of a [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal $G$-bundle]] is exactly the list of clauses guaranteeing these desiderata, so it is worth seeing, clause by clause, what each one does here and what would break if it were dropped. The bundle is a triple $(P, M, \pi)$ with $\pi : P \to M$ a surjective submersion and a right $G$-action such that (i) $\pi$ is a surjective submersion; (ii) $G$ acts on the right with $\pi(p \cdot g) = \pi(p)$; (iii) $G$ acts freely and transitively on each fibre; (iv) around each point there is a $G$-equivariant local trivialisation $\psi_U : \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ over $U$.

**Drop the submersion in clause (i)** and the base is no longer a smooth quotient: one could not differentiate along $M$, and the local model $U \times G$ would fail. For $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ this clause holds because in the chart $\Psi_U$ the projection is literally the coordinate projection $(m, h) \mapsto m$, a submersion; if the fibres were not all of the same dimension $k^2$ — as would happen for a "vector bundle" whose fibre rank jumped — no chart of this form could exist and $\pi$ would not be a submersion. The constancy of the rank of $E$ is precisely what clause (i) needs.

**Drop freeness in clause (iii)** and the fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m) \cong GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ would collapse: a non-trivial $h$ fixing a frame $p$ would mean $p \circ h = p$, hence $h = \mathrm{id}$ since $p$ is an isomorphism, so freeness is automatic here and is exactly the statement that a matrix is determined by its action on a basis. Were the action not free — say if we quotiented frames by their orientation and then tried to act by all of $GL_k$ — the fibre would be smaller than $GL_k$ and the "structure group" would not match the acting group. **Drop transitivity** and the fibre would split into several $GL_k$-orbits; but any two frames $p, q$ of the same $E_m$ are related by the single matrix $h = p^{-1} \circ q$, so there is exactly one orbit, and this is what lets us identify the fibre with $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ once a reference frame is fixed. Transitivity is the reason $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ has structure group $GL_k$ and not some larger or disconnected object.

**Drop equivariance in clause (iv)** — keep only that $\pi^{-1}(U) \cong U \times GL_k$ as manifolds, without demanding the trivialisation intertwine the two right actions — and the local product would carry no memory of how frames transform, so the change-of-frame law $\Psi_{U'}^{-1} \circ \Psi_U(m, h) = (m, g(m) h)$ would be an accident of the chart rather than a genuine feature; the associated-bundle machinery of the next section, which reconstructs $E$ and every tensor bundle from $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ by twisting with a representation, depends entirely on this equivariance and would produce nonsense without it. The equivariant trivialisation is what makes $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ remember that its points are frames.

Finally, why the **right** action, and why an isomorphism rather than a bare tuple? Because a frame is naturally a map *into* the fibre, $p : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$, the group of the model space $\mathbb{R}^k$ acts by precomposition on the source, and precomposition is a right action: $(p \circ h_1) \circ h_2 = p \circ (h_1 h_2)$, so acting first by $h_1$ then by $h_2$ is acting by $h_1 h_2$, in that order. If we insisted on calling this a left action and writing $h \cdot p := p \circ h$, the axiom $(h_1 h_2) \cdot p = h_1 \cdot (h_2 \cdot p)$ would demand $p \circ (h_1 h_2) = (p \circ h_2) \circ h_1 = p \circ (h_2 h_1)$, which fails as soon as $h_1 h_2 \neq h_2 h_1$ — that is, for every non-abelian $GL_k$ with $k \geq 2$. To get a genuine left action one must insert the inverse, $h \cdot p := p \circ h^{-1}$; the composition action *as written* is a right action and nothing else. This is not pedantry: it is why a right principal bundle is the correct framework and why the inverse $\rho(g^{-1})$ appears in the associated-bundle construction. A reader who has followed this paragraph could have invented the right-action convention unaided.

# The Definition

Let $E \to M$ be a smooth real vector bundle of rank $k$.

**The frame bundle as a set with an action.** For each $m \in M$ let
$$\operatorname{Fr}(E_m) := \{\, p : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m \ \mid\ p \text{ is a linear isomorphism} \,\}$$
be the set of frames of the fibre $E_m$ (equivalently, the set of ordered bases of $E_m$). The **frame bundle** of $E$ is the disjoint union
$$\operatorname{Fr}(E) := \bigsqcup_{m \in M} \operatorname{Fr}(E_m),$$
with projection $\pi : \operatorname{Fr}(E) \to M$ determined by $\pi(p) = m \iff p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$. The group $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ acts on $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ on the right, fibrewise, by $p \cdot h = p \circ h$.

**The smooth structure by charts.** Let $U \subseteq M$ be an open set over which $E$ admits a local frame $e = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ (equivalently, over which $E$ is trivial). Define
$$\Psi_U : U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \longrightarrow \pi^{-1}(U), \qquad \Psi_U(m, h) = e(m) \cdot h = e(m) \circ h. \tag{24}$$
The atlas of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ consists of the pairs $\big(\pi^{-1}(U), \Psi_U^{-1}\big)$ as $U$ ranges over trivialising opens; a point of $\pi^{-1}(U)$ receives the coordinates $(m, h) \in U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^{k^2}$ (composing $\Psi_U^{-1}$ with charts of $U$). With this structure, and the projection $\pi$ and the right $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-action above, $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a smooth principal $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundle over $M$ of total dimension $n + k^2$.

The remainder of this section proves that last sentence in full: that the charts $(24)$ are compatible and define a Hausdorff, second-countable smooth structure, and that all four clauses of the definition of a principal bundle hold. Haydys states the charts and then writes "the rest of the properties required in the definition of the principal bundle are clear from the construction"; Bär's Example 2.2.5 states the action is free and transitive but does not write the local triviality. We write out both.

> [!note]- Complete verification that $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a principal $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundle
> We must show: (0) each $\Psi_U$ is a bijection; (1) the charts are smoothly compatible, so $(24)$ defines a smooth atlas; (2) the resulting topology is Hausdorff and second countable; and then that clauses (i)–(iv) of the definition of a principal bundle hold. Assume $k \geq 1$ (for $k = 0$ every fibre $E_m = \{0\}$ has the empty tuple as its only frame, $\operatorname{Fr}(E) = M$, $GL_0$ is trivial, and every clause is immediate).
>
> **Step 0 — each $\Psi_U$ is a bijection.** Fix a trivialising open $U$ with local frame $e$, so $e(m) : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$ is an isomorphism for every $m \in U$.
> - *Well-defined into $\pi^{-1}(U)$.* For $(m, h) \in U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ the map $e(m) \circ h : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$ is a composition of isomorphisms (since $h \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ is invertible), hence an isomorphism, hence a frame of $E_m$; thus $\Psi_U(m, h) \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m) \subseteq \pi^{-1}(U)$ (because $m \in U$).
> - *Injective.* Suppose $\Psi_U(m, h) = \Psi_U(m', h')$, i.e. $e(m) \circ h = e(m') \circ h'$. Applying $\pi$ gives $m = m'$ (both sides are frames over their respective base points, and equal frames have equal base points). Then $e(m) \circ h = e(m) \circ h'$, and precomposing with $e(m)^{-1}$ (an isomorphism, so left-cancellable) gives $h = h'$.
> - *Surjective.* Let $p \in \pi^{-1}(U)$, so $p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ for some $m \in U$. Set $h := e(m)^{-1} \circ p : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k$; it is a composition of isomorphisms, hence $h \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, and $\Psi_U(m, h) = e(m) \circ e(m)^{-1} \circ p = p$.
>
> Hence $\Psi_U : U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \to \pi^{-1}(U)$ is a bijection, with inverse $\Psi_U^{-1}(p) = \big(\pi(p),\, e(\pi(p))^{-1} \circ p\big)$.
>
> **Step 1 — the charts are smoothly compatible.** Let $U, U'$ be trivialising opens with local frames $e, e'$, and put $W := U \cap U'$. Over $W$ the two frames are related by the smooth change-of-frame map $g : W \to GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ with $e = e' \cdot g$, that is $e(m) = e'(m) \circ g(m)$ (Haydys (6); $g$ is smooth because it is the transition function of $E$ between the two trivialisations, and transition functions of a smooth vector bundle are smooth by the definition of [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundle]]). For $(m, h) \in W \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$,
> $$\Psi_U(m, h) = e(m) \circ h = e'(m) \circ g(m) \circ h = \Psi_{U'}\big(m,\, g(m) h\big) \qquad \text{(by } e(m) = e'(m) \circ g(m) \text{ and the definition (24))},$$
> so, applying $\Psi_{U'}^{-1}$,
> $$\Psi_{U'}^{-1} \circ \Psi_U (m, h) = \big(m,\, g(m)\, h\big) \qquad \text{(transition formula)}.$$
> This map $W \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \to W \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ is smooth: its first component is $m \mapsto m$, and its second component $(m, h) \mapsto g(m) h$ is the composite $W \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \xrightarrow{(g, \mathrm{id})} GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \xrightarrow{\mu} GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ of the smooth map $g$ with matrix multiplication $\mu$, which is smooth (polynomial in the entries). Its inverse is $(m, h) \mapsto (m, g(m)^{-1} h)$, smooth by the same argument together with the smoothness of inversion in $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$. Hence every transition map is a diffeomorphism, and the charts $(24)$ form a smooth atlas. Since $E$ admits a local frame over some neighbourhood of every point of $M$ (by local triviality of $E$), the opens $\pi^{-1}(U)$ cover $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.
>
> **Step 2 — the topology is Hausdorff and second countable.** We give $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ the topology in which each $\Psi_U$ is a homeomorphism onto the open set $\pi^{-1}(U)$; this is consistent because the transition maps of Step 1 are homeomorphisms on the overlaps.
> - *Second countable.* $M$ is second countable, hence Lindelöf, so the open cover of $M$ by trivialising opens has a countable subcover $\{U_i\}_{i \in \mathbb{N}}$. Each $\pi^{-1}(U_i) \cong U_i \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ is second countable (a product of the second-countable $U_i$ with the second-countable $GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{k^2}$), and $\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \bigcup_i \pi^{-1}(U_i)$ is a countable union of second-countable open subspaces, hence second countable.
> - *Hausdorff.* Let $p \neq q$ in $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$. If $\pi(p) \neq \pi(q)$, choose disjoint opens $A \ni \pi(p)$, $B \ni \pi(q)$ in $M$ (as $M$ is Hausdorff); then $\pi^{-1}(A)$ and $\pi^{-1}(B)$ are disjoint open sets separating $p$ and $q$ ($\pi$ is continuous, being the coordinate projection $(m, h) \mapsto m$ in every chart). If instead $\pi(p) = \pi(q) = m$, pick a trivialising open $U \ni m$; then $p, q \in \pi^{-1}(U)$ and $\Psi_U^{-1}(p) \neq \Psi_U^{-1}(q)$ in the Hausdorff space $U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, so disjoint opens there separate them, and their $\Psi_U$-images separate $p$ and $q$.
>
> Thus $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $\dim(U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})) = n + k^2$.
>
> **Clause (i): $\pi$ is a surjective submersion.** Surjective: for every $m \in M$ the fibre $E_m$ is a $k$-dimensional vector space, hence has a basis, hence $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m) \neq \varnothing$. In the chart $\Psi_U$ the map $\pi$ reads $(m, h) \mapsto m$, the projection $U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \to U$, which is a submersion; hence $\pi$ is a smooth surjective submersion.
>
> **Clause (ii): the right $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-action is smooth and fibre-preserving.** For $p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ and $h' \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, $p \cdot h' = p \circ h'$ is again an isomorphism $\mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$, so $\pi(p \cdot h') = m = \pi(p)$. Smoothness: in the chart $\Psi_U$, if $p = \Psi_U(m, h) = e(m) \circ h$ then $p \cdot h' = e(m) \circ h \circ h' = \Psi_U(m,\, h h')$, so the action reads $\big((m, h), h'\big) \mapsto (m, h h')$ in coordinates, which is smooth (matrix multiplication).
>
> **Clause (iii): the action is free and transitive on each fibre.** Fix $m$; the fibre is $\pi^{-1}(m) = \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$. *Transitive:* given $p, q \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$, set $h := p^{-1} \circ q \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$; then $p \cdot h = p \circ p^{-1} \circ q = q$. *Free:* if $p \cdot h = p$, i.e. $p \circ h = p$, then precomposing with $p^{-1}$ gives $h = \mathrm{id}$; so the stabiliser of every frame is trivial.
>
> **Clause (iv): equivariant local trivialisations.** For a trivialising open $U$ define $\psi_U := \Psi_U^{-1} : \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, a diffeomorphism by Steps 0–1. It covers $\pi$: $\mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U(p) = \pi(p)$ by the formula for $\Psi_U^{-1}$ in Step 0. It is equivariant: if $\psi_U(p) = (m, h)$, i.e. $p = \Psi_U(m, h)$, then by the Clause (ii) computation $p \cdot h' = \Psi_U(m, h h')$, so
> $$\psi_U(p \cdot h') = (m, h h') = (m, h) \cdot h' = \psi_U(p) \cdot h' \qquad \text{(} GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \text{ acting on } U \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \text{ by right multiplication on the second factor)}.$$
> These $U$ cover $M$, so clause (iv) holds.
>
> **Conclusion.** All four clauses hold, and the total space is a Hausdorff, second-countable smooth manifold on which the atlas is smooth; therefore $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a smooth principal $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundle over $M$. $\blacksquare$

**Equivalent description via ordered bases (Bär's form).** Writing frames as ordered bases, $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m) = \{(b_1, \dots, b_k) : (b_i) \text{ a basis of } E_m\}$ and the right action is $(b_1, \dots, b_k) \cdot A = \big(\sum_i A_{i1} b_i, \dots, \sum_i A_{ik} b_i\big)$ for $A = (A_{ij}) \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$; under the identification of the frame $(b_1, \dots, b_k)$ with the isomorphism $\hat{e}_j \mapsto b_j$, this is precisely $p \cdot A = p \circ A$, and the verification just given is unchanged. Bär calls $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ (the case $E = TM$) *the frame bundle of $M$*; we write $\operatorname{Fr}(M) := \operatorname{Fr}(TM)$.

**The orthonormal frame bundle $O(E)$.** Suppose $E$ is a [[Def - Euclidean Vector Bundle and Metric Connection|Euclidean vector bundle]], meaning each fibre $E_m$ carries a Euclidean inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_m$ depending smoothly on $m$ (that is, $\langle s_1, s_2 \rangle$ is a smooth function on $M$ for all smooth sections $s_1, s_2$). A frame $p : \mathbb{R}^k \to E_m$ is **orthonormal** if it is an isometry from $(\mathbb{R}^k, \text{standard inner product})$ to $(E_m, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_m)$, equivalently if $(p_1, \dots, p_k) = (p(\hat{e}_1), \dots, p(\hat{e}_k))$ is an orthonormal basis of $E_m$. Set
$$O(E) := \{\, p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E) \ \mid\ p \text{ is orthonormal} \,\},$$
with the restriction of $\pi$ and the restriction of the right action to the orthogonal group $O(k) = \{h \in GL_k(\mathbb{R}) : h^t h = \mathrm{id}\}$ (which does preserve orthonormality: if $p$ is an isometry and $h \in O(k)$ then $p \circ h$ is an isometry). The group $O(k)$ acts freely and transitively on each fibre $O(E_m)$ of orthonormal frames, by the same argument as for $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ with $GL_k$ replaced by $O(k)$: given orthonormal $p, q$, the matrix $h = p^{-1} \circ q$ is orthogonal because $p, q$ are isometries. That $O(E)$ is a smooth principal $O(k)$-bundle — the charts $\Psi_U(m, h) = e_O(m) \circ h$ built from a local *orthonormal* frame $e_O$ (obtained by applying the [[Thm - Gram-Schmidt Procedure|Gram–Schmidt procedure]] to any local frame $e$), with structure group the Lie group $O(k)$ — is proved as a reduction of the structure group; see [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group]], where the smooth structure is established and the identification of Euclidean structures with $O(k)$-reductions of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is carried out in full. We defer that verification there rather than repeat the chart computation.

**The unitary frame bundle $U(E)$.** Identically, if $E$ is a [[Def - Complex Vector Bundle and Hermitian Structure|complex vector bundle with a Hermitian structure]] $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_m$, the **unitary frame bundle** is $U(E) := \{p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E) : p \text{ is a Hermitian isometry } \mathbb{C}^k \to E_m\}$, a principal $U(k)$-bundle, with $U(k) = \{h \in GL_k(\mathbb{C}) : h^* h = \mathrm{id}\}$; the smooth structure again follows from the [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|reduction theorem]], with Gram–Schmidt taken over $\mathbb{C}$.

**The table of structure groups.** Taking the sub-frame-bundle of bases that *respect* a chosen fibre structure yields, in each case, a principal bundle whose structure group is the subgroup of $GL_k(\mathbb{K})$ stabilising that structure. Collected (Bär's table, Example 2.2.6, with our rank index $k$):

| $\mathbb{K}$ | structure on $E$ | admissible bases | structure group $G$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ | none | all bases | $GL_k(\mathbb{K})$ |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | Riemannian (Euclidean) | orthonormal | $O(k)$ |
| $\mathbb{C}$ | Hermitian | unitary | $U(k)$ |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | oriented | positively oriented | $GL^+_k(\mathbb{R}) = \{h : \det h > 0\}$ |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | Riemannian and oriented | oriented orthonormal | $SO(k) = O(k) \cap GL^+_k(\mathbb{R})$ |

In each row $G$ is the stabiliser in $GL_k(\mathbb{K})$ of the extra structure on the model fibre $\mathbb{K}^k$: the standard inner product for $O(k)$ and $U(k)$; the standard orientation for $GL^+_k(\mathbb{R})$; both for $SO(k)$. The sub-frame-bundle of admissible bases is then a $G$-reduction of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, and its smooth-manifold and principal-bundle structure is verified exactly as for $O(E)$; the orthonormal and unitary rows are [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reduction theorem]], and the oriented row is [[Ex - Orientations Correspond to GL-plus Reductions]]. We record the table here and verify each row on those pages rather than duplicate the argument five times.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The frame bundle has a clean structural description that makes its universal role explicit. Regard $\mathbb{R}^k$ as the fixed model fibre. Then
$$\operatorname{Fr}(E)_m = \operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{R}^k, E_m)$$
is the set of linear isomorphisms from the model to the fibre, and $GL_k(\mathbb{R}) = \operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{R}^k, \mathbb{R}^k)$ acts on it by precomposition. Because any two isomorphisms into $E_m$ differ by a unique automorphism of the source — $q = p \circ (p^{-1} q)$ — the fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E)_m$ is a **right $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-torsor**: a set on which the group acts freely and transitively, so that it looks like the group after any one point is chosen as origin but carries no preferred origin of its own (in particular it is not a group, having no identity frame). This is the structural meaning of clause (iii): a principal $G$-bundle is a smoothly varying family of right $G$-torsors, and the frame bundle is the family $m \mapsto \operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{R}^k, E_m)$.

Dually, $E$ is recovered from $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ by the associated-bundle construction with the standard representation $\rho_{\mathrm{std}} : GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \to GL(\mathbb{R}^k)$, $\rho_{\mathrm{std}}(h) = h$: one forms $\operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_{\rho_{\mathrm{std}}} \mathbb{R}^k = (\operatorname{Fr}(E) \times \mathbb{R}^k)/GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ with $(p, x) \cdot h = (p \cdot h,\, h^{-1} x)$, and the map $[p, x] \mapsto p(x) \in E_{\pi(p)}$ is a well-defined isomorphism of vector bundles onto $E$ (well-defined because $[p \cdot h, h^{-1} x] \mapsto (p \circ h)(h^{-1} x) = p(x)$, the same point). This is proved in full on [[Thm - Vector Bundles are Associated to Their Frame Bundles]]. So the pair (frame bundle, standard representation) contains exactly the data of $E$, and every natural bundle built from $E$ — the dual $E^*$, tensor powers $E^{\otimes r}$, exterior powers $\Lambda^r E$, $\operatorname{End}(E)$ — is $\operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_\rho V$ for the corresponding representation $\rho$ on the corresponding model space $V$. The frame bundle is thus the single object from which all the tensor bundles of $E$ are functorially generated, which is the structural reason gauge theory works with principal bundles rather than with each vector bundle separately.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Officially $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is a disjoint union of sets of bases given a chart-by-chart smooth structure; operationally it is *the space whose points are the choices you keep having to make*. Every local statement in the vector-bundle chapter began "choose a local frame $e$"; a point of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ over $m$ is exactly one such choice at $m$, and a local section of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ over $U$ is exactly a local frame of $E$ over $U$. The frame bundle converts "a choice depending smoothly on the point, together with the rule for how the answer changes when the choice changes" into "a section of a principal bundle, together with the right action of the structure group" — and the second formulation has the advantage that global questions (does a global frame exist? is $E$ trivial?) become questions about sections of a single space.

This is the geometer's instance of a pattern that recurs across mathematics: replacing an object by *its bundle of admissible presentations, with the change-of-presentation group acting*. In linear algebra the same move turns "a vector space" into "its $GL$-torsor of bases"; in Riemannian geometry the orthonormal frame bundle $O(TM)$ is the home of Cartan's *moving frames* (repères mobiles), where a connection is encoded by a $\mathfrak{so}(k)$-valued one-form on $O(TM)$ rather than by Christoffel symbols in a coordinate chart; in the theory of $G$-structures a reduction of $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ to a subgroup $G \subseteq GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ is exactly a piece of geometry (an orientation for $GL^+$, a Riemannian metric for $O(n)$, an almost complex structure for $GL_{n/2}(\mathbb{C})$, a symplectic form for $Sp$); and in physics a local frame is a *gauge* and the change-of-frame map is a *gauge transformation*, so $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is the total space on which the gauge group of $E$ acts. In every case the compression is the same: intrinsic geometry is what survives the action of the structure group on the frame bundle, and the connections, curvatures, and characteristic classes of the next chapters are built directly on $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and its reductions.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the trivial bundle, $\operatorname{Fr}(M \times \mathbb{R}^k) \cong M \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$.** Let $E = M \times \mathbb{R}^k$ be the trivial rank-$k$ bundle, with fibre $E_m = \{m\} \times \mathbb{R}^k$ identified with $\mathbb{R}^k$. A frame of $E_m$ is an isomorphism $p : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k$, i.e. an element of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$; so, taking the constant global frame $e_0(m) = \mathrm{id}$ (the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^k$ at every point), the single chart $\Psi_M(m, h) = e_0(m) \circ h = h$ is a global bijection $M \times GL_k(\mathbb{R}) \to \operatorname{Fr}(E)$. It is $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-equivariant — $\Psi_M(m, h) \cdot h' = h h' = \Psi_M(m, h h')$ — and covers $\pi$, so it is a global equivariant trivialisation and $\operatorname{Fr}(M \times \mathbb{R}^k) \cong M \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ as principal $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundles. Every clause is verified by inspection in this single chart: $\pi = \mathrm{pr}_1$ is a submersion (i); the action is $(m,h)\cdot h' = (m, hh')$ (ii); it is free and transitive on each $\{m\} \times GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ (iii); the identity chart is the equivariant trivialisation (iv). The full worked identification is [[Ex - The Frame Bundle of a Trivial Bundle and the Trivial Principal Bundle]].

**Is an instance — $\operatorname{Fr}(TS^2)$, the frame bundle of the tangent bundle of the sphere.** For $E = TS^2$ (rank $k = 2$ over the $2$-manifold $M = S^2$), $\operatorname{Fr}(TS^2)$ is a principal $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$-bundle of dimension $2 + 4 = 6$; its orthonormal reduction $O(TS^2)$ (with the round metric) is a principal $O(2)$-bundle of dimension $3$, whose oriented component $SO(TS^2)$ is a principal $SO(2) = U(1)$-bundle over $S^2$. This bundle is **not trivial**: it admits no global section, because a global section of $\operatorname{Fr}(TS^2)$ would be a global frame of $TS^2$, in particular a nowhere-vanishing vector field on $S^2$, which the hairy-ball theorem forbids. The complete argument is [[Ex - The Frame Bundle of TS^2 Admits No Global Section]]; its transition cocycle in stereographic charts is computed in [[Ex - The Cocycle of the Frame Bundle of the Sphere in Stereographic Charts]]. This example is the standard witness that $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ detects the non-triviality of $E$.

**Is NOT an instance — the left action $h \cdot p = p \circ h$.** Take the same set $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ of frames, keep the smooth structure and the projection, but declare $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ to act *on the left* by $h \cdot p := p \circ h$. This is **not** a principal bundle in the sense of this series, because it is not a left action at all: for $h_1, h_2 \in GL_k(\mathbb{R})$,
$$(h_1 h_2) \cdot p = p \circ (h_1 h_2) = (p \circ h_2) \circ h_1 = h_1 \cdot (h_2 \cdot p),$$
whereas a left action must satisfy $(h_1 h_2) \cdot p = h_1 \cdot (h_2 \cdot p)$; the two agree only when $h_1 h_2 = h_2 h_1$, which fails for $k \geq 2$ (for instance $h_1 = \big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, $h_2 = \big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ have $h_1 h_2 = \big(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\big) \neq \big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\big) = h_2 h_1$). The composition action is intrinsically a *right* action, and to obtain a genuine left action one must strengthen the definition by inserting the inverse, $h \cdot p := p \circ h^{-1}$, whereupon $(h_1 h_2) \cdot p = p \circ (h_1 h_2)^{-1} = p \circ h_2^{-1} \circ h_1^{-1} = h_1 \cdot (h_2 \cdot p)$ holds. This is exactly why the associated-bundle construction carries $\rho(g^{-1})$ and why principal bundles in this series are right bundles.

**Corollary — $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ has a global section if and only if $E$ is trivial.** A global section $\sigma : M \to \operatorname{Fr}(E)$ of the frame bundle is, by definition, an assignment of a frame $\sigma(m)$ of $E_m$ to each $m$, smoothly — that is, a global frame $(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k)$ of $E$, hence a global trivialisation $E \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^k$ via $\psi^{-1}(m, x) = \sigma(m)(x)$; conversely a global trivialisation supplies a global frame, hence a global section. On the other hand, a principal bundle admits a global section if and only if it is trivial:

> **Theorem (sections and triviality of a principal bundle).** A principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$ has a global section $\sigma : M \to P$ if and only if it is trivial, that is, isomorphic as a principal $G$-bundle to $M \times G$; more generally, local trivialisations over $U$ correspond bijectively to local sections over $U$.

This is proved in full on [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]] (the trivialisation from a section $\sigma$ is $\psi^{-1}(m, g) = \sigma(m) \cdot g$, whose inverse exists by freeness and transitivity). Combining the two equivalences: $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is trivial as a principal bundle $\iff$ it has a global section $\iff E$ has a global frame $\iff E$ is trivial as a vector bundle. Thus the frame bundle is trivial exactly when $E$ is, and the non-triviality of $\operatorname{Fr}(TS^2)$ above is the same fact as the non-triviality of $TS^2$.

**Calibration check.** Three things a reader should be able to carry out from what is on this page. First, verify freeness directly from the isomorphism picture: if $p \circ h = p$ with $p$ an isomorphism, left-cancel $p^{-1}$ to get $h = \mathrm{id}$ — so no non-identity matrix fixes any frame, and the fibre is a torsor. Second, check that the transition maps of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ are $(m, h) \mapsto (m, g(m) h)$ where $g$ is the transition cocycle of $E$, and conclude that $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and $E$ have the *same* transition functions (composed with the standard representation for $E$), which is why they are trivial together. Third, count dimensions: $\dim \operatorname{Fr}(E) = n + k^2$, so $\operatorname{Fr}(TS^2)$ is $6$-dimensional and $O(TS^2)$ is $3$-dimensional — and $SO(TS^2)$, being a principal $U(1)$-bundle over $S^2$, is the total space that will reappear as (a cousin of) the Hopf bundle.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Associated bundles *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> With $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ in hand, every tensor bundle of $E$ is $\operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_\rho V$ for a representation $\rho$ of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$; see [[Def - Associated Bundle]] and [[Thm - Vector Bundles are Associated to Their Frame Bundles]]. This is the mechanism that lets one connection on $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ differentiate sections of all of them at once.

> [!tip] Reduction of the structure group *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> Geometric structures on $E$ are reductions of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ to subgroups of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$: a Euclidean metric to $O(k)$, an orientation to $GL^+_k(\mathbb{R})$, both to $SO(k)$, a complex structure to $GL_{k/2}(\mathbb{C})$. See [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group]] and [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group]].

> [!tip] Connections and curvature on principal bundles *(from Gauge Theory IV)*
> A **[[Def - Connection on a Vector Bundle|connection]]** on $E$ becomes a $\mathfrak{gl}_k$-valued connection one-form on $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, invariant under the right $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-action; the frame bundle is where the choice-free formulation of connection and curvature lives.

> [!tip] Structure group and $G$-bundles *(from Gauge Theory III–IV)*
> The passage from $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ (structure group $GL_k$) to a principal $G$-bundle for an abstract Lie group $G$ is the step from bundles of frames to gauge theory proper; the frame bundle is the concrete prototype every principal $G$-bundle is modelled on.
