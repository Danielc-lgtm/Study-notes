# Problem Statement

Throughout, $E\to M$ is a smooth real vector bundle of rank $k\ge 1$ over a smooth manifold $M$ (Hausdorff, second countable), and unless stated otherwise $M$ is connected. We follow the series convention that Lie groups act on the **right** of principal bundles, and we write
$$GL^+(k;\mathbb R):=\{A\in GL(k;\mathbb R):\det A>0\},\qquad GL^-(k;\mathbb R):=\{A\in GL(k;\mathbb R):\det A<0\}.$$
The frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)\to M$ is the principal $GL(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle whose fibre over $m$ is $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)=\{\text{linear isomorphisms }p\colon\mathbb R^k\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }E_m\}$, identified with the ordered bases $(p(e_1),\dots,p(e_k))$ of $E_m$, and whose right action is precomposition, $p\cdot A:=p\circ A$.

An **orientation** of the vector bundle $E$ is a choice, for every $m\in M$, of an orientation $\mathcal O_m$ of the fibre $E_m$ (an equivalence class of ordered bases in the sense of [[Def - Orientation of a Vector Space|the vector-space definition]]) that is **continuous** in the following sense: every point of $M$ lies in the domain of a local frame $s=(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ of $E$ that is positively oriented at every point of its domain, that is, $(s_1(m'),\dots,s_k(m'))\in\mathcal O_{m'}$ for all $m'$ in the domain. The bundle $E$ is **orientable** if it admits an orientation, and **oriented** if equipped with one. A frame $p\in\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is **positively oriented** (for a given orientation) if $(p(e_1),\dots,p(e_k))\in\mathcal O_m$. This is the fibrewise analogue of [[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold|the definition of orientation of a smooth manifold]], recovered below in the case $E=TM$. There is no separate vault page for "orientation of a vector bundle"; the definition just given is the one used on this page.

Prove the following three statements, which together make up Bär's table row *"$\mathbb R$, oriented, positively oriented bases, $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$"* (Bär, *Gauge Theory*, §2.2, table on p. 42), together with its structural meaning.

**Part A (the correspondence).** Orientations of $E$ are in natural bijection with $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-structures on $E$, i.e. with $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundles of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$. Explicitly, an orientation $\mathcal O$ is sent to the set
$$\operatorname{Fr}^+(E):=\{p\in\operatorname{Fr}(E):p\text{ is positively oriented}\},$$
which is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle, and a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is sent to the unique orientation whose positively oriented frames are exactly the elements of $P$; these two assignments are mutually inverse. By [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|the reduction–subbundle dictionary]] a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle is the same datum as a reduction of the structure group of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ from $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ to $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, so an orientation of $E$ is exactly such a reduction.

**Part B (the orientability criterion).** The following are equivalent: (i) $E$ is orientable; (ii) $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ admits a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction; (iii) $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ decomposes as a disjoint union $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=P_+\sqcup P_-$ of two open $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundles, interchanged by right multiplication by any fixed $R\in GL^-(k;\mathbb R)$; (iv) the **orientation double cover** $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E):=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\to M$ is disconnected, i.e. (for $M$ connected) has exactly two connected components. This is the precise content of "$\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ has a two-component subbundle structure".

**Part C (relation to manifold orientation).** For $E=TM$ this recovers [[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold|the orientation of the manifold M]]: $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ is the frame bundle of $M$, an orientation of the vector bundle $TM$ is an orientation of $M$ in the sense of Differential Geometry IX, and a $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$-reduction is exactly an oriented atlas.

The single algebraic fact behind everything is that the determinant sorts invertible matrices into two sign classes:
$$\operatorname{sgn}\circ\det\colon GL(k;\mathbb R)\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}\quad\text{is a group homomorphism with kernel }GL^+(k;\mathbb R),$$
so $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is a normal subgroup of index $2$ and $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$; this uses that $\det$ is multiplicative ([[Thm - Determinant is Multiplicative|determinant is multiplicative]], $\det(AB)=\det A\det B$).

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *"structure equals reduction" identification*: we are handed a piece of fibrewise geometric data on $E$ (a coherent choice of orientation in each fibre) and asked to show it is the same thing as a subbundle of the frame bundle cut out by a subgroup $G\le GL(k;\mathbb R)$. It is the exact analogue, for $G=GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, of the exercise identifying fibre metrics with $O(k)$-structures and fibrewise volume forms with $SL_k(\mathbb R)$-structures ([[Ex - Fibrewise Volume Forms Correspond to SL(k,R)-Structures|the volume-form drill]]); only the subgroup, and the invariant it fixes, change.

**Assumption pattern.** The recognisable trigger is: *a subgroup $G\le GL(k;\mathbb R)$ is exactly the stabiliser, inside the transitive action on frames, of the geometric datum in question.* For orientation the datum on $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is "which orientation class the frame represents", and $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is exactly the set of change-of-basis matrices that preserve an orientation. Whenever a datum has this form, the positively-adapted frames form a subbundle and the correspondence runs.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: (1) use the transitive free $GL(k;\mathbb R)$-action on each frame fibre to translate "two frames give the same orientation" into "they differ by an element of $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$"; (2) use continuity of the orientation to produce positively oriented local frames, hence equivariant local trivialisations of $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ by $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$; (3) invoke [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|Proposition 2 of the reductions page]] (a $G$-subbundle is the same as a reduction, for closed $G$) to rename the subbundle a reduction; (4) for Part B use covering-space bookkeeping together with the index-$2$ fact $GL/GL^+\cong\mathbb Z/2$; (5) for Part C match transition functions with Jacobians.

**Key decision point.** The one genuinely non-obvious move is *what makes $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, rather than any other subgroup, the right stabiliser*: the sign of $\det$ is precisely the obstruction to two ordered bases lying in the same orientation class, so "positively oriented" is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-invariant notion and nothing smaller or larger will do. The second decision is realising that the *global* obstruction to orientability is not visible fibrewise at all — each fibre always has two orientations — but lives in whether those pointwise choices can be threaded together, which is exactly the connectedness of the double cover $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following operations from the topic page's Legal Operations, specialised to $G=GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$:

1. **Coordinatise a frame fibre by the structure group.** On each fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ the action of $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ is free and transitive, so once one frame is fixed every other is $p\cdot A$ for a unique $A$; this turns geometric relations between frames into statements about the matrix $A$.

2. **Read a geometric invariant off the structure-group element.** Two frames $p,p\cdot A$ represent the same orientation if and only if $\det A>0$; the invariant "orientation class" is detected by the sign of the determinant of the transition matrix.

3. **Cut out an adapted subbundle.** Collect the frames adapted to the datum — here the positively oriented frames $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ — and verify it is invariant under the stabiliser subgroup and locally trivial, hence a $G$-subbundle.

4. **Manufacture equivariant local trivialisations from local sections.** A positively oriented local frame $s$ is a local section of $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$; the map $(m,A)\mapsto s(m)\cdot A$ trivialises $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ over its domain, exactly as on [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the sections–triviality page]].

5. **Rename a subbundle as a reduction.** Apply [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|Proposition 2]] (for a closed subgroup $G\le H$, a $G$-subbundle of a principal $H$-bundle is the same datum as a reduction to $G$) to pass between the subbundle picture and the reduction picture.

6. **Use the index-$2$ quotient to build a double cover.** Since $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ has index $2$, the associated bundle with fibre $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$ is a double cover of $M$; sections of it are orientations.

7. **Count components of a covering to detect triviality.** A connected double cover of a connected base admits a global section if and only if it is disconnected, i.e. has two components; this converts orientability into a component count.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> Fix a fibre $E_m$ and one frame $p_0\in\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$. Every other frame is $p_0\cdot A$ for a unique $A\in GL(k;\mathbb R)$ (freeness and transitivity of the action on the fibre). The basis of $p_0\cdot A=p_0\circ A$ is the basis of $p_0$ transformed by the matrix $A$. When do a basis and its $A$-transform represent the *same* orientation of $E_m$?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> They do exactly when $\det A>0$ — that is the whole point of [[Def - Orientation of a Vector Space|the vector-space definition of orientation]]. So "positively oriented" is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-invariant condition on frames: if $p$ is positively oriented and $\det A>0$ then $p\cdot A$ is too, and conversely. Use this to show $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is invariant under $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ and that $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is free and transitive on each fibre $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For local triviality you need a *smooth* positively oriented local frame near each point. That is exactly what continuity of the orientation hands you. Given such a frame $s$ over $U$, check that $(m,A)\mapsto s(m)\cdot A$ is an equivariant diffeomorphism $U\times GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)|_U$. Note also that $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is open in $GL(k;\mathbb R)$, so $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is open in $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For the converse (a subbundle gives an orientation): a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $P$ has, over each fibre, a single $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit; declare its frames positive. Two local sections of $P$ differ by a $GL^+$-valued transition function, so they induce the *same* fibre orientation on the overlap — that is why the pointwise choice is continuous.

> [!note]- Hint 5
> For Part B, form the quotient $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. Its fibre is $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$ — a two-point set (the two orientations of the fibre). So $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)\to M$ is a double cover, and an orientation is a global section of it. A double cover of a connected space is either connected (no section) or two disjoint copies of the base (a section). That dichotomy *is* the two-component criterion.

# Solution

The engine is a single algebraic observation — two ordered bases of a vector space represent the same orientation exactly when their change-of-basis matrix has positive determinant — promoted to bundles. Fibrewise it identifies "positively oriented frame" with a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-invariant condition; continuity of the orientation upgrades the fibrewise statement to a smooth subbundle, giving the correspondence of Part A. Part B is then the observation that, although every fibre always carries two orientations, threading them together globally is obstructed by exactly the connectedness of a double cover; Part C is the special case $E=TM$, where frames of $TM$ are frames of the manifold and positive-determinant transitions are an oriented atlas.

**Step 1: Fibrewise, "same orientation" means "differ by $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$".**

Fix $m\in M$. On the fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ the group $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ acts freely and transitively, and two frames represent the same orientation of $E_m$ if and only if they differ by an element of $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> A frame $p\in\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is an isomorphism $p\colon\mathbb R^k\to E_m$; its ordered basis is $\beta(p):=(p(e_1),\dots,p(e_k))$, where $(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb R^k$. The right action is $p\cdot A=p\circ A$, so
> $$\beta(p\cdot A)=\big((p\circ A)(e_1),\dots,(p\circ A)(e_k)\big)=\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^k A_{i1}\,p(e_i),\ \dots,\ \sum_{i=1}^k A_{ik}\,p(e_i)\Big)\qquad(\text{since }A e_j=\sum_i A_{ij}e_i).$$
> Thus the basis of $p\cdot A$ is obtained from the basis of $p$ by the change-of-basis matrix $A$. By [[Def - Orientation of a Vector Space|the definition of orientation of a vector space]], two ordered bases $\beta,\beta'$ of $E_m$ satisfy $\beta\sim\beta'$ (same orientation class) if and only if the unique matrix $B$ carrying $\beta$ to $\beta'$ has $\det B>0$. Here that matrix is $A$, so
> $$\beta(p\cdot A)\sim\beta(p)\iff\det A>0\iff A\in GL^+(k;\mathbb R).$$
> **Freeness and transitivity.** These hold for $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ as a principal $GL(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle ([[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]]); concretely, if $p\cdot A=p$ then $p\circ A=p$ and, $p$ being invertible, $A=\operatorname{id}$, so the action is free; and given $p,p'\in\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ the composite $A:=p^{-1}\circ p'\in GL(k;\mathbb R)$ is the unique matrix with $p'=p\cdot A$, so the action is transitive. Combining, for fixed $p$ every frame is $p\cdot A$ for a unique $A$, and it lies in the same orientation class as $p$ precisely when $A\in GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$.

**Step 2: An orientation gives a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$.**

Given an orientation $\mathcal O=(\mathcal O_m)$ of $E$, the set $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)=\{p:\beta(p)\in\mathcal O_{\pi(p)}\}$ of positively oriented frames is an open $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Invariance under $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, freeness and transitivity on fibres.** Let $p\in\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ over $m$ and $A\in GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. By Step 1, $\beta(p\cdot A)\sim\beta(p)\in\mathcal O_m$, so $\beta(p\cdot A)\in\mathcal O_m$ and $p\cdot A\in\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$: the subset is $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-invariant. The restricted action is free (restriction of a free action). It is transitive on the fibre $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$: given $p,p'\in\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$, by transitivity of the full action there is a unique $A$ with $p'=p\cdot A$, and since $\beta(p),\beta(p')$ both lie in $\mathcal O_m$ they are $\sim$, so $\det A>0$ by Step 1, i.e. $A\in GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. Each fibre $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$ is thus a single $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit, hence nonempty (both orientations are realised by some basis).
>
> **Local triviality (hence embedded submanifold and principal $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle).** Fix $m_0\in M$. By **continuity of the orientation**, there is an open $U\ni m_0$ and a smooth local frame $s=(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ of $E$ over $U$ with $(s_1(m'),\dots,s_k(m'))\in\mathcal O_{m'}$ for all $m'\in U$; regarded as a section $s\colon U\to\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, $m'\mapsto\big(x\mapsto\sum_j x_j s_j(m')\big)$, it takes values in $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$. Define
> $$\Psi_U\colon U\times GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)|_U,\qquad \Psi_U(m',A):=s(m')\cdot A.$$
> This is smooth (composite of the smooth section $s$ and the smooth action) and covers the identity of $U$. It is a bijection: over each $m'$, $A\mapsto s(m')\cdot A$ is a bijection onto the single orbit $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_{m'})$ by freeness and transitivity from the previous paragraph. Its inverse is $p\mapsto(\pi(p),\,s(\pi(p))^{-1}\circ p)$, smooth because $p\mapsto s(\pi(p))^{-1}\circ p$ is a composite of smooth maps (the same computation of the local gauge as on [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the sections–triviality page]]). Hence $\Psi_U$ is a diffeomorphism and $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)|_U\cong U\times GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ equivariantly. Because such $U$ cover $M$, the set $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is an embedded submanifold of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and, with the restricted action, a principal $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle. Finally $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is **open** in $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$: $\Psi_U$ is the restriction of the trivialisation $U\times GL(k;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Fr}(E)|_U$ to the open set $U\times GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ (as $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is open in $GL(k;\mathbb R)$, the sign of $\det$ being continuous), so its image is open.
>
> Therefore $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ in the sense of [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|the definition of a G-structure]].

**Step 3: A $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle gives an orientation, inverse to Step 2.**

Conversely, a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ determines a unique orientation whose positive frames are exactly the elements of $P$, and the two assignments $\mathcal O\mapsto\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ and $P\mapsto\mathcal O^P$ are mutually inverse. This is Part A.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Constructing the orientation $\mathcal O^P$.** For each $m$, the fibre $P_m$ is a single $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit in $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ (it is a principal $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle, so its fibres are orbits and are nonempty). By Step 1 all frames in one $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit represent the **same** orientation class of $E_m$; call it $\mathcal O^P_m$. Concretely $\mathcal O^P_m:=\beta(p)$'s class for any $p\in P_m$, well defined because any two elements of $P_m$ differ by an element of $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$.
>
> **Continuity of $\mathcal O^P$.** Fix $m_0$. Since $P$ is a principal $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-bundle it is locally trivial, so there is an open $U\ni m_0$ and a smooth local section $\sigma\colon U\to P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$. As a local frame of $E$, $\sigma$ is positively oriented at every point of $U$ by construction of $\mathcal O^P$ ($\sigma(m')\in P_{m'}$, so $\beta(\sigma(m'))\in\mathcal O^P_{m'}$). This is exactly a positively oriented local frame around $m_0$, so $\mathcal O^P$ is continuous. Hence $\mathcal O^P$ is an orientation of $E$.
>
> **Mutual inverseness.** Start from an orientation $\mathcal O$, form $P:=\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ (Step 2), then form $\mathcal O^{P}$. For each $m$, $P_m=\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$ consists exactly of the frames with $\beta(p)\in\mathcal O_m$, so $\mathcal O^{P}_m=\mathcal O_m$; thus $\mathcal O^{\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)}=\mathcal O$. Conversely start from a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $P$, form $\mathcal O^P$, then form $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ for $\mathcal O^P$. A frame $p$ over $m$ is $\mathcal O^P$-positive iff $\beta(p)\in\mathcal O^P_m$ iff $\beta(p)$ lies in the common orientation class of $P_m$ iff (by Step 1, as $P_m$ is one $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit and $p$ shares its class exactly when $p\in P_m$) $p\in P_m$. Hence the $\operatorname{Fr}^+$ built from $\mathcal O^P$ is $P$ again. The assignments are inverse bijections.
>
> **Renaming as a reduction.** The subgroup $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\le GL(k;\mathbb R)$ is closed: its complement $GL^-(k;\mathbb R)=\{\det<0\}$ is open (preimage of the open set $(-\infty,0)$ under the continuous $\det$), so $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is closed (indeed clopen). By [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|Proposition 2 of the reductions page]] — for a closed subgroup $G\le H$, $G$-subbundles of a principal $H$-bundle $Q$ correspond bijectively to reductions of $Q$ to $G$, via $P\mapsto\big([p,h]\mapsto p\cdot h\colon P\times_\iota H\xrightarrow{\cong}Q\big)$ — a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is the same datum as a reduction of its structure group to $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. Composing bijections, **orientations of $E$ correspond bijectively to $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reductions of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$**, which is clause 4 of the quoted theorem.

**Step 4: The orientability criterion (Part B), including the two-component picture.**

The four conditions (i)–(iv) of Part B are equivalent.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **(i) $\Leftrightarrow$ (ii).** By Part A, an orientation is exactly a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction; so "$E$ orientable" (an orientation exists) is "$\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ admits a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction".
>
> **(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii).** Let $P_+\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ be a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle (Part A/Step 2). Fix any $R\in GL^-(k;\mathbb R)$ (for instance $R=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)$, with $\det R=-1$) and set $P_-:=P_+\cdot R=\{p\cdot R:p\in P_+\}$. Then $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=P_+\sqcup P_-$: for any $p\in\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ pick $p_0\in (P_+)_m$ (nonempty) and write $p=p_0\cdot A$ with unique $A\in GL(k;\mathbb R)$ (Step 1); if $\det A>0$ then $p\in P_+$ (invariance, Step 2), and if $\det A<0$ then $A=(AR^{-1})R$ with $\det(AR^{-1})>0$, so $p=(p_0\cdot(AR^{-1}))\cdot R\in P_+\cdot R=P_-$; the two cases are exclusive because $\det A$ has one sign. Both pieces are open ($P_+$ is open by Step 2, and $P_-=P_+\cdot R$ is the image of the open $P_+$ under the diffeomorphism $R_R$ of right multiplication by $R$, hence open), and each is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle ($P_-$ carries the $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-action $p\cdot R\mapsto(p\cdot R)\cdot A'=(p\cdot(RA'R^{-1}))\cdot R$ with $RA'R^{-1}\in GL^+$; more simply, $P_-=\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ for the *opposite* orientation $-\mathcal O$, so it is a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle by Step 2). Right multiplication by $R$ maps $P_+$ onto $P_-$ and, since $R^2\in GL^+$ need not be $\operatorname{id}$ but $\det(R^2)>0$, maps $P_-$ back into $P_+$: $P_-\cdot R=P_+\cdot R^2\subseteq P_+$ because $\det(R^2)>0$; equality holds as $R$ acts invertibly. So $R$ interchanges the two pieces.
>
> **(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (ii).** Given a decomposition $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=P_+\sqcup P_-$ into two $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundles, the piece $P_+$ is itself a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle, hence a reduction.
>
> **(ii) $\Leftrightarrow$ (iv).** Form the associated bundle $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E):=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, the quotient of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ by the (right) action of $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. Its fibre over $m$ is $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$, and since $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ acts freely and transitively on $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ this fibre is a torsor over $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$, a **two-element set** — its two points are precisely the two orientations of $E_m$ (Step 1). Over a trivialising $U$ with $\operatorname{Fr}(E)|_U\cong U\times GL(k;\mathbb R)$, we get $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)|_U\cong U\times\big(GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\big)\cong U\times\mathbb Z/2$, so $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)\to M$ is a smooth covering map with two sheets — a **double cover**. A continuous (equivalently smooth, on a covering) global section of $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ chooses one orientation of $E_m$ for each $m$, and the local product structure over each $U$ shows the choice is realised there by a positively oriented local frame; thus **sections of $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ are exactly orientations of $E$**, and $E$ is orientable iff $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ has a global section. Now for a connected base $M$: a double cover $q\colon\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)\to M$ has total space with at most two components (each component of the total space maps by a covering onto $M$, and the sheets over a point are distributed among the components, so there are at most two). If $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ is connected it admits no section, because a section $\varsigma$ would have open image $\varsigma(M)$ (a section of a covering is an open map onto a union of sheets) which is also closed (its complement is the union of the other sheets, open), giving a proper clopen subset of the connected total space — impossible. If $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ is disconnected it has exactly two components, each mapped homeomorphically onto $M$ by $q$ (a connected covering space of $M$ that is a single sheet over each point is a homeomorphism), and the inverse of either is a global section. Hence *(iv) $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$ has two components $\iff$ it admits a section $\iff$ (i) $E$ is orientable*, and by (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) this closes the cycle.

**Step 5: The fibres of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ genuinely have two components (enrichment), and Part C.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> **The two components are honest.** The word "two-component" is exact because $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ has **precisely two** connected components, $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ and $GL^-(k;\mathbb R)$, so each fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)\cong GL(k;\mathbb R)$ has exactly two, and the reduction $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ picks one per fibre coherently. To see the count: $\det\colon GL(k;\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$ is continuous and $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}=(-\infty,0)\sqcup(0,\infty)$ is disconnected, so $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ has at least the two nonempty open pieces $GL^\pm(k;\mathbb R)$. Each piece is path-connected. For $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$: by the [[Ex - Polar decomposition unique for invertible operators|polar decomposition]], every $A\in GL(k;\mathbb R)$ factors uniquely as $A=QS$ with $Q\in O(k)$ orthogonal and $S$ symmetric positive-definite; the path $t\mapsto Q\big((1-t)S+tI\big)$, $t\in[0,1]$, stays in $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ because $(1-t)S+tI$ is symmetric positive-definite (a convex combination of the positive-definite $S$ and $I$), hence invertible, and $\det\big(Q((1-t)S+tI)\big)=\det Q\cdot\det\big((1-t)S+tI\big)$ keeps the sign of $\det Q$ throughout (the second factor is positive); it connects $A$ to $Q$ within the same determinant-sign class. If $\det A>0$ then $\det Q=+1$, i.e. $Q\in SO(k)$, which is path-connected ([[Ex - SO(n) is connected|SO(n) is connected]]), so $Q$ connects to $I$ inside $SO(k)\subseteq GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$; concatenating, $A$ connects to $I$ inside $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$. Thus $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is path-connected, and $GL^-(k;\mathbb R)=GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cdot R$ (image under the homeomorphism of right multiplication by $R\in GL^-$) is path-connected too. So there are exactly two components, matching [[Ex - The orthogonal group has two components|the two components of O(k)]] under polar decomposition.
>
> **Part C: the case $E=TM$.** When $E=TM$, the frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ is by definition the frame bundle of the manifold $M$ ([[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]]), a principal $GL(n;\mathbb R)$-bundle with $n=\dim M$. An orientation of the vector bundle $TM$ in the sense of the Problem Statement is a continuous pointwise orientation of the tangent spaces, which is verbatim [[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold|the definition of an orientation of the smooth manifold M]] (continuous pointwise orientation, equivalently an oriented atlas, equivalently a nowhere-vanishing top form). Under the correspondence of Part A, this is a $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$-reduction of $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$. This matches the atlas formulation directly: for a chart $(U,\varphi)$ with coordinates $(x^1,\dots,x^n)$ the coordinate frame $(\partial_{x^1},\dots,\partial_{x^n})$ is a local section of $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$; on an overlap of two charts the transition function of $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ is the Jacobian $D(\widetilde\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1})$ of the chart change ([[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold|the transition functions of TM are the Jacobians]]), so a family of charts whose coordinate frames are all positively oriented is exactly an **oriented atlas** — a smooth atlas all of whose Jacobians have positive determinant, i.e. a $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$-valued cocycle. Thus the general vector-bundle statement of Parts A–B specialises, at $E=TM$, to the manifold orientation theory of Differential Geometry IX; in particular $E=TM$ orientable $\iff$ $M$ orientable, and the orientation double cover $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(TM)$ is the orientation double cover of $M$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** For a rank-$k$ real vector bundle $E\to M$: (A) orientations of $E$ correspond bijectively to $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reductions of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, via $\mathcal O\mapsto\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$; (B) $E$ is orientable iff $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ admits such a reduction, iff $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=P_+\sqcup P_-$ splits into two $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundles, iff the orientation double cover $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ has two components (for $M$ connected); (C) for $E=TM$ this is the orientation of $M$.
>
> Write $\beta(p)=(p(e_1),\dots,p(e_k))$ for the ordered basis of a frame $p\colon\mathbb R^k\to E_m$; the action is $p\cdot A=p\circ A$, so $\beta(p\cdot A)$ is $\beta(p)$ transformed by the matrix $A$. By [[Def - Orientation of a Vector Space|the vector-space definition]], $\beta(p\cdot A)$ and $\beta(p)$ lie in the same orientation class iff $\det A>0$, i.e. iff $A\in GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$; and $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ acts freely and transitively on each frame fibre.
>
> *(A).* Given an orientation $\mathcal O$, set $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)=\{p:\beta(p)\in\mathcal O_{\pi(p)}\}$. It is $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-invariant, and $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ acts freely and transitively on each fibre $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E_m)$ (any two positive frames differ by $A$ with $\det A>0$). A positively oriented local frame $s$ — which exists near every point by continuity of $\mathcal O$ — gives an equivariant diffeomorphism $U\times GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)|_U$, $(m,A)\mapsto s(m)A$, so $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ is an open principal $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle. Conversely a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle $P$ has one $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-orbit per fibre; declaring its frames positive defines an orientation $\mathcal O^P$ (well defined since one orbit is one orientation class; continuous via local sections of $P$). These are mutually inverse: $\operatorname{Fr}^+(E)$ recovers $\mathcal O$, and $\mathcal O^P$ recovers $P$. Since $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ is closed in $GL(k;\mathbb R)$, [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|the reduction–subbundle bijection]] renames a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundle a $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction; composing, orientations correspond bijectively to reductions.
>
> *(B).* (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) is (A). (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii): with $R=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)\in GL^-(k;\mathbb R)$, put $P_-:=P_+R$; every frame $p=p_0A$ ($p_0\in (P_+)_m$) lies in $P_+$ if $\det A>0$ and in $P_-$ if $\det A<0$, so $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=P_+\sqcup P_-$ with both open $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-subbundles interchanged by $R$. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii): take $P_+$. (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iv): $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ has two-point fibres $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$, so is a double cover; its sections are orientations, and a double cover of a connected base has a section iff it is disconnected, iff it has two components. So all four are equivalent.
>
> *(C).* For $E=TM$, $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ is the frame bundle of $M$; an orientation of the vector bundle $TM$ is an orientation of the manifold $M$; the transition functions of $TM$ are chart Jacobians, so a $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$-reduction is an oriented atlas. Hence Parts A–B specialise to the manifold orientation theory. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting shortcut
> One is tempted to argue "$E$ is orientable because each fibre $E_m$ has two orientations, so just pick one at each point". This ignores the continuity requirement, which is the entire content of the problem: an arbitrary function $m\mapsto\mathcal O_m$ into the two-point set is *not* an orientation unless it is realised by positively oriented local frames — equivalently unless it is a section, not merely a set-map, of the double cover $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)$. The Möbius line bundle over $S^1$ ([[Ex - The Möbius Bundle is Nontrivial|the Möbius bundle]]) has a fibrewise choice at every point but no continuous one; its orientation double cover is the connected double cover $S^1\to S^1$, $z\mapsto z^2$, which has one component and no section. What would make the shortcut legal is precisely the extra hypothesis that the double cover is trivial — which is orientability itself.

# Key Takeaways

**Extra fibrewise structure on a bundle is the same thing as a reduction of its frame bundle to the stabiliser subgroup, and the recipe is always the same three checks.** The pattern proved here — orientation $\leftrightarrow$ $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction — is one instance of a uniform machine: a geometric datum on $E$ (a metric, a volume form, a complex structure, an orientation) singles out a subgroup $G\le GL(k;\mathbb R)$, namely the matrices that preserve the datum on $\mathbb R^k$; the frames "adapted" to the datum form a $G$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$; and by the closed-subgroup reduction dictionary this subbundle *is* a reduction. To run the machine one always verifies the same three things: that the adapted frames are $G$-invariant and $G$ acts transitively on them fibrewise (so each fibre is one $G$-orbit), that adapted local frames exist (this is where the datum's "continuity/smoothness" hypothesis is spent), and that the resulting local trivialisations are equivariant. Recognising a "structure = reduction" problem, and knowing these are the three obligations, collapses what looks like a bundle-theoretic construction into a short fibrewise computation plus a trivialisation. The companion drills [[Ex - Fibrewise Volume Forms Correspond to SL(k,R)-Structures|the volume-form drill]] and [[Ex - Complex Structures Correspond to GL(k,C)-Structures|the complex-structure drill]] are the same argument with $\det=1$ and with $AI_{\mathrm{st}}=I_{\mathrm{st}}A$ replacing $\det>0$.

**The obstruction to a global structure lives in the component group of the structure group, made into a covering space.** Orientation is invisible fibrewise — every fibre has exactly two orientations, always — so orientability is entirely a *global threading* question, and the object that measures it is the double cover $\widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}(E)=\operatorname{Fr}(E)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$ built from the index-$2$ quotient $GL(k;\mathbb R)/GL^+(k;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The diagnostic transferable far beyond orientation is: *when a structure's stabiliser $G$ sits inside $GL(k;\mathbb R)$ with quotient a group $\Gamma=GL(k;\mathbb R)/G$, the possible obstructions to a $G$-reduction are organised by the associated $\Gamma$-bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)/G$, and existence of the reduction is existence of a section of it.* For orientation $\Gamma=\mathbb Z/2$ and the obstruction is a single connectedness question (equivalently the first Stiefel–Whitney class $w_1(E)\in H^1(M;\mathbb Z/2)$); for $SO(k)\subset O(k)$ the same $\mathbb Z/2$ story reappears; for $O(k)\subset GL(k;\mathbb R)$ the quotient is contractible so no obstruction survives and every bundle admits a metric. When you meet a reduction problem, ask first what the quotient group is: a connected quotient means no obstruction, a disconnected one means a covering-space (characteristic-class) obstruction.

**The manifold notion of orientation is the tangent-bundle special case, and reading it that way unifies "oriented atlas", "nowhere-vanishing top form", and "$w_1=0$".** The three formulations of orientation of a smooth manifold that Differential Geometry IX proves equivalent are not three coincidences but three faces of the single statement "$\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ reduces to $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$": the oriented atlas is the $GL^+(n;\mathbb R)$-valued cocycle of coordinate frames, the nowhere-vanishing top form is a trivialisation of the associated real line bundle $\det E^*=\Lambda^k E^*$ (a section of a further associated bundle), and the vanishing of $w_1$ is the triviality of the orientation double cover. When a manifold-orientation fact is needed for a general vector bundle — the tangent bundle of an oriented surface, the normal bundle of a submanifold, the determinant line bundle in index theory — the right move is to work at the level of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and its $GL^+(k;\mathbb R)$-reduction, where all three faces are literally the same object, rather than choosing one formulation and translating. This is also the entry point to Stiefel–Whitney and Chern–Weil obstruction theory, where every "can this structure be globally chosen?" question is answered by a characteristic class of exactly this reduction-theoretic origin.
