# Notation

Throughout, $G$ is a [[Def - Lie Group|Lie group]] with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$, and $\pi : P \to M$ is a [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal $G$-bundle]] over a smooth manifold $M$. The group acts on $P$ on the **right**, $R_g(p) = p \cdot g$, freely and with $\pi$ inducing $P/G \cong M$ (the standing right-action convention of the series). We write $P_m = \pi^{-1}(m)$ for the fibre over $m \in M$; each $P_m$ is a right $G$-torsor, so once one point $p \in P_m$ is fixed, every other point of $P_m$ is $p \cdot g$ for a unique $g \in G$.

The **adjoint representation** of $G$ is $\operatorname{Ad} : G \to \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$, $\operatorname{Ad}_g = d_e(C_g)$ where $C_g(h) = g h g^{-1}$ is conjugation; for a matrix group $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$ one has $\operatorname{Ad}_g X = g X g^{-1}$ (see [[Def - Adjoint Representation]]). The **conjugation action** of $G$ on the manifold $G$ itself is written $\alpha : G \to \operatorname{Diff}(G)$, $\alpha_g(h) = g h g^{-1} = C_g(h)$; it is a left action of $G$ on the space $G$ by group automorphisms.

Given a left action $\rho : G \to \operatorname{Diff}(F)$ of $G$ on a manifold $F$, the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated bundle]] is the orbit space
$$P \times_\rho F := (P \times F)/G, \qquad (p, f) \sim (p \cdot g,\ \rho(g)^{-1} f) \ \text{ for all } g \in G,$$
with equivalence classes written $[p, f]$ and bundle projection $\pi_\rho([p, f]) = \pi(p)$. When $\rho$ is a linear representation on a vector space $V$, the bundle $P \times_\rho V$ is a [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundle]] with $[p, v] + \lambda [p, w] = [p, v + \lambda w]$ (the same $p$ used for both classes). We write $\Gamma(\cdot)$ for smooth sections, $\operatorname{End} E$ for the endomorphism bundle of a vector bundle $E$, $\operatorname{Aut} E$ for the bundle whose fibre over $m$ is $\operatorname{GL}(E_m)$, and $e \in G$ for the identity. The full symbol registry is on [[Gauge Theory III — Fibre Bundles, Principal Bundles, and Associated Bundles]].

This is a compound page: it defines two interlocking notions — the vector bundle $\operatorname{ad} P = P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$ and the bundle of groups $\operatorname{Ad} P = P \times_\alpha G$ — because they are the infinitesimal and the global halves of one object (the bundle of "symmetries of $P$ living over each point"): $\operatorname{ad} P$ is fibrewise the Lie algebra of $\operatorname{Ad} P$, and neither is fully usable in gauge theory without the other.

> [!warning] Convention: the two adjoint symbols, and the source clash
> The series follows the standard Lie-theoretic convention (Lee, and Bär): $\operatorname{Ad}_g X = g X g^{-1}$ is the **group** adjoint representation on $\mathfrak{g}$, while $\operatorname{ad}_X Y = [X, Y]$ is the **Lie algebra** adjoint. Haydys instead writes the group adjoint representation with a lowercase $\operatorname{ad}$: his "$\operatorname{ad} : G \to \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$, $\operatorname{ad}_g \xi = g \xi g^{-1}$" (p. 16) is our $\operatorname{Ad}$. To convert, read every one of Haydys's lowercase $\operatorname{ad}_g$ (with a **group** subscript $g \in G$) as $\operatorname{Ad}_g$. The **bundle** built from this representation is written $\operatorname{ad} P$ (lowercase) by both sources and by everyone else, even though it is assembled from $\operatorname{Ad}$: on this page $\operatorname{ad} P := P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$, with the uppercase representation.

> [!warning] Convention: the associated-bundle sign
> We identify $[p \cdot g,\ \rho(g)^{-1} f] = [p, f]$; equivalently $[p, \rho(g) f] = [p \cdot g^{-1}, f]$. This is the convention under which, for the conjugation action, Bär's relation $[p, a] \sim [p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} a h]$ (Bär, Remark 2.7.7) is recovered, since $\alpha_h^{-1}(a) = h^{-1} a h$; and under which $\operatorname{ad} P$'s relation reads $[p \cdot g,\ g^{-1} \xi g] = [p, \xi]$ for a matrix group. The opposite sign convention $[p \cdot g, f] \sim [p, \rho(g) f]$ appears in some texts; it replaces $\rho(g)^{-1}$ by $\rho(g)$ throughout and flips the equivariance law of section-defining functions below from $\hat{f}(p g) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \hat{f}(p)$ to $\hat{f}(p g) = \operatorname{Ad}_g \hat{f}(p)$.

# Axiom Motivation

The right way to arrive at these two bundles is to ask a single question that gauge theory forces on us: *what are the infinitesimal and finite symmetries of a principal bundle, organised so that they live over the base manifold?* A principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$ has, at each point $m$ of the base, a fibre $P_m$ on which $G$ acts freely and transitively. The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ and the group $G$ themselves are the abstract models of "infinitesimal motion" and "finite motion" in the structure group. But $\mathfrak{g}$ and $G$ are the *same* over every point; a bundle-theoretic construction must instead attach a copy of $\mathfrak{g}$ (respectively $G$) to each $m \in M$ in a way that remembers how the trivialisations of $P$ are glued. The naive product $M \times \mathfrak{g}$ (respectively $M \times G$) forgets that gluing; the correct object twists the copies together exactly as $P$ is twisted.

The desideratum is precise. We want a vector bundle whose sections are the objects that measure the difference between two connections on $P$ (the space of connections is affine, modelled on $\Omega^1$ of *some* bundle), that carry the curvature $F_A$, and that supply the "infinitesimal gauge transformations". And we want a fibre bundle of *groups* whose global sections are exactly the [[Def - Gauge Transformation|gauge transformations]] of $P$ — the automorphisms of $P$ covering the identity of $M$. Both must be genuinely bundle-theoretic, so that in a local trivialisation they reduce to $\mathfrak{g}$-valued and $G$-valued functions with the correct transition law, and both must be canonically associated to $P$ with no extra choices.

The [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated-bundle construction]] $P \times_\rho F$ is the exact tool: it manufactures a fibre bundle with fibre $F$ from $P$ and any left $G$-action $\rho$ on $F$, twisting copies of $F$ by the same cocycle that twists $P$. So the only question is *which action* to feed it. Here the per-clause failure analysis is genuinely illuminating, because the wrong action gives the wrong object.

**Why the adjoint representation for $\operatorname{ad} P$, and not the trivial action.** If we build $P \times_\rho \mathfrak{g}$ with the *trivial* action $\rho(g) = \operatorname{id}$, we get $M \times \mathfrak{g}$, a globally trivial bundle whose sections are just $\mathfrak{g}$-valued functions. This cannot be right: the difference of two connections transforms, under a change of local section $s' = s \cdot g$, by $A' - A'' = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(A - A'')$ (the inhomogeneous Maurer–Cartan term cancels in the difference), so the objects that measure "difference of connections" transform by $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$, not trivially. Only the choice $\rho = \operatorname{Ad}$ produces a bundle whose local representatives glue by $\operatorname{Ad}$, matching the transformation law of $A' - A''$ and of the curvature $F_A$. Concretely, for a nonabelian $G$ over a base that supports a nontrivial $P$ (for instance an $SU(2)$-bundle over $S^4$), $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a nontrivial vector bundle, and a section of $M \times \mathfrak{g}$ could never carry the curvature $F_A \in \Omega^2(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$.

**Why the conjugation action for $\operatorname{Ad} P$, and not left translation.** The bundle of finite symmetries must have *group* fibres, because a gauge transformation is applied fibrewise and gauge transformations compose. Feeding the associated-bundle machine the conjugation action $\alpha_g(a) = g a g^{-1}$ gives fibres that are groups (conjugation is by automorphisms, so it descends to a group structure on each fibre — proved below). If instead we fed it *left translation* $L_g(a) = g a$, we would get $P \times_L G$, which is not a bundle of groups at all: left translation does not fix the identity $e$, so there is no way to single out an identity in each fibre, and in fact $P \times_L G \cong P$ as a $G$-space (proved in the Examples). Left translation gives back the principal bundle; only conjugation gives the bundle of groups whose sections are the gauge group.

**Why $\operatorname{ad} P$ and $\operatorname{Ad} P$ are inseparable.** A gauge transformation $\psi$ can be written $\psi(p) = p \cdot \hat{f}(p)$ for a function $\hat{f} : P \to G$; the automorphism condition $\psi(p g) = \psi(p) g$ forces the equivariance $\hat{f}(p g) = g^{-1} \hat{f}(p) g = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \hat{f}(p)$, which is exactly the condition making $\hat{f}$ a section of $\operatorname{Ad} P = P \times_\alpha G$. Differentiating this at a one-parameter family of gauge transformations through the identity produces functions valued in $\mathfrak{g}$ with the equivariance $\xi(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi(p)$ — sections of $\operatorname{ad} P$. So $\operatorname{ad} P$ is literally the linearisation of $\operatorname{Ad} P$ at its identity section: the Lie algebra bundle of the group bundle. To motivate one without the other would misrepresent both.

Could a reader invent these from the analysis above? The construction $P \times_\rho F$ being given, yes: the requirement that difference-of-connections transform by $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$ dictates $\rho = \operatorname{Ad}$ for the vector bundle, and the requirement that gauge transformations be *fibrewise group* objects with an identity dictates $\rho = \alpha$ (conjugation) for the group bundle, with left translation excluded because it destroys the identity.

# The Definition

Let $\pi : P \to M$ be a [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal $G$-bundle]] with structure group $G$ and Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$.

**The adjoint bundle $\operatorname{ad} P$.** The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is a $G$-representation via the [[Def - Adjoint Representation|adjoint representation]] $\operatorname{Ad} : G \to \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$. The **adjoint bundle** is the associated vector bundle
$$\operatorname{ad} P := P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g} = (P \times \mathfrak{g})/G, \qquad [p \cdot g,\ \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi] = [p, \xi],$$
a vector bundle over $M$ of rank $\dim \mathfrak{g}$, with fibrewise vector-space operations $[p, \xi] + \lambda [p, \eta] = [p, \xi + \lambda \eta]$. It moreover carries a fibrewise Lie bracket, $[[p, \xi], [p, \eta]] := [p, [\xi, \eta]]$, which is well defined because $\operatorname{Ad}_g$ is a Lie algebra automorphism (so $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}[\xi, \eta] = [\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi, \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \eta]$), making $\operatorname{ad} P$ a **bundle of Lie algebras**.

For a matrix structure group $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$ with associated vector bundle $E := P \times_{\mathrm{std}} \mathbb{R}^k$ (the standard representation), $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a subbundle of $\operatorname{End} E$, namely the subbundle whose fibre over $m$ consists of those endomorphisms of $E_m$ that lie in the image of $\mathfrak{g}$; this is proved in the verification below.

**The group bundle $\operatorname{Ad} P$.** The group $G$ acts on the manifold $G$ on the left by conjugation, $\alpha_g(a) = g a g^{-1}$. The **conjugation bundle** (or **adjoint group bundle**) is the associated fibre bundle
$$\operatorname{Ad} P := P \times_\alpha G = (P \times G)/G, \qquad [p \cdot g,\ g^{-1} a g] = [p, a],$$
with fibre the manifold $G$. Its fibres carry a group structure: the fibrewise multiplication
$$[p, a] \cdot [p, b] := [p, a b]$$
is well defined (proved below), makes each fibre $(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ a group isomorphic to $G$, and varies smoothly, so $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is a **bundle of groups** (a smooth fibre bundle each of whose fibres is a Lie group and whose fibrewise multiplication, inversion, and identity are smooth). It has the canonical **global identity section** $m \mapsto [p, e]$ (for any $p \in P_m$; independent of $p$, proved below), it is **not** a principal $G$-bundle in general (proved below), and its fibrewise Lie algebra bundle is $\operatorname{ad} P$ (proved below).

We now verify, clause by clause, every property asserted above.

> [!note]- Verification 1: $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a vector bundle, and for $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k$ it is a subbundle of $\operatorname{End} E$
> **What must be shown.** First, that $\operatorname{ad} P = P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$ is a smooth vector bundle of rank $\dim \mathfrak{g}$. Second, that for $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$ with $E = P \times_{\mathrm{std}} \mathbb{R}^k$, the natural map $\operatorname{ad} P \to \operatorname{End} E$ is a fibrewise-injective vector bundle morphism, so its image is a subbundle isomorphic to $\operatorname{ad} P$.
>
> **Step 1: $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a vector bundle.** Since $\operatorname{Ad} : G \to \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$ is a linear representation on the finite-dimensional vector space $\mathfrak{g}$, the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated-bundle construction]] applied to a linear action yields a vector bundle: over any open $U \subseteq M$ on which $P$ trivialises by a local section $s : U \to P$, the map
> $$U \times \mathfrak{g} \to (\operatorname{ad} P)|_U, \qquad (m, \xi) \mapsto [s(m), \xi]$$
> is a diffeomorphism onto $(\operatorname{ad} P)|_U$ that is linear in $\xi$ on each fibre (by the definition $[p, \xi] + \lambda[p, \eta] = [p, \xi + \lambda\eta]$), hence a local trivialisation as a vector bundle (by the associated-bundle theorem, [[Def - Associated Bundle]]). On an overlap $U \cap U'$ with $s' = s \cdot g$ for a transition $g : U \cap U' \to G$, the two trivialisations are related by $[s'(m), \xi] = [s(m) \cdot g(m),\ \xi] = [s(m),\ \operatorname{Ad}_{g(m)} \xi]$ (by the equivalence relation, since $[p \cdot g, \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \eta] = [p, \eta]$ with $\eta = \operatorname{Ad}_g \xi$), so the vector-bundle transition function is $m \mapsto \operatorname{Ad}_{g(m)} \in \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$, smooth because $\operatorname{Ad}$ and $g$ are smooth. Therefore $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a smooth vector bundle of rank $\dim \mathfrak{g}$.
>
> **Step 2: the map into $\operatorname{End} E$.** Recall $E = P \times_{\mathrm{std}} \mathbb{R}^k$, so $E_m = \{[p, v] : v \in \mathbb{R}^k\}$ for any fixed $p \in P_m$, and $\operatorname{End} E = P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ where $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k$ acts on $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k) = \mathfrak{gl}_k$ by $g \cdot X = g X g^{-1} = \operatorname{Ad}_g X$ (this is exactly how the induced connection and the bundle of endomorphisms of an associated vector bundle are built; the class $[p, X]$ acts on $E_m$ by $[p, X] \cdot [p, v] = [p, X v]$, which is well defined since $[p g, g^{-1} X g] \cdot [p g, g^{-1} v] = [p g, g^{-1} X g \cdot g^{-1} v] = [p g, g^{-1} X v] = [p, X v]$). Define
> $$\iota : \operatorname{ad} P \to \operatorname{End} E, \qquad \iota([p, \xi]) = [p, \xi],$$
> reading $\xi \in \mathfrak{g} \subseteq \mathfrak{gl}_k = \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ on the right-hand side. This is well defined: if $[p, \xi] = [p g, \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi]$ in $\operatorname{ad} P$, then since $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi = g^{-1} \xi g$ is the same element whether computed in $\mathfrak{g}$ or in $\mathfrak{gl}_k$ (the inclusion $\mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{gl}_k$ intertwines $\operatorname{Ad}$ of $G$ with conjugation in $\mathfrak{gl}_k$, because $G \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_k$), the image classes agree in $\operatorname{End} E$. The map $\iota$ is linear on each fibre and covers $\operatorname{id}_M$.
>
> **Step 3: $\iota$ is fibrewise injective, hence its image is a subbundle.** On the fibre over $m$, fix $p \in P_m$; then $\iota$ reads as the fixed linear inclusion $\mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{gl}_k$, $\xi \mapsto \xi$, which is injective because $\mathfrak{g}$ is a subspace of $\mathfrak{gl}_k$ (the Lie algebra of a matrix subgroup sits inside $\mathfrak{gl}_k$). A vector bundle morphism of constant rank $\dim \mathfrak{g}$ has image a subbundle (its local trivialisations restrict to those of $\operatorname{ad} P$ under the trivialising sections $s$, since in the frame from $s$ the image is the constant subspace $\mathfrak{g} \subseteq \mathfrak{gl}_k$, which is $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant, so the subspaces glue). Therefore $\operatorname{ad} P \cong \iota(\operatorname{ad} P) \subseteq \operatorname{End} E$ is a subbundle. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Verification 2: fibrewise multiplication on $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is well defined, and each fibre is a group isomorphic to $G$
> **What must be shown.** That $[p, a] \cdot [p, b] := [p, a b]$ does not depend on the common representative $p \in P_m$ chosen for the two classes; that with this product each fibre $(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ is a group; and that it is isomorphic to $G$.
>
> **Step 0: two classes over $m$ share a representative.** Given $[p_1, a], [p_2, b] \in (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$, we have $p_1, p_2 \in P_m$, so $p_2 = p_1 \cdot h$ for a unique $h \in G$ (the fibre is a right $G$-torsor). Then $[p_2, b] = [p_1 \cdot h,\ b] = [p_1,\ h b h^{-1}]$ (by the equivalence relation $[p \cdot h, h^{-1} c h] = [p, c]$ read with $c = h b h^{-1}$, since $h^{-1}(h b h^{-1}) h = b$). Hence both classes can be written over the single representative $p := p_1$, as $[p, a]$ and $[p, h b h^{-1}]$. So the product below is defined for every pair.
>
> **Step 1: independence of the representative.** Replace $p$ by $p \cdot h$ for arbitrary $h \in G$; the same two classes become $[p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} a h]$ and $[p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} b h]$ (by the equivalence relation). Compute the product with this representative:
> $$[p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} a h] \cdot [p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} b h] = [p \cdot h,\ (h^{-1} a h)(h^{-1} b h)] \qquad \text{(definition of the fibrewise product)}$$
> $$= [p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} a b h] \qquad \text{(since } (h^{-1} a h)(h^{-1} b h) = h^{-1} a (h h^{-1}) b h = h^{-1} (a b) h\text{)}$$
> $$= [p,\ a b] \qquad \text{(by the equivalence relation } [p \cdot h,\ h^{-1} c h] = [p, c] \text{ with } c = a b\text{).}$$
> This equals $[p, a] \cdot [p, b] = [p, a b]$ computed with the representative $p$. Therefore the product is independent of the representative and $\cdot$ is well defined on the fibre.
>
> **Step 2: the fibre is a group.** With the representative $p \in P_m$ fixed, the map $\varphi_p : G \to (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$, $a \mapsto [p, a]$, is a bijection (every class over $m$ has a unique representative over $p$, again because $P_m$ is a torsor). By the definition of the product, $\varphi_p(a) \cdot \varphi_p(b) = [p, a][p, b] = [p, ab] = \varphi_p(ab)$, so $\varphi_p$ is a group homomorphism from $(G, \cdot)$ onto the fibre with the operation $\cdot$; being a bijective homomorphism from a group, it transports the group axioms, so $(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ is a group, with identity $\varphi_p(e) = [p, e]$ and inverses $[p, a]^{-1} = [p, a^{-1}]$. In particular $\varphi_p$ is a **group isomorphism** $G \cong (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$.
>
> **Step 3: smoothness of the fibrewise operations.** In a local trivialisation from a section $s : U \to P$, the fibre coordinate is $\varphi_{s(m)}^{-1} : (\operatorname{Ad} P)|_U \to U \times G$, and the fibrewise product reads $(m, a)(m, b) = (m, ab)$, which is smooth because multiplication in the Lie group $G$ is smooth; likewise inversion $(m, a) \mapsto (m, a^{-1})$ and the identity section $m \mapsto (m, e)$ are smooth. Therefore $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is a smooth bundle of groups with typical fibre $G$. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Verification 3: the global identity section and the failure to be principal
> **The global section.** Define $s_e : M \to \operatorname{Ad} P$ by $s_e(m) = [p, e]$ for any $p \in P_m$. This is well defined (independent of $p$): if $p' = p \cdot h$ then $[p', e] = [p \cdot h,\ e] = [p,\ h e h^{-1}] = [p, e]$ (by the equivalence relation, since $h e h^{-1} = e$). It is smooth: in a local trivialisation from $s : U \to P$ it reads $m \mapsto (m, e)$. And $s_e(m)$ is the identity of the fibre group $(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ by Verification 2, Step 2. So $\operatorname{Ad} P$ has a canonical global identity section, corresponding under the isomorphism $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P) \cong \mathcal{G}(P)$ below to the identity gauge transformation $\operatorname{id}_P$.
>
> **Why $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is not a principal $G$-bundle in general.** A principal $G$-bundle is a fibre bundle with fibre the *space* $G$ carrying a free, fibre-transitive right $G$-action whose transition functions act by *left translation* $L_g$. The bundle $\operatorname{Ad} P$ has fibre the space $G$, but its transition functions act by *conjugation* $\alpha_g = C_g$, not by left translation, and conjugation fixes the identity $e$ while a nontrivial left translation moves it. Concretely: the existence of the canonical global section $s_e$ obstructs principality unless the bundle is trivial, because a principal bundle admits a global section if and only if it is trivial (a proved fact, [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]]: a principal $G$-bundle is trivial iff it has a smooth global section). More structurally, the structure group of $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is $\operatorname{Inn}(G) \subseteq \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ (inner automorphisms), acting on the fibre $G$ preserving its group structure; a $G$-principal bundle instead has structure group $G$ acting by left translation, which does not preserve the group structure of the fibre. For nonabelian $G$ and a $P$ with nontrivial holonomy of its conjugation cocycle these are genuinely different, so $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is a bundle of groups but not a principal bundle. (When $G$ is abelian, conjugation is trivial and $\operatorname{Ad} P = M \times G$ is trivial as both a group bundle and — vacuously — anything else; see the Examples.) $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Verification 4: the fibrewise Lie algebra of $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is $\operatorname{ad} P$
> **What must be shown.** That the vector bundle whose fibre over $m$ is the Lie algebra $T_{s_e(m)}(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ of the fibre group $(\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$, assembled over $m$, is canonically isomorphic to $\operatorname{ad} P$ as a bundle of Lie algebras.
>
> **Step 1: the fibrewise Lie algebra.** By Verification 2, for fixed $p \in P_m$ the map $\varphi_p : G \to (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$ is a group isomorphism, hence a Lie group isomorphism (it is the restriction of the smooth trivialisation), so its differential at $e$,
> $$d_e \varphi_p : \mathfrak{g} = T_e G \ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ T_{[p,e]} (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m =: \operatorname{Lie}\big((\operatorname{Ad} P)_m\big),$$
> is a Lie algebra isomorphism onto the Lie algebra of the fibre group. This identifies $\operatorname{Lie}((\operatorname{Ad} P)_m)$ with $\mathfrak{g}$, but the identification depends on the choice of $p$.
>
> **Step 2: how the identification changes with $p$.** Replace $p$ by $p \cdot h$. Then $\varphi_{p h}(a) = [p h, a] = [p,\ h a h^{-1}] = \varphi_p(\alpha_h(a)) = \varphi_p(C_h(a))$ (by the equivalence relation and the definition $\alpha_h = C_h$). Hence $\varphi_{p h} = \varphi_p \circ C_h$ as maps $G \to (\operatorname{Ad} P)_m$, and differentiating at $e$,
> $$d_e \varphi_{p h} = d_e \varphi_p \circ d_e C_h = d_e \varphi_p \circ \operatorname{Ad}_h \qquad \text{(by } \operatorname{Ad}_h = d_e C_h\text{, the definition of the adjoint representation).}$$
> Therefore the coordinate of a fixed tangent vector $X \in \operatorname{Lie}((\operatorname{Ad} P)_m)$ transforms, under $p \mapsto p h$, by $(d_e \varphi_{ph})^{-1} X = \operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}} (d_e \varphi_p)^{-1} X$; that is, its $\mathfrak{g}$-representative $\xi = (d_e\varphi_p)^{-1}X$ over $p$ becomes $\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}} \xi$ over $p h$.
>
> **Step 3: this is exactly $\operatorname{ad} P$.** The transformation law "$\xi$ over $p$ becomes $\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}} \xi$ over $p \cdot h$" is precisely the equivalence relation of $\operatorname{ad} P = P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$, namely $[p, \xi] = [p h, \operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}} \xi]$. So the assignment $\operatorname{Lie}((\operatorname{Ad} P)_m) \ni X \mapsto [p, (d_e\varphi_p)^{-1} X] \in (\operatorname{ad} P)_m$ is independent of $p$ and is a linear isomorphism; it is a Lie algebra isomorphism because each $d_e \varphi_p$ is one and the fibrewise bracket on $\operatorname{ad} P$ was defined as $[[p, \xi], [p, \eta]] = [p, [\xi, \eta]]$. Assembling over $m$ gives a canonical isomorphism of Lie algebra bundles between the fibrewise Lie algebra bundle of $\operatorname{Ad} P$ and $\operatorname{ad} P$. Therefore $\operatorname{ad} P$ is the Lie algebra bundle of the group bundle $\operatorname{Ad} P$. $\blacksquare$

# Categorical / Structural Definition

Both bundles are instances of a single functorial construction, and naming that structure clarifies what is canonical about them. Fix the principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$. The [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated-bundle construction]] is a functor from the category of smooth left $G$-manifolds (objects: manifolds $F$ with a smooth left $G$-action; morphisms: $G$-equivariant smooth maps) to the category of fibre bundles over $M$:
$$P \times_G (-) : \ G\text{-}\mathbf{Man} \ \longrightarrow \ \mathbf{Bun}_M, \qquad F \ \longmapsto \ P \times_G F, \qquad (\phi : F \to F') \ \longmapsto \ \big([p, f] \mapsto [p, \phi(f)]\big),$$
where the induced map is well defined precisely because $\phi$ is $G$-equivariant ($\phi(\rho(g) f) = \rho'(g) \phi(f)$ makes $[p g, \rho(g)^{-1} f] \mapsto [p g, \rho'(g)^{-1}\phi(f)]$ consistent). This functor preserves the algebraic structure carried by $F$: a $G$-equivariant *product* on $F$ (a $G$-equivariant map $F \times F \to F$, using $P \times_G(F \times F) \cong (P \times_G F) \times_M (P \times_G F)$) induces a fibrewise product on $P \times_G F$; the same for units and inverses.

Under this reading:

- $\operatorname{ad} P = P \times_G \mathfrak{g}$ is the image of the object $\mathfrak{g}$ with its adjoint $G$-action. Because $\mathfrak{g}$ is a *vector space* with $G$ acting linearly and the bracket $[\cdot, \cdot] : \mathfrak{g} \times \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is $G$-equivariant (as $\operatorname{Ad}_g$ is a Lie algebra automorphism), the functor sends this to a **bundle of Lie algebras** — a Lie algebra object internal to $\mathbf{Bun}_M$.
- $\operatorname{Ad} P = P \times_G G_\alpha$ is the image of the object $G$ *acting on itself by conjugation* (written $G_\alpha$ to distinguish it from the fibre-forgetting principal action). Conjugation acts by group automorphisms, so multiplication $G \times G \to G$, inversion $G \to G$, and the unit $\{\ast\} \to G$ are all $G$-equivariant maps; the functor therefore sends $G_\alpha$ to a **group object in $\mathbf{Bun}_M$** — a bundle of groups — with a canonical unit section (the image of the equivariant unit map). This is the structural reason $\operatorname{Ad} P$ automatically has the global identity section and $\operatorname{ad} P$ automatically is its Lie algebra bundle: the Lie functor $\operatorname{Lie} : \mathbf{LieGrp} \to \mathbf{LieAlg}$ is $G$-equivariant for the conjugation-versus-adjoint actions (that equivariance is the identity $\operatorname{Ad}_g = d_e C_g$), so it commutes with $P \times_G (-)$.

Contrast $P \times_G G_L$, the image of $G$ acting on itself by *left translation* $L$. Left translation is free and transitive but not by automorphisms, so this object has no equivariant unit and no equivariant multiplication; the functor produces not a group bundle but a bundle with a free fibre-transitive residual $G$-action — which is $P$ itself, as shown in the Examples. The single functor $P \times_G(-)$ thus manufactures the vector bundle, the group bundle, and (from the wrong action) the principal bundle back again, according to what algebraic structure the input $G$-manifold carries.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Operationally, $\operatorname{ad} P$ is *the bundle in which the curvature and the difference of connections live*, and $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is *the bundle whose sections are the gauge transformations*. This is more useful than the official "associated bundle for the adjoint / conjugation action", because it tells you where each appears: the curvature of a principal connection is $F_A \in \Omega^2(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$; the space of connections $\mathcal{A}(P)$ is an affine space modelled on $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ (Haydys, Theorem 41); the [[Def - Gauge Transformation|gauge group]] $\mathcal{G}(P)$ is $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ (Haydys eq. 57–58; Bär, Remark 2.7.7); and infinitesimal gauge transformations are $\Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P) = \operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G}(P))$.

The construction compresses several apparently different objects into one pattern. In the theory of vector bundles, when $P = \operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is the [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]] of a rank-$k$ vector bundle $E$, the adjoint bundle becomes the endomorphism bundle $\operatorname{ad} \operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{End} E$ and the conjugation bundle becomes the automorphism bundle $\operatorname{Ad} \operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{Aut} E$ (both verified in the Examples). So "curvature is an $\operatorname{End} E$-valued two-form" for vector bundles and "curvature is an $\operatorname{ad} P$-valued two-form" for principal bundles are the *same* statement, read through $E = \operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_{\operatorname{GL}_k} \mathbb{R}^k$ and [[Thm - Vector Bundles are Associated to Their Frame Bundles]].

In Lie theory, $\operatorname{ad} P$ and $\operatorname{Ad} P$ globalise the pair $(\mathfrak{g}, G)$ with the Lie functor relating them; the fibrewise statement "the Lie algebra of $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is $\operatorname{ad} P$" is the bundle version of "$\operatorname{Lie}(G) = \mathfrak{g}$ with $\operatorname{Ad} = d_e C$". In physics, $\operatorname{ad} P$ is the bundle in which the *gauge field strength* takes values and in which *matter in the adjoint representation* (for instance the gluon field, or a scalar Higgs field in the adjoint) lives, while $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ is the group of *local gauge symmetries* of a Yang–Mills theory. When $G = U(1)$ (electromagnetism) both collapse: $\operatorname{ad} P = M \times i\mathbb{R}$ and $\operatorname{Ad} P = M \times U(1)$, recovering the fact that the electromagnetic gauge group is just $C^\infty(M; U(1))$ and the field strength is an ordinary imaginary-valued two-form.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — abelian $G$: $\operatorname{ad} P = M \times \mathfrak{g}$ and $\operatorname{Ad} P = M \times G$.** Suppose $G$ is abelian. Then $g a g^{-1} = a$ for all $a$, so $\alpha_g = \operatorname{id}_G$ and, differentiating, $\operatorname{Ad}_g = \operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$ for all $g$. The equivalence relation of $\operatorname{ad} P$ becomes $[p g, \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \xi] = [p g, \xi] = [p, \xi]$, so $[p, \xi]$ depends on $p$ only through $\pi(p) = m$; the map $\operatorname{ad} P \to M \times \mathfrak{g}$, $[p, \xi] \mapsto (\pi(p), \xi)$, is therefore well defined, and it is a vector bundle isomorphism (bijective: inverse $(m, \xi) \mapsto [p, \xi]$ for any $p \in P_m$, well defined by the same computation; fibrewise linear). The same computation with $\alpha$ trivial gives $\operatorname{Ad} P \cong M \times G$ as bundles of groups, with $[p, a] \mapsto (\pi(p), a)$ a fibrewise group isomorphism. This is verified clause by clause: well-definedness used $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} = \operatorname{id}$ (abelianness); linearity used the fibre operations of $\operatorname{ad} P$; the group-isomorphism used $[p,a][p,b]=[p,ab] \mapsto (m,ab)$. For instance with $G = U(1)$ and $P = S^3 \to \mathbb{C}P^1$ the Hopf bundle, $\operatorname{ad} P = \mathbb{C}P^1 \times i\mathbb{R}$ is trivial even though $P$ is not. (Recorded as [[Ex - The Adjoint Bundle of an Abelian Principal Bundle is Trivial]].)

**Is an instance — the frame bundle: $\operatorname{ad} \operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{End} E$ and $\operatorname{Ad} \operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{Aut} E$.** Let $E \to M$ be a real rank-$k$ vector bundle and $P = \operatorname{Fr}(E)$ its [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]], a principal $\operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundle, with $E = \operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{std}} \mathbb{R}^k$ ([[Thm - Vector Bundles are Associated to Their Frame Bundles]]). Here $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{gl}_k(\mathbb{R}) = \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ and $\operatorname{Ad}_g X = g X g^{-1}$. By Verification 1, $\operatorname{ad}\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$, and since $\operatorname{End} E = \operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ with the identical action $g \cdot X = g X g^{-1}$, the map $\iota$ of Verification 1 is here an *isomorphism* (not merely an inclusion), because $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{gl}_k = \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ is the *whole* space, not a proper subspace. Hence $\operatorname{ad}\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{End} E$. For the group bundle, $G = \operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$ acting on itself by conjugation gives $\operatorname{Ad}\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{Fr}(E) \times_\alpha \operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb{R})$, whose fibre over $m$ is the group of invertible elements $\operatorname{GL}(E_m)$ — this is $\operatorname{Aut} E$, the bundle whose sections are the vector bundle automorphisms of $E$ covering $\operatorname{id}_M$; the fibrewise product $[p, a][p, b] = [p, ab]$ matches composition of automorphisms $[p,a] \cdot [p,v] = [p, av]$. So $\operatorname{Ad}\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \operatorname{Aut} E$, and $\Gamma(\operatorname{Aut} E) = \mathcal{G}(E)$ is the gauge group of $E$ in the sense of the series conventions. (Compare [[Ex - End(E) as an Associated Bundle of the Frame Bundle]].)

**Is NOT an instance — left translation gives $P$, not a group bundle.** Let $G$ act on the manifold $G$ by *left* translation $L_g(a) = g a$, and form $P \times_L G = (P \times G)/G$ with $[p g,\ g^{-1} a] = [p, a]$. This is *not* a bundle of groups; instead it is $P$ itself. Define $\Phi : P \times_L G \to P$, $\Phi([p, a]) = p \cdot a$. It is well defined: $\Phi([p g,\ g^{-1} a]) = (p g)(g^{-1} a) = p a = \Phi([p, a])$ (using associativity of the right action and $g g^{-1} = e$). It is a bijection: the inverse is $q \mapsto [q, e]$, since $\Phi([q, e]) = q \cdot e = q$, and $[\,p a,\, e\,] = [\,p,\ a\,]$ (from $[p g, g^{-1} a] = [p, a]$ with $g = a$ gives $[p a, e] = [p a, a^{-1} a] = [p, a]$). It is a diffeomorphism over $M$ (smooth with smooth inverse in local trivialisations, where it reads $(m, a) \mapsto s(m) \cdot a$). There is no fibrewise group structure to preserve: attempting $[p, a][p, b] := [p, ab]$ fails to be well defined, because under $p \mapsto p g$ the classes become $[pg, g^{-1}a]$, $[pg, g^{-1}b]$ and the candidate product $[pg, g^{-1}a g^{-1} b]$ is *not* equal to $[pg, g^{-1}(ab)] = [p, ab]$ unless $g^{-1} a g^{-1} b = g^{-1} a b$, i.e. unless $g^{-1} = e$; so the left-translation "product" depends on the representative and is no operation at all. The conclusion is exactly the per-clause failure predicted in the Axiom Motivation: conjugation, which fixes $e$ and acts by automorphisms, gives a group bundle; left translation, which moves $e$, gives back the principal bundle. This is the reason $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is built from $\alpha$, not $L$.

**Corollary — $\operatorname{ad} P$ carries the curvature and models the space of connections.** Because $\operatorname{ad} P$ is a vector bundle whose local representatives glue by $\operatorname{Ad}$, and because the difference of two principal connections and the curvature both transform by $\operatorname{Ad}$ under change of local section, the curvature of a principal connection is a well-defined element $F_A \in \Omega^2(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ and the affine space of connections $\mathcal{A}(P)$ is modelled on the vector space $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ (Haydys, Theorem 41). We do not reprove those statements here; they belong to the connection chapter, and each uses $\operatorname{ad} P$ exactly as defined above.

**Calibration check.** Three verifications the reader can carry out from this page alone. First, that $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ is a group under pointwise multiplication: for sections $s, t \in \Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ define $(s \cdot t)(m) := s(m) \cdot t(m)$ using the fibrewise product of Verification 2; this is a smooth section because the bundle multiplication is smooth (Verification 2, Step 3) and $s, t$ are smooth; the identity is the global section $s_e : m \mapsto [p, e]$ of Verification 3; the inverse of $s$ is $s^{-1}(m) := s(m)^{-1} = [p, a(m)^{-1}]$, smooth because inversion in $\operatorname{Ad} P$ is smooth; associativity holds pointwise because each fibre is a group. Hence $\big(\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P), \cdot\big)$ is a group, and it is exactly the [[Def - Gauge Transformation|gauge group]] $\mathcal{G}(P)$ under Haydys eq. (57)–(58) and Bär Remark 2.7.7. Second, that in Haydys's equivariance law (58) — printed in the source as $\hat{f}(pg) = g^{-1} p g$, a typo — the corrected law is $\hat{f}(pg) = g^{-1}\hat{f}(p) g = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat{f}(p)$, which is precisely the condition making $\hat{f} : P \to G$ descend to a section of $\operatorname{Ad} P = P \times_\alpha G$; check the algebra: $\psi(pg) = \psi(p) g$ and $\psi(q) = q \hat{f}(q)$ give $pg\,\hat{f}(pg) = \psi(pg) = \psi(p) g = p \hat{f}(p) g$, so $\hat{f}(pg) = g^{-1}\hat{f}(p) g$. Third, that for abelian $G$ the count is consistent: $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P) = \Gamma(M \times G) = C^\infty(M; G)$, matching Haydys Example 60 and Bär Example 2.7.5.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The gauge group as $\Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ *(from Gauge Theory V)*
> The identification $\mathcal{G}(P) \cong \Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ turns the gauge group — a priori an infinite-dimensional group of bundle automorphisms — into the sections of a finite-rank fibre bundle of groups, so that its Lie algebra is $\Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ and its action on connections and curvature can be computed fibrewise. See [[Def - Gauge Transformation]].

> [!tip] The space of connections is affine over $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ *(from Gauge Theory IV)*
> Because two principal connections differ by an $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant horizontal $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form, and such forms are exactly $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ (via [[Thm - Bundle-Valued Forms on the Base are Basic Equivariant Forms on the Total Space]]), the curvature lands in $\Omega^2(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ and Yang–Mills theory is analysis on the affine space $\mathcal{A}(P)$.

> [!tip] The Bianchi identity lives on $\operatorname{ad} P$ *(from Gauge Theory IV)*
> The covariant exterior derivative on $\operatorname{ad} P$-valued forms, induced by a connection, satisfies $d^A F_A = 0$; this is the invariant home of the local identity $dF_A + [A \wedge F_A] = 0$, and it makes sense only because $\operatorname{ad} P$ is the bundle in which $F_A$ takes values.
