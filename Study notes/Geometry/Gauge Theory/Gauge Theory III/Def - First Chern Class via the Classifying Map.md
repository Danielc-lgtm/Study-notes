# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth, Hausdorff, second-countable, **compact** manifold, and all bundles are smooth. A **complex line bundle** $L \to M$ is a complex vector bundle of rank one; a **Hermitian line bundle** is such an $L$ together with a smoothly varying Hermitian inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on its fibres (see [[Def - Complex Vector Bundle and Hermitian Structure]]). A **principal $U(1)$-bundle** $P \to M$ is a principal bundle for the circle group $U(1) = \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : |\lambda| = 1\}$, with $U(1)$ acting on the right. We write $\underline{\mathbb{C}} = M \times \mathbb{C}$ for the trivial line bundle and $L^{\vee} = \operatorname{Hom}(L; \mathbb{C})$ for the dual line bundle.

Complex projective space $\mathbb{CP}^N = (\mathbb{C}^{N+1} \setminus \{0\})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ is the space of complex lines through the origin in $\mathbb{C}^{N+1}$ (see [[Def - Complex Projective Space as a Quotient]]); a point is written $[z] = [z_0 : \cdots : z_N]$. The **tautological line bundle** over it is
$$\mathcal{O}(-1) = \big\{([z], v) \in \mathbb{CP}^N \times \mathbb{C}^{N+1} : v \in \mathbb{C}\cdot z\big\} \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } \mathbb{CP}^N, \qquad \pi([z], v) = [z],$$
whose fibre over the line $[z]$ is that line itself. We write $\iota_N : \mathbb{CP}^N \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{N+1}$, $[z_0 : \cdots : z_N] \mapsto [z_0 : \cdots : z_N : 0]$ for the standard linear inclusion, and $\iota_1 : \mathbb{CP}^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^N$ for the standard $\mathbb{CP}^1 = \{[z_0 : z_1 : 0 : \cdots : 0]\}$.

We write $H^k_{dR}(X)$ for the [[Def - de Rham Cohomology|de Rham cohomology]] of $X$ with real coefficients, $[\alpha] \in H^k_{dR}(X)$ for the class of a closed form $\alpha$, and $f^* : H^k_{dR}(X) \to H^k_{dR}(Y)$ for the map induced by a smooth $f : Y \to X$. The symbol $w(g) \in \mathbb{Z}$ denotes the **winding number** of a smooth map $g : S^1 \to U(1)$, the integer counting the net number of times $g$ wraps the circle (see [[Thm - Winding Number of a Map from the Circle to U(1)]]). We write $\operatorname{Pic}(M)$ for the set of isomorphism classes of complex line bundles over $M$, a group under tensor product with unit $[\underline{\mathbb{C}}]$ and inverse $[L]^{-1} = [L^{\vee}]$.

This is a compound page: it defines three interlocking notions — the topological first Chern class $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P)$ of a $U(1)$-bundle as the pulled-back de Rham generator, the degree $\deg(P)$ of a line bundle over a closed oriented surface as a clutching winding number, and (as a scope remark) the topological second Chern number of an $SU(2)$-bundle — because all three are the same idea, *a bundle is a homotopy class of maps into a classifying space, and a characteristic number is what that homotopy class pairs against a fixed cohomology generator*, realised in the three cases $U(1)$, $U(1)$ over a surface, and $SU(2)$.

> [!warning] Convention: sign of the first Chern class
> Following Haydys, the first Chern class carries a **minus sign**: $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P) := -f^*[\omega_N]$, where $f$ is a classifying map and $[\omega_N]$ is the positively normalised generator (below). The minus sign is a pure convention, chosen so that $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ agrees with the Chern–Weil class $\big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F_{\nabla}\big]$ built from the curvature of a unitary connection (established on [[Thm - First Chern Class of a Line Bundle from Curvature]]). With it, the tautological bundle satisfies $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -[\omega_N]$ and $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1$, matching the series' orientation and sign ledger. An author who drops the minus sign obtains $-c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$; to convert, negate.

> [!warning] Convention: de Rham coefficients, not integral cohomology
> Haydys defines $c_1(P) := -f^*a$ with $a \in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty; \mathbb{Z})$ a generator of *integral singular* cohomology and $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ a map into the infinite classifying space. This series has singular cohomology available only from chapter XII onward, so until then it works with **de Rham cohomology** and with a **finite** classifying space: $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P) := -f^*[\omega_N] \in H^2_{dR}(M)$ for a smooth $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$, $N < \infty$. The two definitions agree under the de Rham theorem (the class $a$ maps to $[\omega_N]$ under $H^2(\cdot;\mathbb{Z}) \to H^2_{dR}(\cdot)$); the integrality of the resulting real class — that $\int_\Sigma c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every closed oriented surface $\Sigma$ — is a theorem of this series, not an assumption, proved via the clutching degree on [[Thm - Classification of Principal U(1)-Bundles by the First Chern Class]].

# Axiom Motivation

We want a single number, or a single cohomology class, that tells a Hermitian line bundle apart from the trivial one and, more ambitiously, that classifies line bundles completely. The problem is that a line bundle is a slippery object: it is an equivalence class of gluing data, and the gluing data can be changed by an isomorphism without changing the bundle. Any invariant we propose must be blind to that change. The design question is therefore not "what quantity can we extract from $L$?" — there are many — but "what quantity can we extract from $L$ that depends on $L$ only through its isomorphism class?"

The classifying map supplies the answer, and it is worth seeing why it is forced rather than chosen. Every complex line bundle over a compact manifold is a pullback of one universal bundle: there is a smooth map $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ (into some finite projective space) with $f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong L$, and this map is unique up to homotopy once we are allowed to enlarge $N$ (this is the content of [[Thm - Line Bundles over Compact Manifolds are Pulled Back from Projective Space]]). So the entire isomorphism-class information of $L$ is repackaged as a homotopy class of maps $[f] \in [M; \mathbb{CP}^N]$. The homotopy class is exactly the invariant we asked for: it forgets the individual gluing data and remembers only the bundle. The trouble is that a homotopy class of maps is hard to compute with. The remedy, standard throughout algebraic topology, is to pair the homotopy class against a fixed cohomology class on the target: since $H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N) \cong \mathbb{R}$ is one-dimensional (this is [[Thm - The de Rham Cohomology of Complex Projective Space]]), a single generator $[\omega_N]$ turns the intractable class $[f]$ into a concrete cohomology class $f^*[\omega_N] \in H^2_{dR}(M)$. The first Chern class is this pulled-back generator (up to the sign convention).

Now the desideratum that shapes every clause of the definition: **the class $f^*[\omega_N]$ must not depend on any of the choices made to write it down.** Three choices were made — the classifying map $f$ within its homotopy class, the projective space $\mathbb{CP}^N$ that $f$ lands in, and (for a line bundle, as opposed to a $U(1)$-bundle) the Hermitian structure used to reduce $L$ to a principal $U(1)$-bundle. Each choice is neutralised by exactly one input, and dropping that input breaks the definition:

Suppose we **drop homotopy invariance of de Rham cohomology** and try to define $c_1$ from a single representative $f$ of its homotopy class. Then two homotopic classifying maps $f_0 \simeq f_1$, both legitimately classifying $L$, could give $f_0^*[\omega_N] \neq f_1^*[\omega_N]$, and $c_1$ would depend on which representative we drew. What rescues us is precisely that homotopic smooth maps induce the *same* map on de Rham cohomology — for a smooth homotopy $F : M \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ with $F_0 = f_0$, $F_1 = f_1$, one has $f_0^* = f_1^*$ on $H^\bullet_{dR}$, by [[Thm - Homotopy Invariance of de Rham Cohomology]]. Without this the invariant is not even well-defined.

Suppose we **drop the compatibility $\iota_N^*[\omega_{N+1}] = [\omega_N]$** of the generators under the standard inclusions, i.e. we normalise the generator of each $H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N)$ independently. Two classifying maps for the same $L$ may land in projective spaces of different dimension, say $f_0 : M \to \mathbb{CP}^{N_0}$ and $f_1 : M \to \mathbb{CP}^{N_1}$; they are homotopic only after composing with inclusions into a common $\mathbb{CP}^N$. If the generators were not matched by the inclusions, the two pullbacks would disagree by the mismatch factor, and again $c_1$ would depend on a choice. What rescues us is that the normalised generators form a compatible system, $\iota_N^*[\omega_{N+1}] = [\omega_N]$ (part of [[Thm - The de Rham Cohomology of Complex Projective Space]]), so pulling back the generator commutes with stabilising to larger $N$.

Suppose we **drop the requirement that a line bundle be reduced to a $U(1)$-bundle** and try to read a classifying map straight off the $GL(1,\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}^\times$ structure. Then there is no canonical circle to wind around, and the classifying-space picture — whose classifying bundle $S^{2N+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ is a $U(1)$-bundle — does not apply verbatim. What rescues us is that every complex line bundle admits a Hermitian structure ([[Ex - Every Complex Vector Bundle Admits a Hermitian Structure]]), and any two Hermitian structures are homotopic through Hermitian structures, so the reduction to a principal $U(1)$-bundle $P \subset \operatorname{Fr}(L)$ exists and is unique up to homotopy; the resulting Chern class is independent of the Hermitian structure chosen ([[Ex - The First Chern Class does not Depend on the Hermitian Structure]]). This is why the definition is stated for $U(1)$-bundles first and transported to line bundles second.

The degree of a line bundle over a surface is the same invariant seen through a different, more elementary window. When $M = \Sigma$ is a closed oriented surface, a line bundle is built by clutching: trivialise it over a disk $D$ and over the complementary surface-with-boundary $\Sigma \setminus D^\circ$, and record the single transition function $g : \partial D \to U(1)$ on the overlap circle. Its winding number $w(g)$ is a plain integer, computable by hand. The desideratum is the same — it must not depend on the trivialisations chosen — and the input that neutralises the ambiguity is the vanishing of the winding number of any circle map that extends over a bounding surface ([[Ex - A Map Extending over a Bounding Manifold has Degree Zero]]). A reader who has understood the classifying-map story could reconstruct all of this: pair the homotopy class of gluing data against the one generator, and check that the pairing is choice-independent by killing each ambiguity with the one theorem built to kill it.

# The Definition

## The topological first Chern class

Let $M$ be a compact manifold and $P \to M$ a principal $U(1)$-bundle; equivalently (below) let $L \to M$ be a complex line bundle with a chosen Hermitian structure, and $P \subset \operatorname{Fr}(L)$ its associated principal $U(1)$-bundle. Fix, once and for all, the **positively normalised generator**
$$[\omega_N] \in H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N), \qquad \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \iota_1^*\omega_N = 1,$$
the unique generator of the one-dimensional space $H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N) \cong \mathbb{R}$ whose integral over the standard $\mathbb{CP}^1$ equals $1$ (its existence, uniqueness, and the compatibility $\iota_N^*[\omega_{N+1}] = [\omega_N]$ are [[Thm - The de Rham Cohomology of Complex Projective Space]]). Choose a smooth **classifying map** $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$, $N < \infty$, with $f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong L$ (equivalently $f^*(S^{2N+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^N) \cong P$); one exists by [[Thm - Line Bundles over Compact Manifolds are Pulled Back from Projective Space]]. The **topological first Chern class** of $P$ is
$$\boxed{\,c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P) := -f^*[\omega_N] \in H^2_{dR}(M).\,}$$
For a Hermitian line bundle $L$ we set $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) := c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P)$.

> [!note]- Well-definedness of $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ (independence of all choices)
> We must show the class $-f^*[\omega_N]$ is independent of the classifying map $f$, of the dimension $N$, and (for a line bundle) of the Hermitian structure. We prove each in turn.
>
> **Step 0 — the choices exist.** By [[Thm - Line Bundles over Compact Manifolds are Pulled Back from Projective Space]] — restated: *for a complex line bundle $L$ over a compact manifold $M$ there is $N < \infty$ and a smooth $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ with $f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong L$; any two such maps become smoothly homotopic after composition with standard inclusions into a common $\mathbb{CP}^{N'}$* — a classifying map exists, so the definition is not vacuous. By [[Thm - The de Rham Cohomology of Complex Projective Space]] the generator $[\omega_N]$ exists and is unique, so $f^*[\omega_N]$ is a well-defined element of $H^2_{dR}(M)$ for each fixed $f$.
>
> **Step 1 — independence of the representative within a homotopy class.** Suppose $f_0, f_1 : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ are smoothly homotopic, $f_0 \simeq f_1$. By [[Thm - Homotopy Invariance of de Rham Cohomology]] — restated: *smoothly homotopic maps induce equal homomorphisms on de Rham cohomology* — we have $f_0^* = f_1^*$ as maps $H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N) \to H^2_{dR}(M)$, hence $f_0^*[\omega_N] = f_1^*[\omega_N]$ (applying the equal maps to the class $[\omega_N]$). Therefore the class depends only on the homotopy class $[f]$.
>
> **Step 2 — independence of the dimension $N$.** Let $f_0 : M \to \mathbb{CP}^{N_0}$ and $f_1 : M \to \mathbb{CP}^{N_1}$ both classify $L$. Composing with standard inclusions $\iota : \mathbb{CP}^{N_0} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{N}$ and $\iota' : \mathbb{CP}^{N_1} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^{N}$ into a common $\mathbb{CP}^N$ (with $N \ge \max(N_0, N_1)$), the classification theorem's uniqueness clause gives $\iota \circ f_0 \simeq \iota' \circ f_1$ in $\mathbb{CP}^N$. Now compute, using naturality of pullback and the generator compatibility:
> $$f_0^*[\omega_{N_0}] = f_0^*\big(\iota^*[\omega_N]\big) = (\iota \circ f_0)^*[\omega_N] \qquad \text{(since } \iota^*[\omega_N] = [\omega_{N_0}] \text{ by the compatibility } \iota_N^*[\omega_{N+1}] = [\omega_N] \text{ iterated; functoriality } (\iota \circ f_0)^* = f_0^* \iota^*\text{)},$$
> $$= (\iota' \circ f_1)^*[\omega_N] \qquad \text{(by Step 1, since } \iota \circ f_0 \simeq \iota' \circ f_1\text{)},$$
> $$= f_1^*\big(\iota'^*[\omega_N]\big) = f_1^*[\omega_{N_1}] \qquad \text{(functoriality; compatibility again).}$$
> Combining the three displayed lines, $f_0^*[\omega_{N_0}] = f_1^*[\omega_{N_1}]$, so the class is independent of $N$.
>
> **Step 3 — independence of the Hermitian structure.** A complex line bundle $L$ admits a Hermitian structure ([[Ex - Every Complex Vector Bundle Admits a Hermitian Structure]]), and the reduction of $L$ to a principal $U(1)$-bundle $P \subset \operatorname{Fr}(L)$ determined by that structure is unique up to homotopy of reductions ([[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group]]); homotopic reductions have homotopic classifying maps, so by Steps 1–2 they give the same class. The independence is worked out explicitly in [[Ex - The First Chern Class does not Depend on the Hermitian Structure]].
>
> **Conclusion.** The class $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P) = -f^*[\omega_N]$ is independent of the classifying map within its homotopy class, of the projective space $\mathbb{CP}^N$, and of the Hermitian structure. Therefore $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ is a well-defined function of the isomorphism class of $P$ (equivalently of $L$), valued in $H^2_{dR}(M)$. $\blacksquare$

## The degree of a line bundle over a closed oriented surface

Let $\Sigma$ be a **closed, connected, oriented surface** and $L \to \Sigma$ a complex line bundle, equivalently a principal $U(1)$-bundle $P \to \Sigma$. Fix an embedded closed disk $D \subset \Sigma$, oriented by the orientation of $\Sigma$, with boundary circle $\partial D$ carrying the induced boundary orientation, and write $\Sigma' := \Sigma \setminus D^\circ$ for the complementary compact surface-with-boundary $\partial \Sigma' = \partial D$. Both $D$ and $\Sigma'$ are homotopy equivalent to one-dimensional complexes, over which every $U(1)$-bundle is trivial, so $P$ is described by a **clutching function**: choosing trivialisations $P|_D \cong D \times U(1)$ and $P|_{\Sigma'} \cong \Sigma' \times U(1)$, the two agree on a collar of $\partial D$ up to a smooth transition map $g : \partial D \to U(1)$ (see [[Thm - Clutching Construction for Bundles over a Closed Manifold]]). The **degree** of $P$ is
$$\boxed{\,\deg(P) := w(g) \in \mathbb{Z},\,}$$
the [[Thm - Winding Number of a Map from the Circle to U(1)|winding number]] of the clutching function $g : \partial D \cong S^1 \to U(1)$. For a Hermitian line bundle $L$ we set $\deg(L) := \deg(P)$.

> [!note]- Well-definedness of $\deg$ (independence of the trivialisations and the disk)
> We must show $w(g)$ does not depend on the two trivialisations or on the disk $D$.
>
> **Step 0 — the clutching description exists.** By [[Thm - Clutching Construction for Bundles over a Closed Manifold]] — restated: *a bundle over $\Sigma = D \cup_{\partial D} \Sigma'$ with structure group $U(1)$ is determined up to isomorphism by its transition function $g : \partial D \to U(1)$, and every such $g$ arises; conversely $D$ and $\Sigma'$ each carry a global trivialisation because each is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of circles, over which $U(1)$-bundles are trivial* — a clutching function exists.
>
> **Step 1 — the residual freedom in $g$.** Changing the trivialisation over $D$ multiplies $g$ by the restriction to $\partial D$ of a smooth map $a : D \to U(1)$; changing the trivialisation over $\Sigma'$ multiplies $g$ by the restriction of a smooth map $b : \Sigma' \to U(1)$. Because $U(1)$ is abelian the new clutching function is $g' = (a|_{\partial D})\, g\, (b|_{\partial D})$, and any two clutching descriptions of $P$ are related this way.
>
> **Step 2 — the winding number is additive.** By [[Thm - Winding Number of a Map from the Circle to U(1)]] — restated: *$w : C^\infty(S^1; U(1)) \to \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $w(g_1 g_2) = w(g_1) + w(g_2)$ (it is a group homomorphism from pointwise product to addition) and $w$ depends only on the homotopy class of its argument* — we have
> $$w(g') = w\big((a|_{\partial D})\, g\, (b|_{\partial D})\big) = w(a|_{\partial D}) + w(g) + w(b|_{\partial D}) \qquad \text{(by additivity of } w\text{).}$$
>
> **Step 3 — the two extra terms vanish.** The map $a|_{\partial D} : \partial D \to U(1)$ is the boundary value of $a : D \to U(1)$, a map defined on the whole disk $D$ whose boundary is $\partial D$. By [[Ex - A Map Extending over a Bounding Manifold has Degree Zero]] — restated: *if $h : \partial W \to U(1)$ extends to a smooth map on a compact oriented manifold $W$ with $\partial W = S^1$, then $w(h) = 0$* — we get $w(a|_{\partial D}) = 0$. Likewise $b|_{\partial D}$ is the boundary value of $b : \Sigma' \to U(1)$, defined on the compact surface $\Sigma'$ with $\partial \Sigma' = \partial D$, so $w(b|_{\partial D}) = 0$. (The two induced orientations on $\partial D$ — from $D$ and from $\Sigma'$ — are opposite, but $w = 0$ is unaffected by reversing orientation, since $-0 = 0$.)
>
> **Step 4 — independence of the disk.** Any two embedded disks $D_0, D_1 \subset \Sigma$ are related by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of $\Sigma$ isotopic to the identity (the disk theorem for connected surfaces), and an isotopy of $\Sigma$ pulls one clutching description to the other without changing the homotopy class of the transition function, hence without changing $w(g)$ by Step 2's homotopy invariance of $w$.
>
> **Conclusion.** Combining Steps 2 and 3, $w(g') = w(g)$, and by Step 4 the disk may be moved freely. Therefore $\deg(P) = w(g)$ is independent of the trivialisations and of the disk, so it is a well-defined function of the isomorphism class of $P$, valued in $\mathbb{Z}$. $\blacksquare$

## Line bundles, $U(1)$-bundles, and $\operatorname{Pic}(M)$

The two definitions above are phrased for $U(1)$-bundles and read off for line bundles, and this is legitimate because the two classification problems coincide. Every complex line bundle $L$ admits a Hermitian structure, unique up to homotopy ([[Ex - Every Complex Vector Bundle Admits a Hermitian Structure]]); a Hermitian structure is exactly a reduction of the structure group from $GL(1,\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}^\times$ to $U(1)$, i.e. a principal $U(1)$-bundle $P \subset \operatorname{Fr}(L)$ ([[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group]]); and $L$ is recovered from $P$ as the associated line bundle $P \times_{U(1)} \mathbb{C}$. Passing between $L$ and $P$ is therefore a bijection on isomorphism classes:
$$\{\text{complex line bundles on } M\}/\!\cong \ \ \xleftrightarrow{\ 1:1\ }\ \ \{\text{Hermitian line bundles on } M\}/\!\cong \ \ \xleftrightarrow{\ 1:1\ }\ \ \{\text{principal } U(1)\text{-bundles on } M\}/\!\cong.$$
We denote the resulting set of isomorphism classes by $\operatorname{Pic}(M)$; it is an abelian group under tensor product of line bundles, with unit the trivial bundle $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$ and with $L^{\vee}$ inverse to $L$. This is the group the series uses in place of the singular cohomology group $H^2(M; \mathbb{Z})$ that appears in the sources, until chapter XII makes the latter available.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

There is a clean structural reading. The assignment $L \mapsto [L] \in \operatorname{Pic}(M)$ is a contravariant functor from smooth manifolds to abelian groups: a smooth map $h : N \to M$ induces $h^* : \operatorname{Pic}(M) \to \operatorname{Pic}(N)$ by pullback of bundles, respecting composition. The classification theorem [[Thm - Line Bundles over Compact Manifolds are Pulled Back from Projective Space]] says this functor is *represented*, on compact manifolds, by the projective spaces: $\operatorname{Pic}(M) \cong \varinjlim_N [M; \mathbb{CP}^N]$, the isomorphism sending $[L]$ to the homotopy class of a classifying map, with $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ the universal bundle carried by the identity of $\mathbb{CP}^N$.

Against this backdrop the first Chern class is nothing but the **image of the universal generator under the representing isomorphism**. Fix the generator $[\omega_N] \in H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N)$; then $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ is the composite
$$\operatorname{Pic}(M) \cong \varinjlim_N [M; \mathbb{CP}^N] \xrightarrow{\ [f]\, \mapsto\, -f^*[\omega_N]\ } H^2_{dR}(M).$$
Because pullback of the universal generator is natural, $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ is automatically a natural transformation of functors: $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(h^*L) = h^* c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L)$ for every smooth $h$ (proved concretely in Examples below). This is the abstract reason a characteristic class is "characteristic": it is a natural transformation from the bundle functor to a cohomology functor, and every such transformation is a pullback of a universal class. The degree over a surface is the same transformation composed with integration $H^2_{dR}(\Sigma) \xrightarrow{\int_\Sigma} \mathbb{R}$, which lands in $\mathbb{Z}$.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The first Chern class is one instance of the single most productive construction in the topology of bundles: **classify by a homotopy class of maps into a universal space, then extract a number by pairing against a universal cohomology class.** Real line bundles are classified by maps into $\mathbb{RP}^\infty$ and measured by the first Stiefel–Whitney class $w_1 \in H^1(\cdot; \mathbb{Z}/2)$; complex vector bundles of rank $r$ by maps into the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}_r(\mathbb{C}^\infty)$ and measured by Chern classes $c_1, \dots, c_r$; principal $SU(2)$-bundles by maps into $\mathbb{HP}^\infty$ and measured by the second Chern class $c_2$. The present page is the $r = 1$ complex case, where the Grassmannian degenerates to projective space and the single class $c_1$ classifies completely. The recurring structural fact is that these classifying spaces are *representing objects* for bundle functors, and a characteristic class is the pullback of a distinguished cohomology class on the representing object; this is why characteristic classes are automatically natural and why they see only isomorphism classes.

**True name.** The official definition — pull back the generator under a classifying map — is not how $c_1$ is computed in practice, and it is worth naming the operational characterisation. Operationally, on a Hermitian line bundle $L$ with a unitary connection $\nabla$ of curvature $F_{\nabla} \in \Omega^2(M; i\mathbb{R})$, the first Chern class is the de Rham class of a curvature $2$-form,
$$c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) = \Big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi} F_{\nabla}\Big] \in H^2_{dR}(M),$$
independent of the connection; this Chern–Weil formula is what one integrates, and its equality with the classifying-map definition is [[Thm - First Chern Class of a Line Bundle from Curvature]]. Over a closed oriented surface the degree is then the operational integral $\deg(L) = \int_\Sigma c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) = \int_\Sigma \tfrac{i}{2\pi} F_{\nabla}$, a curvature integral that is always an integer — the surprising integrality that the clutching definition makes transparent and the curvature definition makes computable. The two operational faces, "winding of the transition function" and "integral of the curvature", are reconciled on [[Thm - Classification of Principal U(1)-Bundles by the First Chern Class]].

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the trivial bundle has $c_1^{\mathrm{top}} = 0$.** The trivial $U(1)$-bundle $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$ is classified by a constant map $f \equiv [z_0] : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$: pulling $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ back along a constant map gives a trivial bundle, since the fibre and the trivialising line are constant in $M$. For a constant map the differential vanishes, $df_m = 0$ for all $m$, so for any $2$-form $\omega$ the pullback vanishes pointwise, $(f^*\omega)_m = f_m^* \omega = 0$ (the pullback of a positive-degree form under a map of zero differential is zero). Hence $f^*[\omega_N] = [f^*\omega_N] = [0] = 0$, and
$$c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\underline{\mathbb{C}}) = -f^*[\omega_N] = 0.$$
This is the required calibration: the invariant that is supposed to detect nontriviality must vanish on the trivial bundle, and it does.

**Is an instance — the tautological bundle has $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -[\omega_N] \neq 0$.** The tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ is classified by the identity map $\operatorname{id} : \mathbb{CP}^N \to \mathbb{CP}^N$, because $\operatorname{id}^*\mathcal{O}(-1) = \mathcal{O}(-1)$ tautologically. Therefore
$$c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -\operatorname{id}^*[\omega_N] = -[\omega_N].$$
This class is nonzero: $[\omega_N]$ generates $H^2_{dR}(\mathbb{CP}^N) \cong \mathbb{R}$ and satisfies $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \iota_1^*\omega_N = 1 \neq 0$, so $[\omega_N] \neq 0$, hence $-[\omega_N] \neq 0$. Consequently $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is a nontrivial line bundle, detected exactly by $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$. Restricting to $\mathbb{CP}^1$ and integrating, $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \iota_1^*\omega_N = -1$, in agreement with the series' orientation and sign ledger on [[Def - The Hopf Bundle]] and with [[Ex - The Tautological Bundle over CP^1 has Degree Minus One]].

**Is an instance — the Hopf bundle over $S^2$ has degree $\pm 1$.** Identify $S^2 = \mathbb{CP}^1$ and let $P = S^3 \to S^2$ be the Hopf $U(1)$-bundle ([[Def - The Hopf Bundle]]). Trivialising over the northern and southern hemispheres (two disks meeting in the equatorial circle), the transition function on the equator $S^1$ is $z \mapsto z$ up to orientation, whose winding number is $\pm 1$; hence $\deg(P) = w(g) = \pm 1 \neq 0$, so the Hopf bundle is nontrivial. The exact sign is fixed by the orientation convention (the ledger records $\deg(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1$ over $\mathbb{CP}^1$), and the winding number is computed clause by clause in [[Ex - The Hopf Bundle is Nontrivial via the Winding Number of its Transition Function]]. That the trivial bundle over $S^2$ has degree $0$ (its clutching function is the constant $g \equiv 1$, with $w(1) = 0$) confirms $\deg$ separates the Hopf bundle from the trivial one.

**Is NOT an instance — a non-example for the *degree*: an odd-dimensional or non-orientable base.** The degree $\deg(P)$ is defined only for a closed *connected oriented surface*. It is not defined, for example, for a $U(1)$-bundle over $\mathbb{RP}^2$ (non-orientable) or over $S^1$ (not a surface): the clutching-and-winding recipe presupposes a $2$-disk whose complement is a bordered surface and an orientation to make the winding number a well-defined integer rather than a sign-ambiguous one. Over such bases one still has $c_1^{\mathrm{top}} \in H^2_{dR}$, but the reduction to a single integer via winding fails. This marks the precise scope of the second definition: the topological first Chern class is universal, the degree is its surface-specialisation.

**Corollary — naturality of $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$.** For any smooth map $h : N \to M$ of compact manifolds and any complex line bundle $L \to M$,
$$c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(h^*L) = h^*\, c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L).$$
*Proof.* Let $f : M \to \mathbb{CP}^N$ classify $L$, so $f^*\mathcal{O}(-1) \cong L$. Then $(f \circ h)^*\mathcal{O}(-1) = h^*(f^*\mathcal{O}(-1)) \cong h^*L$ (by functoriality of bundle pullback), so $f \circ h$ classifies $h^*L$. Therefore
$$c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(h^*L) = -(f \circ h)^*[\omega_N] = -h^*\big(f^*[\omega_N]\big) = h^*\big(-f^*[\omega_N]\big) = h^*\, c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) \qquad \text{(functoriality } (f\circ h)^* = h^* f^* \text{; } h^* \text{ is linear).}$$
Therefore $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ is natural. $\blacksquare$

**Calibration check.** First, the naturality corollary is genuinely usable: with $h$ the inclusion $\mathbb{CP}^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^N$ it recovers $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)|_{\mathbb{CP}^1}) = \iota_1^* c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -\iota_1^*[\omega_N] = -[\omega_1]$, consistent with the two instances above. Second, $\deg(\underline{\mathbb{C}}) = 0$ because the trivial bundle extends over all of $\Sigma$, so its clutching function extends over the disk and hence has winding number $0$ by the bounding-manifold argument. Third — a check the reader can carry out from the page — verify that $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ is well-defined on the trivial bundle by choosing *two* different constant classifying maps and confirming both give $0$: each has zero differential, so each pullback of $[\omega_N]$ is $0$, matching Step 1 of the well-definedness argument.

> [!tip] Classification of $U(1)$-bundles *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> With $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ in hand, the classification statement becomes clean: over a compact base the map $[P] \mapsto c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(P)$ is a bijection between isomorphism classes of $U(1)$-bundles and an integral lattice in $H^2_{dR}(M)$; over a closed oriented surface it is exactly $\deg : \operatorname{Pic}(\Sigma) \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } \mathbb{Z}$. See **[[Thm - Classification of Principal U(1)-Bundles by the First Chern Class]]** and, for surjectivity, **[[Ex - Line Bundles of Every Degree on a Closed Oriented Surface]]**.

> [!tip] The Chern–Weil face of $c_1$ *(from Gauge Theory VI)*
> The same class is computed from curvature: $c_1^{\mathrm{top}}(L) = \big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi} F_{\nabla}\big]$ for any unitary connection. This is proved in **[[Thm - First Chern Class of a Line Bundle from Curvature]]**, and the general apparatus of pulling invariant polynomials of curvature back to closed forms is **[[Def - Chern Classes]]** via the Chern–Weil homomorphism.

> [!tip] The second Chern number of an $SU(2)$-bundle *(from Gauge Theory III / XI)*
> The quaternionic analogue defines, for a principal $SU(2) \cong Sp(1)$-bundle over a closed oriented four-manifold, a topological second Chern number. Haydys' formula $c_2(P) := -f^*b$ pulls back a generator $b$ of the integral cohomology of $\mathbb{HP}^\infty$ under a classifying map $f : M \to \mathbb{HP}^\infty$; that generator lives in **the integral cohomology $H^4(\mathbb{HP}^\infty; \mathbb{Z})$**, which this series neither proves nor uses (it requires singular cohomology, available only from chapter XII). Instead the series *defines* the second Chern number of an $SU(2)$-bundle over a closed oriented four-manifold as its **clutching degree** on **[[Thm - Classification of Principal SU(2)-Bundles over Four-Manifolds]]**, and identifies it with the Chern–Weil integral $\int_M \tfrac{1}{8\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}(F_A \wedge F_A)$ in chapter VI; the higher Chern classes $c_1, \dots, c_r$ of a $U(r)$-bundle are likewise developed there via Chern–Weil rather than by pulling back generators from a Grassmannian.
