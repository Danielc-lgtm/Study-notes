# Notation

Throughout, $G$ is a [[Def - Lie Group|Lie group]] with identity element $e$ and Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$, and $M$ is a smooth manifold (smooth means $C^\infty$; all manifolds are Hausdorff and second countable). A **principal bundle** will be a surjective smooth map $\pi\colon P \to M$ from a smooth manifold $P$ (the **total space**) to $M$ (the **base**), carrying a right action of $G$; we write $P_m := \pi^{-1}(m)$ for the **fibre** over $m \in M$.

Standing action convention for the whole series: **Lie groups act on principal bundles on the right.** We write the action of $g \in G$ on $p \in P$ as $p \cdot g$ or $R_g(p) := p \cdot g$, so that $(p \cdot g) \cdot h = p \cdot (gh)$ and $p \cdot e = p$. The action is **free** when $p \cdot g = p$ forces $g = e$, and **transitive on the fibre** $P_m$ when for every $p, q \in P_m$ there is $g \in G$ with $q = p \cdot g$ (see [[Def - Free, Transitive, Effective, and Proper Group Actions]]). For $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ the **fundamental vector field** $\xi_P$ on $P$ is $\xi_P(p) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, p \cdot \exp(t\xi)$, the velocity of the orbit curve of the one-parameter subgroup $t \mapsto \exp(t\xi)$ (see [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action]]).

We write $\mu_G\colon G \times G \to G$, $\mu_G(g,h) = gh$ for the group multiplication, $L_g\colon G \to G$, $L_g(h) = gh$ for left translation, $\operatorname{pr}_1\colon U \times G \to U$ and $\operatorname{pr}_2\colon U \times G \to G$ for the projections, and $\operatorname{id}_U$ for the identity of $U$. The **orbit map** based at $p \in P$ is $\ell_p\colon G \to P$, $\ell_p(g) := p \cdot g$; its image lies in the fibre $P_{\pi(p)}$ by clause (ii) below. The typical fibre of a [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundle]] is written $F$; for a principal bundle it will turn out that $F = G$.

> [!warning] Convention: right action and the notation for the orbit space
> The series acts on the right and writes the orbit space of the right action as $P/G$, with quotient map $\pi\colon P \to P/G$, $\pi(p) = p \cdot G$. Bär occasionally writes $G \backslash P$ for this same orbit space of a right action; the object is identical, only the notation differs, and we always use $P/G$. Haydys and Bär agree that the action is on the right; the fundamental vector field and every downstream sign convention in the series are fixed by that choice.

# Axiom Motivation

We want to name, once and for all, the geometric object that carries a gauge symmetry. The concrete situation from which the notion is abstracted is the one met in [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|the frame bundle]]: given a rank-$k$ real [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundle]] $E \to M$, the natural bookkeeping device for "a choice of frame at each point" is the set $\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \bigsqcup_{m \in M} \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ of all ordered bases of all fibres. Haydys states the motivation exactly: the computations describing how connection matrices, curvature, and every other local object depend on the choice of a local frame "become formidable quite soon," and the cure is "to consider all possible frames at once rather than choosing local trivializations when needed." The change of local frame $e' = e \cdot g$ is a right action of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ on frames, and it is this action that we must axiomatise. Doing so isolates the structural features shared by every such construction — the frame bundle, the bundle of orthonormal frames, the Hopf fibration, a homogeneous space $G \to G/H$ — and lets us prove theorems about all of them at once.

What must the definition capture? A frame $p \in \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is a basis, and any other basis of the same $E_m$ is obtained from $p$ by a unique element of $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$: the group acts **freely** (a nonzero change of basis moves the basis) and **transitively on each fibre** (any two bases are related by some change of basis). The fibre $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is therefore a copy of the group $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ — but *only after a basepoint is chosen*, because there is no distinguished "identity frame." This is the exact structure of a torsor, and the definition must record it. It must also record that the family is **locally trivial** — that near each point of $M$ the total space looks like $U \times G$ — and, crucially, that the local model $U \times G$ is compatible with the group action: sliding a frame by $g$ must correspond, in the local coordinates, to right-multiplying the $G$-coordinate by $g$. This last compatibility is what pins the structure group to $G$ acting on itself by translation, rather than to the much larger group of all diffeomorphisms of the fibre.

The definition therefore has four clauses, and the discipline of the vault is to ask, for each, what breaks if it is dropped. We test the four clauses of the definition in the form that Bär gives (a [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundle]] together with a right $G$-action that is (i) free, (ii) transitive on fibres, (iii) admits equivariant local trivialisations); the equivalent four-clause form of Haydys is treated in [The Definition](#The%20Definition).

**Drop freeness.** Keep transitivity on fibres and local triviality, but allow the action to have a nontrivial stabiliser. Then the fibre is no longer a copy of $G$; it is the homogeneous space $G/H$, where $H$ is the common stabiliser. Concretely, let $M$ be a single point and let $P = S^2$ carry the rotation action of $G = SO(3)$, $x \cdot g := g^{-1}x$ (a right action, since $x \cdot (gh) = (gh)^{-1}x = h^{-1}g^{-1}x = (x \cdot g) \cdot h$). This action is transitive — any unit vector is carried to any other by a rotation — but it is *not free*: the stabiliser of $x \in S^2$ is the circle $SO(2)_x$ of rotations about the axis through $x$. As a result the fibre $S^2$ is the homogeneous space $SO(3)/SO(2)$, and $\dim S^2 = 2 < 3 = \dim SO(3)$, so the orbit map $\ell_x\colon SO(3) \to S^2$ is a two-parameter-fibred submersion, nowhere a local diffeomorphism, and $S^2$ is not a copy of the group at all. Without freeness the corollary that the fibre is diffeomorphic to $G$ (proved below) fails outright, and the entire "coordinatise the fibre by the group" mechanism collapses. The strengthening that would *guarantee* the corollary is exactly freeness, and nothing weaker: freeness is necessary and sufficient for the orbit map to be injective.

**Drop transitivity on fibres.** Keep freeness and local triviality. Then each fibre is a disjoint union of several $G$-orbits rather than a single one, and the object is a bundle whose fibre is strictly larger than $G$. Concretely, let $G = SO(2)$ act on $P = M \times SO(3)$ by right multiplication on the second factor, $(x, A) \cdot g := (x, Ag)$, with $\pi = \operatorname{pr}_1$. This action is free (right multiplication in a group is free), it preserves fibres, and the identity chart $M \times SO(3) \to M \times SO(3)$ is equivariant; but on each fibre $\{x\} \times SO(3)$ the $SO(2)$-orbits are the right cosets $A \cdot SO(2)$, of which there is a whole two-sphere $SO(3)/SO(2) \cong S^2$. The action is not transitive on fibres, and $P$ is not a principal $SO(2)$-bundle — it is a fibre bundle with fibre $SO(3)$ on which $SO(2)$ merely acts. Transitivity is precisely the demand that the fibre be a single orbit, so that after choosing a basepoint the fibre *is* $G$.

**Drop the equivariance of the trivialisations.** Keep freeness and transitivity on fibres. Bär phrases clause (iii) as an existence statement — the local trivialisations "can be chosen" equivariant — and the reason is subtle: given a smooth free fibrewise-transitive right action on a fibre bundle with fibre $G$, one can *build* an equivariant trivialisation from a local section, so the clause is less a restriction on which bundles qualify than a normalisation selecting the good atlas. Its genuine force is definitional: it fixes the structure group as $G$ acting on itself by translation, rather than the larger group $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ of Lie-group automorphisms or the still larger $\operatorname{Diff}(G)$. If one keeps a fibre bundle with fibre $G$ but severs the compatibility with any right $G$-action, the object need not be principal at all: the adjoint group bundle $\operatorname{Ad}P := P \times_{\operatorname{conj}} G$ (introduced in [Relate to Other Fields](#Relate%20to%20Other%20Fields%20%2F%20Compression)) is a fibre bundle whose typical fibre is $G$, yet it carries no free transitive right $G$-action — instead each of its fibres is a copy of $G$ *as a group*, complete with a distinguished identity element $[p, e]$. The contrast is exactly the torsor–group distinction: $\operatorname{Ad}P$ remembers the identity, a principal bundle forgets it. The delicate case that shows why clause (iii) is an "exists" and not a "for all" is the mapping torus $E_\phi$ of a Lie-group automorphism $\phi \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$: when $\phi$ is inner one can absorb it and recover equivariant charts, so $E_\phi$ is principal, whereas an outer $\phi$ glues the fibres by an automorphism that no right translation can undo. ⚠️ [unverified: the precise claim that the mapping torus of an outer automorphism admits no compatible fibrewise right $G$-action is stated here as illustration and not proved on this page; the adjoint-bundle example above is the fully verified witness for this clause.]

Could a reader invent the definition from this alone? The desiderata are: a locally trivial family (local triviality), whose fibres are torsors under $G$ (free plus transitive), with the local model compatible with the action (equivariance). Freeness and transitivity together say each fibre is a $G$-torsor; local triviality says the family is glued from trivial pieces; equivariance says the gluing respects the $G$-action. Each clause is forced by one of the three failures above, and the definition is the conjunction.

# The Definition

We record the two equivalent forms in which the sources state the notion, then prove they define the same class of objects. Throughout, $G$ is a Lie group and $\pi\colon P \to M$ a smooth surjective map with a smooth right $G$-action.

**Form B (Bär, Definition 2.2.1).** A [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundle]] $(P, \pi, M)$ together with a smooth right action of the Lie group $G$ on $P$ is a **principal $G$-bundle** if and only if:

1. **Freeness.** The action is free: $p \cdot g = p$ implies $g = e$.
2. **Transitivity on fibres.** For every $p \in P$, $p \cdot G = P_{\pi(p)}$; that is, $G$ acts transitively on each fibre.
3. **Equivariant trivialisations.** Each point of $M$ has a neighbourhood $U$ and a fibre-bundle trivialisation $\psi_U\colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ (a diffeomorphism with $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$) that is $G$-equivariant:
$$\psi_U(p \cdot g) = \psi_U(p) \cdot g \qquad \text{for all } p \in \pi^{-1}(U),\ g \in G,$$
where $G$ acts on $U \times G$ by right multiplication on the second factor, $(x, h) \cdot g := (x, hg)$; equivalently the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} \pi^{-1}(U) \times G & \xrightarrow{\ \text{action}\ } & \pi^{-1}(U) \\ {\scriptstyle \psi_U \times \operatorname{id}_G}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \psi_U} \\ (U \times G) \times G & \xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{id}_U \times \mu_G\ } & U \times G \end{array}$$
commutes.

The group $G$ is the **structure group**. When such $\psi_U$ exist over all of $M$ (a single global equivariant trivialisation) the bundle is **trivial**.

**Form A (Haydys, Definition 23).** A **principal $G$-bundle** is a triple $(P, M, \pi)$ where:

1'. $P$ and $M$ are smooth manifolds and $\pi\colon P \to M$ is a surjective submersion;
2'. $G$ acts smoothly on $P$ on the right with $\pi(p \cdot g) = \pi(p)$ for all $p \in P$, $g \in G$ (the action **preserves fibres**);
3'. $G$ acts freely and transitively on each fibre $\pi^{-1}(m)$;
4'. each $m \in M$ has a neighbourhood $U$ and a $G$-equivariant diffeomorphism (a local trivialisation) $\psi_U\colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ with $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$ and $\psi_U(p \cdot g) = \psi_U(p) \cdot g$.

**The two forms are equivalent.** We show that a quadruple satisfying (1)–(3) is precisely one satisfying (1')–(4').

> [!note]- Proof that Form A and Form B define the same objects
> We must show each set of clauses implies the other, for the same data $(P, \pi, M, G)$ and the same action.
>
> **Direction A $\Rightarrow$ B.** Assume (1'), (2'), (3'), (4'). We produce the fibre-bundle structure and clauses (1), (2), (3) of Form B.
>
> **Step 1 — the equivariant trivialisations exhibit a fibre bundle with fibre $G$.** By (4'), each $m \in M$ has a neighbourhood $U$ and a diffeomorphism $\psi_U\colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ with $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$. This is exactly the data of a [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundle]] trivialisation with typical fibre $G$: the maps $\psi_U$ are diffeomorphisms over $U$, and $G$ is a smooth manifold, so $(P, \pi, M)$ is a fibre bundle with fibre $G$ (by the definition of a fibre bundle, an atlas of such $\psi_U$ over an open cover of $M$ is all that is required). Thus the "fibre bundle" hypothesis of Form B holds.
>
> **Step 2 — clauses (1) and (2) of Form B.** Clause (1) (freeness) is the first half of (3'), verbatim. Clause (2) (transitivity on fibres, $p \cdot G = P_{\pi(p)}$) is the second half of (3') together with (2'): by (2') the orbit $p \cdot G$ is contained in $P_{\pi(p)}$ (each $p \cdot g$ has $\pi(p \cdot g) = \pi(p)$), and by transitivity in (3') the orbit exhausts the fibre, so $p \cdot G = P_{\pi(p)}$.
>
> **Step 3 — clause (3) of Form B.** The maps $\psi_U$ of (4') are equivariant by hypothesis, $\psi_U(p \cdot g) = \psi_U(p) \cdot g$, which is clause (3). Hence all of Form B holds. This proves A $\Rightarrow$ B.
>
> **Direction B $\Rightarrow$ A.** Assume $(P, \pi, M)$ is a fibre bundle with a right $G$-action satisfying (1), (2), (3). We produce (1'), (2'), (3'), (4').
>
> **Step 1 — $\pi$ is a surjective submersion (clause 1').** Being a fibre bundle, $P$ and $M$ are smooth manifolds and $\pi$ is smooth. Fix $p \in P$, set $m = \pi(p)$, and choose an equivariant trivialisation $\psi_U\colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ from clause (3) with $m \in U$. Then $\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(U)} = \operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U$ (since $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$). The projection $\operatorname{pr}_1\colon U \times G \to U$ is a surjective submersion, and $\psi_U$ is a diffeomorphism, so their composite is a submersion at $p$; as $p$ was arbitrary, $\pi$ is a submersion. It is surjective because every fibre $P_m = \psi_U^{-1}(\{m\} \times G)$ is nonempty ($G \neq \varnothing$). This is (1').
>
> **Step 2 — the action preserves fibres (clause 2').** By clause (2), $p \cdot g \in p \cdot G = P_{\pi(p)}$, so $\pi(p \cdot g) = \pi(p)$ for all $p, g$. This is (2').
>
> **Step 3 — free and transitive on fibres (clause 3').** Freeness is clause (1). Transitivity on the fibre $P_m$ is clause (2): for $p, q \in P_m$, clause (2) gives $q \in P_m = p \cdot G$, so $q = p \cdot g$ for some $g$. This is (3').
>
> **Step 4 — equivariant trivialisations (clause 4').** These are exactly the $\psi_U$ of clause (3): diffeomorphisms over $U$, equivariant, with $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$. This is (4'). Hence all of Form A holds. This proves B $\Rightarrow$ A.
>
> **Conclusion.** A quadruple satisfies Form A if and only if it satisfies Form B; the only genuine content of the equivalence is that a fibre-bundle projection is automatically a surjective submersion (Step 1 of B $\Rightarrow$ A) and, conversely, that an atlas of equivariant fibre-preserving diffeomorphisms to $U \times G$ is exactly the data making $\pi$ a fibre bundle with fibre $G$ (Step 1 of A $\Rightarrow$ B). $\blacksquare$

We adopt Form B as the primary definition (a fibre bundle with a free, fibrewise-transitive, equivariantly-trivialised right action), because it makes the class of objects and the notion of morphism inherit directly from [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundles]]; Form A is the convenient checklist when one is handed a bare surjective submersion with a group action and wants to certify it.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The structural reading of the definition is that a principal $G$-bundle is a **locally trivial family of $G$-torsors**. We make this precise.

A **$G$-torsor** (or **principal homogeneous space**) is a nonempty smooth manifold $T$ equipped with a smooth right $G$-action that is free and transitive. Equivalently, $T$ is a manifold on which $G$ acts so that for every pair $(t, t') \in T \times T$ there is a *unique* $g \in G$ with $t' = t \cdot g$ (existence is transitivity, uniqueness is freeness). A torsor is "the group $G$ with its identity element forgotten": choosing any basepoint $t_0 \in T$ yields a diffeomorphism $G \to T$, $g \mapsto t_0 \cdot g$ (an instance of the orbit map, shown to be a diffeomorphism in the corollary below), but there is no canonical basepoint, so $T$ is not a group.

In these terms the definition reads: **a principal $G$-bundle is a fibre bundle $\pi\colon P \to M$ each of whose fibres $P_m$ is a $G$-torsor, the torsor structures fitting together into a smooth global right $G$-action, and locally trivialised by equivariant charts.** Clauses (1) and (2) of Form B say precisely that each fibre is a $G$-torsor; clause (3) says the family of torsors is locally modelled on the trivial torsor bundle $U \times G$ in a way compatible with the $G$-action.

The functorial payoff — the reason this structural view is the right one — is that a $G$-torsor is exactly the datum needed to *associate* other fibres to $P$. Given any smooth left $G$-space $F$, the fibre $P_m$ (a right torsor) and $F$ combine into $P_m \times_G F$, a copy of $F$ with the $G$-ambiguity divided out; letting $m$ vary produces the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated bundle]] $P \times_G F$. A principal bundle is thus a "universal frame": a single geometric object from which every associated tensor, spinor, or homogeneous fibre is manufactured by a representation of $G$. This is the sense in which the principal bundle is the fundamental object and the vector bundles one started from ([[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundles]] and their associates) are derived — a point developed on [[Def - Associated Bundle]] and [[Thm - Free Proper Actions Give Principal Bundles]].

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Operationally, a principal $G$-bundle is *a bundle of $G$-torsors with structure group $G$*, and the single fact that makes it usable is the corollary below: **choosing a point in a fibre coordinatises that whole fibre by the group.** Everything a gauge theorist does with $P$ — writing a connection, transforming it under gauge, extracting curvature on the base — is bookkeeping for that coordinatisation and its ambiguity.

**Physics: local sections are local gauges.** Bär's Remark 2.2.19 gives the physical dictionary. A local trivialisation of $P$ over $U$ is the same data as a local section $s\colon U \to P$ (the equivalence is [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]], restated in the corollaries below), and such a section is what physicists call a **local gauge**: a coherent choice of "reference frame" throughout $U$ against which fields are measured. Bär's illustration is the abelian case $G = \mathbb{R}$: a local section identifies the points of each fibre $P_m$ with real numbers, and if the fibre points are the possible outcomes of a measurement, then a choice of local section is a **choice of a system of units**. Different sections differ by a $G$-valued function — a change of units, or gauge transformation — which is the origin of the transition functions of [[Def - Transition Functions and the Cocycle Condition]]. The lack of a canonical section is the lack of a canonical gauge: this is the physical content of "the fibre is a torsor, not a group."

**The adjoint group bundle, and why the fibre is not a group.** Because each fibre $P_m$ is a torsor and not a group, one cannot multiply points of $P_m$. What one *can* do is form the associated bundle for the conjugation action of $G$ on itself, $c\colon G \to \operatorname{Aut}(G)$, $c_g(h) = ghg^{-1}$: the **adjoint group bundle** $\operatorname{Ad}P := P \times_c G$, whose fibres *are* groups (isomorphic to $G$, with well-defined multiplication $[p, g] \cdot [p, g'] = [p, gg']$ and identity $[p, e]$). The passage $P \rightsquigarrow \operatorname{Ad}P$ is exactly the passage from a torsor to a group, and it is why the equivariance clause matters: $\operatorname{Ad}P$ is a fibre bundle with fibre $G$ that is *not* principal, witnessing that "fibre $G$" alone is far weaker than "principal." The Lie-algebra analogue $\operatorname{ad}P := P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$ is the vector bundle in which curvature and the infinitesimal gauge algebra live; both are constructed on [[Def - Associated Bundle]].

**Algebra: torsors over other structures.** The torsor idea is not confined to Lie groups. An affine space is a torsor over its vector group of translations; a $\mathbb{Z}$-torsor is a "principally homogeneous $\mathbb{Z}$-set" (a labelled bi-infinite line with no chosen origin); in Galois theory the set of primitive $n$-th roots of a fixed element is a torsor over the appropriate group. In every case the pattern is the one here: a set on which a group acts simply transitively, so that it is a copy of the group missing only a basepoint. The principal bundle globalises this: it is a torsor "spread smoothly over $M$."

# Examples / Corollaries

We first record the corollary that gives the definition its force, then verify the standard instances and a non-instance.

**Corollary (the orbit map is a diffeomorphism; Bär Remark 2.2.2).** *Let $\pi\colon P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle and $p \in P$. Then the orbit map $\ell_p\colon G \to P_{\pi(p)}$, $\ell_p(g) = p \cdot g$, is a diffeomorphism onto the fibre. In particular every fibre is diffeomorphic to $G$, and the typical fibre of a principal $G$-bundle is $G$.*

> [!note]- Full proof of the corollary
> Fix $p \in P$ and write $m = \pi(p)$. We show $\ell_p\colon G \to P_m$ is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse. We give the direct proof through an equivariant trivialisation, then the intrinsic freeness argument that clause (1) makes $d_e\ell_p$ injective, since the latter is the mechanism that survives to the theory of connections.
>
> **Step 0 — $\ell_p$ maps into $P_m$ and is smooth.** By clause (2') / clause (2), $\pi(p \cdot g) = \pi(p) = m$, so $\ell_p(g) \in P_m$ for all $g$. The map $g \mapsto p \cdot g$ is smooth because it is the restriction to $\{p\} \times G$ of the smooth action $P \times G \to P$. The fibre $P_m$ is a smooth embedded submanifold of $P$ (it is the fibre of the submersion $\pi$ over the regular value $m$), and $\ell_p$ is smooth as a map into it.
>
> **Step 1 — in a trivialisation, $\ell_p$ is a left translation.** Choose an equivariant trivialisation $\psi_U\colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times G$ with $m \in U$ (clause (3)). Write $\psi_U(p) = (m, g_0)$ for a unique $g_0 \in G$ (the second component, since $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$ forces the first component to be $\pi(p) = m$). Then, using equivariance $\psi_U(p \cdot g) = \psi_U(p) \cdot g$ and the definition of the action on $U \times G$,
> $$\psi_U(\ell_p(g)) = \psi_U(p \cdot g) = \psi_U(p) \cdot g = (m, g_0) \cdot g = (m, g_0 g) \qquad \text{(equivariance of } \psi_U \text{; action on } U \times G \text{ is right multiplication).}$$
> Thus, under the diffeomorphism $\psi_U$, the orbit map $\ell_p$ becomes $g \mapsto (m, g_0 g)$, i.e. the composite of left translation $L_{g_0}\colon G \to G$, $h \mapsto g_0 h$, with the inclusion $G \cong \{m\} \times G \hookrightarrow U \times G$.
>
> **Step 2 — $\ell_p$ is a diffeomorphism onto $P_m$.** The trivialisation restricts to a diffeomorphism $\psi_U|_{P_m}\colon P_m \to \{m\} \times G \cong G$ (Step 0 identifies $P_m$ as a submanifold, and $\psi_U$ carries it diffeomorphically onto $\{m\} \times G$ because $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$). By Step 1 the diagram
> $$\ell_p = (\psi_U|_{P_m})^{-1} \circ (\text{inclusion}) \circ L_{g_0}$$
> exhibits $\ell_p$ as a composite of the diffeomorphism $L_{g_0}$ (left translation in a Lie group is a diffeomorphism, with inverse $L_{g_0^{-1}}$), the identification $G \cong \{m\} \times G$, and the diffeomorphism $(\psi_U|_{P_m})^{-1}$. A composite of diffeomorphisms is a diffeomorphism, so $\ell_p\colon G \to P_m$ is a diffeomorphism. In particular $P_m \cong G$, and since $m$ was arbitrary the typical fibre is $G$.
>
> **Step 3 — the intrinsic reason freeness gives $d_e\ell_p$ injective (Bär's argument).** We show, without invoking a trivialisation, that freeness alone forces the differential $d_e\ell_p\colon \mathfrak{g} = T_eG \to T_pP$ to be injective; this is the fact that the trivialisation-free theory (connections, horizontal spaces) actually uses. By definition of the [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action|fundamental vector field]], for $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$,
> $$d_e\ell_p(\xi) = \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, \ell_p(\exp(t\xi)) = \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, p \cdot \exp(t\xi) = \xi_P(p) \qquad \text{(chain rule; definition of } \xi_P \text{).}$$
> Suppose $d_e\ell_p(\xi) = \xi_P(p) = 0$. The fundamental vector field $\xi_P$ has flow $\Phi_t(q) = q \cdot \exp(t\xi)$: indeed $\tfrac{d}{dt} \Phi_t(q) = \tfrac{d}{ds}\big|_{s=0}\, q \cdot \exp(t\xi)\exp(s\xi) = \xi_P(\Phi_t(q))$, so $t \mapsto \Phi_t(q)$ is the integral curve of $\xi_P$ from $q$. Because $\xi_P(p) = 0$, the constant curve $t \mapsto p$ is *also* an integral curve of $\xi_P$ through $p$ (its velocity is $0 = \xi_P(p)$). By the uniqueness of integral curves — [[Thm - Fundamental Theorem on Flows|the fundamental theorem on flows]], which states that through each point there is a unique maximal integral curve of a smooth vector field — the two integral curves coincide:
> $$p \cdot \exp(t\xi) = \Phi_t(p) = p \qquad \text{for all } t \qquad \text{(uniqueness of integral curves, } \xi_P(p) = 0 \text{).}$$
> By **freeness** (clause (1)), $p \cdot \exp(t\xi) = p$ forces $\exp(t\xi) = e$ for all $t$; differentiating at $t = 0$, $\xi = \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\exp(t\xi) = 0$. Hence $d_e\ell_p(\xi) = 0 \Rightarrow \xi = 0$, so $d_e\ell_p$ is injective. Since $\dim \mathfrak{g} = \dim G = \dim P_m$ (Step 2) and $d_e\ell_p$ is an injective linear map between spaces of equal dimension, it is an isomorphism, confirming from the intrinsic side that $\ell_p$ is an immersion at $e$ — and, by the identity $\ell_p = \ell_{p \cdot g} \circ L_{g^{-1}}$ (checked directly: $\ell_{p \cdot g}(L_{g^{-1}}(h)) = (p \cdot g) \cdot (g^{-1}h) = p \cdot h = \ell_p(h)$), an immersion everywhere. This re-proves that $\ell_p$ is a local diffeomorphism; combined with the bijectivity from clauses (1) and (2) it is a diffeomorphism, in agreement with Step 2. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary (the fibre carries no natural group structure).** The fibre $P_m$ is a $G$-torsor. Making it a group requires choosing an identity, and there is no canonical choice: each $p \in P_m$ furnishes, via the diffeomorphism $\ell_p\colon G \to P_m$, a group structure on $P_m$ with identity $p$, but the structure from $p$ and the structure from $p' = p \cdot g_0$ disagree. Indeed $\ell_{p'} = \ell_p \circ L_{g_0}$ (from $\ell_{p'}(g) = p' \cdot g = p \cdot (g_0 g) = \ell_p(g_0 g)$), so the two identifications $G \cong P_m$ differ by left translation $L_{g_0}$, which is a group isomorphism only when $g_0 = e$. Hence no group structure on $P_m$ is preserved under change of basepoint, and $P_m$ has no natural group structure — it is a torsor. This is the fibrewise shadow of the [Categorical](#Categorical%20%2F%20Structural%20Definition) reading.

**Corollary (local sections and local gauges).** Because clause (3) provides an equivariant trivialisation $\psi_U$ over a neighbourhood $U$ of any point, the formula $s(x) := \psi_U^{-1}(x, e)$ defines a smooth **local section** $s\colon U \to P$ (a smooth right inverse of $\pi$, $\pi \circ s = \operatorname{id}_U$, since $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi_U = \pi$ gives $\pi(s(x)) = \operatorname{pr}_1(x, e) = x$). Conversely a local section determines a local trivialisation. The full bijection is the sibling theorem, restated here at the point of use:

> [!note] Sections correspond to trivialisations (restatement of [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]])
> For a principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$ and open $U \subseteq M$, the assignment $\psi_U \mapsto s := \psi_U^{-1}(\cdot, e)$ is a bijection between equivariant local trivialisations over $U$ and smooth local sections over $U$, with inverse $s \mapsto \psi_U$ where $\psi_U(p) = (\pi(p), g(p))$ and $g(p)$ is the unique element with $p = s(\pi(p)) \cdot g(p)$. In particular $P$ is trivial if and only if it admits a global section.
>
> The complete proof — including the smoothness of $p \mapsto g(p)$ — is on that page; we invoke only the existence of local sections, which follows immediately from clause (3) as shown above.

These local sections are the **local gauges** of [Relate to Other Fields](#Relate%20to%20Other%20Fields%20%2F%20Compression).

**Is an instance — the trivial (product) bundle $M \times G$.** Let $P = M \times G$, $\pi = \operatorname{pr}_1$, with the right action $(x, h) \cdot g := (x, hg)$. We verify Form B clause by clause. It is a [[Def - Fibre Bundle|fibre bundle]] with fibre $G$ (the identity map is a global trivialisation). **Freeness:** $(x, h) \cdot g = (x, hg) = (x, h)$ forces $hg = h$, so $g = e$ (cancel $h$ in the group $G$). **Transitivity on fibres:** the fibre over $x$ is $\{x\} \times G$; for $(x, h), (x, h') \in \{x\} \times G$ take $g = h^{-1}h'$, then $(x, h) \cdot g = (x, h h^{-1} h') = (x, h')$. **Equivariant trivialisation:** the identity $\psi = \operatorname{id}_{M \times G}$ has $\operatorname{pr}_1 \circ \psi = \operatorname{pr}_1 = \pi$ and $\psi((x, h) \cdot g) = (x, hg) = (x, h) \cdot g = \psi(x, h) \cdot g$. All clauses hold, so $M \times G$ is a principal $G$-bundle; it is the trivial bundle, and the constant-identity map $x \mapsto (x, e)$ is a global section.

**Is an instance — the quotient $G \to G/H$ of a Lie group by a closed subgroup.** Let $H \leq G$ be a closed subgroup, acting on $G$ on the right by group multiplication, $g \cdot h := gh$ for $h \in H$. Then $G \to G/H$, $g \mapsto gH$, is a principal $H$-bundle. We verify the clauses, invoking the homogeneous-space theorem for the manifold structure and local sections:

> [!note] The homogeneous space is a manifold (restatement of [[Thm - Homogeneous Space is a Smooth Manifold]])
> For a Lie group $G$ and a closed subgroup $H \leq G$, the quotient $G/H$ has a unique smooth manifold structure for which the projection $\pi\colon G \to G/H$ is a smooth surjective submersion admitting smooth local sections. The complete proof is on that page.

**Freeness:** for the right $H$-action, $g \cdot h = gh = g$ forces $h = e$ (cancel $g$). **Transitivity on fibres:** the fibre of $\pi$ over $gH$ is the coset $gH = \{gh : h \in H\}$, which is a single $H$-orbit of $g$, so $H$ acts transitively on it. **Preserves fibres:** $\pi(gh) = ghH = gH = \pi(g)$ since $hH = H$. **Equivariant trivialisations:** by the restated theorem, $\pi$ admits smooth local sections $s\colon U \to G$; each such section produces an equivariant trivialisation $\psi_U(g') := (\pi(g'), h(g'))$ where $h(g')$ is the unique element of $H$ with $g' = s(\pi(g')) \cdot h(g')$ (unique by freeness and transitivity on the fibre), and $\psi_U(g' \cdot h) = (\pi(g'), h(g')h) = \psi_U(g') \cdot h$ by construction. All clauses hold, so $G \to G/H$ is a principal $H$-bundle. Two special cases recur throughout the series: $SO(3) \to SO(3)/SO(2) \cong S^2$ (the rotation group over the sphere, with fibre the circle) and $SU(2) \to SU(2)/U(1) \cong S^2$, the latter being the Hopf bundle in disguise (see [[Def - The Hopf Bundle]] and, for $SU(2) \cong S^3$, [[Ex - SU(2) is Diffeomorphic to S^3]]).

**Is an instance — the Hopf bundle $S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^n$.** With $U(1) = \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : |\lambda| = 1\}$ acting on the unit sphere $S^{2n+1} = \{z \in \mathbb{C}^{n+1} : |z| = 1\}$ by scalar multiplication, $z \cdot \lambda := z\lambda$, and $\pi(z) = [z] \in \mathbb{CP}^n$ (the complex line through $z$; see [[Def - Complex Projective Space as a Quotient]]), the map $\pi\colon S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^n$ is a principal $U(1)$-bundle. **Freeness:** $z\lambda = z$ with $z \neq 0$ forces $\lambda = 1$ (some coordinate $z_j \neq 0$ gives $z_j\lambda = z_j$). **Transitivity on fibres:** the fibre $\pi^{-1}([z])$ is $\{z\lambda : |\lambda| = 1\}$, exactly the $U(1)$-orbit of $z$, by the definition of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ as the space of complex lines. Because $U(1)$ is **compact** and acts freely, principality (the fibre-bundle structure and equivariant trivialisations) follows from the free-compact criterion, restated at the point of use:

> [!note] Free compact actions give principal bundles (restatement of [[Thm - Free Proper Actions Give Principal Bundles]])
> If a Lie group $G$ acts smoothly, freely and properly on the right on a manifold $P$ — in particular if $G$ is compact and the action is free — then $\pi\colon P \to P/G$ is a principal $G$-bundle. The complete proof, including the construction of the equivariant trivialisations from local sections of the quotient, is on that page.

Applying it to the free action of the compact group $U(1)$ on $S^{2n+1}$ with $P/U(1) = \mathbb{CP}^n$ certifies the Hopf bundle as principal. It is the standing example of a *nontrivial* principal bundle: for $n = 1$ it is $S^3 \to S^2$, which admits no global section, so by the sections corollary above it is not the trivial bundle $S^2 \times U(1)$. The full construction, the explicit local sections, and the transition function $z/|z|$ are on [[Def - The Hopf Bundle]]. (Series index convention: we write the Hopf bundle as $S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^n$; Bär's Example 2.2.4 writes $S^{2n-1} \to \mathbb{CP}^n$, an off-by-one that we correct to $S^{2n-1} \to \mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$.)

**Is an instance — the frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.** For a rank-$k$ real [[Def - Vector Bundle|vector bundle]] $E \to M$, the set $\operatorname{Fr}(E) = \bigsqcup_m \operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ of ordered bases carries the right $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-action $e \cdot h$ (change of basis), which is free and transitive on each $\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ because any two bases of the vector space $E_m$ are related by a unique invertible matrix. It is a principal $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$-bundle — its structure group is $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$ — with the smooth structure and equivariant charts built in full on [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle]]. This is the fundamental example that motivated the definition.

**Is NOT an instance — a non-free fibrewise-transitive action.** The rotation action of $G = SO(3)$ on $P = S^2$ over a one-point base (from the [Axiom Motivation](#Axiom%20Motivation)) is transitive on the single fibre $S^2$ but not free: the stabiliser of $x \in S^2$ is a circle $SO(2)_x$. It fails clause (1). Concretely the orbit map $\ell_x\colon SO(3) \to S^2$ is not injective — every rotation about the axis through $x$ fixes $x$ — so no fibre coordinatisation by the group exists, and $S^2$ is the homogeneous space $SO(3)/SO(2)$, not a copy of $SO(3)$. It is therefore not a principal $SO(3)$-bundle, only a $G$-space; freeness is exactly what it lacks.

**Calibration check.** Three quick verifications from what is on the page. First, a principal $G$-bundle is **trivial if and only if it has a global section**: one direction is the trivial bundle's section $x \mapsto (x, e)$; the converse is the sections corollary (a global section builds a global equivariant trivialisation) — the full statement is [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality]] and it shows $S^3 \to S^2$ is nontrivial precisely because it has no section. Second, the structure group of the frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ of a rank-$k$ real bundle is $GL_k(\mathbb{R})$, and reducing it to $O(k)$, $SO(k)$, or $U(m)$ is the content of a $G$-structure ([[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group]]). Third, verify directly from the definition that in the trivial bundle $M \times G$ every fibre $\{x\} \times G$ is a $G$-torsor with the "obvious" basepoint $(x, e)$, and that this basepoint is *not* canonical for an abstract principal bundle — which is why $M \times G$ is a special (trivialised) principal bundle rather than the generic one.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Associated bundles *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> Every vector bundle, tensor bundle, and homogeneous fibre over $M$ is manufactured from a principal bundle and a left $G$-space by the associated-bundle construction $P \times_G F$; the principal bundle is the universal frame from which they all descend. See **[[Def - Associated Bundle]]**.

> [!tip] Connections on a principal bundle *(from Gauge Theory IV)*
> A connection is a $G$-invariant choice of horizontal complement to the vertical (fibre) directions, encoded as a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form $\omega$ on $P$ with $\omega(\xi_P) = \xi$ and $R_g^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega$; the fundamental vector fields of this page are exactly the vertical directions it must split off. See **[[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle]]**.

> [!tip] Transition functions and classification by cocycles *(from Gauge Theory III)*
> A principal bundle is reconstructed from its local sections' transition functions $g_{\alpha\beta}\colon U_{\alpha\beta} \to G$, and isomorphism classes correspond to cohomologous cocycles; this is how one counts the principal bundles over a given base. See **[[Def - Transition Functions and the Cocycle Condition]]** and **[[Thm - Principal Bundles are Classified by Cocycles]]**.

> [!tip] Gauge transformations *(from Gauge Theory V)*
> The automorphisms of $P$ covering the identity of $M$ — the vertical, $G$-equivariant diffeomorphisms $\psi\colon P \to P$ — form the gauge group, acting on connections; they are the sections of the adjoint group bundle $\operatorname{Ad}P$ introduced above. See **[[Def - Gauge Transformation]]**.
