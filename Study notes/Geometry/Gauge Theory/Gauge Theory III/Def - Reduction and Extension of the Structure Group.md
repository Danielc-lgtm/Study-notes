# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth manifold (Hausdorff, second countable, $C^\infty$), and all Lie-group actions on principal bundles are **right** actions, written $p\cdot g$ or $pg$; this is the standing convention of the series. We write $\pi\colon P\to M$ for a **[[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal $G$-bundle]]**: a fibre bundle with a smooth, free, fibre-preserving right $G$-action that is transitive on each fibre $P_m:=\pi^{-1}(m)$, together with $G$-equivariant local trivialisations $\psi_U\colon\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times G$, $\psi_U(pg)=\psi_U(p)\cdot g$. For a real vector bundle $E\to M$ of rank $k$, $\operatorname{Fr}(E)=\bigsqcup_{m\in M}\operatorname{Fr}(E_m)$ is its **[[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]]**, a principal $GL_k(\mathbb R)$-bundle whose fibre over $m$ is the set of ordered bases (equivalently, linear isomorphisms $e\colon\mathbb R^k\to E_m$) of $E_m$, with $GL_k(\mathbb R)$ acting on the right by $e\cdot h=e\circ h$.

We fix a **[[Def - Lie Group Homomorphism|Lie group homomorphism]]** $\varphi\colon G\to H$, that is, a smooth map $\varphi$ with $\varphi(g_1g_2)=\varphi(g_1)\varphi(g_2)$ (so $\varphi(e_G)=e_H$ and $\varphi(g^{-1})=\varphi(g)^{-1}$). When $G$ is a subgroup of $H$ and the inclusion $\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow H$ is the homomorphism, we always take $G$ to be a **closed** subgroup, so that by the [[Thm - The Closed Subgroup Theorem|closed subgroup theorem]] — a closed subgroup of a Lie group is an embedded Lie subgroup — $G$ is an embedded submanifold of $H$ and $\iota$ is a smooth embedding. For the frame-bundle version we take a closed subgroup $G\le GL_k(\mathbb R)$.

Given a principal $G$-bundle $P\to M$, an $H$-space $H$ under right multiplication, and $\varphi$, we form the quotient $(P\times H)/G$ of the product manifold $P\times H$ by a $G$-action defined below; its class of $(p,h)$ is written $[p,h]$, and the quotient projection is $q\colon P\times H\to(P\times H)/G$. We write $\operatorname{pr}_1\colon P\times H\to P$ and $\operatorname{pr}_2\colon P\times H\to H$ for the two projections. For a Euclidean vector bundle $(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$, $O(E)\subset\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ denotes the set of orthonormal frames, and for an oriented Euclidean bundle $SO(E)\subset O(E)$ the set of positively oriented orthonormal frames.

> [!note] This is a compound page
> It defines three interlocking notions — the **extension** $P\times_\varphi H$ of a principal bundle along a homomorphism, a **$G$-structure** on a vector bundle, and a **reduction** of the structure group of a principal bundle — because they are three views of one operation: extension pushes a bundle's structure group forward along $\varphi$, and a reduction (equivalently, for a subgroup, a $G$-structure) is a chosen inverse of that operation. The three cannot be motivated or verified apart from one another, so they share a page.

> [!warning] Convention: a source misprint
> Bär (the second source, §2.2) writes the descended $H$-action as living on "$P\times_\varphi G$" in the line "This $H$-action on $P\times_\varphi G$ is free". The total space is $P\times_\varphi H=(P\times H)/G$, not $P\times_\varphi G$; the symbol $G$ there is a typographical slip for $H$. We use the corrected form $P\times_\varphi H$ everywhere.

# Axiom Motivation

A [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal bundle]] carries a structure group, and the structure group is the smallest amount of data that says *which symmetries relate one local trivialisation to another*: the transition functions of a principal $G$-bundle take values in $G$. Two questions then present themselves, and they are inverse to each other. First, if we have a $G$-bundle and a homomorphism $\varphi\colon G\to H$ into a *larger* group, can we manufacture from it an $H$-bundle in a canonical way — can we *enlarge* the structure group? Second, if we have an $H$-bundle, can we *shrink* its structure group down to a subgroup $G\le H$ — can we find a $G$-bundle that gives back our $H$-bundle upon enlarging? The first operation always succeeds and is called **extension**; the second is a genuine geometric question with a yes-or-no answer that carries content, and a witness to a "yes" is called a **reduction**.

The reason to care is that *extra geometric structure on a vector bundle is exactly a reduction of its structure group*. This is the single most useful dictionary in the subject, and it is worth seeing why it must be true before it is stated. A rank-$k$ real vector bundle $E$ has structure group $GL_k(\mathbb R)$: with no further structure, the frames of $E_m$ form a torsor under all of $GL_k(\mathbb R)$, because any basis can be turned into any other by an invertible matrix. Now suppose $E$ carries a fibre metric. Among all frames, the *orthonormal* ones form a distinguished subset, and any orthonormal frame can be turned into any other orthonormal frame by — and only by — an orthogonal matrix. So the orthonormal frames form a torsor under $O(k)\le GL_k(\mathbb R)$: the metric has cut the structure group down from $GL_k(\mathbb R)$ to $O(k)$. A metric *is* an $O(k)$-reduction. The same happens for every kind of pointwise-linear-algebraic structure — an orientation cuts to $GL_k^+(\mathbb R)$, a volume form to $SL_k(\mathbb R)$, a complex structure to $GL_m(\mathbb C)$, a Hermitian metric to $U(m)$ — and the reason is always the identical torsor argument: *the frames compatible with the structure are a torsor under the subgroup preserving the structure*. Haydys makes this the definition (his Definition 26): a $G$-structure on $E$ is a $G$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

Why the definitions have the exact clauses they have is best seen by asking what breaks if a clause is dropped. The extension is built as the quotient $(P\times H)/G$ by the twisted action $(p,h)\cdot g=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)$. Consider first the choice of a **twisted** rather than a naive diagonal action. If we quotiented $P\times H$ by $(p,h)\cdot g=(pg,h)$ instead — leaving $H$ untouched — we would get $(P/G)\times H=M\times H$, the *trivial* $H$-bundle, no matter what $P$ was; all the topological information in $P$ would be discarded. The twist $h\mapsto\varphi(g)^{-1}h$ is precisely what threads $P$'s transition data through $\varphi$ into the new bundle, so that (as the transition-function computation below shows) the extension's cocycle is $\varphi\circ g_{\alpha\beta}$. Drop the twist and the construction becomes content-free. Next, the requirement that $G$ act **freely** on $P$ is what makes the twisted action free on $P\times H$ (if $(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)=(p,h)$ then $pg=p$ forces $g=e$), and freeness is in turn what lets the [[Thm - Free Proper Actions Give Principal Bundles|quotient be a manifold]] and the residual $H$-action be free on fibres; without it $[p,h]$ would not determine $h$ up to the $H$-action and $P\times_\varphi H$ would not be a principal bundle at all. Finally, for a $G$-structure the requirement that $G\le GL_k(\mathbb R)$ be **closed** is what makes a $G$-subbundle an *embedded* submanifold of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and makes the reduction $\leftrightarrow$ subbundle dictionary a genuine equivalence: a non-closed subgroup (for instance an irrational line in the torus $T^2\le GL_2(\mathbb C)$) is only an immersed subgroup, its "subbundle" is not embedded, and the local model $U\times G\hookrightarrow U\times H$ ceases to be an embedding, so the identification of reductions with subbundles fails.

One should also see that reduction can *fail* — otherwise the notion would be vacuous. A rank-$2$ real bundle admits an $O(2)$-structure always (every bundle has a metric, by a partition of unity), but it admits an $SO(2)$-structure — equivalently a $U(1)$-structure, since $SO(2)\cong U(1)$ — if and only if it is orientable, and there are non-orientable rank-$2$ bundles (the Möbius line bundle plus a trivial line, verified below). So "$E$ has a $U(1)$-structure" is a real constraint, not an automatic feature; a reduction is a piece of geometry, and its non-existence is a topological obstruction. This is the entire reason the language is worth having: it converts questions about extra structure on bundles into questions about lifting structure groups, where the tools of characteristic classes (chapter VI) and classification (chapter III §3.6) apply.

# The Definition

## Extension of the structure group along a homomorphism

Let $\pi\colon P\to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle and let $\varphi\colon G\to H$ be a Lie group homomorphism. Make $G$ act on the product manifold $P\times H$ on the right by
$$(p,h)\cdot g:=(p\cdot g,\ \varphi(g)^{-1}h)\qquad(p\in P,\ h\in H,\ g\in G).$$
This is a right action: for the identity, $(p,h)\cdot e=(pe,\varphi(e)^{-1}h)=(p,h)$; and for composition,
$$\big((p,h)\cdot g\big)\cdot g'=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)\cdot g'=\big(pgg',\ \varphi(g')^{-1}\varphi(g)^{-1}h\big)=\big(p(gg'),\ \varphi(gg')^{-1}h\big)=(p,h)\cdot(gg')\qquad(\text{since }\varphi(g')^{-1}\varphi(g)^{-1}=(\varphi(g)\varphi(g'))^{-1}=\varphi(gg')^{-1}).$$

> [!note] Definition (extension / associated principal bundle)
> The **$H$-principal bundle associated to $P$ with respect to $\varphi$** is
> $$P\times_\varphi H:=(P\times H)/G,$$
> the quotient of $P\times H$ by the action above, with projection $\pi'\colon P\times_\varphi H\to M$, $\pi'[p,h]:=\pi(p)$, and with the right $H$-action
> $$[p,h]\cdot h':=[p,\,hh']\qquad(h'\in H).$$
> When $\varphi=\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow H$ is the inclusion of a closed subgroup, $P\times_\iota H$ is said to be obtained from $P$ by **extension of the structure group** from $G$ to $H$.

That this construction deserves the name — that $(P\times_\varphi H,\pi',M)$ really is a principal $H$-bundle, that $\pi'$ and the $H$-action are well defined and smooth — is not automatic and is proved in full immediately below.

> [!note] Proposition 1 (the extension is a principal $H$-bundle)
> For every principal $G$-bundle $\pi\colon P\to M$ and every Lie group homomorphism $\varphi\colon G\to H$, the triple $(P\times_\varphi H,\pi',M)$ with the right $H$-action $[p,h]\cdot h'=[p,hh']$ is a principal $H$-bundle. (Bär proves this for $G$ compact and asserts the general case; we prove it for all $G$.)
>
> > [!note]- Complete formal proof
> > We must produce: (0) that the $G$-action on $P\times H$ is smooth, free and proper; (1) the smooth manifold structure on $P\times_\varphi H$ and the smooth surjective submersion $\pi'$; (2) that the $H$-action descends, is smooth, fibre-preserving and free; (3) that $H$ is transitive on each fibre of $\pi'$; (4) $H$-equivariant local trivialisations. We use two standing facts about $P$: because $P$ is a principal $G$-bundle, **$G$ acts freely on $P$**, and $P$ admits **$G$-equivariant local trivialisations** $\psi_U\colon\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times G$ with $\psi_U(pg)=\psi_U(p)g$ (both from [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|the definition of a principal bundle]]).
> >
> > **Step 0a — the $G$-action on $P\times H$ is smooth and free.** Smoothness is clear: $(p,h,g)\mapsto(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)$ is a composite of the smooth bundle action, the smooth $\varphi$, inversion in $H$, and multiplication in $H$. For freeness, suppose $(p,h)\cdot g=(p,h)$, i.e. $pg=p$ and $\varphi(g)^{-1}h=h$. From $pg=p$ and the **freeness of $G$ on $P$** we get $g=e$; the second equation is then automatic. Hence the stabiliser of every point is trivial and the action is free.
> >
> > **Step 0b — the $G$-action on $P\times H$ is proper.** We use the sequential criterion for properness of a smooth action of a Lie group on a second-countable manifold (from [[Def - Free, Transitive, Effective, and Proper Group Actions|the definition of a proper action]]): an action $X\times G\to X$ is proper if and only if, whenever $x_n\to x$ in $X$ and $x_n\cdot g_n\to y$ in $X$, the sequence $(g_n)$ has a subsequence convergent in $G$. Let $(p_n,h_n)\to(p,h)$ and $(p_n,h_n)\cdot g_n=(p_ng_n,\varphi(g_n)^{-1}h_n)\to(p',h')$ in $P\times H$. Reading off the first factors, $p_n\to p$ and $p_ng_n\to p'$ in $P$. Applying $\pi$ (continuous) gives $\pi(p_ng_n)=\pi(p_n)\to\pi(p)$ and $\pi(p_ng_n)\to\pi(p')$, so $\pi(p')=\pi(p)=:m$, and $p,p'$ lie in the fibre $P_m$. Choose a $G$-equivariant trivialisation $\psi_U\colon\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times G$ over a neighbourhood $U\ni m$. Since $p_n\to p$ and $p_ng_n\to p'$ with $p,p'\in\pi^{-1}(U)$, for all large $n$ both $p_n$ and $p_ng_n$ lie in $\pi^{-1}(U)$; write $\psi_U(p_n)=(m_n,a_n)$, and then by **equivariance** $\psi_U(p_ng_n)=\psi_U(p_n)g_n=(m_n,a_ng_n)$. Continuity of $\psi_U$ gives $a_n\to a$ and $a_ng_n\to b$ in $G$ (where $\psi_U(p)=(m,a)$, $\psi_U(p')=(m,b)$), so
> > $$g_n=a_n^{-1}(a_ng_n)\longrightarrow a^{-1}b\qquad(\text{by continuity of inversion and multiplication in }G).$$
> > Thus $(g_n)$ converges, in particular it has a convergent subsequence, and the action is proper.
> >
> > **Step 1 — manifold structure and the projection $\pi'$.** By Steps 0a–0b the $G$-action on $P\times H$ is smooth, free and proper, so by [[Thm - Free Proper Actions Give Principal Bundles|the theorem that free proper actions give principal bundles]] — a smooth free proper right action of a Lie group $G$ on a manifold $N$ makes $N\to N/G$ a principal $G$-bundle, in particular $N/G$ is a smooth manifold, the quotient map $q$ is a smooth surjective submersion, and $q$ admits smooth local sections, and every smooth map out of $N$ constant on $G$-orbits descends to a smooth map on $N/G$ — the quotient $P\times_\varphi H=(P\times H)/G$ is a smooth manifold and $q\colon P\times H\to P\times_\varphi H$ is a smooth surjective submersion. The map $\pi\circ\operatorname{pr}_1\colon P\times H\to M$, $(p,h)\mapsto\pi(p)$, is **constant on $G$-orbits**: $\pi\circ\operatorname{pr}_1\big((p,h)g\big)=\pi(pg)=\pi(p)$ (by fibre-preservation of the action on $P$). Hence it descends to a unique smooth $\pi'\colon P\times_\varphi H\to M$ with $\pi'\circ q=\pi\circ\operatorname{pr}_1$, i.e. $\pi'[p,h]=\pi(p)$; this is exactly the formula in the definition, now shown well defined and smooth. It is **surjective** because $\pi$ is, and a **submersion** because $\pi'\circ q=\pi\circ\operatorname{pr}_1$ is a submersion (a composite of the submersions $\operatorname{pr}_1$ and $\pi$) while $q$ is a submersion: at any point $d(\pi'\circ q)=d\pi'\circ dq$ is surjective and $dq$ is surjective, so $d\pi'$ is surjective.
> >
> > **Step 2 — the $H$-action descends, is free and fibre-preserving.** Let $H$ act on $P\times H$ on the right by $(p,h)\cdot h'=(p,hh')$; this is a smooth right action. It **commutes** with the $G$-action:
> > $$\big((p,h)\cdot g\big)\cdot h'=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)\cdot h'=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}hh')=\big((p,hh')\big)\cdot g=\big((p,h)\cdot h'\big)\cdot g\qquad(\text{by associativity in }H).$$
> > Therefore, for each fixed $h'$, the map $(p,h)\mapsto(p,hh')$ carries $G$-orbits to $G$-orbits and descends to a well-defined map $[p,h]\mapsto[p,hh']$; smoothness of the descended map follows because its composite with the surjective submersion $q$ is the smooth map $q\circ(\cdot\,h')$ (descent of smooth maps through $q$). This is the action $[p,h]\cdot h'=[p,hh']$, and it is a right $H$-action since $[p,h]\cdot(h'h'')=[p,h(h'h'')]=[p,(hh')h'']=([p,h]\cdot h')\cdot h''$. It is **fibre-preserving**: $\pi'([p,h]\cdot h')=\pi'[p,hh']=\pi(p)=\pi'[p,h]$. It is **free**: if $[p,hh']=[p,h]$, then by definition of the quotient there is $g\in G$ with
> > $$(p,hh')=(p,h)\cdot g=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}h)\ \Longrightarrow\ p=pg\ \Longrightarrow\ g=e\ (\text{freeness of }G\text{ on }P)\ \Longrightarrow\ hh'=\varphi(e)^{-1}h=h\ \Longrightarrow\ h'=e.$$
> >
> > **Step 3 — $H$ is transitive on each fibre of $\pi'$.** The fibre of $\pi'$ over $m$ is $\{[p,h]:\pi(p)=m\}=q(P_m\times H)$. Take two of its points $[p,h]$ and $[p',h']$ with $\pi(p)=\pi(p')=m$. Since $P$ is a principal $G$-bundle, **$G$ acts transitively on the fibre $P_m$**, so there is $g\in G$ with $p'=pg$. Then
> > $$[p',h']=[pg,h']=\big[(p,\varphi(g)h')\cdot g\big]=[p,\varphi(g)h']\qquad(\text{because }(p,\varphi(g)h')\cdot g=(pg,\varphi(g)^{-1}\varphi(g)h')=(pg,h')),$$
> > and therefore $[p',h']=[p,\varphi(g)h']=[p,h]\cdot\big(h^{-1}\varphi(g)h'\big)$. So any two points of the fibre differ by the $H$-action; $H$ is transitive on fibres.
> >
> > **Step 4 — $H$-equivariant local trivialisations.** Let $U\subset M$ be open and small enough that $P$ admits a smooth local section $s\colon U\to P$, $\pi\circ s=\operatorname{id}_U$ (such $s$ exist because $P$ is locally trivial: over a trivialising $U$, take $s(m)=\psi_U^{-1}(m,e)$). Define
> > $$\Psi_U\colon U\times H\to(\pi')^{-1}(U),\qquad \Psi_U(m,h):=[s(m),h].$$
> > It is smooth (composite of $s$, the inclusion, and $q$) and covers the identity: $\pi'\big(\Psi_U(m,h)\big)=\pi(s(m))=m$. It is **$H$-equivariant**: $\Psi_U(m,h)\cdot h'=[s(m),h]\cdot h'=[s(m),hh']=\Psi_U(m,hh')$. We exhibit a smooth inverse. Given $[p,h']\in(\pi')^{-1}(U)$, set $m:=\pi(p)\in U$. Then $p$ and $s(m)$ lie in the same fibre $P_m$, so by freeness and transitivity of $G$ on $P_m$ there is a **unique** $g\in G$ with $p=s(m)g$; write $g=\tau(p)$. The assignment $p\mapsto\tau(p)$ is smooth, because in the trivialisation $\psi_U(p)=(m,a(p))$ and $\psi_U(s(m))=(m,a_0(m))$ one has $p=s(m)\tau(p)\Leftrightarrow a(p)=a_0(m)\tau(p)$, whence $\tau(p)=a_0(\pi(p))^{-1}a(p)$, a composite of smooth maps (this is the smoothness of the local gauge established on [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the sections–triviality page]]). Now
> > $$[p,h']=[s(m)\tau(p),h']=[s(m),\varphi(\tau(p))h']\qquad(\text{by the identity of Step 3 with }g=\tau(p)),$$
> > so the map $\Phi_U([p,h']):=(m,\varphi(\tau(p))h')=(\pi(p),\varphi(\tau(p))h')$ is a well-defined smooth map $(\pi')^{-1}(U)\to U\times H$ (well defined because it is computed from the representative through the descent of $p\mapsto\varphi(\tau(p))h'$, which is $G$-invariant: replacing $(p,h')$ by $(pg_0,\varphi(g_0)^{-1}h')$ gives $\varphi(\tau(pg_0))\varphi(g_0)^{-1}h'=\varphi(\tau(p)g_0)\varphi(g_0)^{-1}h'=\varphi(\tau(p))h'$). One checks $\Phi_U\circ\Psi_U(m,h)=\Phi_U([s(m),h])=(m,\varphi(\tau(s(m)))h)=(m,\varphi(e)h)=(m,h)$ (since $s(m)=s(m)e$ gives $\tau(s(m))=e$) and $\Psi_U\circ\Phi_U([p,h'])=\Psi_U(m,\varphi(\tau(p))h')=[s(m),\varphi(\tau(p))h']=[p,h']$. Thus $\Psi_U$ is a diffeomorphism with smooth inverse $\Phi_U$, and it is $H$-equivariant, so it is a principal-bundle trivialisation.
> >
> > **Conclusion.** Steps 1–4 verify every clause of the [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|definition of a principal $H$-bundle]] for $(P\times_\varphi H,\pi',M)$: a smooth manifold total space, a smooth surjective submersion to $M$, a smooth free fibre-preserving $H$-action transitive on fibres, and $H$-equivariant local trivialisations. Therefore $P\times_\varphi H$ is a principal $H$-bundle, for every Lie group $G$. $\blacksquare$

## $G$-structures on a vector bundle

Let $E\to M$ be a real vector bundle of rank $k$, with frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, a principal $GL_k(\mathbb R)$-bundle. A **$G$-subbundle** of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$, for a closed subgroup $G\le GL_k(\mathbb R)$, is a subset $P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ that is an embedded submanifold, is invariant under the restricted right $G$-action ($p\in P,\ g\in G\Rightarrow pg\in P$), and is such that $\pi|_P\colon P\to M$, with this restricted action, is itself a principal $G$-bundle.

> [!note] Definition ($G$-structure, structure group)
> For a closed subgroup $G\le GL_k(\mathbb R)$, a **$G$-structure** on $E$ is a $G$-subbundle $P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$. In this case $G$ is called the **structure group** of $E$ (relative to that $G$-structure).

This is Haydys's Definition 26. The motivating instance, worked in full in the Examples section, is a fibre metric on $E$: its orthonormal frames form an $O(k)$-structure $O(E)\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

## Reduction of the structure group

> [!note] Definition (reduction of the structure group)
> Let $Q\to M$ be a principal $H$-bundle and $\varphi\colon G\to H$ a Lie group homomorphism. A **reduction of $Q$ to the structure group $G$** (along $\varphi$) is a principal $G$-bundle $P\to M$ together with an isomorphism $P\times_\varphi H\cong Q$ of principal $H$-bundles (an $H$-equivariant diffeomorphism covering $\operatorname{id}_M$). When $\varphi=\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow H$ is the inclusion of a closed subgroup one speaks simply of a reduction of the structure group of $Q$ to $G$.

For $Q=\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ and $H=GL_k(\mathbb R)$, a reduction to a closed subgroup $G$ is the same datum as a $G$-structure. That these two definitions — the subbundle one and the reduction one — genuinely coincide is the content of the next proposition; it is what licenses using the words interchangeably.

> [!note] Proposition 2 (reductions are the same as $G$-subbundles)
> Let $Q\to M$ be a principal $H$-bundle and $G\le H$ a closed subgroup with inclusion $\iota$. There is a bijection, natural in $Q$, between $G$-subbundles $P\subseteq Q$ and reductions of $Q$ to $G$ (taken up to isomorphism of reductions): a $G$-subbundle $P\subseteq Q$ gives the reduction $\Phi\colon P\times_\iota H\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Q$, $[p,h]\mapsto p\cdot h$; and a reduction $\Psi\colon P\times_\iota H\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Q$ gives the $G$-subbundle $\Psi(\{[p,e]:p\in P\})\subseteq Q$. (Here a $G$-subbundle of a principal $H$-bundle $Q$ means an embedded submanifold $P\subseteq Q$ invariant under the restricted $G$-action and forming a principal $G$-bundle over $M$.)
>
> > [!note]- Complete formal proof
> > We prove the two directions and that they are mutually inverse. Throughout, on a $G$-subbundle $P\subseteq Q$ the $G$-action is the **restriction to $G\le H$ of the $H$-action on $Q$**, so $p\cdot g$ (as a $G$-action) equals $p\cdot g$ (as the $H$-action, with $g=\iota(g)\in H$); we use this without further comment.
> >
> > **Direction 1 (subbundle $\Rightarrow$ reduction). Assume** $P\subseteq Q$ is a $G$-subbundle. Define $\Phi\colon P\times_\iota H\to Q$ on representatives by $\Phi([p,h])=p\cdot h$ (the $H$-action of $Q$).
> >
> > *$\Phi$ is well defined.* If $[p,h]=[p',h']$ then $(p',h')=(p,h)\cdot g=(pg,\iota(g)^{-1}h)=(pg,g^{-1}h)$ for some $g\in G$; hence $p'\cdot h'=(pg)\cdot(g^{-1}h)=p\cdot(gg^{-1}h)=p\cdot h$ (associativity of the $H$-action, with $g\in H$). So the value is independent of the representative.
> >
> > *$\Phi$ is smooth.* The map $P\times H\to Q$, $(p,h)\mapsto p\cdot h$ is smooth (restriction of the smooth $H$-action of $Q$ to the embedded submanifold $P\times H$), and it is constant on the $G$-orbits by the previous paragraph, so it descends to a smooth map on the quotient $P\times_\iota H$ (descent of smooth maps through the quotient submersion $q$).
> >
> > *$\Phi$ is $H$-equivariant and covers $\operatorname{id}_M$.* Equivariance: $\Phi([p,h]\cdot h')=\Phi([p,hh'])=p\cdot(hh')=(p\cdot h)\cdot h'=\Phi([p,h])\cdot h'$. Over the base: $\pi_Q(\Phi([p,h]))=\pi_Q(p\cdot h)=\pi_Q(p)=\pi_P(p)=\pi'([p,h])$, using that the $H$-action of $Q$ is fibre-preserving and that $\pi_Q|_P=\pi_P$ (as $P\subseteq Q$ lies over $M$).
> >
> > *$\Phi$ is bijective.* **Injective:** suppose $\Phi([p,h])=\Phi([p',h'])$, i.e. $p\cdot h=p'\cdot h'$. Applying $\pi_Q$ gives $\pi_P(p)=\pi_P(p')=:m$, so $p,p'\in P_m$. Since $P$ is a principal $G$-bundle, **$G$ acts transitively (and freely) on $P_m$**, so there is a unique $g\in G$ with $p'=p\cdot g$. Because $Q$ is a principal $H$-bundle, **$H$ acts freely on $Q$**, so from $p\cdot h=p'\cdot h'=(p\cdot g)\cdot h'=p\cdot(gh')$ we get $h=gh'$, whence $h'=g^{-1}h$. Therefore $[p',h']=[pg,g^{-1}h]=[(p,h)\cdot g]=[p,h]$; the map is injective. **Surjective:** let $x\in Q$, $m:=\pi_Q(x)$. The fibre $P_m$ is nonempty (as $P\to M$ is a bundle), so pick $p\in P_m$; since $H$ is transitive on the fibre $Q_m$ there is $h\in H$ with $x=p\cdot h$, and then $x=\Phi([p,h])$. Thus $\Phi$ is a bijection.
> >
> > *Conclusion of Direction 1.* $\Phi$ is a smooth $H$-equivariant bijection covering $\operatorname{id}_M$ between principal $H$-bundles. A fibre-preserving $H$-equivariant smooth bijection of principal $H$-bundles over $M$ is automatically a diffeomorphism: on each fibre it is $H$-equivariant between $H$-torsors hence a bijection, and in local trivialisations it takes the form $(m,h)\mapsto(m,c(m)h)$ for a smooth $H$-valued map $c$ (its inverse is $(m,h)\mapsto(m,c(m)^{-1}h)$, smooth), so it is a diffeomorphism. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism of principal $H$-bundles, and $(P,\Phi)$ is a reduction of $Q$ to $G$.
> >
> > **Direction 2 (reduction $\Rightarrow$ subbundle). Assume** $(P,\Psi)$ is a reduction, $\Psi\colon P\times_\iota H\xrightarrow{\cong}Q$. Consider the map $j\colon P\to Q$, $j(p):=\Psi([p,e])$.
> >
> > *$j$ is injective.* First $p\mapsto[p,e]$ is injective: if $[p,e]=[p',e]$ then $(p',e)=(pg,\iota(g)^{-1})=(pg,g^{-1})$ for some $g\in G$, forcing $g^{-1}=e$, so $g=e$ and $p'=p$. Composing with the injective $\Psi$ gives $j$ injective.
> >
> > *$j$ is $G$-equivariant into the restricted $H$-action.* For $g\in G$,
> > $$j(pg)=\Psi([pg,e])=\Psi([p,\iota(g)e])=\Psi([p,g])=\Psi([p,e]\cdot g)=\Psi([p,e])\cdot g=j(p)\cdot g,$$
> > where $[pg,e]=[p,\iota(g)e]$ holds because $(p,\iota(g))\cdot g=(pg,\iota(g)^{-1}\iota(g))=(pg,e)$, and $\Psi$ is $H$-equivariant (restricted to $g\in G\le H$).
> >
> > *$j$ is a smooth embedding with image a $G$-subbundle.* Work over a trivialising open set $U$ for $P$ with section $\sigma\colon U\to P$; then $P|_U\cong U\times G$ via $\sigma(m)g\leftrightarrow(m,g)$, and (by Step 4 of Proposition 1) $P\times_\iota H|_U\cong U\times H$ via $[\sigma(m),h]\leftrightarrow(m,h)$. Under these identifications $p=\sigma(m)g\mapsto[\sigma(m)g,e]=[\sigma(m),\iota(g)]=[\sigma(m),g]$, i.e. the map $P\to P\times_\iota H$, $p\mapsto[p,e]$ becomes the inclusion $U\times G\hookrightarrow U\times H$, $(m,g)\mapsto(m,g)$. Because $G\le H$ is a **closed** subgroup, by [[Thm - The Closed Subgroup Theorem|the closed subgroup theorem]] it is an embedded Lie subgroup, so $U\times G\hookrightarrow U\times H$ is a smooth embedding; composing with the diffeomorphism $\Psi$, the map $j$ is locally a smooth embedding, and being globally injective it is a smooth embedding. Its image $j(P)=\Psi(\{[p,e]\})$ is locally $\Psi(U\times G)$, an embedded submanifold; it is invariant under the restricted $G$-action by the equivariance of $j$; and $j\colon P\to j(P)$ is a $G$-equivariant diffeomorphism, which transports the principal-$G$-bundle structure of $P$ onto $j(P)$. Hence $j(P)$ is a $G$-subbundle of $Q$.
> >
> > **The two directions are mutually inverse.** Starting from a $G$-subbundle $P\subseteq Q$, Direction 1 builds $\Phi([p,h])=ph$; then Direction 2 applied to $(P,\Phi)$ returns $\Phi(\{[p,e]\})=\{p\cdot e:p\in P\}=P$, the original subbundle. Conversely, starting from a reduction $(P,\Psi)$, Direction 2 builds the subbundle $P':=j(P)=\Psi(\{[p,e]\})$; Direction 1 applied to $P'$ builds $\Phi'([p',h])=p'h$, and the map $P\times_\iota H\to P'\times_\iota H$ induced by the $G$-equivariant diffeomorphism $j\colon P\to P'$ intertwines $\Psi$ with $\Phi'$ (both send $[p,h]$, respectively $[j(p),h]$, to $\Psi([p,h])=\Psi([p,e]\cdot h)=\Psi([p,e])\cdot h=j(p)\cdot h=\Phi'([j(p),h])$), so $(P,\Psi)$ and $(P',\Phi')$ are isomorphic reductions. Therefore the two constructions are inverse bijections. $\blacksquare$

# Categorical / Structural Definition

Extension is a functor and reduction is the search for a preimage under it. Fix the base $M$. Let $\mathbf{Prin}_G(M)$ be the groupoid whose objects are principal $G$-bundles over $M$ and whose morphisms are $G$-bundle isomorphisms covering $\operatorname{id}_M$. A Lie group homomorphism $\varphi\colon G\to H$ induces a functor
$$\varphi_*\colon\mathbf{Prin}_G(M)\longrightarrow\mathbf{Prin}_H(M),\qquad P\longmapsto P\times_\varphi H,$$
sending a $G$-bundle isomorphism $f\colon P\to P'$ to the $H$-bundle isomorphism $[p,h]\mapsto[f(p),h]$ (well defined because $f$ is $G$-equivariant). This functor is compatible with composition of homomorphisms, $(\psi\circ\varphi)_*\cong\psi_*\circ\varphi_*$ (both send $P$ to $P\times_{\psi\varphi}K$, via $[p,k]\mapsto[[p,e_H],k]$), and $(\operatorname{id}_G)_*\cong\operatorname{id}$. In this language a **reduction of $Q\in\mathbf{Prin}_H(M)$ along $\varphi$ is an object $P\in\mathbf{Prin}_G(M)$ together with an isomorphism $\varphi_*P\cong Q$** — a choice of preimage of $Q$ under $\varphi_*$, up to isomorphism. The failure of $\varphi_*$ to be essentially surjective is exactly the obstruction to reducing every $H$-bundle to $G$; the failure of injectivity on isomorphism classes measures how many inequivalent reductions a single $H$-bundle can carry.

The torsor picture makes the same statement concrete. A principal $G$-bundle assigns to each $m$ a right $G$-torsor $P_m$. Extension replaces the torsor $P_m$ by the $H$-torsor $P_m\times_G H=(P_m\times H)/G$, the "induced torsor" of representation theory (induction of the trivial $G$-set to $H$); a reduction chooses, coherently in $m$, a $G$-sub-torsor of each $H$-torsor $Q_m$. For $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ the $H=GL_k(\mathbb R)$-torsor is the set of all frames of $E_m$, and a $G$-structure chooses the $G$-torsor of frames compatible with the extra structure.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** A $G$-structure is *the choice of a preferred class of frames closed under the subgroup $G$*, and a reduction is *a compatible such choice made bundle-wide*. Operationally, to hand someone a $G$-structure is to tell them, in each fibre, which frames are "allowed", subject to: the allowed frames form one $G$-orbit, and the allowedness varies smoothly. Every named geometric structure on a vector bundle is of this form, and the dictionary (proved on [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the Euclidean-and-Hermitian reductions page]]) reads: a fibre metric is an $O(k)$-structure; a metric together with an orientation is an $SO(k)$-structure; an orientation alone is a $GL_k^+(\mathbb R)$-structure; a fibrewise volume form is an $SL_k(\mathbb R)$-structure; a complex structure (for $k=2m$) is a $GL_m(\mathbb C)$-structure; a Hermitian metric on a complex bundle is a $U(m)$-structure. This is Haydys's Remark following Definition 26: *orientations and scalar products on the fibres correspond to reductions of the structure group.*

**Transition functions.** Extension has a one-line description at the level of cocycles, which is often the fastest way to compute with it. Recall (from [[Def - Transition Functions and the Cocycle Condition|the cocycle description]]) that a principal $G$-bundle $P$ with local sections $s_\alpha\colon U_\alpha\to P$ has transition functions $g_{\alpha\beta}\colon U_{\alpha\beta}\to G$ defined by $s_\beta=s_\alpha\cdot g_{\alpha\beta}$ on $U_{\alpha\beta}=U_\alpha\cap U_\beta$.

> [!note] Remark (extension composes transition functions with $\varphi$)
> With $s'_\alpha(u):=[s_\alpha(u),e]$ the induced local sections of $P'=P\times_\varphi H$, the transition functions of $P'$ are $g'_{\alpha\beta}=\varphi\circ g_{\alpha\beta}$.
>
> *Proof.* On $U_{\alpha\beta}$ we compute $s'_\beta$ in two ways. On one hand, by definition of transition functions, $s'_\beta=s'_\alpha\cdot g'_{\alpha\beta}=[s_\alpha,e]\cdot g'_{\alpha\beta}=[s_\alpha,g'_{\alpha\beta}]$ (using $[p,e]\cdot h'=[p,h']$). On the other hand,
> $$s'_\beta=[s_\beta,e]=[s_\alpha g_{\alpha\beta},e]=\big[(s_\alpha,\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta}))\cdot g_{\alpha\beta}\big]=[s_\alpha,\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta})]\qquad(\text{since }(s_\alpha,\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta}))\cdot g_{\alpha\beta}=(s_\alpha g_{\alpha\beta},\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta})^{-1}\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta}))=(s_\alpha g_{\alpha\beta},e)).$$
> Comparing, $[s_\alpha,g'_{\alpha\beta}]=[s_\alpha,\varphi(g_{\alpha\beta})]$; since the $H$-action is free on fibres of $P'$ (Proposition 1, Step 2), the second entries agree: $g'_{\alpha\beta}=\varphi\circ g_{\alpha\beta}$. $\blacksquare$ The proof that these $g'_{\alpha\beta}$ indeed reconstruct $P'$, and the full cocycle account of the correspondence, is on [[Thm - Principal Bundles are Classified by Cocycles|the classification-by-cocycles page]].

So extension is "post-compose the cocycle with $\varphi$", and reduction is "can the $H$-cocycle be conjugated into the image $\varphi(G)$?" — the group-theoretic heart of every obstruction computation in chapter III §3.6 and chapter VI.

**Where this recurs.** The same enlarge/shrink pair organises: the passage from a general linear connection to a metric one (structure group $GL_k\to O(k)$); the definition of a spin structure as a reduction of the frame bundle's $SO(n)$-structure along the double cover $\operatorname{Spin}(n)\to SO(n)$ (chapter VIII), which is an extension along a *non-injective* homomorphism run backwards and is genuinely obstructed (by $w_2$); and complexification $E\rightsquigarrow E\otimes\mathbb C$, which is extension of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ along $GL_k(\mathbb R)\hookrightarrow GL_k(\mathbb C)$. In representation theory the identical formula $(P\times H)/G$ with a representation $\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{Aut}(V)$ in place of $H$ produces [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated vector bundles]] $P\times_\rho V$; extension of the structure group is the special case where the representation is a homomorphism into another group acting on itself.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the orthonormal frame bundle $O(E)\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.** Let $(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ be a Euclidean vector bundle of rank $k$ and $O(E)=\{e\in\operatorname{Fr}(E):e\text{ is orthonormal}\}$. We verify $O(E)$ is an $O(k)$-structure clause by clause. *Invariance:* if $e=(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ is orthonormal and $h\in O(k)$, then $(eh)_i=\sum_j e_j h_{ji}$ and $\langle(eh)_i,(eh)_{i'}\rangle=\sum_{j,j'}h_{ji}h_{j'i'}\langle e_j,e_{j'}\rangle=\sum_j h_{ji}h_{ji'}=(h^t h)_{ii'}=\delta_{ii'}$ (since $h^th=1$ for $h\in O(k)$), so $eh\in O(E)$; and conversely two orthonormal frames of $E_m$ differ by exactly an element of $O(k)$ (the change-of-basis matrix $h$ between orthonormal bases satisfies $h^th=1$), so $O(k)$ acts freely and transitively on each $O(E)_m$. *Embedded subbundle and smoothness:* over any $U$ carrying a local frame, applying [[Thm - Gram-Schmidt Procedure|the Gram–Schmidt procedure]] to that frame yields a smooth orthonormal frame $e_O\colon U\to O(E)$, and $(m,h)\mapsto e_O(m)h$ is a diffeomorphism $U\times O(k)\to\pi^{-1}(U)\cap O(E)$ giving $O(E)$ the structure of a smooth embedded $O(k)$-subbundle; the full verification (that Gram–Schmidt is smooth in the frame entries, so these charts are compatible) is carried out on [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reductions theorem page]], which we invoke here. Hence a Euclidean structure gives an $O(k)$-structure; conversely, given an $O(k)$-structure $P\subseteq\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ one recovers the metric by $\langle p x_1,p x_2\rangle:=x_1^t x_2$ for $p\in P_m$, $x_i\in\mathbb R^k$ (writing $v_i=p\cdot x_i$), which is independent of the choice of $p\in P_m$ precisely because any other choice is $p'=ph$ with $h\in O(k)$, and then $v_i=p'\cdot x'_i$ forces $x'_i=h^{-1}x_i$, so
$$(x_1')^t x_2'=(h^{-1}x_1)^t(h^{-1}x_2)=x_1^t(h^{-1})^th^{-1}x_2=x_1^tx_2\qquad(\text{since }(h^{-1})^th^{-1}=(hh^t)^{-1}=1\text{ for }h\in O(k)).$$

**Is an instance — the oriented orthonormal frame bundle $SO(E)$.** If $E$ is Euclidean and oriented, $SO(E)=\{e\in O(E):e\text{ positively oriented}\}$ is an $SO(k)$-structure. By the same computation as above, $SO(k)$ preserves orthonormality; it preserves orientation because $\det h=+1$ for $h\in SO(k)$; and any two positively oriented orthonormal bases differ by a matrix that is orthogonal ($h^th=1$) and orientation-preserving ($\det h>0$, hence $\det h=+1$ since $h$ is orthogonal), i.e. by an element of $SO(k)$. So $SO(k)$ acts freely and transitively on each fibre $SO(E)_m$, and $SO(E)$ is a smooth $SO(k)$-subbundle (embedded, as the positively oriented component of $O(E)$). Thus a metric together with an orientation is an $SO(k)$-structure.

**Is an instance — $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ inside $\operatorname{Fr}(E\otimes\mathbb C)$ (complexification as extension).** Let $E$ be a real rank-$k$ bundle and $E\otimes\mathbb C=E\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ its complexification, a complex rank-$k$ bundle. A real frame $e=(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ of $E_m$ is, after tensoring with $1\in\mathbb C$, a complex frame $(e_1\otimes 1,\dots,e_k\otimes 1)$ of $(E\otimes\mathbb C)_m$; this defines an embedding $\operatorname{Fr}(E)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Fr}(E\otimes\mathbb C)$ intertwining the right $GL_k(\mathbb R)$-action on the left with the restricted action of the closed subgroup $GL_k(\mathbb R)\le GL_k(\mathbb C)$ on the right (a real change of frame acts on the complexified frame by the same matrix, now viewed over $\mathbb C$). Its image is a $GL_k(\mathbb R)$-subbundle of $\operatorname{Fr}(E\otimes\mathbb C)$, so $\operatorname{Fr}(E\otimes\mathbb C)\cong\operatorname{Fr}(E)\times_{\iota}GL_k(\mathbb C)$ is the extension of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ along $GL_k(\mathbb R)\hookrightarrow GL_k(\mathbb C)$, by Proposition 2. This is the frame-bundle shadow of "a real bundle carries a canonical real structure on its complexification"; conjugation of frames is the involution whose fixed subbundle is $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$.

**Is NOT an instance — a non-orientable real rank-$2$ bundle has no $U(1)$-structure.** Since $SO(2)=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}\cong U(1)$ as a closed subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb R)$, a $U(1)$-structure on a real rank-$2$ bundle $E$ is exactly an $SO(2)$-structure, hence (by the two instances above and the reductions dictionary) a fibre metric together with an orientation. In particular a $U(1)$-structure forces $E$ to be orientable, because $SO(2)\le GL_2^+(\mathbb R)$ and an $SO(2)$-structure is in particular a $GL_2^+(\mathbb R)$-structure, which is an orientation. Now take $E=\gamma\oplus\underline{\mathbb R}$ over $S^1$, where $\gamma\to S^1$ is the Möbius line bundle and $\underline{\mathbb R}=S^1\times\mathbb R$ is the trivial line. The Möbius bundle $\gamma$ is non-orientable: covering $S^1$ by two arcs, any local trivialisations differ on one of the two overlaps by the sign $-1$, and no choice of local frames removes this sign — this is exactly the nontriviality of the Möbius bundle established, at the level of its $\{\pm1\}$-cocycle, on [[Ex - The Möbius Strip as a Mapping Torus is a Nontrivial Bundle|the Möbius-bundle page]]. Consequently the transition matrices of $E=\gamma\oplus\underline{\mathbb R}$ have determinant $-1$ on that overlap, and no change of frames can make them all lie in $GL_2^+(\mathbb R)$; so $E$ is non-orientable and admits **no** $GL_2^+(\mathbb R)$-structure, hence no $SO(2)=U(1)$-structure. A reduction is therefore a genuine question with a "no" answer here — the very fact that makes the theory nontrivial.

**Corollary (calibration) — reduction to the trivial group $\Leftrightarrow$ triviality.** *A principal $H$-bundle $Q\to M$ admits a reduction to the trivial group $\{e\}$ if and only if $Q$ is trivial.* (This is Bär's Example 2.2.11.)

> [!note]- Proof
> Take $\varphi=\iota\colon\{e\}\hookrightarrow H$. **($\Leftarrow$) Assume** $Q$ is trivial, $Q\cong M\times H$. A principal $\{e\}$-bundle over $M$ is a bundle whose fibres are single points, so $P:=M$ (with $\pi=\operatorname{id}$ and the only, trivial, $\{e\}$-action) is one, and its extension is
> $$P\times_\iota H=(M\times H)/\{e\}=M\times H\cong Q,$$
> since the $\{e\}$-action is trivial and the quotient does nothing. Hence $(M,\ \cong)$ is a reduction of $Q$ to $\{e\}$. **($\Rightarrow$) Assume** $Q$ admits a reduction to $\{e\}$: a principal $\{e\}$-bundle $P\to M$ with $P\times_\iota H\cong Q$. A principal $\{e\}$-bundle $P\to M$ has one-point fibres, so $\pi\colon P\to M$ is a bijective local diffeomorphism, i.e. a diffeomorphism, and its inverse is a global section $s=\pi^{-1}$; thus $P\cong M$ and $P\times_\iota H\cong M\times H$ is the trivial $H$-bundle. Therefore $Q\cong M\times H$ is trivial. Equivalently and more invariantly: by [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the sections–triviality theorem]] — a principal bundle is trivial if and only if it admits a global smooth section — a reduction of $Q$ to $\{e\}$ is the same as a global section of $Q$ (the image of $P\times\{e\}$ under the isomorphism is exactly a global section, by Proposition 2), so its existence is equivalent to triviality of $Q$. $\blacksquare$

**Calibration check.** Three quick verifications a reader can carry out from this page. (i) The extension of the *trivial* $G$-bundle $M\times G$ along $\varphi\colon G\to H$ is the trivial $H$-bundle: its transition functions are all $\varphi\circ 1=1$ by the transition-function remark, so it is trivial by classification, or directly $(M\times G)\times_\varphi H\cong M\times H$ via $[(m,g),h]\mapsto(m,\varphi(g)h)$. (ii) Extension along the identity $\operatorname{id}_G\colon G\to G$ returns the same bundle, $P\times_{\operatorname{id}}G\cong P$ via $[p,g]\mapsto pg$ (this is Direction 1 of Proposition 2 with $H=G$, $P=Q$). (iii) The structure group of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ for a rank-$k$ real bundle is $GL_k(\mathbb R)$, and "$E$ carries a metric" is the statement that $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ reduces to $O(k)$; since every vector bundle carries a metric (partition of unity), *every* $GL_k(\mathbb R)$-frame bundle reduces to $O(k)$ — the one reduction that is always available.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Euclidean, Hermitian, symplectic and complex structures as reductions *(from Gauge Theory III §3.2)*
> The dictionary between extra fibrewise structure and reductions is made precise and proved for every classical group on [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reductions theorem]]: metrics $\leftrightarrow O(k)$, volume forms $\leftrightarrow SL_k(\mathbb R)$, orientations $\leftrightarrow GL_k^+(\mathbb R)$, complex structures $\leftrightarrow GL_m(\mathbb C)$, Hermitian metrics $\leftrightarrow U(m)$.

> [!tip] Spin structures *(from Gauge Theory VIII)*
> A **spin structure** on an oriented Riemannian bundle is a reduction of its $SO(n)$-frame bundle along the double cover $\operatorname{Spin}(n)\to SO(n)$ — a lift of the structure group through a non-injective homomorphism. Its existence is obstructed by the second Stiefel–Whitney class, the first genuinely topological obstruction the reduction language will meet.

> [!tip] Classification and characteristic classes *(from Gauge Theory III §3.6 and VI)*
> "Which $H$-bundles reduce to $G$?" becomes, via the transition-function remark, "which $H$-cocycles are cohomologous to ones valued in $\varphi(G)$?", and the answer is computed by **characteristic classes**: the obstruction to reducing to $G$ lives in the cohomology of $M$ with coefficients built from $H/\varphi(G)$.
