# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth, oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension $n$, with metric $g = \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$; we write $|v|^2 = \langle v, v\rangle$ for $v \in T_mM$. Sections of a vector bundle $E \to M$ are written $\Gamma(E)$, and $\Omega^p(M; E) = \Gamma(\Lambda^p T^*M \otimes E)$. The musical isomorphism $\flat : T_mM \to T_m^*M$ sends $v \mapsto v^\flat = \langle v, \cdot\rangle$, with inverse $\sharp$; it is an isometry for the induced metric on $T_m^*M$. The interior product (contraction) of a form $\phi$ with a vector $v$ is $\iota_v \phi$, and $\wedge$ is the exterior product; see [[Def - Interior Product (Contraction with a Vector Field)|interior product]].

The oriented orthonormal frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO} = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(M) \to M$ is the principal $SO(n)$-bundle whose fibre over $m$ is the set of orientation-preserving linear isometries $p : \mathbb{R}^n \to T_mM$; equivalently, oriented orthonormal bases $(b_1, \dots, b_n) = (p(\varepsilon_1), \dots, p(\varepsilon_n))$ of $T_mM$, where $(\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n)$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The right $SO(n)$-action is $p \cdot g = p \circ g$. It is the reduction of the full [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]] $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ to the structure group $SO(n)$ determined by the metric and orientation (see [[Def - Reduction and Extension of the Structure Group|reduction of the structure group]]), and it carries the [[Def - Levi-Civita Connection|Levi-Civita connection]] as a principal connection $\omega \in \Omega^1(\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}; \mathfrak{so}(n))$ — the identification of the Levi-Civita connection with a principal connection on $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ is established in the Riemannian-geometry chapters and is taken as given here. ⚠️ [The dedicated construction of $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ with its principal Levi-Civita connection is a `[NEEDED FROM I–VI]` item; this page names it and uses only its defining properties.]

We use $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for the Clifford algebra of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the sign convention of the series, recalled from [[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication|the Clifford algebra page]]:

![[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication#The Definition]]

Concretely $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the associative unital $\mathbb{R}$-algebra generated by $e_1, \dots, e_n$ subject to $e_i e_j + e_j e_i = -2\delta_{ij}$, so that $e_i^2 = -1$ and $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ for $i \neq j$; it has real dimension $2^n$ with basis $\{e_I = e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k} : I = (i_1 < \dots < i_k)\}$. For a Euclidean space $U$, $\operatorname{Cl}(U)$ is defined the same way, so that $\mathbb{R}^n$ (or $U$) sits inside $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ as the degree-one part, and $u \cdot u = -|u|^2 \cdot 1$ for $u \in U$. We write $\operatorname{Aut}(A)$ for the group of unital $\mathbb{R}$-algebra automorphisms of an algebra $A$, and $\operatorname{End}(E)$ for the endomorphism bundle of $E$.

> [!warning] Convention: Clifford sign
> The series uses $u \cdot u = -|u|^2 \cdot 1$ (Haydys's convention). The vault's [[Def - Clifford Algebra|Clifford algebra page]] and its [[Thm - Clifford Algebra Universal Property|universal property]] are stated for a general quadratic form as $\varphi(v)^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1$. The dictionary is $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) = \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n, Q)$ with $Q(v) = -|v|^2$; every invocation of the universal property below applies it with this $Q$. The opposite convention $u \cdot u = +|u|^2$ yields $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n, +|\cdot|^2)$, a genuinely different algebra ($\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^2, +|\cdot|^2) \cong M_2(\mathbb{R})$ rather than $\mathbb{H}$); a module over one is not a module over the other, and the Dirac operator built from it would square with the wrong sign.

This is a compound page: it defines three interlocking notions — the **Clifford bundle** $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ of an oriented Riemannian manifold (a bundle of algebras, with a canonical connection), the **derivation property** of that connection with respect to the fibrewise Clifford product, and a **bundle of Clifford modules** over $M$ — because they are one package: the Clifford bundle is the algebra whose bundle-representations are the bundles of Clifford modules, and the connection is exactly the structure that will let the next page differentiate Clifford products and define the Dirac operator.

# Axiom Motivation

The goal of this chapter is to build, on a Riemannian manifold, a first-order differential operator $D$ whose square is a Laplacian up to a curvature term — Dirac's factorisation of the Laplacian, transplanted from flat space to a manifold. On $\mathbb{R}^n$ the recipe is algebraic: one chooses constant matrices $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n$ with $\gamma_i \gamma_j + \gamma_j \gamma_i = -2\delta_{ij}$, sets $D = \sum_i \gamma_i \partial_i$, and computes $D^2 = -\sum_{i,j} \gamma_i \gamma_j \partial_i \partial_j = -\sum_i \partial_i^2 = \Delta$, the anticommutation collapsing the cross terms. To do the same on $M$ we must replace the constant matrices $\gamma_i$ by a Clifford multiplication that varies smoothly from point to point, and we must replace $\partial_i$ by a covariant derivative. The obstacle is that "Clifford multiplication by a tangent vector" only makes sense once the Clifford relation $v \cdot v = -|v|^2$ is available at every point *and* fits together consistently across points; and the anticommutation trick in $D^2$ only survives if the differentiation is compatible with the multiplication. The Clifford bundle and its connection are precisely the objects that supply both. We therefore want:

1. an algebra $\operatorname{Cl}(T_mM)$ at every point, assembled into a smooth **bundle of algebras** $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$, so that "Clifford multiplication by $v \in T_mM$" is a fibrewise operation;
2. a canonical **connection** $\nabla^{LC}$ on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ that is a **derivation** of the fibrewise product, so that the product rule $\nabla(\phi \cdot s) = (\nabla^{LC}\phi)\cdot s + \phi\cdot\nabla s$ holds and the eventual $D^2$ computation goes through;
3. a notion of a bundle $E$ on which $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ **acts** — a bundle of Clifford modules — because the Dirac operator does not act on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ itself but on an auxiliary bundle (spinors, forms, or a twist of these).

The construction that delivers all three is the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated bundle]] to the frame bundle. The single design decision is *which* frame bundle, and the answer is forced by the Clifford relation.

**Why the orthonormal frame bundle, not the full frame bundle.** Suppose we tried to build $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ from the full [[Def - Frame Bundle of a Vector Bundle|frame bundle]] $\operatorname{Fr}(TM)$ with structure group $GL(n, \mathbb{R})$, gluing the model fibre $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ by the natural action of a frame change $g \in GL(n, \mathbb{R})$. For the fibrewise product to be well-defined, each transition $g$ must act by an *algebra automorphism* of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ — it must send the generators to elements again satisfying the defining relation. But the defining relation $u \cdot u = -|u|^2$ mentions the Euclidean norm, and a general linear map does not preserve it. Take $g = \operatorname{diag}(2, 1, \dots, 1)$: it sends $e_1 \mapsto 2e_1$, and $(2e_1)\cdot(2e_1) = 4 e_1^2 = -4 \cdot 1 \neq -1 = -|e_1|^2 \cdot 1$. So $g$ does not extend to an algebra homomorphism of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, the gluing fails to respect the product, and $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ has no consistent multiplication. The relation is preserved *exactly* by the maps with $|gv| = |v|$ for all $v$, that is by the orthogonal group $O(n)$; adding the orientation restricts to $SO(n)$. This is why the correct home is the orthonormal frame bundle, and why the metric is indispensable data — without it there is no Clifford relation to be preserved and no bundle of algebras. (Orientation is a convenience, not a necessity, for $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ alone: $O(n)$ acts by automorphisms just as well, so $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ could be built from $\operatorname{Fr}_O$. We use $SO(n)$ and $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ because the volume element, self-duality, and the spinor bundle of §8.3 all require an orientation, and it costs nothing to carry it here.)

**Why the connection must be metric.** The connection on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is induced from a principal connection on the frame bundle. For the induced connection to be a *derivation* of the Clifford product — clause (2) of the wish-list — parallel transport along a curve must be an *algebra automorphism* of the fibres, and by the same computation as above this holds precisely when parallel transport is an isometry, that is when the connection is [[Def - Metric-Compatible Connection|metric-compatible]]. A non-metric connection would parallel-transport an orthonormal frame to a non-orthonormal one, its induced action on $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ would not preserve the relation, and the product rule would break. The Levi-Civita connection is the canonical metric connection, so it is the natural — and, for the torsion-free identities used later, the correct — choice.

**Why the sign $v \cdot (v \cdot e) = -|v|^2 e$ in the module axiom, and not $+$.** A bundle of Clifford modules must carry a fibrewise map $T_mM \otimes E_m \to E_m$ satisfying $v \cdot (v \cdot e) = -|v|^2 e$, matching the algebra's relation, so that the action extends to a genuine representation of $\operatorname{Cl}(M) = \operatorname{Cl}(TM, -|\cdot|^2)$. If we dropped the axiom entirely, "$v \cdot e$" would be an arbitrary bilinear pairing with no relation to the Clifford algebra, and there would be no algebra bundle acting — the associated Dirac operator would satisfy no useful identity. If we kept an axiom but flipped the sign to $v\cdot(v\cdot e) = +|v|^2 e$, the pairing would be a module over the *other* Clifford algebra $\operatorname{Cl}(TM, +|\cdot|^2)$; the resulting first-order operator would square (in the flat model) to $-\Delta$ rather than $+\Delta$, destroying the positivity that makes the Weitzenböck argument and the vanishing theorems of §8.4 work. The sign is not cosmetic: it is the sign that makes $D^2$ a nonnegative Laplacian.

A reader who has internalised these four points can reconstruct the definition: assemble $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ over $M$ by gluing along $SO(n)$ (the only frame changes that preserve the relation), take the Levi-Civita connection (the metric one, so parallel transport is an automorphism), and call $E$ a bundle of Clifford modules when it carries a fibrewise $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-action reproducing the algebra's sign.

# The Definition

We build the three notions in order. Each construction rests on one proposition, proved in full.

## The $SO(n)$-action extends to algebra automorphisms

> **Proposition 1.** For each $g \in SO(n) \subseteq O(n) \subseteq GL(n, \mathbb{R})$ there is a unique unital algebra automorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(g) : \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ whose restriction to the degree-one subspace $\mathbb{R}^n \subseteq \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ equals $g$. The assignment $g \mapsto \operatorname{Cl}(g)$ is a group homomorphism $SO(n) \to \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n))$.

*Proof.* We must produce, for each fixed $g$, an algebra homomorphism extending $g$, show it is unique, show it is invertible, and show the assignment is multiplicative.

**Step 0 — the data for the universal property.** Let $\iota : \mathbb{R}^n \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be the canonical inclusion of the degree-one part, so that $\iota(v)^2 = -|v|^2 \cdot 1$ for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ (the defining Clifford relation, in the form $Q(v) = -|v|^2$). Define the linear map
$$\varphi := \iota \circ g : \mathbb{R}^n \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n), \qquad \varphi(v) = \iota(gv).$$

**Step 1 — $\varphi$ is Clifford-compatible.** For every $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$,
$$\varphi(v)^2 = \iota(gv)^2 = -|gv|^2 \cdot 1 = -|v|^2 \cdot 1 \qquad \text{(Clifford relation for } \iota\text{, then } |gv| = |v| \text{ since } g \in O(n) \text{ is orthogonal).}$$
Thus $\varphi(v)^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1$ with $Q(v) = -|v|^2$, which is exactly the hypothesis of the universal property with target algebra $A = \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

**Step 2 — extension and uniqueness.** We invoke the [[Thm - Clifford Algebra Universal Property|universal property of the Clifford algebra]]: for any unital associative $\mathbb{R}$-algebra $A$ and any linear map $\varphi : V \to A$ with $\varphi(v)^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1_A$ for all $v$, there is a *unique* algebra homomorphism $\tilde\varphi : \operatorname{Cl}(V, Q) \to A$ with $\tilde\varphi \circ \iota = \varphi$. Applying it to our $\varphi$ (with $V = \mathbb{R}^n$, $Q = -|\cdot|^2$, $A = \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$) produces a unique unital algebra homomorphism
$$\operatorname{Cl}(g) : \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n), \qquad \operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \iota = \iota \circ g,$$
that is, $\operatorname{Cl}(g)|_{\mathbb{R}^n} = g$. This establishes existence and uniqueness of the extension.

**Step 3 — multiplicativity.** For $g, h \in SO(n)$, the composite $\operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(h)$ is an algebra homomorphism (composition of homomorphisms), and on the degree-one part
$$\big(\operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(h)\big) \circ \iota = \operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ (\iota \circ h) = (\operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \iota) \circ h = \iota \circ g \circ h = \iota \circ (gh) \qquad \text{(Step 2 applied to } h \text{, then to } g\text{).}$$
So $\operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(h)$ is an algebra homomorphism extending $gh$; by the uniqueness clause of Step 2 (applied to $gh$), it must equal $\operatorname{Cl}(gh)$. The same uniqueness with $g = \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}^n}$ gives $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{id}) = \operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)}$, since the identity homomorphism extends $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}^n}$.

**Step 4 — invertibility.** From Step 3, $\operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(g^{-1}) = \operatorname{Cl}(g g^{-1}) = \operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{id}) = \operatorname{id}$ and likewise $\operatorname{Cl}(g^{-1}) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(g) = \operatorname{id}$. Hence $\operatorname{Cl}(g)$ is a bijective algebra homomorphism, that is a unital algebra automorphism, with inverse $\operatorname{Cl}(g^{-1})$.

**Conclusion.** Each $g \in SO(n)$ extends uniquely to $\operatorname{Cl}(g) \in \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n))$, and $g \mapsto \operatorname{Cl}(g)$ is a group homomorphism $SO(n) \to \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n))$. $\blacksquare$

This is the functoriality corollary of the universal property, specialised to the tautological representation of $SO(n)$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$; it is a smooth representation of $SO(n)$ on the finite-dimensional vector space $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (each $\operatorname{Cl}(g)$ acts as a matrix whose entries are polynomial in the entries of $g$, by the tensor-algebra formula for the extension), so it is a bona fide $SO(n)$-representation
$$\rho := \operatorname{Cl}(\cdot) : SO(n) \to GL\big(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)\big),$$
landing in $\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n))$.

## The Clifford bundle

> **Definition (Clifford bundle).** The **Clifford bundle** of the oriented Riemannian manifold $M$ is the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated bundle]]
> $$\operatorname{Cl}(M) := \operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \times_{SO(n)} \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n),$$
> formed from the oriented orthonormal frame bundle and the representation $\rho = \operatorname{Cl}(\cdot)$ of Proposition 1. Its points are equivalence classes $[p, a]$ with $p \in \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$, $a \in \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, under $[p \cdot g, a] = [p, \rho(g)a] = [p, \operatorname{Cl}(g)a]$ for $g \in SO(n)$.

The fibrewise product is defined on representatives by
$$[p, a] \cdot [p, b] := [p, ab],$$
where $ab$ is the Clifford product in $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$; this makes $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ a **bundle of algebras**. We check that the product does not depend on the choices.

> **Proposition 2.** The fibrewise product $[p, a] \cdot [p, b] := [p, ab]$ is well-defined, independent of the representative frame $p$, and gives each fibre $\operatorname{Cl}(M)_m$ the structure of a unital associative algebra isomorphic to $\operatorname{Cl}(T_mM) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(T_m^*M)$.

*Proof.* **Well-definedness.** A different representative of the same two classes uses the same frame (we may always pick a common $p$, since the two classes lie in the same fibre); the only ambiguity is the simultaneous frame change $p \rightsquigarrow p \cdot g$. Rewriting both classes through $p \cdot g$: since $[p, a] = [p\cdot g, \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} a]$ and $[p, b] = [p \cdot g, \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} b]$, the product computed with the frame $p \cdot g$ is
$$[p\cdot g, \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} a] \cdot [p \cdot g, \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} b] = [p \cdot g,\ (\operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} a)(\operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} b)] = [p \cdot g,\ \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}(ab)] \qquad \text{(} \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} \text{ is an algebra homomorphism, by Proposition 1),}$$
and $[p \cdot g, \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}(ab)] = [p, ab]$ by the equivalence relation. So the product computed with $p \cdot g$ agrees with the product computed with $p$: it is independent of the frame. Bilinearity, associativity, and the unit $[p, 1]$ are inherited from $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ because $[p, \cdot]$ is a linear isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{Cl}(M)_m$ for each fixed $p$.

**Identification of the fibre.** A frame $p : \mathbb{R}^n \to T_mM$ is a linear isometry, hence preserves the quadratic forms $-|\cdot|^2$, so by the functoriality clause of the [[Thm - Clifford Algebra Universal Property|universal property]] it extends to an algebra isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(p) : \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{Cl}(T_mM)$. Define $\Psi_m : \operatorname{Cl}(M)_m \to \operatorname{Cl}(T_mM)$ by $\Psi_m([p, a]) = \operatorname{Cl}(p)(a)$. This is well-defined: replacing $p$ by $p \cdot g$ and $a$ by $\operatorname{Cl}(g)a$ (the same class),
$$\operatorname{Cl}(p \cdot g)(\operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} a') \big|_{a' = \operatorname{Cl}(g)a} = \operatorname{Cl}(p) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(g) \circ \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}(a) = \operatorname{Cl}(p)(a) \qquad \text{(} \operatorname{Cl}(p\cdot g) = \operatorname{Cl}(p)\circ\operatorname{Cl}(g) \text{ by functoriality).}$$
It is an algebra isomorphism because $\operatorname{Cl}(p)$ is and $\Psi_m([p,a]\cdot[p,b]) = \operatorname{Cl}(p)(ab) = \operatorname{Cl}(p)(a)\operatorname{Cl}(p)(b)$. Finally the metric isometry $\flat : T_mM \to T_m^*M$ preserves $-|\cdot|^2$, so by functoriality once more it extends to an algebra isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(T_mM) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(T_m^*M)$; this is the identification that lets Clifford multiplication act interchangeably on tangent vectors and cotangent covectors. $\blacksquare$

The tangent bundle sits inside the Clifford bundle as its degree-one part: because the restriction $\rho(g)|_{\mathbb{R}^n} = g$ is the tautological representation, the associated subbundle $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \times_{SO(n)} \mathbb{R}^n$ is canonically $TM$, and it includes into $\operatorname{Cl}(M) = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \times_{SO(n)} \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Under $\Psi_m$ this is the inclusion $T_mM \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(T_mM)$.

## The Levi-Civita connection on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is a derivation

Because $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is an associated bundle to $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$, the principal Levi-Civita connection on $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ induces a covariant derivative on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$, which we denote by the same symbol $\nabla^{LC}$. We recall the mechanism, then prove it respects the product.

> **Recall — the induced covariant derivative on an associated bundle.** ([[Thm - Principal Connections Induce Covariant Derivatives on Associated Bundles|induced covariant derivative]].) Let $P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with principal connection, let $\rho : G \to GL(V)$ be a representation, and let $E = P \times_\rho V$. A section $s \in \Gamma(E)$ corresponds bijectively to a $G$-equivariant function $\hat s : P \to V$, meaning $\hat s(p \cdot g) = \rho(g)^{-1}\hat s(p)$, via $s(m) = [p, \hat s(p)]$ for any $p$ over $m$. The induced covariant derivative $\nabla$ is characterised by
> $$\widehat{\nabla_X s} = X^H(\hat s),$$
> where $X^H$ is the horizontal lift of $X$ determined by the connection and $X^H(\hat s)$ is the derivative of the $V$-valued function $\hat s$ along $X^H$.

Here $P = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$, $G = SO(n)$, $\rho = \operatorname{Cl}(\cdot)$, $V = \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and $\nabla = \nabla^{LC}$.

> **Proposition 3.** The induced connection $\nabla^{LC}$ on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is a derivation of the fibrewise Clifford product: for all $\phi, \psi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{Cl}(M))$ and every vector field $X$ on $M$,
> $$\nabla^{LC}_X(\phi \cdot \psi) = (\nabla^{LC}_X \phi) \cdot \psi + \phi \cdot (\nabla^{LC}_X \psi).$$

*Proof.* We must show the two equivariant functions of $\nabla^{LC}_X(\phi\cdot\psi)$ and of $(\nabla^{LC}_X\phi)\cdot\psi + \phi\cdot(\nabla^{LC}_X\psi)$ coincide; since $s \mapsto \hat s$ is a bijection, equality of the functions gives equality of the sections.

**Step 0 — the equivariant function of a product.** Let $\hat\phi, \hat\psi : \operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be the equivariant functions of $\phi, \psi$, so $\hat\phi(p\cdot g) = \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}\hat\phi(p)$ and likewise for $\hat\psi$. Consider the pointwise product $\hat\phi \cdot \hat\psi : p \mapsto \hat\phi(p)\hat\psi(p) \in \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. It is equivariant:
$$(\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi)(p \cdot g) = \hat\phi(p\cdot g)\,\hat\psi(p\cdot g) = \big(\operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}\hat\phi(p)\big)\big(\operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}\hat\psi(p)\big) = \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1}\big(\hat\phi(p)\hat\psi(p)\big) \qquad \text{(} \operatorname{Cl}(g)^{-1} \text{ an algebra homomorphism, Proposition 1),}$$
so $\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi$ is the equivariant function of some section, and evaluating the definition of the product gives $(\phi\cdot\psi)(m) = [p, \hat\phi(p)]\cdot[p,\hat\psi(p)] = [p, \hat\phi(p)\hat\psi(p)]$, whose equivariant function is $\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi$. Therefore
$$\widehat{\phi\cdot\psi} = \hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi.$$

**Step 1 — Leibniz rule for the horizontal derivative.** Fix $m$, a point $p \in \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ over $m$, and the horizontal lift $X^H_p$ of $X_m$. The Clifford product $\mu : \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \times \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, $(a, b) \mapsto ab$, is a *fixed* bilinear map, independent of $p$. Writing the functions in the constant basis $\{e_I\}$ as $\hat\phi = \sum_I \hat\phi^I e_I$, $\hat\psi = \sum_J \hat\psi^J e_J$ with scalar component functions $\hat\phi^I, \hat\psi^J : \operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \to \mathbb{R}$, we have $\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi = \sum_{I, J}\hat\phi^I \hat\psi^J\,(e_I e_J)$ with $e_I e_J \in \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ constant. Applying the derivation $X^H_p$ and the ordinary product rule to each scalar product $\hat\phi^I \hat\psi^J$,
$$X^H_p(\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi) = \sum_{I,J}\big(X^H_p(\hat\phi^I)\,\hat\psi^J + \hat\phi^I\, X^H_p(\hat\psi^J)\big)(e_I e_J) = \big(X^H_p\hat\phi\big)\cdot\hat\psi(p) + \hat\phi(p)\cdot\big(X^H_p\hat\psi\big) \qquad \text{(product rule on the scalar functions } \hat\phi^I\hat\psi^J \text{; } e_I e_J \text{ constant).}$$
This is the Leibniz rule for $X^H$ with respect to the bilinear pairing $\mu$.

**Step 2 — assemble.** By the characterisation of the induced connection ($\widehat{\nabla^{LC}_X s} = X^H \hat s$) applied to $s = \phi\cdot\psi$ and Step 0,
$$\widehat{\nabla^{LC}_X(\phi\cdot\psi)} = X^H(\widehat{\phi\cdot\psi}) = X^H(\hat\phi\cdot\hat\psi) = (X^H\hat\phi)\cdot\hat\psi + \hat\phi\cdot(X^H\hat\psi) \qquad \text{(Step 0, then Step 1),}$$
while, again by the characterisation of the induced connection and Step 0,
$$\widehat{(\nabla^{LC}_X\phi)\cdot\psi + \phi\cdot(\nabla^{LC}_X\psi)} = \widehat{\nabla^{LC}_X\phi}\cdot\hat\psi + \hat\phi\cdot\widehat{\nabla^{LC}_X\psi} = (X^H\hat\phi)\cdot\hat\psi + \hat\phi\cdot(X^H\hat\psi).$$
The two right-hand sides are identical, so the equivariant functions of $\nabla^{LC}_X(\phi\cdot\psi)$ and of $(\nabla^{LC}_X\phi)\cdot\psi + \phi\cdot(\nabla^{LC}_X\psi)$ agree.

**Conclusion.** Since $s \mapsto \hat s$ is a bijection between sections and equivariant functions, the two sections are equal, and $\nabla^{LC}$ is a derivation of the Clifford product. $\blacksquare$

The mechanism behind Proposition 3 is worth naming: the fibrewise product is a *parallel* bundle map, because it comes from the $SO(n)$-equivariant multiplication on the model fibre, and a parallel bilinear operation always obeys the Leibniz rule for the induced connection. This is the structural fact that a $G$-equivariant map between representation spaces descends to a covariantly-constant bundle morphism.

## Bundle of Clifford modules

> **Definition (bundle of Clifford modules).** A **bundle of Clifford modules** over $M$ (also called a **bundle of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-modules**) is a smooth real (or complex) vector bundle $E \to M$ together with a smooth bundle map
> $$\operatorname{Cl} : TM \otimes E \to E, \qquad (v, e) \mapsto v \cdot e,$$
> fibrewise bilinear, satisfying the **Clifford module relation**
> $$v \cdot (v \cdot e) = -|v|^2\, e \qquad \text{for all } m \in M,\ v \in T_mM,\ e \in E_m.$$

Polarising the relation (replace $v$ by $v + w$ and subtract the two single-vector relations) gives the equivalent bilinear form
$$v \cdot (w \cdot e) + w \cdot (v \cdot e) = -2\langle v, w\rangle\, e \qquad \text{for all } v, w \in T_mM,\ e \in E_m,$$
the fibrewise Clifford relation. The equivalent packaging is as a representation of the algebra bundle:

> **Proposition 4.** A bundle-of-Clifford-modules structure on $E$ is the same data as a smooth homomorphism of algebra bundles $c : \operatorname{Cl}(M) \to \operatorname{End}(E)$ (fibrewise a unital $\mathbb{R}$-algebra homomorphism), via restriction to the degree-one part $TM \subseteq \operatorname{Cl}(M)$.

*Proof.* **From an algebra-bundle homomorphism to a module structure.** Given $c : \operatorname{Cl}(M) \to \operatorname{End}(E)$, restrict to $TM \subseteq \operatorname{Cl}(M)$ (the degree-one part, Proposition 2) and set $v \cdot e := c(v)(e)$. For $v \in T_mM$, the element $v$ satisfies $v \cdot v = -|v|^2 \cdot 1$ inside $\operatorname{Cl}(T_mM)$ (the defining relation); applying the algebra homomorphism $c_m$,
$$c_m(v)^2 = c_m(v \cdot v) = c_m(-|v|^2 \cdot 1) = -|v|^2\,\operatorname{id}_{E_m} \qquad \text{(} c_m \text{ multiplicative and unital),}$$
so $v\cdot(v\cdot e) = c_m(v)^2 e = -|v|^2 e$: the module relation holds. The map $(v, e) \mapsto c(v)e$ is smooth and bilinear because $c$ and the evaluation $\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes E \to E$ are smooth bundle maps.

**From a module structure to an algebra-bundle homomorphism.** Given the bundle map $\operatorname{Cl} : TM \otimes E \to E$, define fibrewise $c_m : T_mM \to \operatorname{End}(E_m)$ by $c_m(v)e = v\cdot e$, a linear map with $c_m(v)^2 = -|v|^2\operatorname{id}$ (the module relation). Then $c_m$ is a linear map into the unital associative algebra $\operatorname{End}(E_m)$ satisfying $c_m(v)^2 = Q(v)\operatorname{id}$ with $Q(v) = -|v|^2$, so by the [[Thm - Clifford Algebra Universal Property|universal property]] it extends uniquely to a unital algebra homomorphism
$$\tilde c_m : \operatorname{Cl}(T_mM) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(M)_m \to \operatorname{End}(E_m).$$
These assemble to a bundle map $c : \operatorname{Cl}(M) \to \operatorname{End}(E)$. Smoothness: on a local orthonormal frame $(e_1, \dots, e_n)$ of $TM$ over an open set $U$, the basis element $e_I = e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}$ of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ maps to $\tilde c(e_I) = c(e_{i_1})\cdots c(e_{i_k})$, a product of the smooth endomorphisms $c(e_{i_j})$, hence a smooth section of $\operatorname{End}(E)$ over $U$; since the $e_I$ frame $\operatorname{Cl}(M)|_U$ and $\tilde c$ is linear on fibres, $\tilde c$ is smooth. The two constructions are mutually inverse because each is determined by its value on $TM$, where both give $v \cdot e$. $\blacksquare$

Because of Proposition 4 we speak interchangeably of "Clifford multiplication on $E$" and "the $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-action on $E$", and write $\phi \cdot e$ for $c(\phi)(e)$ when $\phi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{Cl}(M))$; this is the notation used in clause (3) of the Dirac-bundle definition on the next page. Nothing on this page assumes a compatible metric or connection on $E$; those extra conditions, which upgrade a bundle of Clifford modules to a **Dirac bundle**, are added on [[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|the Dirac-bundle page]].

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The whole construction is the fibrewise application of one functor. Let $\mathbf{Euc}_n^{+}$ be the groupoid whose objects are the oriented $n$-dimensional Euclidean vector spaces $(V, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ and whose morphisms are the orientation-preserving linear isometries, and let $\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}$ be the category of unital associative $\mathbb{R}$-algebras. The assignment
$$\operatorname{Cl} : \mathbf{Euc}_n^{+} \to \mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}, \qquad (V, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle) \mapsto \operatorname{Cl}(V, -|\cdot|^2), \qquad (f : V \to W) \mapsto \operatorname{Cl}(f),$$
is a functor: the universal property gives $\operatorname{Cl}(f)$ for each isometry $f$ and Proposition 1's Steps 3–4 are exactly the statements $\operatorname{Cl}(g \circ h) = \operatorname{Cl}(g)\circ\operatorname{Cl}(h)$ and $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{id}) = \operatorname{id}$. The Clifford bundle is this functor applied to the tangent bundle *frame by frame*: an oriented Riemannian manifold gives, at each point, an object $T_mM$ of $\mathbf{Euc}_n^{+}$, and the transition functions of $TM$ (valued in $SO(n)$, the isometry morphisms) are pushed through $\operatorname{Cl}$ to glue the algebras. Equivalently, and this is the definition adopted above, $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is the bundle associated to $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ by the composite representation $SO(n) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Cl}(\cdot)} \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)) \hookrightarrow GL(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n))$.

In this language a bundle of Clifford modules is a **representation of the algebra bundle** $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$: a bundle $E$ with a homomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(M) \to \operatorname{End}(E)$ of algebra bundles (Proposition 4). The Levi-Civita connection $\nabla^{LC}$ is the connection induced by the principal Levi-Civita connection through the functor, and Proposition 3 is the general fact that a functor-induced connection differentiates the functor's algebraic operations by the Leibniz rule — the multiplication natural transformation $\operatorname{Cl}\otimes\operatorname{Cl}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}$ is parallel.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** The Clifford bundle is *the exterior bundle with a deformed product*. As a vector bundle, the symbol map $a \mapsto a\cdot 1$ identifies $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ with $\Lambda T^*M$ (equivalently $\Lambda TM$), because both are associated to $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ by $2^n$-dimensional $SO(n)$-representations that are isomorphic (the associated graded of the Clifford filtration is the exterior algebra). What is genuinely new is not the underlying bundle but the *product*: $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ carries the fibrewise Clifford multiplication, which mixes degrees ($v\cdot\phi$ has a degree-raising part $v^\flat\wedge\phi$ and a degree-lowering part $-\iota_v\phi$), whereas $\Lambda T^*M$ carries the degree-additive wedge. The operational content of "bundle of Clifford modules" is therefore: *a bundle on which one-forms act by an operation that squares to $-|v|^2$* — the minimal data needed to write a Dirac operator.

**Connections to other structures.** The construction "associated bundle of a functor applied fibrewise" is the same pattern that builds the tensor bundles, the density bundle, and the bundle of exterior algebras; see [[Def - Induced Connection on Tensor Bundles|induced connection on tensor bundles]], which is Proposition 3 for the tensor functor rather than the Clifford functor. In algebraic topology and index theory, a $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-module structure is the geometric input to the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro construction and to $K$-theory orientations; the Dirac operator of a bundle of Clifford modules is the model for every operator whose index the Atiyah–Singer theorem computes. In physics, a bundle of Clifford modules on spacetime is where a spinor field lives, and Clifford multiplication by a covector is the action of a gamma matrix; the reason the same algebra governs both the exterior calculus (through $d + d^*$, see [[Thm - The Hodge-de Rham Operator is a Dirac Operator|the Hodge–de Rham operator as a Dirac operator]]) and spinors is precisely that both are $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-modules.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the exterior bundle $\Lambda T^*M$.** Let $E = \Lambda T^*M = \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Lambda^k T^*M$ with Clifford multiplication
$$v \cdot \phi := v^\flat \wedge \phi - \iota_v \phi, \qquad v \in T_mM,\ \phi \in \Lambda T_m^*M,$$
where $v^\flat = \langle v, \cdot\rangle \in T_m^*M$. We verify the module relation clause by clause. Abbreviate $a = v^\flat$ and compute $(v\cdot)(v\cdot\phi)$:
$$v\cdot(v\cdot\phi) = (a\wedge - \iota_v)(a\wedge\phi - \iota_v\phi) = a\wedge a\wedge\phi - a\wedge\iota_v\phi - \iota_v(a\wedge\phi) + \iota_v\iota_v\phi \qquad \text{(bilinear expansion).}$$
We evaluate the four terms:
$$a\wedge a\wedge\phi = 0 \qquad \text{(the wedge of the 1-form } a \text{ with itself vanishes),}$$
$$\iota_v\iota_v\phi = 0 \qquad \text{(} \iota_v \text{ is an antiderivation with } \iota_v^2 = 0),$$
$$\iota_v(a\wedge\phi) = (\iota_v a)\,\phi - a\wedge\iota_v\phi = |v|^2\,\phi - a\wedge\iota_v\phi \qquad \text{(} \iota_v \text{ antiderivation on the 1-form } a\text{; } \iota_v a = a(v) = \langle v, v\rangle = |v|^2).$$
Substituting,
$$v\cdot(v\cdot\phi) = 0 - a\wedge\iota_v\phi - \big(|v|^2\phi - a\wedge\iota_v\phi\big) + 0 = -a\wedge\iota_v\phi - |v|^2\phi + a\wedge\iota_v\phi = -|v|^2\,\phi \qquad \text{(the two } a\wedge\iota_v\phi \text{ terms cancel).}$$
So the relation $v\cdot(v\cdot\phi) = -|v|^2\phi$ holds. The map $(v, \phi) \mapsto v^\flat\wedge\phi - \iota_v\phi$ is smooth and fibrewise bilinear (built from the smooth bundle maps $\flat$, $\wedge$, and $\iota$), so $\Lambda T^*M$ is a bundle of Clifford modules. This globalises the flat-space example $\operatorname{E4.1.2}$ from [[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication|the Clifford algebra page]]: the fibrewise formula is exactly the model-space Clifford action on $\Lambda(\mathbb{R}^n)^*$, and it is $SO(n)$-equivariant (both $\wedge$ and $\iota$ commute with the induced action of orthogonal maps), so it descends to the associated bundle.

> [!warning] Convention: sign of the exterior Clifford action
> The sibling page [[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication|Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication]] writes the model action as $u\cdot\phi = \iota_u\phi - u^\flat\wedge\phi$, the negative of the one used here. Both satisfy $(\,\cdot\,)^2 = -|u|^2$ (the relation is quadratic, hence invariant under an overall sign), so both are genuine $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-module structures; they are intertwined by the grading automorphism $\alpha : \phi \mapsto (-1)^{\deg\phi}\phi$. We adopt $v\cdot\phi = v^\flat\wedge\phi - \iota_v\phi$ throughout §8.2 because it makes the Dirac operator come out as $d + d^*$ with the standard signs on [[Thm - The Hodge-de Rham Operator is a Dirac Operator|the Hodge–de Rham page]]; the recipe to convert is: negate $v\cdot$, or equivalently pre-compose with $\alpha$.

**Is an instance — the Clifford bundle acting on itself.** $E = \operatorname{Cl}(M)$ with $v\cdot\phi := $ (image of $v$ under $TM \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}(M)$) times $\phi$, i.e. left multiplication, is a bundle of Clifford modules: $v\cdot(v\cdot\phi) = (v\cdot v)\cdot\phi = (-|v|^2 1)\cdot\phi = -|v|^2\phi$ by associativity and the algebra relation. The corresponding algebra-bundle homomorphism (Proposition 4) is the left regular representation $L : \operatorname{Cl}(M) \to \operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Cl}(M))$, $L(a)b = ab$, whose fibrewise injectivity (a unital algebra acts faithfully on itself) shows $\operatorname{Cl}(M)_m \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Cl}(M)_m)$.

**Is an instance — the spinor bundle (constructed in §8.3).** On a spin manifold, the spinor bundle is $P \times_{Spin(n), \rho} S$ for a spin structure $P$ and the complex spinor representation $\rho$; since $\rho$ extends to a representation of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, the spinor bundle is a bundle of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-modules, and it is the one that carries the *spin* Dirac operator. This is the motivating example; it is built in full on [[Def - Spin Structure and Spin-c Structure|the spin-structure page]] and named on [[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|the Dirac-operator page]]. We record it here as an instance and do not reprove the relation, which is inherited from the module structure of $S$ over $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ established in §8.1.

**Is NOT an instance — the tangent bundle with a naive product.** One might hope to make $TM$ *itself* a bundle of Clifford modules with $v \cdot w$ some natural bilinear multiplication of tangent vectors. On an oriented Riemannian $3$-manifold the most natural candidate is the fibrewise cross product $v \cdot w := v \times w$ (defined by $\langle v\times w, u\rangle = \operatorname{vol}(v, w, u)$). It fails the module relation: by the vector triple product identity,
$$v\cdot(v\cdot w) = v\times(v\times w) = \langle v, w\rangle\, v - |v|^2\, w,$$
which equals $-|v|^2 w$ only when $\langle v, w\rangle v = 0$. Taking $w = v$ gives $v\times(v\times v) = v\times 0 = 0 \neq -|v|^2 v$ for $v \neq 0$, so the relation is violated. The cross product is not a Clifford module structure.

This is not a failure of the particular candidate but of the dimension. **No** bilinear multiplication makes the tangent space of a surface into a $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^2)$-module. Suppose $c : \mathbb{R}^2 \otimes \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ were bilinear with $c(v)^2 = -|v|^2\operatorname{id}$, writing $c(v) := (w \mapsto v\cdot w) \in \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^2)$. Polarising, $c(u)c(w) + c(w)c(u) = -2\langle u, w\rangle\operatorname{id}$. For the standard orthonormal basis $e_1, e_2$ set $A := c(e_1)$, $B := c(e_2)$, $C := AB$; then
$$A^2 = B^2 = -\operatorname{id} \quad (\text{relation}), \qquad AB = -BA \quad (\text{polarised relation, } \langle e_1, e_2\rangle = 0),$$
$$C^2 = ABAB = -AABB = -(-\operatorname{id})(-\operatorname{id}) = -\operatorname{id} \qquad \text{(using } BA = -AB \text{ then } A^2 = B^2 = -\operatorname{id}).$$
So $A, B, C$ satisfy exactly Hamilton's relations, and the linear map $\Phi : \mathbb{H} \to \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^2) = M_2(\mathbb{R})$ sending $1, i, j, k \mapsto \operatorname{id}, A, B, C$ is a unital $\mathbb{R}$-algebra homomorphism. Its kernel is a two-sided ideal of $\mathbb{H}$; since $\mathbb{H}$ is a division algebra its only two-sided ideals are $0$ and $\mathbb{H}$, and $\Phi(1) = \operatorname{id} \neq 0$ rules out $\ker\Phi = \mathbb{H}$, so $\ker\Phi = 0$ and $\Phi$ is injective. As $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{H} = 4 = \dim_{\mathbb{R}}M_2(\mathbb{R})$, $\Phi$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{H} \cong M_2(\mathbb{R})$. But $\mathbb{H}$ is a division algebra (every nonzero element is invertible) while $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ has zero divisors — for instance $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} = 0$ with both factors nonzero — and an algebra isomorphism carries invertible elements to invertible elements, a contradiction. Hence no bilinear $c$ exists on $\mathbb{R}^2$, and $TM$ is never a bundle of Clifford modules when $\dim M = 2$. The obstruction is dimensional: a real $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^2) \cong \mathbb{H}$-module has real dimension a multiple of $4$ (the dimension of the irreducible $\mathbb{H}$-module), so the $2$-dimensional fibre $T_mM$ cannot carry the structure at all.

**Calibration check.** Three quick verifications the reader can carry out from the page. (i) The fibre dimension of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is $2^n$, since it is associated to $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ via the $2^n$-dimensional representation $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$; in particular for a surface ($n = 2$) the fibre is $4$-dimensional, matching $\Lambda T^*M = \Lambda^0 \oplus \Lambda^1 \oplus \Lambda^2$ of dimension $1 + 2 + 1 = 4$. (ii) The derivation property (Proposition 3) forces $\nabla^{LC}(1) = 0$ for the unit section $1$ of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$: apply the derivation to $1 = 1\cdot 1$ to get $\nabla^{LC}1 = \nabla^{LC}1\cdot 1 + 1\cdot\nabla^{LC}1 = 2\nabla^{LC}1$, whence $\nabla^{LC}1 = 0$. (iii) On $\Lambda T^*M$ the exterior action satisfies $v\cdot w\cdot + w\cdot v\cdot = -2\langle v,w\rangle$; check on $\phi = 1 \in \Lambda^0$ that $v\cdot(w\cdot 1) + w\cdot(v\cdot 1) = v\cdot w^\flat + w\cdot v^\flat = (v^\flat\wedge w^\flat - \langle v, w\rangle) + (w^\flat\wedge v^\flat - \langle w, v\rangle) = -2\langle v, w\rangle$, since $v^\flat\wedge w^\flat = -w^\flat\wedge v^\flat$ and $\iota_v w^\flat = \langle v, w\rangle$.
