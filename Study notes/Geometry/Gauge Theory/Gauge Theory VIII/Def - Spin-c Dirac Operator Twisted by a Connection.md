# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth, oriented, Riemannian $n$-manifold, and $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO} \to M$ is its bundle of oriented orthonormal frames, a principal $SO(n)$-bundle carrying the [[Def - Levi-Civita Connection|Levi-Civita connection]], which we regard as a [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle|principal connection]] $\omega \in \Omega^1(\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}; \mathfrak{so}(n))$. We write $\Gamma(E)$ for smooth sections of a vector bundle $E \to M$ and $\Omega^p(M; E) = \Gamma(\Lambda^p T^*M \otimes E)$. Lie groups act on principal bundles on the **right**, $R_g(p) = p \cdot g$; the fundamental vector field of $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ is $\xi_P(p) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, p \cdot \exp(t\xi)$. A principal connection $\omega$ on a principal $G$-bundle $P$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $1$-form with $\omega(\xi_P) = \xi$ for all $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $R_g^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega$ for all $g \in G$.

The [[Def - Spin Group and the Double Cover of SO(n)|spin group]] $\operatorname{Spin}(n) \subset \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the subgroup generated by products of an even number of unit vectors; $\xi : \operatorname{Spin}(n) \to SO(n)$, $\xi(g)v = g v g^{-1}$, is the [[Thm - Spin(n) inside the Clifford Algebra is a Double Cover of SO(n)|double covering homomorphism]], with kernel the central subgroup $\{\pm 1\}$, and its differential $d\xi : \mathfrak{spin}(n) \to \mathfrak{so}(n)$ is a Lie-algebra isomorphism (both facts proved on that page). Here $\mathfrak{spin}(n) = \operatorname{span}\{e_i e_j : i < j\} \subset \operatorname{Cl}^0(\mathbb{R}^n)$. We write $\slashed{S}$ (unadorned) for a fixed complex Hermitian [[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication|Clifford module]] over $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, with $\rho : \operatorname{Spin}(n) \to U(\slashed{S})$ the restriction of the module action ($\rho(g) = g\,\cdot$, Clifford multiplication by $g$); we write $\slashed{S}$ again (in bold context or as a bundle) for the associated **spinor bundle** $P \times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n),\rho}\slashed{S}$, following the standard abuse by which the representation and the bundle it induces share a symbol. The spin$^c$ group is $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n) = \operatorname{Spin}(n) \times U(1)/\{\pm 1\}$ (diagonal $\{\pm 1\}$), with $\rho_0[g,z] = \xi(g)$, $\rho_{\det}[g,z] = z^2$, determinant line bundle $L_{\det} = P \times_{\rho_{\det}}\mathbb{C}$, and $U(1)$-structure bundle $P_{\det} = P/\operatorname{Spin}(n)$, all as on [[Def - Spin Structure and Spin-c Structure|the spin/spin$^c$-structure page]]. The Clifford contraction $\operatorname{Cl} : T^*M \otimes E \to E$ turns a covariant derivative $\nabla$ into the [[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|Dirac operator]] $D = \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla$, that is $Ds = \sum_i e_i \cdot \nabla_{e_i}s$ in a local orthonormal frame $(e_1,\dots,e_n)$.

This is a compound page: it defines five interlocking notions — the Levi-Civita connection lifted to a spin structure; the spinor bundle and its spin Dirac operator $\slashed{D}$; the twisted spinor bundle $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ and its twisted Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_A$; the spin$^c$ connection $\tau^*(\omega + A)$ together with the spin$^c$ spinor bundle and its Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_A$; and the chiral block form in dimension four — because they are one construction seen at four levels of generality (untwisted spin, twisted spin, spin$^c$), each obtained from the previous by feeding a further principal connection into the same associated-bundle machine, and the chirality decomposition is common to all of them in dimension four.

> [!warning] Convention: Clifford sign
> We use the sign $v \cdot v = -\lvert v\rvert^2\,1$ of Haydys and Lawson–Michelsohn, so that $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the vault's $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n, -\lvert\cdot\rvert^2)$: where the vault's [[Def - Clifford Algebra|Clifford-algebra page]] writes $v^2 = Q(v)$ for a possibly indefinite $Q$, our algebra is that page's $\operatorname{Cl}$ of the negative-definite form $Q = -\lvert\cdot\rvert^2$. Every polarised relation reads $u\cdot v + v\cdot u = -2\langle u, v\rangle\,1$.

> [!warning] Convention: the same $\slashed{D}$ in a frame
> The vault's [[Def - Spin Connection and the Dirac Operator|spin-connection page]] defines the Dirac operator $\slashed{D}$ by writing the spin connection $1$-form $\tfrac14\omega_{ab}\gamma^a\gamma^b$ in a local orthonormal frame and contracting with the gamma matrices. That operator is exactly the $\slashed{D} = \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla$ of this page: the spin connection there is the local form of the connection induced by $\tau^*\omega$ here (Part (a)), and the gamma matrices are Clifford multiplication in the fixed module $\slashed{S}$.

# Axiom Motivation

By the end of §8.2 we have the algebraic and bundle-theoretic ingredients of a Dirac operator in hand: the [[Def - Clifford Algebra and Clifford Multiplication|Clifford algebra]] $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, its complex modules $\slashed{S}$, the [[Def - Spin Group and the Double Cover of SO(n)|spin group]] sitting inside it, the [[Def - Clifford Bundle and Bundle of Clifford Modules|Clifford bundle]] $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$, and the notion of a [[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|Dirac bundle]] — a bundle of Clifford modules equipped with a metric and a connection satisfying three compatibility conditions, from which the Dirac operator $D = \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla$ is read off. What is missing is the geometric object itself. A Dirac bundle needs a connection, and on the spinor bundle there is exactly one connection that deserves to be there: the one inherited from the Riemannian geometry of $M$ through its frame bundle. The purpose of this page is to build that connection, verify that the spinor bundle with it is genuinely a Dirac bundle, and then enlarge the construction in the two directions that gauge theory demands: coupling the spinors to an external gauge field (twisting), and replacing a spin structure — which many four-manifolds do not admit — by a spin$^c$ structure, which they always do.

The first question is why the Levi-Civita connection lifts at all. A spin structure is a principal $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-bundle $P$ with an equivariant double cover $\tau : P \to \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$; the Levi-Civita connection lives downstairs, as a $1$-form $\omega$ valued in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ on $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$. To make it a connection on $P$ we must produce a $1$-form on $P$ valued in $\mathfrak{spin}(n)$. The mechanism is that the covering $\xi : \operatorname{Spin}(n)\to SO(n)$ is a local diffeomorphism, so its differential $d\xi : \mathfrak{spin}(n)\to\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is an isomorphism of Lie algebras; we may therefore pull $\omega$ back along $\tau$ and translate it into $\mathfrak{spin}(n)$ by $(d\xi)^{-1}$. Without this, there would be no canonical connection on $P$, hence no canonical spinor covariant derivative, hence no canonical Dirac operator — the whole edifice would depend on arbitrary choices. That the two connection axioms survive the pull-back is not automatic; it rests on $\tau$ being equivariant and $\xi$ being a homomorphism, and it is verified below in a form general enough to reuse for spin$^c$.

The second and third questions are why one twists and why one passes to spin$^c$. One twists because the physics and the geometry both want spinors that carry an internal charge: a section of $\slashed{S}\otimes E$, where $E$ is an auxiliary Hermitian bundle associated to a gauge field, is a spinor field minimally coupled to that field, and the twisted Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_A$ is the coupled Dirac operator whose kernel and index carry gauge-theoretic information. One passes to spin$^c$ because the obstruction to a spin structure — the second Stiefel–Whitney class $w_2(M)$ — is nonzero for many important four-manifolds (for instance $\mathbb{CP}^2$), whereas every closed oriented four-manifold admits a spin$^c$ structure. A spin$^c$ structure is the minimal enlargement that repairs the obstruction: it allows the frame bundle to be lifted not to $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ but to $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n) = \operatorname{Spin}(n)\times U(1)/\{\pm1\}$, in which an auxiliary determinant circle absorbs the sign ambiguity of the spin lift. Heuristically, as Haydys's Remark 125 puts it, a spin$^c$ spinor is a product $\slashed{S}_0 \otimes L_0$ of a would-be spinor bundle $\slashed{S}_0$ and a would-be square root $L_0$ of the determinant line; in general neither factor exists globally, and only the well-defined product $\slashed{S}$ carries geometric meaning. The spin$^c$ formalism is precisely the device that makes this product rigorous without pretending its factors exist.

This last picture also explains, before any computation, the factor $\tfrac12$ that will govern how the spin$^c$ Dirac operator responds to a change of connection (and, downstream, the factor $2$ in the gauge action $A\cdot g = A + 2g^{-1}\,dg$ of chapter XI). Think of $\slashed{S}$ heuristically as $\slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$. The determinant line bundle is then $L_{\det} = L_0^{\,2}$: on the model, the central circle $z \in U(1)$ acts on $L_0$ with weight one (by $z\,\cdot$) and on $\slashed{S}_0$ trivially, whereas $\rho_{\det}[g,z] = z^2$ has weight two, so $L_{\det}$ is the square of $L_0$. A connection on the determinant bundle $P_{\det}$ is a connection on $L_{\det} = L_0^{\,2}$, and the central $U(1)$ of $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$ covers the determinant circle by the squaring map $z\mapsto z^2$, whose differential is multiplication by $2$. Consequently an infinitesimal generator $i\theta$ of the determinant circle acts on the spinor $\slashed{S}$ — which sees the central circle with weight one — as $\tfrac12\, i\theta$. If we shift the connection $A_0$ on $L_0$ by $a_0$, the induced connection $A = A_0^{\otimes 2}$ on $L_{\det}$ shifts by $2a_0$; read the other way, a shift $a$ of $A$ moves the spinor connection by $\tfrac12 a$. Every appearance of $\tfrac12$ or of $2$ in the spin$^c$ theory traces back to this one fact: the determinant circle is the square of the circle the spinor actually feels. We record this heuristic here; the honest derivation, phrased entirely in the well-defined bundle $\slashed{S}$, is [[Thm - Variation of the Twisted and Spin-c Dirac Operators with the Connection|the variation theorem]].

There is one clause whose necessity is easy to overlook and worth isolating, because dropping it destroys the construction: the requirement, in the spin$^c$ module structure $[g,z]\cdot s := z\,\rho(g)s$, that $\rho(-1) = -\operatorname{id}$. This is what makes the formula descend to the quotient by the diagonal $\{\pm1\}$. Were $\rho(-1)$ anything other than $-\operatorname{id}$ — say if one tried to use a representation of $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ that does not extend the Clifford action, on which $-1$ acts trivially — the two representatives $(g,z)$ and $(-g,-z)$ of the same class in $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$ would act differently, $(-z)\rho(-g)s \neq z\rho(g)s$, and $\slashed{S}$ would fail to be a $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-module at all. The spinor representation is singled out among all representations of $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ exactly by extending to the Clifford algebra, and it is this extension that forces $\rho(-1) = (-1)\cdot = -\operatorname{id}$ and thereby makes the spin$^c$ spinor bundle exist.

# The Definition

We build the construction in five parts, in increasing generality. Parts (a) and (b) are the spin case; part (c) twists it by an external gauge field; part (d) is the spin$^c$ generalisation; part (e) records the heuristic that fixes the numerical factors. We assume $M$ is oriented and Riemannian, and — for parts (a)–(c) — spin, with a chosen [[Def - Spin Structure and Spin-c Structure|spin structure]] $\tau : P \to \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$.

## (a) The Levi-Civita connection on a spin structure

Because $\xi : \operatorname{Spin}(n)\to SO(n)$ is a local diffeomorphism, its differential $d\xi : \mathfrak{spin}(n)\to\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is a Lie-algebra isomorphism ([[Thm - Spin(n) inside the Clifford Algebra is a Double Cover of SO(n)|the double-cover page]], part (iii)). We define the **Levi-Civita connection on the spin structure** to be
$$\omega_P := (d\xi)^{-1}\circ\, \tau^*\omega \;\in\; \Omega^1\big(P; \mathfrak{spin}(n)\big),$$
where $\omega\in\Omega^1(\operatorname{Fr}_{SO};\mathfrak{so}(n))$ is the [[Def - Levi-Civita Connection|Levi-Civita connection]] on the frame bundle. That $\omega_P$ is a principal connection on $P$ is the content of the following lemma, stated once in a form that also covers the spin$^c$ case of part (d).

> [!note]- Verification: a general lifting lemma, and its application here
> **Lemma (lifting a connection along an equivariant covering).** Let $\pi : \tilde G \to G$ be a homomorphism of Lie groups that is a local diffeomorphism, so that $d\pi : \tilde{\mathfrak{g}} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is a Lie-algebra isomorphism. Let $\tilde P \to M$ be a principal $\tilde G$-bundle, $\bar P \to M$ a principal $G$-bundle, and $\tau : \tilde P \to \bar P$ a $\pi$-equivariant smooth map covering $\operatorname{id}_M$, meaning $\tau(p\cdot g) = \tau(p)\cdot\pi(g)$ for all $p\in\tilde P$, $g\in\tilde G$. If $\bar\omega$ is a principal connection on $\bar P$, then
> $$\tilde\omega := (d\pi)^{-1}\circ\tau^*\bar\omega \in \Omega^1(\tilde P; \tilde{\mathfrak{g}})$$
> is a principal connection on $\tilde P$.
>
> *Proof.* We must show two things: that $\tilde\omega$ reproduces fundamental vector fields, $\tilde\omega(\xi_{\tilde P}) = \xi$ for every $\xi\in\tilde{\mathfrak g}$; and that $\tilde\omega$ is equivariant, $R_g^*\tilde\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\tilde\omega$ for every $g\in\tilde G$. Both are the defining axioms of a [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle|principal connection]].
>
> **Step 0 — fundamental vector fields are $\tau$-related.** Fix $\xi\in\tilde{\mathfrak g}$ and $p\in\tilde P$. We claim $d\tau_p\big(\xi_{\tilde P}(p)\big) = \big(d\pi(\xi)\big)_{\bar P}\big(\tau(p)\big)$, that is, the fundamental field of $\xi$ upstairs is $\tau$-related to the fundamental field of $d\pi(\xi)$ downstairs. Indeed,
> $$d\tau_p\big(\xi_{\tilde P}(p)\big) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\tau\big(p\cdot\exp(t\xi)\big) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\tau(p)\cdot\pi\big(\exp(t\xi)\big) \qquad(\text{by }\pi\text{-equivariance of }\tau)$$
> $$= \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\tau(p)\cdot\exp\big(t\,d\pi(\xi)\big) = \big(d\pi(\xi)\big)_{\bar P}\big(\tau(p)\big) \qquad(\text{since }\pi\circ\exp = \exp\circ\, d\pi,\ \text{[[Thm - Lie Group Homomorphism Induces Lie Algebra Homomorphism|homomorphism–exp naturality]]}).$$
>
> **Step 1 — reproduction of fundamental fields.** Using Step 0 and that $\bar\omega$ reproduces fundamental fields of $\bar P$ ($\bar\omega(\zeta_{\bar P}) = \zeta$ for $\zeta\in\mathfrak g$, the first connection axiom for $\bar\omega$),
> $$\tilde\omega\big(\xi_{\tilde P}(p)\big) = (d\pi)^{-1}\Big(\bar\omega_{\tau(p)}\big(d\tau_p\,\xi_{\tilde P}(p)\big)\Big) = (d\pi)^{-1}\Big(\bar\omega_{\tau(p)}\big((d\pi(\xi))_{\bar P}(\tau(p))\big)\Big) \qquad(\text{by Step 0})$$
> $$= (d\pi)^{-1}\big(d\pi(\xi)\big) = \xi \qquad(\text{first axiom for }\bar\omega,\ \text{then }(d\pi)^{-1}\circ d\pi = \operatorname{id}).$$
> So $\tilde\omega$ satisfies the first connection axiom.
>
> **Step 2 — equivariance.** Fix $g\in\tilde G$. By naturality of pull-back and $\pi$-equivariance of $\tau$ (which reads $\tau\circ R_g = R_{\pi(g)}\circ\tau$),
> $$R_g^*\tilde\omega = (d\pi)^{-1}\circ R_g^*\tau^*\bar\omega = (d\pi)^{-1}\circ(\tau\circ R_g)^*\bar\omega = (d\pi)^{-1}\circ(R_{\pi(g)}\circ\tau)^*\bar\omega \qquad(\tau\circ R_g = R_{\pi(g)}\circ\tau)$$
> $$= (d\pi)^{-1}\circ\tau^*\big(R_{\pi(g)}^*\bar\omega\big) = (d\pi)^{-1}\circ\tau^*\big(\operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(g)^{-1}}\bar\omega\big) \qquad(\text{second axiom for }\bar\omega)$$
> $$= (d\pi)^{-1}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(g)^{-1}}\circ\,\tau^*\bar\omega \qquad(\operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(g)^{-1}}\text{ is a fixed linear map, so it commutes with }\tau^*).$$
> It remains to move $\operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(g)^{-1}}$ across $(d\pi)^{-1}$. Differentiating the homomorphism identity $\pi(h k h^{-1}) = \pi(h)\pi(k)\pi(h)^{-1}$ in $k$ at $k = e$ gives $d\pi\circ\operatorname{Ad}_h = \operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(h)}\circ\, d\pi$ for all $h\in\tilde G$; taking $h = g^{-1}$ and inverting yields $(d\pi)^{-1}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{\pi(g)^{-1}} = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\circ\,(d\pi)^{-1}$. Substituting,
> $$R_g^*\tilde\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\circ\,(d\pi)^{-1}\circ\tau^*\bar\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\tilde\omega \qquad(\text{intertwining identity, then definition of }\tilde\omega).$$
> So $\tilde\omega$ satisfies the second connection axiom.
>
> **Conclusion.** $\tilde\omega$ reproduces fundamental fields and is equivariant, so it is a principal connection on $\tilde P$. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Application to part (a).** Take $\tilde G = \operatorname{Spin}(n)$, $G = SO(n)$, $\pi = \xi$ (a local diffeomorphism by the double-cover page), $\tilde P = P$, $\bar P = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$, $\tau$ the spin structure (equivariant by definition), and $\bar\omega = \omega$ the Levi-Civita connection. The lemma gives that $\omega_P = (d\xi)^{-1}\circ\tau^*\omega$ is a principal connection on $P$.

Following Haydys, and slightly abusing terminology, we still call $\omega_P$ the **Levi-Civita connection**; it is the unique connection on $P$ that projects to the Riemannian Levi-Civita connection under the double cover.

## (b) The spinor bundle and the spin Dirac operator

Fix a complex Hermitian $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$-module $\slashed{S}$, and let $\rho : \operatorname{Spin}(n) \to U(\slashed{S})$ be the restriction to $\operatorname{Spin}(n)\subset\operatorname{Cl}^0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ of the module action, so that $\rho(g)s = g\cdot s$ for $g\in\operatorname{Spin}(n)$. For $n = 3$ this is the fundamental representation $\slashed{S} = \mathbb{H}$ of $Sp(1) = \operatorname{Spin}(3)$; for $n = 4$ it is $\slashed{S} = \slashed{S}^+\oplus\slashed{S}^-$ with $\slashed{S}^\pm = \mathbb{H}$; both are constructed and shown Hermitian and irreducible on [[Thm - Low-Dimensional Clifford Algebras and the Quaternionic Spinor Modules|the low-dimensional page]].

> [!note] For general $n$, the module used is the distinguished one
> For every $n\geq 3$ there is, up to isomorphism, a unique complex representation $\rho : \operatorname{Spin}(n)\to\operatorname{End}(\slashed{S})$ that extends to a complex irreducible representation of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$; this is Haydys's Definition and is proved in **Lawson–Michelsohn, *Spin Geometry*, Chapter I §5** (Theorems I.5.7–I.5.8). It is neither the unique nor an irreducible $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-representation — the distinguishing property is the extension to the Clifford algebra. This series proves existence and uniqueness only for $n = 3, 4$ (the low-dimensional page), which are the only dimensions it uses; the general statement is recorded as context.

The **spinor bundle** is the [[Def - Associated Bundle|associated bundle]]
$$\slashed{S} := P \times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n),\,\rho}\slashed{S},$$
whose fibre is a copy of the model $\slashed{S}$ and whose points are equivalence classes $[p, s]$ with $[p\cdot g, s] = [p, \rho(g)s]$. It inherits a fibrewise Hermitian metric from the module (well-defined because $\rho$ acts by unitaries) and, from the Levi-Civita connection $\omega_P$ of part (a), an induced [[Thm - Principal Connections Induce Covariant Derivatives on Associated Bundles|covariant derivative]] $\nabla$. We claim $\slashed{S}$ is a **bundle of Clifford modules** and, with this metric and connection, a **Dirac bundle**; its Dirac operator is the **spin Dirac operator** $\slashed{D} = \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla$.

> [!note]- Verification: Clifford multiplication descends to $\slashed{S}$
> The tangent bundle is $TM \cong \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}\times_{SO(n)}\mathbb{R}^n \cong P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n)}\mathbb{R}^n$, where $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ acts on $\mathbb{R}^n$ through $\xi$; a tangent vector is a class $[p, v]$ with $[p\cdot g, v] = [p, \xi(g)v]$. Define the fibrewise Clifford multiplication
> $$\operatorname{Cl} : TM\otimes\slashed{S}\to\slashed{S}, \qquad [p, v]\otimes[p, s]\;\longmapsto\;[p, v\cdot s],$$
> where $v\cdot s$ is the module action of $v\in\mathbb{R}^n\subset\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ on $s\in\slashed{S}$, taken at a common representative $p$. **Well-definedness** requires that the class $[p, v\cdot s]$ be unchanged when the representative is changed by $g\in\operatorname{Spin}(n)$; concretely, replacing $p$ by $p\cdot g$ replaces $v$ by $\xi(g)^{-1}v$ and $s$ by $\rho(g)^{-1}s$, and we must check $[p\cdot g, (\xi(g)^{-1}v)\cdot(\rho(g)^{-1}s)] = [p, v\cdot s]$, i.e.
> $$\rho(g)\big((\xi(g)^{-1}v)\cdot(\rho(g)^{-1}s)\big) = v\cdot s \quad\text{for all }g.$$
> Setting $u = \xi(g)^{-1}v$, $t = \rho(g)^{-1}s$, this is the **equivariance identity** $\rho(g)(u\cdot t) = (\xi(g)u)\cdot(\rho(g)t)$. We prove it directly. On the left, $\rho(g)(u\cdot t) = g\cdot(u\cdot t) = (g u)\cdot t$ by associativity of the module action (since $\rho(g) = g\,\cdot$). On the right, using $\rho(g) = g\,\cdot$ and $\xi(g)u = g u g^{-1}$ (the definition of $\xi$; for $g$ even the twisted and untwisted adjoints agree, so $\xi(g)u = gug^{-1}$ — [[Def - Spin Group and the Double Cover of SO(n)|the spin-group page]]),
> $$(\xi(g)u)\cdot(\rho(g)t) = (g u g^{-1})\cdot(g\cdot t) = \big((gug^{-1})g\big)\cdot t = (gu)\cdot t \qquad(\text{associativity, then }g^{-1}g = 1).$$
> The two sides agree, so the equivariance identity holds and $\operatorname{Cl}$ is well-defined. The Clifford relation is inherited fibrewise: $v\cdot(v\cdot s) = [p, v\cdot(v\cdot s)] = [p, -\lvert v\rvert^2 s] = -\lvert v\rvert^2[p,s]$, since the module satisfies $v\cdot(v\cdot s) = -\lvert v\rvert^2 s$ and $\xi(g)$ is an orthogonal transformation, so $\lvert v\rvert$ is representative-independent. Hence $\slashed{S}$ is a bundle of Clifford modules. The same computation with a general Clifford element $\varphi\in\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ in place of $v$ (using $\xi(g)$ extended to the algebra automorphism $\varphi\mapsto g\varphi g^{-1}$, which is how $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ acts on the fibre of [[Def - Clifford Bundle and Bundle of Clifford Modules|$\operatorname{Cl}(M)$]]) shows $\slashed{S}$ is a bundle of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$-modules.

> [!note]- Verification: $\slashed{S}$ is a Dirac bundle (conditions (1)–(3))
> A [[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|Dirac bundle]] is a bundle of Clifford modules with a metric and a connection satisfying: (1) $\nabla$ is metric; (2) $\langle v\cdot e_1, v\cdot e_2\rangle = \lvert v\rvert^2\langle e_1, e_2\rangle$; (3) $\nabla(\varphi\cdot s) = (\nabla^{LC}\varphi)\cdot s + \varphi\cdot\nabla s$ for $\varphi\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Cl}(M))$, $s\in\Gamma(\slashed{S})$.
>
> **Condition (2) — Clifford compatibility of the metric.** This is a fibrewise statement about the model: $\langle v\cdot s_1, v\cdot s_2\rangle = \lvert v\rvert^2\langle s_1, s_2\rangle$ for all $v\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $s_1, s_2\in\slashed{S}$. For the quaternionic modules of $n = 3, 4$ this is proved on [[Thm - Low-Dimensional Clifford Algebras and the Quaternionic Spinor Modules|the low-dimensional page]] (it is exactly "Haydys's condition (2)" verified there); for general $n$ it is the Hermitian-module property of the distinguished module (Lawson–Michelsohn I.5). Because both the bundle metric and $\operatorname{Cl}$ are induced fibrewise from the model, the identity holds pointwise on $\slashed{S}$, and $\lvert v\rvert$ is representative-independent since $\xi(g)\in SO(n)$.
>
> **Condition (1) — the connection is metric.** The model metric $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on $\slashed{S}$ is $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-invariant: each $g\in\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ is a product of an even number of unit vectors, and by condition (2) with $\lvert v\rvert = 1$ each unit-vector multiplication is an isometry, so $\rho(g)$ is unitary and $\langle\rho(g)s_1, \rho(g)s_2\rangle = \langle s_1, s_2\rangle$. An invariant model inner product defines a section $h$ of the associated bundle $\operatorname{Sym}^2\slashed{S}^*$ (real part) that is induced from a $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-fixed element of the model $\operatorname{Sym}^2\slashed{S}^*$; a section induced from a fixed model element is parallel for the induced connection (this is the invariant-element principle below). Parallelism of the metric section is precisely metric compatibility: $X\langle s_1, s_2\rangle = \langle\nabla_X s_1, s_2\rangle + \langle s_1, \nabla_X s_2\rangle$. Hence (1) holds.
>
> **Condition (3) — the Leibniz rule over $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$.** Both the Levi-Civita connection $\nabla^{LC}$ on $\operatorname{Cl}(M) = P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n)}\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and the connection $\nabla$ on $\slashed{S} = P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n)}\slashed{S}$ are induced from the **same** principal connection $\omega_P$ on $P$ (the connection $\nabla^{LC}$ on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ is induced from $\omega$ on $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$, which is $\omega_P$ pushed down by the local diffeomorphism $\xi$; they are the same connection viewed through the double cover). The Clifford multiplication is a section $\mu$ of $\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(M)\otimes\slashed{S}, \slashed{S})$ induced from the model map $m : \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)\otimes\slashed{S}\to\slashed{S}$, $\varphi\otimes s\mapsto\varphi\cdot s$. This model map is $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-equivariant — the equivariance identity of the previous callout, extended to all of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, says exactly that $m$ is a fixed point of the $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-action on $\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)\otimes\slashed{S}, \slashed{S})$. By the invariant-element principle, $\mu$ is parallel: $\nabla\mu = 0$. Writing out $\nabla\mu = 0$ against $\varphi\otimes s$ (Leibniz rule for the induced connection on the $\operatorname{Hom}$-bundle) gives
> $$0 = (\nabla\mu)(\varphi\otimes s) = \nabla\big(\mu(\varphi\otimes s)\big) - \mu\big(\nabla^{LC}\varphi\otimes s\big) - \mu\big(\varphi\otimes\nabla s\big) = \nabla(\varphi\cdot s) - (\nabla^{LC}\varphi)\cdot s - \varphi\cdot\nabla s,$$
> which rearranges to condition (3).
>
> **The invariant-element principle used above.** For an associated bundle $P\times_{G,\sigma}W$ with connection induced from a principal connection $\omega_P$, a section represented in a local $\omega_P$-adapted description by a **constant** model element $w_0\in W$ satisfies $\nabla(\text{that section}) = 0$ provided $w_0$ is $G$-fixed. Concretely, over a local section $s : U\to P$ the induced covariant derivative is $\nabla = d + \sigma_*(A)$ with $A = s^*\omega_P$ ([[Thm - Principal Connections Induce Covariant Derivatives on Associated Bundles|the induced-connection page]]); applied to the constant representative $w_0$ this is $\nabla w_0 = dw_0 + \sigma_*(A)w_0 = 0 + 0$, since $dw_0 = 0$ and $\sigma_*(\zeta)w_0 = 0$ for every $\zeta\in\mathfrak g$ (the infinitesimal form of $\sigma(g)w_0 = w_0$). This applies with $W = \operatorname{Sym}^2\slashed{S}^*$ and $w_0 = $ the metric (condition (1)), and with $W = \operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n)\otimes\slashed{S}, \slashed{S})$ and $w_0 = m$ (condition (3)).

Thus $\slashed{S}$ is a Dirac bundle, and its **spin Dirac operator** is
$$\slashed{D} := \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla : \Gamma(\slashed{S})\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}), \qquad \slashed{D}s = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i\cdot\nabla_{e_i}s \ \text{ in a local orthonormal frame }(e_1,\dots,e_n).$$

## Chirality in dimension four (R4.3.2)

Let $n = 4$. By [[Thm - Spin Groups in Dimensions Three and Four via Quaternions|the quaternionic description]], $\operatorname{Spin}(4) = Sp_+(1)\times Sp_-(1)$, and the module splits as $\slashed{S} = \slashed{S}^+\oplus\slashed{S}^-$ with $\slashed{S}^\pm = \mathbb{H}$ the fundamental representation of $Sp_\pm(1)$; by equation (112), Clifford multiplication by a vector interchanges the summands, $v\cdot : \slashed{S}^\pm\to\slashed{S}^\mp$. Since the splitting is $\operatorname{Spin}(4)$-invariant (each summand is a subrepresentation), the associated subbundles $\slashed{S}^\pm := P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(4)}\slashed{S}^\pm$ are parallel for $\nabla$ (invariant subspaces induce parallel subbundles, by the invariant-element principle applied to the projections). Hence $\nabla_{e_i}$ preserves the splitting while $e_i\cdot$ reverses it, so
$$\slashed{D} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \slashed{D}^- \\ \slashed{D}^+ & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \slashed{D}^\pm : \Gamma(\slashed{S}^\pm)\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}^\mp),$$
where $\slashed{D}^\pm = \sum_i e_i\cdot\nabla_{e_i}\big|_{\slashed{S}^\pm}$. The two chiral halves are formally adjoint to each other, $(\slashed{D}^+)^* = \slashed{D}^-$, by [[Thm - Dirac Operators are Formally Self-Adjoint|formal self-adjointness]] of $\slashed{D}$.

## (c) Twisting by a gauge field

Let $Q\to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with a [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle|principal connection]] $A$, and let $G\to U(k)$ be a unitary representation, with associated Hermitian bundle $E = Q\times_G\mathbb{C}^k$ carrying the induced covariant derivative $\nabla^A$. The **twisted spinor bundle** is the tensor product $\slashed{S}\otimes E$, with:
- Clifford multiplication acting on the spinor factor only, $v\cdot(s\otimes e) := (v\cdot s)\otimes e$;
- the tensor-product Hermitian metric, $\langle s\otimes e, s'\otimes e'\rangle := \langle s, s'\rangle\langle e, e'\rangle$;
- the tensor-product connection $\nabla^{\otimes} := \nabla\otimes 1 + 1\otimes\nabla^A$.

> [!note]- Verification: $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ is a Dirac bundle
> This is the tensoring exercise of §8.2 ([[Def - Spinor Bundle and Dirac Operator|Dirac-bundle page]]); we record it because it is used directly here. $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ is a bundle of Clifford modules: $v\cdot(v\cdot(s\otimes e)) = (v\cdot v\cdot s)\otimes e = (-\lvert v\rvert^2 s)\otimes e = -\lvert v\rvert^2(s\otimes e)$ (Clifford relation on the $\slashed{S}$ factor). We check the three conditions.
>
> **Condition (1).** The tensor-product connection of two metric connections is metric: for the tensor metric, $X\langle s\otimes e, s'\otimes e'\rangle = X(\langle s,s'\rangle\langle e,e'\rangle) = (X\langle s,s'\rangle)\langle e,e'\rangle + \langle s,s'\rangle(X\langle e,e'\rangle)$, and expanding each factor by metricity of $\nabla$ and $\nabla^A$ recombines to $\langle\nabla^\otimes(s\otimes e), s'\otimes e'\rangle + \langle s\otimes e, \nabla^\otimes(s'\otimes e')\rangle$.
>
> **Condition (2).** Since $\operatorname{Cl}$ acts on the $\slashed{S}$ factor only,
> $$\langle v\cdot(s\otimes e), v\cdot(s'\otimes e')\rangle = \langle (v\cdot s)\otimes e, (v\cdot s')\otimes e'\rangle = \langle v\cdot s, v\cdot s'\rangle\langle e, e'\rangle = \lvert v\rvert^2\langle s, s'\rangle\langle e, e'\rangle = \lvert v\rvert^2\langle s\otimes e, s'\otimes e'\rangle,$$
> using condition (2) for $\slashed{S}$ in the third step.
>
> **Condition (3).** For $\varphi\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Cl}(M))$, since $\varphi\cdot(s\otimes e) = (\varphi\cdot s)\otimes e$ and $\nabla^A e$ carries no Clifford index,
> $$\nabla^\otimes\big(\varphi\cdot(s\otimes e)\big) = \nabla^\otimes\big((\varphi\cdot s)\otimes e\big) = \big(\nabla(\varphi\cdot s)\big)\otimes e + (\varphi\cdot s)\otimes\nabla^A e$$
> $$= \big((\nabla^{LC}\varphi)\cdot s + \varphi\cdot\nabla s\big)\otimes e + (\varphi\cdot s)\otimes\nabla^A e \qquad(\text{condition (3) for }\slashed{S})$$
> $$= (\nabla^{LC}\varphi)\cdot(s\otimes e) + \varphi\cdot\big((\nabla s)\otimes e + s\otimes\nabla^A e\big) = (\nabla^{LC}\varphi)\cdot(s\otimes e) + \varphi\cdot\nabla^\otimes(s\otimes e),$$
> where the last equality uses $\varphi\cdot\big((\nabla s)\otimes e\big) = (\varphi\cdot\nabla s)\otimes e$ and $\varphi\cdot(s\otimes\nabla^A e) = (\varphi\cdot s)\otimes\nabla^A e$ (Clifford acts on the spinor factor).

The **twisted Dirac operator** is
$$\slashed{D}_A := \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla^\otimes : \Gamma(\slashed{S}\otimes E)\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}\otimes E), \qquad \slashed{D}_A(s\otimes e) = \sum_i e_i\cdot\nabla^\otimes_{e_i}(s\otimes e).$$
The subscript $A$ records the dependence on the gauge connection.

## (d) The spin$^c$ construction

Now let $M$ carry a [[Def - Spin Structure and Spin-c Structure|spin$^c$ structure]] $P\to M$: a principal $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-bundle with an equivariant map to $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ inducing $P/U(1)\cong\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$. Recall $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n) = \operatorname{Spin}(n)\times U(1)/\{\pm1\}$, with determinant bundle $P_{\det} = P/\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ (a principal $U(1)$-bundle) and $L_{\det} = P\times_{\rho_{\det}}\mathbb{C}$. The exact sequence (120),
$$1\to\{\pm1\}\to\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)\xrightarrow{(\rho_0,\rho_{\det})}SO(n)\times U(1)\to 1,$$
exhibits the map $\tau : P\to P/\{\pm1\} = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}\times_M P_{\det}$ as a fibrewise double cover, and $(\rho_0,\rho_{\det})$ as a local diffeomorphism with differential the isomorphism $\mathfrak{spin}^c(n)\cong\mathfrak{so}(n)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$.

**The spin$^c$ connection.** Let $A$ be a connection on $P_{\det}$ — equivalently a unitary connection on the Hermitian line bundle $L_{\det}$. Together with the Levi-Civita connection $\omega$ on $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ it defines the product connection $\omega\oplus A$ on the fibre product $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}\times_M P_{\det}$, valued in $\mathfrak{so}(n)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$. We define the **spin$^c$ connection**
$$\tau^*(\omega + A) := \big(d(\rho_0,\rho_{\det})\big)^{-1}\circ\tau^*(\omega\oplus A)\;\in\;\Omega^1\big(P; \mathfrak{spin}^c(n)\big).$$
By the general lifting lemma of part (a), applied with $\pi = (\rho_0,\rho_{\det}) : \operatorname{Spin}^c(n)\to SO(n)\times U(1)$ (a local diffeomorphism, and a homomorphism), $\tilde P = P$, $\bar P = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}\times_M P_{\det}$, $\tau$ the double cover (equivariant by the definition of a spin$^c$ structure), and $\bar\omega = \omega\oplus A$, the form $\tau^*(\omega + A)$ is a principal connection on $P$. Thus a unitary connection on the determinant line, together with the fixed Levi-Civita connection, determines a connection on the spin$^c$ bundle.

**The spin$^c$ spinor bundle.** The distinguished module $\slashed{S}$ becomes a $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-module by
$$[g, z]\cdot s := z\,\rho(g)s.$$

> [!note]- Verification: the $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-action is well-defined
> A class in $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n) = \operatorname{Spin}(n)\times U(1)/\{\pm1\}$ has exactly two representatives, $(g, z)$ and $(-g, -z)$. We must check they act identically. Using $\rho(-1) = -\operatorname{id}$ (Clifford multiplication by $-1\in\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ is $-\operatorname{id}$) and $\rho(-g) = \rho(-1)\rho(g) = -\rho(g)$,
> $$(-g, -z)\cdot s = (-z)\,\rho(-g)s = (-z)\big(-\rho(g)s\big) = z\,\rho(g)s = (g, z)\cdot s.$$
> So the formula descends to the quotient, and $\slashed{S}$ is a $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-module. (This is the clause isolated in the Axiom Motivation: $\rho(-1) = -\operatorname{id}$ is precisely what makes the diagonal $\{\pm1\}$ act consistently.)

The **spin$^c$ spinor bundle** is the associated bundle
$$\slashed{S} := P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)}\slashed{S},$$
with the fibrewise Hermitian metric (well-defined since $[g,z]$ acts by $z\rho(g)$, a unitary times a phase, hence unitarily) and the covariant derivative $\nabla^A$ induced from the spin$^c$ connection $\tau^*(\omega + A)$.

> [!note]- Verification: the spin$^c$ spinor bundle is a Dirac bundle
> **Clifford multiplication descends.** Here $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$ acts on $\mathbb{R}^n$ through $\rho_0$, so $TM\cong P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)}\mathbb{R}^n$ with $[p\cdot[g,z], v] = [p, \rho_0[g,z]v] = [p, \xi(g)v]$. Define $\operatorname{Cl}([p,v]\otimes[p,s]) = [p, v\cdot s]$; well-definedness is the equivariance identity $([g,z]\cdot)(v\cdot s) = (\rho_0[g,z]v)\cdot([g,z]\cdot s)$. This follows from the spin case: since $z\in\mathbb{C}$ is a scalar and commutes with Clifford multiplication,
> $$[g,z]\cdot(v\cdot s) = z\,\rho(g)(v\cdot s) = z\big((\xi(g)v)\cdot\rho(g)s\big) = (\xi(g)v)\cdot\big(z\,\rho(g)s\big) = (\rho_0[g,z]v)\cdot([g,z]\cdot s),$$
> using the spin equivariance identity $\rho(g)(v\cdot s) = (\xi(g)v)\cdot\rho(g)s$ from part (b) in the second step and $\rho_0[g,z] = \xi(g)$ in the last. So $\slashed{S}$ is a bundle of Clifford modules.
>
> **Conditions (1)–(3).** The verification is identical in form to part (b), with $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ replaced by $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$ and $\rho$ by $[g,z]\mapsto z\rho(g)$, and with the connection $\tau^*(\omega + A)$ in place of $\omega_P$. Condition (2) is a fibrewise model statement unchanged by the phase $z$ (which cancels in the sesquilinear pairing: $\langle z\,\rho(g)(v\cdot s_1), z\,\rho(g)(v\cdot s_2)\rangle = \lvert z\rvert^2\langle\dots\rangle = \langle\dots\rangle$ since $\lvert z\rvert = 1$). Condition (1) holds because the model metric is $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-invariant ($[g,z]$ acts unitarily) and the invariant-element principle makes the metric section parallel. Condition (3) holds because $\nabla^{LC}$ on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ and $\nabla^A$ on $\slashed{S}$ are both induced from the single connection $\tau^*(\omega + A)$ on $P$ — the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$-summand governs $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ and the module map $m$ is $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-equivariant (the displayed identity above is exactly the equivariance of $m$ restricted to $\mathbb{R}^n$; for general $\varphi$ the $U(1)$-factor acts trivially on $\operatorname{Cl}(M)$ and by the phase on $\slashed{S}$, and the phase passes through $\varphi\cdot$), so $\nabla^A m = 0$ and (3) follows as before.

The **spin$^c$ Dirac operator** is
$$\slashed{D}_A := \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla^A : \Gamma(\slashed{S})\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}), \qquad \slashed{D}_A s = \sum_i e_i\cdot\nabla^A_{e_i}s.$$
In dimension four the chirality decomposition of the module (now as a $\operatorname{Spin}^c(4)$-representation, $\slashed{S} = \slashed{S}^+\oplus\slashed{S}^-$ via $\rho_\pm$) again yields the off-diagonal block form $\slashed{D}_A = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \slashed{D}_A^-\\ \slashed{D}_A^+ & 0\end{pmatrix}$ with $\slashed{D}_A^\pm : \Gamma(\slashed{S}^\pm)\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}^\mp)$, by the same parity argument as in the untwisted case.

## (e) The heuristic $\slashed{S} = \slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$ and the factor $\tfrac12$ (R4.3.4, R4.3.5)

A spin$^c$ spinor bundle behaves, in every local computation, like a twisted spinor bundle $\slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$: here $\slashed{S}_0$ would be the pure spinor bundle of a spin structure and $L_0$ a Hermitian line bundle with $L_{\det} = L_0^{\,2}$. The subtlety, made precise by the spin$^c$ formalism, is that in general **neither $\slashed{S}_0$ nor $L_0$ exists globally** — only their product $\slashed{S}$ does. When both do exist (part (c) of the Examples, and [[Ex - Spin-c Structures on a Spin Manifold|the spin-manifold exercise]]) the spin$^c$ Dirac operator is literally the twisted Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_{A_0}$ of $\slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$.

This heuristic pins down the numerical factor in the connection dependence. On the model, the central circle $z\in U(1)\subset\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$ acts on the spinor $\slashed{S}$ with weight one (by $z\cdot$) but has $\rho_{\det}[1,z] = z^2$, so $L_{\det}$ carries weight two and $L_{\det} = L_0^{\,2}$. A connection $A$ on $P_{\det}$ is a connection on $L_0^{\,2}$; if the notional connection on $L_0$ is $A_0$, then $A$ is its square $A_0^{\otimes 2}$, whose connection form is twice that of $A_0$, and shifting $A_0\mapsto A_0 + a_0$ shifts $A\mapsto A + 2a_0$. Equivalently, the determinant circle is the square of the circle the spinor feels, so a shift $a\in\Omega^1(M; i\mathbb{R})$ of the determinant connection $A$ moves the spinor connection $\nabla^A$ by $\tfrac12 a$. This is why the [[Thm - Variation of the Twisted and Spin-c Dirac Operators with the Connection|variation theorem]] reads $\slashed{D}_{A + a}\psi = \slashed{D}_A\psi + \tfrac12 a\cdot\psi$ in the spin$^c$ case (against $\slashed{D}_{A+a}\psi = \slashed{D}_A\psi + a\cdot\psi$ in the twisted case (c)), and it is the source of the factor $2$ in the chapter XI gauge action $A\cdot g = A + 2\,g^{-1}dg$. The honest proof of the $\tfrac12$, phrased entirely in the well-defined bundle $\slashed{S}$ without invoking $\slashed{S}_0$ or $L_0$, is the variation theorem; here we only record the picture that predicts it.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The four operators of this page are one functor evaluated at increasing data. The associated-bundle construction $(-)\times_{H,\sigma}W$ sends a principal $H$-bundle with connection to a vector bundle with covariant derivative, functorially in $H$-equivariant maps; the spinor bundle is its value at $(P, \omega_P)$ and the fixed spin representation $(\slashed{S}, \rho)$, and Clifford multiplication is the image of the equivariant model map $m$ under the same functor, which is why $m$'s equivariance becomes the Leibniz rule (3) and the model Clifford relation becomes the fibrewise one. Twisting is the monoidal structure: $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ is the tensor product of associated bundles, and $\slashed{D}_A$ is the Dirac operator of the tensor Dirac bundle. The spin$^c$ passage is a change of structure group along the central extension $1\to U(1)\to\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)\to SO(n)\to 1$: a spin$^c$ structure is a lift of the frame bundle across this extension, the determinant bundle $P_{\det}$ is the pushout along $\rho_{\det}$, and the spin$^c$ connection is the unique lift of $(\omega, A)$ across the local diffeomorphism $(\rho_0, \rho_{\det})$, guaranteed by the lifting lemma. In this language the recurring $\tfrac12$ is the weight-one-versus-weight-two mismatch between the module and the determinant character on the central $U(1)$ — a purely representation-theoretic datum of the extension, independent of any manifold.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The construction is the geometric incarnation of **Dirac's factorisation of the Laplacian**. On flat $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the trivial spin structure and constant Clifford matrices $\gamma_i$, the Dirac operator is $\slashed{D} = \sum_i\gamma_i\partial_i$, and $\slashed{D}^2 = \sum_{i,j}\gamma_i\gamma_j\partial_i\partial_j = \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}(\gamma_i\gamma_j + \gamma_j\gamma_i)\partial_i\partial_j = -\sum_i\partial_i^2 = \Delta$, using the polarised relation $\gamma_i\gamma_j + \gamma_j\gamma_i = -2\delta_{ij}$. The whole apparatus of this page is what makes "$\slashed{D}$ is a square root of the Laplacian" survive onto a curved, possibly non-spin manifold with a gauge field: the Weitzenböck formula of §8.4 computes $\slashed{D}_A^2$ as a connection Laplacian plus explicit curvature terms, and the correction terms are exactly the price of curvature and twisting.

In **gauge theory and physics**, $\slashed{D}_A$ is the minimally coupled Dirac operator: a section of $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ is a charged spinor field, and the twisting connection $A$ is the gauge potential to which it couples. The spin$^c$ Dirac operator is the specific coupling that Seiberg–Witten theory uses, where the coupling field is not external but is itself the connection on the determinant line, made dynamical. In **complex geometry**, a Kähler manifold carries a canonical spin$^c$ structure whose spinor bundle is $\Lambda^{0,\bullet}T^*M$ and whose spin$^c$ Dirac operator (with the Chern connection on the anticanonical line) is $\sqrt2(\bar\partial + \bar\partial^*)$; this is recorded as context, since the vault does not develop Dolbeault theory. In **index theory**, the chiral half $\slashed{D}_A^+$ has an index that the Atiyah–Singer theorem expresses as a characteristic number involving $c_1(L_{\det})$ and the $\hat A$-genus, the engine of the four-manifold applications of chapters XI and XIII.

**True name.** The definition is already operational: $\slashed{D}_A = \operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla^A$ is the Clifford contraction of whatever covariant derivative the geometry supplies. Everything on this page is machinery for producing the one canonical $\nabla^A$ (from the Levi-Civita connection, a gauge field, and a determinant connection) and certifying that the resulting bundle satisfies the Dirac-bundle axioms so that $\operatorname{Cl}\circ\nabla^A$ deserves the name Dirac operator.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the trivial spin structure.** Take $M = \mathbb{R}^3$ with its flat metric and the trivial spin structure $P = \mathbb{R}^3\times\operatorname{Spin}(3)$. The Levi-Civita connection is flat, so $\omega_P$ is the trivial (Maurer–Cartan) connection and $\nabla = d$ componentwise. The spinor bundle is the product bundle $\slashed{S} = \mathbb{R}^3\times\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{R}^3\times\mathbb{C}^2$ (complex structure by right multiplication by $\bar\imath$), with Clifford multiplication by $e_1, e_2, e_3$ given by the constant quaternion units, equivalently by the [[Def - The Pauli Matrices|Pauli matrices]] $i\sigma_1, i\sigma_2, i\sigma_3$ ([[Thm - Low-Dimensional Clifford Algebras and the Quaternionic Spinor Modules|low-dimensional page]]). We verify the Dirac-bundle conditions clause by clause. Condition (2): each $e_i\cdot$ acts as multiplication by a unit quaternion, an isometry of $\mathbb{H}$, so $\langle e_i\cdot s_1, e_i\cdot s_2\rangle = \langle s_1, s_2\rangle = \lvert e_i\rvert^2\langle s_1, s_2\rangle$. Condition (1): $\nabla = d$ is metric for the constant Hermitian metric. Condition (3): $\nabla^{LC} = d$ on the (flat, trivial) Clifford bundle and $\nabla = d$ on $\slashed{S}$, and $d(\varphi\cdot s) = (d\varphi)\cdot s + \varphi\cdot ds$ is the ordinary Leibniz rule for the constant bilinear multiplication. The Dirac operator is $\slashed{D} = \sum_{i=1}^3 e_i\cdot\partial_i = i\sigma_1\partial_1 + i\sigma_2\partial_2 + i\sigma_3\partial_3$, a constant-coefficient first-order operator on $\mathbb{H}$-valued functions.

**Is an instance — $\mathbb{R}^4$ with the trivial spin structure, and the explicit quaternionic form.** Take $M = \mathbb{R}^4 = \mathbb{H}$ with coordinates $(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3)$ and the trivial spin structure. The spinor bundle is the product bundle $\slashed{S} = \mathbb{R}^4\times(\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H})$, with $\slashed{S}^\pm = \mathbb{H}$; Clifford multiplication by $h\in\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{R}^4$ is $h\cdot(v_1, v_2) = (hv_2, -\bar h v_1)$ (equation (112)), which is odd, mapping $\slashed{S}^\pm\to\slashed{S}^\mp$. The connection is $\nabla = d$ (flat trivial spin structure), so the Dirac operator has the block form of the chirality paragraph, and tracing through the definition (as done in full in §8.4) gives the explicit chiral halves acting on $u : \mathbb{R}^4\to\mathbb{H}$:
$$\slashed{D}^+(u) = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x_0} + i\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_1} + j\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_2} + k\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_3}, \qquad \slashed{D}^-(u) = -\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_0} + i\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_1} + j\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_2} + k\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_3}.$$
These are the four-dimensional analogues of the Cauchy–Riemann operators $\bar\partial = \tfrac12(\partial_x + i\partial_y)$ and $\partial = \tfrac12(\partial_x - i\partial_y)$, and $\slashed{D}^2 = \operatorname{diag}(\slashed{D}^-\slashed{D}^+, \slashed{D}^+\slashed{D}^-) = \Delta$ with $\Delta = -\sum_{i=0}^3\partial_i^2$ — the flat verification of "the Dirac operator is a square root of the Laplacian" (§8.4, equation (128)). The Dirac-bundle conditions are checked exactly as for $\mathbb{R}^3$: (2) holds because $h\cdot$ has $\lvert h\cdot(v_1,v_2)\rvert^2 = \lvert hv_2\rvert^2 + \lvert\bar h v_1\rvert^2 = \lvert h\rvert^2(\lvert v_1\rvert^2 + \lvert v_2\rvert^2)$; (1) and (3) hold because everything is constant and $\nabla = d$.

**Is an instance — the spin$^c$ structure of a spin manifold twisted by a line bundle (E4.3.3).** Let $M$ be spin with spin structure $P_{\operatorname{Spin}}$, and let $P_0\to M$ be a principal $U(1)$-bundle with associated line bundle $L_0 = P_0\times_{U(1)}\mathbb{C}$. Then
$$P := \big(P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\times_M P_0\big)/\{\pm1\}$$
— quotient by the diagonal $\{\pm1\}$ acting as $(-1)$ on the $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-factor and as $(-1)$ on the $U(1)$-factor — is a principal $\big(\operatorname{Spin}(n)\times U(1)\big)/\{\pm1\} = \operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-bundle. We verify the defining clauses (the full verification is the content of [[Ex - Spin-c Structures on a Spin Manifold|the exercise]]; we record the outcome here). First, $P$ is a spin$^c$ structure: the $\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)$-equivariant map $P\to\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ is induced from $P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\to\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$ (which factors through the diagonal $\{\pm1\}$ because $\{\pm1\}$ maps to $1\in SO(n)$), and $P/U(1) = \big(P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\times_M P_0\big)/\big(\{\pm1\}\times U(1)\big) = P_{\operatorname{Spin}}/\{\pm1\} = \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}$. Second, its spinor bundle is $\slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$: a point of $P\times_{\operatorname{Spin}^c(n)}\slashed{S}$ is a class $[[q_0, q], s]$ with the action $[g, z]\cdot s = z\rho(g)s$, and the map $[[q_0, q], s]\mapsto[q_0, \rho(g)s]\otimes[q, z]$ identifies it with $(P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(n)}\slashed{S})\otimes(P_0\times_{U(1)}\mathbb{C}) = \slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$. Third, its determinant line bundle is $L_{\det} = P\times_{\rho_{\det}}\mathbb{C} = P_0\times_{z\mapsto z^2}\mathbb{C} = L_0^{\,2}$, since the $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$-factor is killed by $\rho_{\det}$ and $\rho_{\det}[g,z] = z^2$. Finally, given a connection $A_0$ on $P_0$ (equivalently a unitary connection on $L_0$), the induced determinant connection is $A = A_0^{\otimes 2}$ on $L_0^{\,2} = L_{\det}$, and the resulting spin$^c$ Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_A$ equals the twisted Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_{A_0}$ of $\slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$ from part (c). This is the honest instance of the heuristic in part (e): here $\slashed{S}_0$ and $L_0$ genuinely exist, and $\slashed{S} = \slashed{S}_0\otimes L_0$ on the nose.

**Is NOT an instance — a Clifford module of the wrong sign.** If one takes a vector space $V$ with a bilinear map satisfying $u\cdot(u\cdot v) = +\lvert u\rvert^2 v$ (the opposite Clifford sign) and forms the analogous associated bundle, the fibrewise relation becomes $v\cdot(v\cdot s) = +\lvert v\rvert^2 s$, so it is a bundle of modules over the **other** Clifford algebra $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n, +\lvert\cdot\rvert^2)$, not over our $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n) = \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^n, -\lvert\cdot\rvert^2)$. Its "Dirac operator" $\sum_i e_i\cdot\nabla_{e_i}$ then squares to $-\Delta$ up to curvature rather than $+\Delta$, and the formal self-adjointness argument (which uses skew-adjointness of Clifford multiplication, a consequence of condition (2) with the correct sign) fails. This is exactly the pitfall the Clifford-sign Convention callout is there to prevent.

**Corollary — the twisted and spin$^c$ Dirac operators are formally self-adjoint.** Because $\slashed{S}\otimes E$ and the spin$^c$ spinor bundle are Dirac bundles (parts (c) and (d)), [[Thm - Dirac Operators are Formally Self-Adjoint|formal self-adjointness]] applies verbatim: for compactly supported sections, $\int_M\langle\slashed{D}_A\psi_1, \psi_2\rangle\,\mathrm{vol} = \int_M\langle\psi_1, \slashed{D}_A\psi_2\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$, and in dimension four the chiral halves satisfy $(\slashed{D}_A^+)^* = \slashed{D}_A^-$. This is used throughout chapter XI.

**Calibration check.** Confirm three things from the page. First, in part (b) the equivariance identity $\rho(g)(v\cdot s) = (\xi(g)v)\cdot\rho(g)s$ used $\rho(g) = g\,\cdot$ and $\xi(g)v = gvg^{-1}$ together with associativity of the module action — re-derive it in one line, $g\cdot(v\cdot s) = (gv)\cdot s = (gvg^{-1})\cdot(g\cdot s)$. Second, in part (d) the well-definedness of $[g,z]\cdot s = z\rho(g)s$ used only $\rho(-1) = -\operatorname{id}$; check that if $\rho(-1)$ were $+\operatorname{id}$ the two representatives would give $\pm z\rho(g)s$, so the action would be ill-defined. Third, the factor $\tfrac12$ in the spin$^c$ variation and the factor $2$ in $L_{\det} = L_0^{\,2}$ are the same fact seen from two sides — verify that $\rho_{\det}[1, z] = z^2$ has differential (multiplication by) $2$ on $\mathfrak u(1)$, while the spinor sees $[1, z]$ with weight one.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The Weitzenböck / Lichnerowicz formula *(from §8.4)*
> With $\slashed{D}_A$ in hand, one computes $\slashed{D}_A^2 = \nabla^{A*}\nabla^A + \mathcal{R}$, a connection Laplacian plus an explicit curvature endomorphism built from the scalar curvature and the twisting curvature $F_A$. This **[[Thm - Dirac Operators are Formally Self-Adjoint|formally self-adjoint]]** square is the origin of vanishing theorems (harmonic spinors force curvature constraints) and, in the spin$^c$ case, of the Seiberg–Witten a priori estimates.

> [!tip] The Seiberg–Witten equations *(from Gauge Theory XI)*
> The spin$^c$ Dirac operator $\slashed{D}_A$ on $\slashed{S}^+$ is one of the two Seiberg–Witten fields; the pair $(A, \psi)$ of a determinant connection and a positive spinor solves $\slashed{D}_A\psi = 0$ and $F_A^+ = \sigma(\psi)$. The factor $\tfrac12$ of part (e) reappears as the factor $2$ in the gauge group's action on connections.

> [!tip] The index of the chiral Dirac operator *(from Gauge Theory XI, XIII)*
> The Fredholm index of $\slashed{D}_A^+ : \Gamma(\slashed{S}^+)\to\Gamma(\slashed{S}^-)$ is a topological invariant computed by the Atiyah–Singer index theorem; it fixes the dimension of the Seiberg–Witten moduli space and, in the spin case, drives Rokhlin's theorem.
