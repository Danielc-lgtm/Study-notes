# Problem Statement

Let $B$ be a smooth manifold and let $P = B \times SU(2)$ be the trivial principal $SU(2)$-bundle over $B$, with the projection $\operatorname{pr}_2 : B \times SU(2) \to SU(2)$ onto the group factor. Equip $P$ with the **product connection**
$$\omega := \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta \in \Omega^{1}(B \times SU(2); \mathfrak{su}(2)),$$
where $\theta \in \Omega^{1}(SU(2); \mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the left Maurer–Cartan form of $SU(2)$. Prove that the curvature of $\omega$ vanishes,
$$\Omega \;=\; d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega \wedge \omega] \;=\; \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\!\Big(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta]\Big) \;=\; 0,$$
by computing $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta]$ **explicitly in quaternion coordinates**: identify $SU(2)$ with the group of unit quaternions $S^{3} \subset \mathbb{H}$, write $\theta = \bar q\,dq$, and show that the two summands cancel. Conclude that for every $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d \ge 1$ the Chern–Weil form $p(F_\omega)$ vanishes, so that every characteristic class $c_p(P)$ of the trivial bundle is zero. This is the quaternionic instance of the mechanism behind [[Thm - Trivial Bundles Have Vanishing Characteristic Classes|the vanishing of characteristic classes on trivial bundles]].

**Recall:**

The objects in play are the Maurer–Cartan form of a Lie group, the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms, the curvature of a principal connection, the Maurer–Cartan structure equation, and the Chern–Weil form of an invariant polynomial.

![[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form#The Definition]]

For a matrix Lie group — and $SU(2)$ is one — the left Maurer–Cartan form is $\theta_g = dL_{g^{-1}} : T_gG \to \mathfrak{g}$, which in the ambient matrix algebra is written $\theta = g^{-1}\,dg$; it is the unique left-invariant $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $1$-form taking each $\xi \in \mathfrak{g} = T_eG$ to itself, that is, $\theta(\tilde\xi) = \xi$ for the left-invariant vector field $\tilde\xi$ generated by $\xi$.

![[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket#The Definition]]

For $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $1$-forms $\alpha, \beta$ the bracket $[\alpha \wedge \beta]$ is the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $2$-form determined by $[\alpha \wedge \beta](X, Y) = [\alpha(X), \beta(Y)] - [\alpha(Y), \beta(X)]$, where $[\cdot,\cdot]$ is the Lie bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$. In particular $[\alpha \wedge \alpha](X, Y) = 2[\alpha(X), \alpha(Y)]$, which is why the structure equation and the curvature formula carry the factor $\tfrac12$.

![[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection#The Definition]]

The curvature of a principal connection $\omega$ is $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega \wedge \omega] \in \Omega^{2}(P; \mathfrak{g})$; a connection with $\Omega = 0$ is called [[Def - Flat Connection|flat]].

![[Thm - The Maurer-Cartan Equation#Statement]]

The Maurer–Cartan equation states $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0$; for a matrix group this reads $d(g^{-1}dg) = -\,g^{-1}dg \wedge g^{-1}dg$, and consequently $d(g^{*}\theta) + \tfrac12[g^{*}\theta \wedge g^{*}\theta] = 0$ for every smooth map $g : U \to G$.

![[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial#The Definition]]

For an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d$ and a connection with curvature $\Omega$, the Chern–Weil form $p(\Omega) \in \Omega^{2d}(P)$ is basic and descends to $p(F_\omega) \in \Omega^{2d}(M)$; it is built by evaluating $p$ on the entries of the curvature, so it is a fixed algebraic (multilinear, wedge-product) expression in $\Omega$ that produces $0$ whenever $\Omega = 0$ and $d \ge 1$.

> [!warning] Convention: Bär's sign slip, bracket versus associative wedge, and his notation $[\omega,\omega]$
> Three conventions govern this page.
> 1. **Bär's printed sign.** In Bär's Remark 2.5.9 (source B, §2.5) the computation of $d\phi(X,Y)$ for the Maurer–Cartan form $\phi$ (his name for $\theta$) is printed as $d\phi(X, Y) = \partial_X(Y(e)) - \partial_Y(X(e)) - [X,Y](e) = [X,Y](e)$. The first two summands are derivatives of the *constant* functions $\phi(Y) = Y(e)$ and $\phi(X) = X(e)$ and are therefore zero, so the correct value is $d\phi(X,Y) = -[X,Y](e)$, not $+[X,Y](e)$. With Bär's printed sign the two terms would add to $2[X,Y]$ rather than cancel. We use the corrected value throughout; it is exactly what [[Thm - The Maurer-Cartan Equation|the Maurer–Cartan equation]] delivers.
> 2. **Bracket versus associative wedge.** Bär writes the structure equation as $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega, \omega]$ using the Lie-algebra bracket. For a matrix (or, here, quaternion) group the Lie bracket is the commutator of an associative product, and one has $\tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = \theta \wedge \theta$, where $\theta \wedge \theta$ denotes the wedge taken with the *associative* multiplication of the ambient algebra (matrix product, or quaternion product). We prove this identity in Step 3; it is the single place the associative structure enters.
> 3. **Notation.** We write $[\alpha \wedge \beta]$ (with the explicit wedge) for the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms, following the series; Bär's $[\alpha, \beta]$ is the same object.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *direct verification* — a one-star drill whose purpose is to make a general vanishing theorem concrete on the smallest non-abelian structure group of gauge theory. The general statement is [[Thm - Trivial Bundles Have Vanishing Characteristic Classes|that a trivial bundle carries a flat connection and hence has vanishing characteristic classes in positive degree]]; the flat connection is the product connection $\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$, and its flatness is nothing other than the Maurer–Cartan equation pulled back along $\operatorname{pr}_2$. The exercise is to see this equation happen, in coordinates, for $SU(2) \cong S^{3}$.

**Assumption pattern.** The only structural fact we use about $SU(2)$ is that it is a group of unit quaternions: $SU(2) \cong \{q \in \mathbb{H} : |q| = 1\} = S^{3}$, with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2) \cong \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H} = \operatorname{span}\{i, j, k\}$ and Lie bracket the quaternion commutator $[\xi, \eta] = \xi\eta - \eta\xi$. The recognisable trigger is that the base $B$ plays no role at all — the curvature is a pullback along $\operatorname{pr}_2$, so the entire computation lives on the group factor $SU(2)$, and $\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}$ of the zero form is the zero form. The problem reduces to a statement purely about the group.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: (i) use naturality of the exterior derivative and of the bracket under pullback to write $\Omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta])$, so it suffices to show $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0$ on $SU(2)$ (this is precisely [[Thm - The Maurer-Cartan Equation|the Maurer–Cartan equation]], which we re-prove here in quaternion coordinates rather than cite); (ii) in the quaternion model $\theta = \bar q\,dq$, differentiate the relation $\bar q q = 1$ to get $d\bar q = -\theta\bar q$, hence $d\theta = -\theta \wedge \theta$; (iii) identify $\tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = \theta \wedge \theta$ from the definition of the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms together with the commutator description of the quaternion bracket; (iv) add the two, obtain $0$, and feed $\Omega = 0$ into [[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial|the Chern–Weil construction]] to conclude $p(F_\omega) = 0$ for $\deg p \ge 1$.

**Key decision point.** The one genuine decision is *how* to compute $d\theta$. Two paths are available. The path in quaternion coordinates is algebraic: write $\theta = \bar q\,dq$, differentiate $\bar q q = 1$, and let associativity of the quaternions do the work; this gives $d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta$ globally in one line. The alternative path expands $\theta$ in a left-invariant coframe $\theta = \theta^{i} i + \theta^{j} j + \theta^{k} k$ and verifies the structure equation component by component; it is more laborious but shows the cancellation in ordinary $2$-forms on $S^{3}$. We take the algebraic path as the proof and use the component computation as a concrete check, so that both readings are available.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations (named descriptively; the numbering will be reconciled with the [[Gauge Theory VI — Chern–Weil Theory, Characteristic Classes, and the Chern–Simons Functional|topic page's Legal Operations]] once it is assembled).

1. **Push curvature through a pullback.** Because $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$ and both $d$ and the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms commute with pullback, the curvature $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]$ equals $\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}$ of the corresponding expression in $\theta$. This turns a computation on the total space $B \times SU(2)$ into a computation on the group factor $SU(2)$ alone.

2. **Model the structure group as unit quaternions.** Replace the abstract $SU(2)$ by $\{q \in \mathbb{H} : |q| = 1\}$ and its Lie algebra by the imaginary quaternions, so that the Maurer–Cartan form becomes the concrete $\mathbb{H}$-valued form $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ and every Lie-theoretic operation becomes an algebraic manipulation of quaternions.

3. **Differentiate a defining identity to extract a derivative.** From $\bar q q = 1$ (the constraint cutting out $S^{3}$ inside $\mathbb{H}$) differentiate to get $d\bar q\,q + \bar q\,dq = 0$, hence $d\bar q = -\theta\bar q$. This is the quaternionic version of $d(g^{-1}) = -g^{-1}(dg)g^{-1}$ for matrix groups.

4. **Convert the Lie bracket of forms into the associative wedge.** Use the definition $[\alpha\wedge\alpha](X,Y) = 2[\alpha(X),\alpha(Y)]$ together with the fact that in an associative algebra $[\xi,\eta] = \xi\eta - \eta\xi$ to identify $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = \theta\wedge\theta$, the wedge taken with quaternion multiplication.

5. **Feed a vanishing curvature into an algebraic invariant.** The Chern–Weil form $p(\Omega)$ is a fixed multilinear wedge-product expression in the entries of $\Omega$ of total form-degree $2d$; when $\Omega = 0$ and $d \ge 1$, every monomial in that expression contains a factor $\Omega$ and so vanishes, giving $p(F_\omega) = 0$ and $c_p(P) = 0$.

What does that let you factor out of $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]$? Once you have done so, you only ever need to work on $SU(2)$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Identify $SU(2)$ with the unit quaternions $S^{3} = \{q \in \mathbb{H} : |q| = 1\}$ (see [[Ex - SU(2) is Diffeomorphic to S^3|the diffeomorphism $SU(2) \cong S^{3}$]]). On a matrix — or quaternion — group the Maurer–Cartan form is $\theta = g^{-1}\,dg$; here $q^{-1} = \bar q$ because $|q| = 1$, so $\theta = \bar q\,dq$. Check first that $\theta$ is genuinely $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued, i.e. that $\bar q\,dq$ is a *purely imaginary* quaternion, by differentiating $\bar q q = 1$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> To compute $d\theta$, do not expand into real coordinates yet. Differentiate $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ directly: $d(dq) = 0$, so $d\theta = d\bar q \wedge dq$. Now you need $d\bar q$; get it from the same identity $\bar q q = 1$ you used in Hint 2. Substituting, everything collapses to a multiple of $\theta \wedge \theta$ — with a sign.

> [!note]- Hint 4

Separately, $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$ uses the *Lie bracket* $[\xi,\eta] = \xi\eta - \eta\xi$. Show these two "$\theta\wedge\theta$"s agree: from $[\alpha\wedge\alpha](X,Y) = 2[\alpha(X),\alpha(Y)]$ and $(\alpha\wedge\alpha)(X,Y) = \alpha(X)\alpha(Y) - \alpha(Y)\alpha(X)$, deduce $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = \theta\wedge\theta$. Add to $d\theta$ and read off $0$. Finally, remember that $p(F_\omega)$ is built from the entries of the curvature, so a zero curvature gives a zero form in every positive degree.

# Solution

The computation has four moves. First we use that a pullback commutes with $d$ and with the bracket, reducing the curvature of the product connection to the Maurer–Cartan combination $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$ on $SU(2)$. Then, in the quaternion model $\theta = \bar q\,dq$, we differentiate the sphere constraint $\bar q q = 1$ to obtain $d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta$ globally. Next we identify the Lie-bracket term $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$ with the associative wedge $\theta\wedge\theta$, so that the two summands cancel and $\Omega = 0$. Finally, a vanishing curvature forces every Chern–Weil form of positive degree to vanish, which is the assertion of the exercise.

Throughout, $\mathbb{H} = \{a + bi + cj + dk : a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{R}\}$ is the algebra of quaternions with $i^{2} = j^{2} = k^{2} = -1$, $ij = k$, $jk = i$, $ki = j$ (and the reversed products carry the opposite sign); the conjugate of $q = a + bi + cj + dk$ is $\bar q = a - bi - cj - dk$, the norm is $|q|^{2} = \bar q q = a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2} + d^{2}$, and $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H} = \operatorname{span}\{i,j,k\}$ is exactly the set of quaternions with $\bar q = -q$. We identify $SU(2)$ with the unit quaternions $S^{3} = \{q : |q| = 1\}$ and $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$, the tangent space $T_1 S^{3}$ at the identity $1 \in \mathbb{H}$; the Lie bracket on $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$ is the quaternion commutator $[\xi,\eta] = \xi\eta - \eta\xi$.

**Step 1: Reduce the curvature of the product connection to a computation on $SU(2)$.**

Because $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$ and pullback commutes with $d$ and with the bracket, $\Omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta])$; it therefore suffices to prove $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = 0$ on $SU(2)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> We are given the product connection $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$ on $P = B \times SU(2)$, with $\operatorname{pr}_2 : B \times SU(2) \to SU(2)$ the projection. Its curvature is, by [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection|the definition of principal curvature]],
> $$\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega \wedge \omega] \qquad \text{(definition of the curvature of a principal connection).}$$
> **Push $d$ through the pullback.** The exterior derivative is natural: $d(\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta) = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(d\theta)$ for any form $\theta$ (pullback commutes with $d$). Hence
> $$d\omega = d(\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta) = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(d\theta) \qquad \text{(naturality of the exterior derivative under pullback).}$$
> **Push the bracket through the pullback.** For $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms the bracket is defined componentwise from the wedge and the Lie bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$, and a smooth map pulls back a wedge of scalar forms to the wedge of the pullbacks while leaving the fixed bilinear map $[\cdot,\cdot]$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ untouched; therefore
> $$[\omega \wedge \omega] = [\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta \wedge \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta] = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}[\theta \wedge \theta] \qquad \text{(naturality of the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms under pullback).}$$
> **Combine.** Since $\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}$ is linear,
> $$\Omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(d\theta) + \tfrac12\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}[\theta \wedge \theta] = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\!\Big(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta]\Big) \qquad \text{(linearity of pullback; combining the two previous displays).}$$
> A pullback of the zero form is the zero form, so it now suffices to prove
> $$d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0 \quad \text{on } SU(2).$$
> This is the Maurer–Cartan equation; we prove it directly in quaternion coordinates in Steps 2 and 3, rather than citing it, so that the exercise is self-contained.

**Step 2: In quaternion coordinates $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ is imaginary, and $d\theta = -\,\theta \wedge \theta$.**

Writing $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ and differentiating the constraint $\bar q q = 1$ gives both that $\theta$ is $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$-valued and that $d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta$, where the wedge uses quaternion multiplication.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **The Maurer–Cartan form in the quaternion model.** On a matrix group the left Maurer–Cartan form is $\theta = g^{-1}\,dg$ (this is the content of [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|its definition]] read in ambient matrix coordinates). Under the identification $SU(2) \cong S^{3} \subset \mathbb{H}$ the group operation is quaternion multiplication and the inverse of a unit quaternion is its conjugate, $q^{-1} = \bar q$ (because $\bar q q = |q|^{2} = 1$). Hence
> $$\theta = q^{-1}\,dq = \bar q\,dq \in \Omega^{1}(S^{3}; \mathbb{H}),$$
> where $dq = da + i\,db + j\,dc + k\,dd$ is the $\mathbb{H}$-valued tautological $1$-form on $\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^{4}$ restricted to $S^{3}$.
>
> **$\theta$ takes values in $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H} = \mathfrak{su}(2)$.** Differentiate the defining identity $\bar q q = 1$ of $S^{3}$. Since $d$ obeys the Leibniz rule for the (associative) quaternion product,
> $$0 = d(\bar q q) = (d\bar q)\,q + \bar q\,(dq) \qquad \text{(Leibniz rule for the quaternion product, } d(1) = 0\text{).} \tag{2.1}$$
> Conjugation is an anti-automorphism, $\overline{q_1 q_2} = \bar q_2\,\bar q_1$, and commutes with $d$; conjugating $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ gives $\overline{\theta} = \overline{\bar q\,dq} = \overline{dq}\,q = (d\bar q)\,q$. By $(2.1)$, $(d\bar q)\,q = -\bar q\,dq = -\theta$, so
> $$\overline{\theta} = -\theta \qquad \text{(conjugating } \theta \text{ and applying } (2.1)\text{),}$$
> which is exactly the condition $\theta \in \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$. Thus $\theta$ is a genuine $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued $1$-form, as a connection form must be.
>
> **Solve $(2.1)$ for $d\bar q$.** Right-multiplying $(2.1)$ by $q^{-1} = \bar q$,
> $$d\bar q = -\bar q\,(dq)\,\bar q = -\,\theta\,\bar q \qquad \text{(right-multiply } (2.1)\text{ by } q^{-1} = \bar q\text{; } \theta = \bar q\,dq\text{).} \tag{2.2}$$
>
> **Differentiate $\theta$.** Because $d(dq) = 0$ and $d$ satisfies the graded Leibniz rule for $\mathbb{H}$-valued forms (the quaternion product is bilinear, so it behaves exactly like a matrix product under $d$),
> $$d\theta = d(\bar q\,dq) = (d\bar q) \wedge dq + \bar q\, d(dq) = (d\bar q) \wedge dq \qquad \text{(graded Leibniz rule, } \bar q \text{ a } 0\text{-form; } d(dq) = 0\text{).}$$
> Substitute $(2.2)$:
> $$d\theta = (-\,\theta\,\bar q) \wedge dq = -\,\theta \wedge (\bar q\,dq) = -\,\theta \wedge \theta \qquad \text{(by } (2.2)\text{; } \bar q \text{ is a } 0\text{-form, so associativity of } \mathbb{H} \text{ regroups it with no sign).} \tag{2.3}$$
> The regrouping $\theta\,\bar q \wedge dq = \theta \wedge \bar q\,dq$ is legitimate because $\bar q$ is a quaternion-valued function (form-degree $0$): moving it across the wedge produces the Koszul sign $(-1)^{1 \cdot 0} = +1$, and associativity of quaternion multiplication lets the coefficients be regrouped. Here and below $\theta \wedge \theta$ denotes the wedge product of $\mathbb{H}$-valued forms taken with the *quaternion product* of coefficients.

**Step 3: The bracket term equals the associative wedge, $\tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = \theta \wedge \theta$; hence the structure equation gives $\Omega = 0$.**

The Lie-bracket form $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$ coincides with the associative-product form $\theta\wedge\theta$ of Step 2, so $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = -\theta\wedge\theta + \theta\wedge\theta = 0$, and therefore $\Omega = 0$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Bracket of a form with itself.** By [[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket|the definition of the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued $1$-forms]], for vector fields $X, Y$,
> $$[\theta \wedge \theta](X, Y) = [\theta(X), \theta(Y)] - [\theta(Y), \theta(X)] = 2[\theta(X), \theta(Y)] \qquad \text{(definition of } [\,\cdot \wedge \cdot\,]\text{; antisymmetry of the Lie bracket).}$$
> **The bracket is the commutator of the quaternion product.** On $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H} = \mathfrak{su}(2)$ the Lie bracket is the quaternion commutator, $[\xi, \eta] = \xi\eta - \eta\xi$. Meanwhile the associative-product wedge acts on $1$-forms by
> $$(\theta \wedge \theta)(X, Y) = \theta(X)\,\theta(Y) - \theta(Y)\,\theta(X) \qquad \text{(definition of the wedge of algebra-valued } 1\text{-forms, quaternion product of coefficients)}$$
> $$= [\theta(X), \theta(Y)] \qquad \text{(quaternion commutator is the Lie bracket on } \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}\text{).}$$
> Comparing the two displays, $[\theta \wedge \theta](X,Y) = 2\,(\theta\wedge\theta)(X,Y)$ for all $X, Y$, hence
> $$\tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = \theta \wedge \theta \qquad \text{(the two previous displays hold for every pair } X, Y\text{).} \tag{3.1}$$
> **Assemble the structure equation.** Adding $(2.3)$ and $(3.1)$,
> $$d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta] = -\,\theta \wedge \theta + \theta \wedge \theta = 0 \qquad \text{(by } (2.3) \text{ and } (3.1)\text{).}$$
> This is the Maurer–Cartan equation on $SU(2)$, established directly in quaternion coordinates. By Step 1,
> $$\Omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\!\Big(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta]\Big) = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(0) = 0 \qquad \text{(by Step 1; the pullback of the zero form is zero).}$$
> Thus the product connection $\omega$ is flat.

**Step 4: A flat connection has vanishing Chern–Weil forms in positive degree.**

Since $\Omega = 0$, every Chern–Weil form $p(F_\omega)$ with $\deg p = d \ge 1$ vanishes, so all characteristic classes $c_p(P)$ of the trivial bundle $P = B \times SU(2)$ are zero.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $p$ be an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial of degree $d \ge 1$. By [[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial|the construction of the Chern–Weil form]], $p(\Omega)$ is obtained by evaluating the polarised, symmetric, multilinear form associated with $p$ on $d$ copies of the curvature: in a basis $Y_1, \dots, Y_r$ of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, writing $\Omega = \sum_{m} \Omega^{m} Y_m$ with $\Omega^{m}$ ordinary $2$-forms,
> $$p(\Omega) = \sum_{m_1, \dots, m_d} \Omega^{m_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge \Omega^{m_d}\; \lambda(Y_{m_1}, \dots, Y_{m_d}) \qquad \text{(basis form of the Chern–Weil expression),}$$
> where $\lambda$ is the symmetric multilinear map polarising $p$. Every summand contains at least one factor $\Omega^{m}$ because $d \ge 1$. By Step 3, $\Omega = 0$, so every component $2$-form $\Omega^{m} = 0$, hence every summand is $0$ and
> $$p(\Omega) = 0 \qquad \text{(each monomial contains a factor } \Omega^{m} = 0 \text{, using } d \ge 1\text{).}$$
> The descended form on the base satisfies $p(F_\omega) = 0$ as well, since it is the unique form on $B$ pulling back to the basic form $p(\Omega)$, and the zero form pulls back to zero. Consequently the de Rham class
> $$c_p(P) = [\,p(F_\omega)\,] = [\,0\,] = 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B) \qquad \text{(the class of the zero form is zero).}$$
> This recovers, for $G = SU(2)$ by an explicit quaternionic computation, the general statement of [[Thm - Trivial Bundles Have Vanishing Characteristic Classes|the vanishing theorem]].

> [!note]- Concrete check in a left-invariant coframe
> It is worth seeing the cancellation of Step 3 happen in ordinary $2$-forms, to confirm the algebra. Write $q = a + bi + cj + dk$ on $S^{3}$ and expand $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ by multiplying out the quaternions. Collecting the coefficients of $i, j, k$ gives the left-invariant coframe
> $$\theta^{i} = a\,db - b\,da + d\,dc - c\,dd, \quad \theta^{j} = a\,dc - c\,da + b\,dd - d\,db, \quad \theta^{k} = a\,dd - d\,da + c\,db - b\,dc,$$
> while the scalar part $a\,da + b\,db + c\,dc + d\,dd = \tfrac12\,d(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}) = \tfrac12\,d(1) = 0$ vanishes, confirming $\theta \in \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$ once more. The commutators $[i,j] = ij - ji = 2k$, $[j,k] = 2i$, $[k,i] = 2j$ give, for the wedge with quaternion product, the $i$-component
> $$(\theta \wedge \theta)^{i} = 2\,\theta^{j} \wedge \theta^{k}$$
> (the diagonal terms $\theta^{m}\wedge\theta^{m}$ vanish because $\theta^{m}$ is a $1$-form, and the off-diagonal terms combine through $jk = i = -kj$). The structure equation's $i$-component is therefore the claim $d\theta^{i} = -2\,\theta^{j}\wedge\theta^{k}$. All three forms $\theta^{i}, \theta^{j}, \theta^{k}$ are left-invariant, and both $d$ and the wedge send left-invariant forms to left-invariant forms; a left-invariant form is determined by its value at the identity $1 \in S^{3}$, so it suffices to check the identity there. At $1 = (a,b,c,d) = (1,0,0,0)$ the constraint $a\,da + \cdots = 0$ reads $da = 0$ on $T_1 S^{3}$, and the coframe restricts to $\theta^{i}|_{1} = db$, $\theta^{j}|_{1} = dc$, $\theta^{k}|_{1} = dd$. Then
> $$d\theta^{i} = 2\,da\wedge db + 2\,dd\wedge dc \;\xrightarrow{\;da = 0\;}\; 2\,dd\wedge dc = -2\,dc\wedge dd, \qquad -2\,\theta^{j}\wedge\theta^{k}\big|_{1} = -2\,dc\wedge dd,$$
> and the two agree. By left-invariance $d\theta^{i} = -2\,\theta^{j}\wedge\theta^{k}$ holds everywhere on $SU(2)$, and the same argument with $(i,j,k)$ cycled handles the $j$- and $k$-components. This is the coordinate face of $d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** The curvature of the product connection $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$ on the trivial bundle $P = B \times SU(2)$ vanishes, and consequently every Chern–Weil form $p(F_\omega)$ of positive degree, and every characteristic class $c_p(P)$, is zero.
>
> Identify $SU(2)$ with the unit quaternions $S^{3} = \{q \in \mathbb{H} : |q| = 1\}$, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$, and the left Maurer–Cartan form with $\theta = \bar q\,dq$ (using $q^{-1} = \bar q$ for $|q| = 1$).
>
> By naturality of the exterior derivative and of the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms under pullback,
> $$\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega] = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\!\big(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]\big),$$
> so it suffices to show $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = 0$ on $SU(2)$.
>
> Differentiating $\bar q q = 1$ gives $(d\bar q)q + \bar q\,dq = 0$; conjugating $\theta$ yields $\overline\theta = (d\bar q)q = -\bar q\,dq = -\theta$, so $\theta \in \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$, and right-multiplying by $\bar q$ gives $d\bar q = -\theta\bar q$. Since $d(dq) = 0$,
> $$d\theta = (d\bar q)\wedge dq = -\theta\bar q \wedge dq = -\theta \wedge (\bar q\,dq) = -\theta\wedge\theta,$$
> the middle regrouping being valid because $\bar q$ has form-degree $0$ and $\mathbb{H}$ is associative; the wedge uses the quaternion product.
>
> On $\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$ the Lie bracket is the quaternion commutator, so $[\theta\wedge\theta](X,Y) = 2[\theta(X),\theta(Y)] = 2\big(\theta(X)\theta(Y) - \theta(Y)\theta(X)\big) = 2(\theta\wedge\theta)(X,Y)$; hence $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = \theta\wedge\theta$. Therefore
> $$d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = -\theta\wedge\theta + \theta\wedge\theta = 0,$$
> and $\Omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}(0) = 0$: the product connection is flat.
>
> Finally, for an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d \ge 1$, the Chern–Weil form $p(\Omega)$ is a sum of monomials each containing at least one factor of a component of $\Omega$; since $\Omega = 0$, $p(\Omega) = 0$, the descended form $p(F_\omega) = 0$, and $c_p(P) = [0] = 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B)$. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting shortcut: "$\theta$ is closed, so $d\theta = 0$"
> One might be tempted to argue that $\theta$ is left-invariant and therefore closed, giving $d\theta = 0$ and $\Omega = \tfrac12\operatorname{pr}_2^{*}[\theta\wedge\theta]$, which one would then have to show vanishes separately. This is wrong: a left-invariant form is *not* closed in general. Precisely, $d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta \ne 0$ on any non-abelian group — for $SU(2)$ the coframe computation above gives $d\theta^{i}|_{1} = -2\,dc\wedge dd \ne 0$. What vanishes is the *sum* $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$, not either summand alone. The flatness of the product connection rests on the cancellation between the exterior derivative of $\theta$ and its self-bracket, and it is exactly this cancellation — the Maurer–Cartan equation — that the exercise is asking you to witness. The shortcut would only be correct if $SU(2)$ were abelian, in which case $[\theta\wedge\theta] = 0$ and indeed $d\theta = 0$; for the abelian group $U(1)$ the whole computation degenerates to $d(g^{-1}dg) = 0$ with no bracket term.

# Key Takeaways

**The flatness of the product connection is the Maurer–Cartan equation in disguise, and the disguise is a single pullback.** The reusable principle is that whenever a connection is built by pulling a canonical group form back along a projection — $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^{*}\theta$ on $B \times G$ — its curvature is the pullback of the group's own curvature combination $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$, which is zero by the [[Thm - The Maurer-Cartan Equation|Maurer–Cartan equation]]. The trigger to recognise this pattern is the appearance of a *trivial* bundle together with the *canonical flat* connection: the base contributes nothing, all the geometry is on the fibre group, and the fibre group's Maurer–Cartan form is always flat. The transferable diagnostic is that any time a computation of curvature or of a characteristic form reduces, after removing pullbacks, to $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]$, the answer is zero without further work; conversely, a non-zero curvature signals that the connection is *not* a pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form, i.e. that the bundle or the connection is genuinely twisted.

**Quaternion coordinates turn Lie-theoretic identities on $SU(2)$ into one-line algebra, because $q^{-1} = \bar q$ and $\mathbb{H}$ is associative.** The core move — differentiate the defining identity $\bar q q = 1$, solve for $d\bar q = -\theta\bar q$, and substitute into $d\theta = (d\bar q)\wedge dq$ — is the exact quaternionic analogue of the matrix computation $d(g^{-1}) = -g^{-1}(dg)g^{-1}$ that yields $d(g^{-1}dg) = -(g^{-1}dg)\wedge(g^{-1}dg)$ for any matrix group. The trigger condition is a structure group with a faithful associative-algebra model: $U(1) \subset \mathbb{C}$, $SU(2) \subset \mathbb{H}$, $U(n)$ and $SU(n) \subset \operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb{C})$. In each case the Maurer–Cartan form is $g^{-1}dg$, its exterior derivative is $-\,g^{-1}dg \wedge g^{-1}dg$, and the only remaining bookkeeping is the factor relating the associative wedge to the Lie bracket, namely $\tfrac12[\alpha\wedge\alpha] = \alpha\wedge\alpha$. The diagnostic to carry forward: when the group sits inside an associative algebra, never expand into real coordinates first — differentiate the algebraic constraint and let associativity collapse the expression, keeping the coordinate expansion only as a check.

**A vanishing curvature kills every characteristic form of positive degree, because those forms are built polynomially from the curvature.** The Chern–Weil form $p(F)$ is not an independent invariant one must analyse case by case; it is a fixed multilinear wedge-product expression of degree $d = \deg p$ in the entries of the curvature, so it inherits the property "vanishes when $F = 0$" for every $p$ with $d \ge 1$ at once. The reusable principle is that flatness is a *uniform* obstruction-killer: proving a single connection flat annihilates the entire Chern–Weil homomorphism above degree zero, which is why "admits a flat connection" is a strong hypothesis and why the presence of a flat connection is the cleanest route to showing a bundle is characteristic-class-trivial. The transferable diagnostic, in the contrapositive form used constantly in the four-manifold theory later in the series, is [[Thm - Trivial Bundles Have Vanishing Characteristic Classes|that a single non-zero characteristic class obstructs both triviality and the existence of any flat connection]]: if some $c_p(P) \ne 0$, then $P$ carries no flat connection and in particular is not the product bundle. This exercise is the positive half of that dichotomy, made fully explicit on $SU(2) \cong S^{3}$; it pairs naturally with the companion computation [[Ex - The Chern-Weil Form of the Trace on a U(1)-Bundle is the Curvature|of the trace form on a $U(1)$-bundle]], where the abelian group makes the bracket term drop out entirely.
