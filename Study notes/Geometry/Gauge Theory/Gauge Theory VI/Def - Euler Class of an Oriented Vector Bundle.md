# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is a smooth manifold (Hausdorff, second countable, $C^\infty$) and $E \to M$ is a smooth **oriented real vector bundle** of even rank $2m$; "oriented" means a continuous choice of orientation of each fibre, equivalently a reduction of the structure group of $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ from $GL_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$ to $GL_{2m}^+(\mathbb{R})$ (the matrices of positive determinant). We write $\operatorname{rk} E = 2m$.

A **Euclidean structure** on $E$ is a smooth fibre metric $g$, that is, a smoothly varying inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_x$ on each fibre $E_x$; a connection $\nabla$ on $E$ is a **metric connection** (compatible with $g$) if $d\langle s_1, s_2\rangle = \langle \nabla s_1, s_2\rangle + \langle s_1, \nabla s_2\rangle$ for all sections $s_1, s_2 \in \Gamma(E)$. Both notions, and the facts recalled below, are on [[Def - Euclidean Vector Bundle and Metric Connection]].

Given a Euclidean structure and the orientation, an **oriented orthonormal frame** over an open set $U \subseteq M$ is a tuple $e = (e_1, \dots, e_{2m})$ of sections of $E|_U$ that is orthonormal ($\langle e_i, e_j\rangle = \delta_{ij}$) and positively oriented at every point. The collection of all oriented orthonormal frames is the **oriented orthonormal frame bundle** $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$, a principal $SO(2m)$-bundle over $M$; this identification is [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reduction theorem]].

In a local frame $e$ the connection matrix $A \in \Omega^1(U; \mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R}))$ is defined by $\nabla e = e \cdot A$ (row-of-sections convention), and the **curvature matrix** is $F = dA + A \wedge A \in \Omega^2(U; \mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R}))$; under a frame change $e' = e\,\gamma$ with $\gamma$ valued in the structure group, $A' = \gamma^{-1} A \gamma + \gamma^{-1} d\gamma$ and $F' = \gamma^{-1} F \gamma$. For a metric connection expressed in an orthonormal frame the connection and curvature matrices are skew-symmetric, $A, F$ valued in $\mathfrak{o}(2m) = \{X : X^t = -X\}$; in an *oriented* orthonormal frame the structure group is $SO(2m)$ and its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(2m) = \mathfrak{o}(2m)$. These conventions are the series' standing conventions for vector-bundle connections.

We write $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ for the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric real $2m \times 2m$ matrices, $O(2m)$ and $SO(2m)$ for the orthogonal and special orthogonal groups, and $H^k_{dR}(M)$ for the [[Def - de Rham Cohomology|de Rham cohomology]] of $M$ with real coefficients. The symbol $\smile$ denotes the cup product on $H^\bullet_{dR}(M)$, realised by the wedge product of closed forms.

The **Pfaffian** $\operatorname{Pf} : \mathfrak{so}(2m) \to \mathbb{R}$ is the polynomial of degree $m$ recalled in the next section; the full account, with all the identities used here, is [[Def - Pfaffian]] and [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian]].

> [!warning] Convention: the Pfaffian normalisation
> Bär (source B) writes, for an $SO(2m)$-bundle, $\lambda(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_m) := \sigma_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \sigma_m$ under the identification $\mathfrak{so}(2m) \cong \Lambda^2 \mathbb{R}^{2m}$, defines his Pfaffian as $\operatorname{Pf}_B(\sigma) := \lambda(\sigma, \dots, \sigma)$, and sets the Euler class to be $e(P) = \big[\operatorname{Pf}_B(\bar\Omega)\,/\,((2\pi)^m\, m!)\big]$. The series' Pfaffian is $\operatorname{Pf}(A) = \frac{1}{2^m m!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_{2m}} \operatorname{sign}(\sigma) \prod_{i=1}^m A_{\sigma(2i-1)\sigma(2i)}$, equivalently determined by $\frac{1}{m!}\,\sigma_A^{\wedge m} = \operatorname{Pf}(A)\, e_1 \wedge \dots \wedge e_{2m}$ for $\sigma_A = \sum_{i<j} A_{ij}\, e_i \wedge e_j$. Hence $\operatorname{Pf}_B(A) = \sigma_A^{\wedge m} = m!\,\operatorname{Pf}(A)$, and because $\operatorname{Pf}$ is homogeneous of degree $m$,
> $$\frac{\operatorname{Pf}_B(F)}{(2\pi)^m\, m!} = \frac{m!\,\operatorname{Pf}(F)}{(2\pi)^m\, m!} = \frac{\operatorname{Pf}(F)}{(2\pi)^m} = \operatorname{Pf}\!\Big(\frac{F}{2\pi}\Big).$$
> The two normalisations agree exactly, and the series writes the Euler class as $e(E) = \big[\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)\big]$.

# Axiom Motivation

The question this definition answers is: **for an oriented real vector bundle, what is the de Rham class that measures the failure of the bundle to admit a nowhere-vanishing section, built out of curvature the way the Chern classes are?** A complex vector bundle carries the [[Def - Chern Classes|Chern classes]] $c_j(E) = \big[c_j\big(\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F\big)\big]$, produced by feeding the curvature into the $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomials $\det(1 + \tfrac{i}{2\pi}\,\cdot\,) = 1 + c_1 + c_2 + \cdots$ and invoking the [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|Chern–Weil theorem]] to see that the resulting classes do not depend on the connection. A real bundle has no complex structure and no canonical way to make its curvature into an anti-Hermitian matrix, so the Chern machinery does not apply to it directly. What a real bundle *can* carry, once we choose a Euclidean structure, is a reduction of the structure group to $SO(2m)$, and the top-degree characteristic class of an $SO(2m)$-bundle is exactly the object we are after.

The Chern–Weil recipe turns any $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant polynomial on $\mathfrak{g}$ into a characteristic class of a $G$-bundle. So the desideratum reduces to a purely algebraic one: **find the invariant polynomials on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$.** The ring of $\operatorname{Ad}(SO(2m))$-invariant polynomials is generated by the Pontryagin polynomials — the even coefficients of $\det(\lambda\, 1 - A)$, which give the [[Def - Pontryagin Classes|Pontryagin classes]] — together with one further generator that the Pontryagin polynomials do *not* produce: the **Pfaffian**. The Pfaffian is a square root of the determinant, $\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = \det A$ for $A \in \mathfrak{so}(2m)$, and it exists precisely because the fibre is even-dimensional and oriented. It is invariant under $\operatorname{Ad}(SO(2m))$ but changes sign under conjugation by an orientation-reversing orthogonal matrix, so it is a genuinely new invariant that lives only on the oriented $SO(2m)$-reduction and is invisible to the full orthogonal or general-linear group. Feeding curvature into it produces a class of top degree $2m$; this is the Euler class.

Three requirements pin the construction down, and each of the three ingredients in the definition is there to meet one of them. We name each and check what breaks if it is dropped.

**Even rank.** The Pfaffian is defined only on even-dimensional skew-symmetric matrices; on $\mathfrak{so}(2k+1)$ every skew matrix is singular ($\det A = \det A^t = \det(-A) = (-1)^{2k+1}\det A = -\det A$, so $\det A = 0$), and there is no polynomial square root of the identically-zero determinant that is not itself zero. If we tried to define an "Euler class" of an odd-rank bundle this way we would get the zero class, carrying no information. This is not a defect of the definition but a theorem of topology mirrored in the algebra: the Euler class of an odd-rank bundle is $2$-torsion and hence vanishes in real cohomology, so there is nothing for a de Rham representative to see. The definition is therefore correctly restricted to $\operatorname{rk} E = 2m$.

**An orientation.** The Pfaffian is $\operatorname{Ad}(SO(2m))$-invariant but not $\operatorname{Ad}(O(2m))$-invariant: under conjugation by $g \in O(2m)$ with $\det g = -1$ it acquires a sign, $\operatorname{Pf}(g^{-1} A g) = \det(g)\operatorname{Pf}(A) = -\operatorname{Pf}(A)$. If the bundle were merely Euclidean without an orientation, its structure group would be $O(2m)$, transition functions could reverse orientation, and $\operatorname{Pf}(F)$ would not patch into a globally defined form — on the overlap where a transition reverses orientation the two local expressions would differ by a sign. Concretely, the Möbius-type real line bundle over $S^1$, and more relevantly the tautological rank-$2$ bundle over $\mathbb{RP}^2$, are non-orientable, and no consistent sign of $\operatorname{Pf}(F)$ exists on them; they have no Euler class in this sense at all. The orientation is exactly the extra datum that upgrades the $O(2m)$-structure to an $SO(2m)$-structure and lets the Pfaffian glue. Reversing the orientation, as we prove below, negates the class — so the Euler class depends on the orientation, not merely on the underlying real bundle.

**A metric (Euclidean structure), used only to get to $SO(2m)$.** The curvature of an arbitrary connection on $E$ is $\mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$-valued, and $\operatorname{Pf}$ is not defined on all of $\mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$, only on skew matrices. Choosing a Euclidean structure and a compatible connection forces the curvature to be $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$-valued in oriented orthonormal frames, which is the domain of $\operatorname{Pf}$. Unlike the situation for Chern classes — where the invariant polynomials $\det(1 + \tfrac{i}{2\pi}\,\cdot\,)$ extend from $\mathfrak{u}(m)$ to all of $\mathfrak{gl}_m(\mathbb{C})$, so one may use *any* connection — the Pfaffian genuinely does not extend to $\mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$, and the reduction to a metric connection is not a convenience but a necessity. What must then be checked, and is checked below, is that the class does not depend on *which* Euclidean structure and *which* metric connection we chose; that independence is what makes $e(E)$ an invariant of the oriented bundle alone.

A reader who accepts that (i) characteristic classes come from invariant polynomials by Chern–Weil, (ii) the new invariant polynomial available on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ and on no larger classical group is the Pfaffian, and (iii) the Pfaffian needs an even rank and an orientation to exist and to glue, could reconstruct the definition below without being handed it. The normalisation by $2\pi$ per pair of dimensions is fixed, as with the Chern classes, so that the class is integral on the cases the series computes — concretely so that $e(TS^2)$ integrates to $2 = \chi(S^2)$ rather than to $2/(2\pi)^{\text{something}}$.

# The Definition

Let $E \to M$ be a smooth oriented real vector bundle of rank $2m$. Choose a Euclidean structure $g$ on $E$ and a metric connection $\nabla$; let $F$ denote the curvature, an $\operatorname{End}(E)$-valued $2$-form that in every oriented orthonormal frame is represented by an $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$-valued $2$-form. The **Euler class** of the oriented bundle $E$ is the de Rham cohomology class

$$e(E) := \Big[\operatorname{Pf}\!\Big(\frac{F}{2\pi}\Big)\Big] \in H^{2m}_{dR}(M).$$

Here $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ is the real $2m$-form obtained by substituting the entries of the matrix $F/2\pi$ (each an ordinary $2$-form) into the Pfaffian polynomial and multiplying with the wedge product; because $\operatorname{Pf}$ is homogeneous of degree $m$ and each entry has even degree $2$, this is a well-defined $2m$-form, and it does not depend on the choice of oriented orthonormal frame because $\operatorname{Pf}$ is $\operatorname{Ad}(SO(2m))$-invariant. The class is independent of the metric connection (by the Chern–Weil theorem) and of the Euclidean structure (by a homotopy argument), and therefore depends only on $E$ together with its orientation. All of these claims are proved in the callout below.

> [!note]- Well-definedness of the Euler class, clause by clause
> We must produce the ingredients, check that $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ is a globally defined closed $2m$-form, and check that its class is independent of every choice. We do this in four steps.
>
> **Step 0 — the ingredients exist and put the curvature in $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$.** A Euclidean structure $g$ on $E$ exists: cover $M$ by trivialising charts, put the standard inner product in each chart, and glue with a partition of unity — a convex combination of inner products is an inner product, so positive-definiteness survives (this is the existence corollary on [[Def - Euclidean Vector Bundle and Metric Connection]]). A metric connection exists: project any connection onto the metric ones, $\nabla^{\mathrm{met}} := \nabla - \tfrac12 (\nabla g)^\sharp$, again on that page. By [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reduction theorem]] — which states that Euclidean structures on a rank-$k$ real bundle correspond to $O(k)$-reductions $\operatorname{Fr}_O(E) \subseteq \operatorname{Fr}(E)$, and that on an oriented bundle these refine to $SO(k)$-reductions — the oriented orthonormal frames form a principal $SO(2m)$-bundle $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$. In an orthonormal frame the compatibility $d\langle e_i, e_j\rangle = \langle \nabla e_i, e_j\rangle + \langle e_i, \nabla e_j\rangle$ reads $0 = A_{ji} + A_{ij}$, so the connection matrix $A$ and hence the curvature matrix $F = dA + A \wedge A$ are skew-symmetric, that is $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$-valued (the skew-symmetry corollary on [[Def - Euclidean Vector Bundle and Metric Connection]]).
>
> **Step 1 — $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ is a globally defined closed $2m$-form.** Under a change of oriented orthonormal frame $e' = e\,\gamma$ with $\gamma$ valued in $SO(2m)$, the curvature matrix transforms by conjugation, $F' = \gamma^{-1} F \gamma$. By part (c) of [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian|the Pfaffian properties]] — the Pfaffian is $\operatorname{Ad}(SO(2m))$-invariant, $\operatorname{Pf}(\gamma^{-1} A \gamma) = \operatorname{Pf}(A)$ for $\gamma \in SO(2m)$ and $A \in \mathfrak{so}(2m)$ — we have $\operatorname{Pf}(F'/2\pi) = \operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ on the overlap, so the locally defined $2m$-forms $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ patch into a single global form on $M$. This form is the Chern–Weil form of the invariant polynomial $\operatorname{Pf} \in I(SO(2m))$ evaluated on the principal $SO(2m)$-bundle $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$ with the induced principal connection. By part (i) of the [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|Chern–Weil theorem]] — *for a principal $G$-bundle with connection and an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d$, the form $p(F)$ is closed* — the form $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ is closed, and therefore defines a class in $H^{2m}_{dR}(M)$.
>
> **Step 2 — independence of the metric connection (fixed Euclidean structure).** Fix $g$ and let $\nabla_0, \nabla_1$ be two $g$-metric connections, with curvatures $F_0, F_1$, both $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$-valued in oriented orthonormal frames. By part (ii) of the [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|Chern–Weil theorem]] — *for two connections on the same principal bundle, the forms $p(F_1) - p(F_0)$ are exact* — the difference $\operatorname{Pf}(F_1/2\pi) - \operatorname{Pf}(F_0/2\pi)$ is exact, so the two connections give the same class. (Concretely, both are connections on the fixed $SO(2m)$-bundle $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$; the affine segment $\nabla_t = (1-t)\nabla_0 + t\nabla_1$ is again a metric connection because the space of metric connections is affine, and the theorem's transgression argument applies.)
>
> **Step 3 — independence of the Euclidean structure.** Let $g_0, g_1$ be two Euclidean structures. The straight-line family $g_t := (1-t) g_0 + t g_1$ is, for each $t \in [0,1]$, again a Euclidean structure, because a convex combination of inner products is an inner product. Let $\operatorname{pr} : M \times [0,1] \to M$ be the projection and consider the pullback bundle $\operatorname{pr}^* E \to M \times [0,1]$, which is oriented of rank $2m$; the family $\{g_t\}$ assembles into a single Euclidean structure $\hat g$ on $\operatorname{pr}^* E$ (its value on $M \times \{t\}$ is $g_t$). Choose a metric connection $\hat\nabla$ on $(\operatorname{pr}^* E, \hat g)$ — one exists by Step 0 — with curvature $\hat F$, and form the class $\hat e := \big[\operatorname{Pf}(\hat F/2\pi)\big] \in H^{2m}_{dR}(M \times [0,1])$. Let $\iota_j : M \to M \times [0,1]$, $\iota_j(x) = (x, j)$, be the two end inclusions. Then $\iota_j^* \operatorname{pr}^* E = E$ with Euclidean structure $g_j$, and $\iota_j^* \hat\nabla$ is a $g_j$-metric connection; pulling the form back commutes with substituting into $\operatorname{Pf}$, so $\iota_j^* \operatorname{Pf}(\hat F/2\pi) = \operatorname{Pf}(F^{(j)}/2\pi)$ where $F^{(j)}$ is the curvature of $\iota_j^* \hat\nabla$. The maps $\iota_0$ and $\iota_1$ are homotopic (the homotopy is $(x,s,t) \mapsto (x, (1-s)\cdot 0 + s\cdot t)$, or simply the identity family), so by the [[Thm - Homotopy Invariance of de Rham Cohomology|homotopy invariance of de Rham cohomology]] — *homotopic smooth maps induce equal maps on $H^\bullet_{dR}$* — we have $\iota_0^* \hat e = \iota_1^* \hat e$ in $H^{2m}_{dR}(M)$, that is $\big[\operatorname{Pf}(F^{(0)}/2\pi)\big] = \big[\operatorname{Pf}(F^{(1)}/2\pi)\big]$. Combining this with Step 2 (which lets us pass from $\iota_j^* \hat\nabla$ to any other $g_j$-metric connection), the class $\big[\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)\big]$ is the same whether computed from $(g_0, \nabla_0)$ or from $(g_1, \nabla_1)$.
>
> **Conclusion.** The class $e(E) = \big[\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)\big] \in H^{2m}_{dR}(M)$ is independent of the Euclidean structure and of the metric connection, so it is an invariant of the oriented bundle $E$ alone. $\blacksquare$

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The Euler class is exactly the value of the Chern–Weil homomorphism on the Pfaffian, and stating it this way makes both its naturality and its place among the characteristic classes transparent. Recall that the [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|Chern–Weil theorem]] produces, for each principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$, a ring homomorphism

$$c_{(\cdot)}(P) : I(G) \longrightarrow H^{\mathrm{even}}_{dR}(M), \qquad p \longmapsto c_p(P) := [p(F_\omega)],$$

from the ring $I(G)$ of $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant real polynomials on $\mathfrak{g}$ (see [[Def - Ad-Invariant Polynomial]]) to even de Rham cohomology, independent of the connection $\omega$; the form $p(F_\omega)$ is the [[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial|Chern–Weil form]]. For $G = SO(2m)$ the Pfaffian is an element $\operatorname{Pf} \in I(SO(2m))$ of degree $m$, by part (c) of [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian|the Pfaffian properties]]. The Euler class is its image:

$$e(E) = c_{\operatorname{Pf}}\big(\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)\big),$$

where $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$ is the oriented orthonormal frame bundle of $E$ (an $SO(2m)$-bundle by [[Thm - Euclidean and Hermitian Structures are Reductions of the Structure Group|the reduction theorem]]), computed with the connection induced by any metric connection on $E$; the $1/(2\pi)^m$ is folded into the normalisation of $\operatorname{Pf}$ via its homogeneity. Reading it this way, the invariance under change of connection is immediate from the theorem, and the fact that the Euler class is *not* a polynomial in the Pontryagin classes is the algebraic statement that $\operatorname{Pf}$ is a genuinely new generator of $I(SO(2m))$, not lying in the subring generated by the characteristic-polynomial coefficients (part (e) of the Pfaffian properties). The dependence on the reduction $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$ rather than on the full frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ is the structural shadow of the fact that $\operatorname{Pf}$ does not extend to an invariant polynomial on $\mathfrak{gl}_{2m}(\mathbb{R})$ or even on $\mathfrak{o}(2m)$ under $O(2m)$: the Euler class lives on the oriented category and there only.

Naturality follows from the same reading: for a smooth map $f : N \to M$ the pullback $f^* E$ inherits an orientation and a Euclidean structure, $\operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(f^* E) = f^* \operatorname{Fr}_{SO}(E)$, and the naturality of the Chern–Weil homomorphism gives $e(f^* E) = f^* e(E)$ in $H^{2m}_{dR}(N)$.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Officially the Euler class is a curvature integral; operationally it is *the obstruction, measured in top-degree cohomology, to trivialising an oriented even-rank bundle enough to admit a nowhere-vanishing section*. The corollary $e(E) = 0$ whenever $E$ has a nowhere-vanishing section (proved below) is the shadow of the sharper topological statement — outside this series — that for $\operatorname{rk} E = \dim M = 2m$ the pairing $\langle e(E), [M]\rangle$ counts, with signs, the zeros of a generic section, so that $e(E)$ is the primary obstruction to finding a section without zeros. The name "Euler" is not decorative: for $E = TM$ the tangent bundle of a closed oriented even-dimensional manifold, that signed zero count is the Euler characteristic $\chi(M)$, by the Poincaré–Hopf theorem. The version of that statement the series actually proves is the special case $\int_{S^2} e(TS^2) = 2 = \chi(S^2)$, computed below; the general identity $\int_\Sigma e(T\Sigma) = \chi(\Sigma)$ is discussed but not proved here (see the Bridges scope remark).

The Euler class compresses several apparently different constructions into one. In the theory of complex line bundles it *is* the first Chern class: an oriented rank-$2$ real bundle is the same thing as a complex line bundle once the orientation and metric single out a complex structure, and then $e(E) = c_1(E)$ (proved below). In Riemannian geometry of surfaces it is the Gauss curvature two-form up to the factor $2\pi$: $e(T\Sigma) = [K\, dA / 2\pi]$, which is why the Euler class and Gauss–Bonnet are two faces of one fact. In the algebra of invariant polynomials it is the square root of the top Pontryagin class, $e(E)^2 = p_m(E)$, because $\operatorname{Pf}^2 = \det$ (proved below). And in obstruction theory it is the top obstruction to a section, as just described. The single mechanism underneath all of these is the Chern–Weil homomorphism applied to the one invariant polynomial — the Pfaffian — that only an oriented even-rank bundle makes available.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the trivial bundle has $e = 0$.** Let $E = M \times \mathbb{R}^{2m}$ with the standard orientation and metric, and take the product (flat) connection $\nabla = d$. Its curvature is $F = 0$, so $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = \operatorname{Pf}(0) = 0$ (the Pfaffian is homogeneous of positive degree $m \ge 1$, hence vanishes at $0$), and therefore $e(E) = [0] = 0$. This is the calibration one wants: a bundle with no twisting has no Euler class. We verify below that any bundle with a nowhere-vanishing section — in particular any trivial bundle, whose constant section $x \mapsto (x, v_0)$ never vanishes — has $e = 0$, giving a second, connection-free proof.

**Corollary — a nowhere-vanishing section forces $e(E) = 0$.** Suppose the oriented rank-$2m$ bundle $E$ admits a section $s \in \Gamma(E)$ with $s(x) \neq 0$ for all $x$. We show $e(E) = 0$.

> [!note]- Proof that $e(E) = 0$ when $E$ has a nowhere-vanishing section
> **Goal.** Produce a Euclidean structure and a metric connection whose curvature is $\mathfrak{so}(2m-1)$-valued in the sense below, and show $\operatorname{Pf}$ vanishes on such curvature.
>
> **Step 1 — split off a trivial line.** Choose any Euclidean structure $g$ on $E$; rescaling, we may assume $\langle s, s\rangle \equiv 1$, so $s$ is a unit section. Let $L := \mathbb{R}\, s \subseteq E$ be the real line subbundle it spans and $E' := L^\perp = \{v \in E : \langle v, s\rangle = 0\}$ its orthogonal complement, a rank-$(2m-1)$ subbundle. Then $E = L \oplus E'$ orthogonally, and $L$ is trivial (trivialised by $s$).
>
> **Step 2 — an adapted metric connection.** Equip $L$ with the flat connection $\nabla^L s = 0$ (metric, since $\langle s, s\rangle = 1$ is constant) and $E'$ with any metric connection $\nabla'$; take $\nabla := \nabla^L \oplus \nabla'$, the direct-sum connection, which is metric for $g = g|_L \oplus g|_{E'}$. In an oriented orthonormal frame $e = (e_1, \dots, e_{2m})$ with $e_1 = s$ and $(e_2, \dots, e_{2m})$ an oriented orthonormal frame of $E'$, the connection matrix is block-diagonal $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & A' \end{pmatrix}$ with $A' \in \Omega^1(\mathfrak{so}(2m-1))$, because $\nabla^L s = 0$ kills the first row and column. Hence the curvature matrix is $F = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & F' \end{pmatrix}$ with $F' \in \Omega^2(\mathfrak{so}(2m-1))$; that is, the first row and first column of $F$ vanish identically.
>
> **Step 3 — the Pfaffian vanishes.** Recall $\operatorname{Pf}(F) = \frac{1}{2^m m!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_{2m}} \operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^m F_{\sigma(2i-1)\,\sigma(2i)}$. Every permutation $\sigma \in S_{2m}$ maps the index $1$ to some position, so in each product $\prod_{i=1}^m F_{\sigma(2i-1)\sigma(2i)}$ the index $1$ appears in exactly one factor $F_{1\,k}$ or $F_{k\,1}$; since the entire first row and first column of $F$ are zero, that factor is $0$, so every term of the sum vanishes. Therefore $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = 0$ as a form, and $e(E) = [0] = 0$.
>
> **Conclusion.** A nowhere-vanishing section reduces the structure group to $SO(2m-1) \hookrightarrow SO(2m)$ (block-diagonal, fixing the $s$-direction), and $\operatorname{Pf}$ restricted to $\mathfrak{so}(2m-1) \subseteq \mathfrak{so}(2m)$ is identically zero, so the Euler class vanishes. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary — reversing the orientation negates the Euler class.** Write $\overline{E}$ for $E$ with the opposite orientation and the same metric. An oriented orthonormal frame for $\overline{E}$ is obtained from one for $E$ by flipping one vector, $\bar e = e \cdot \gamma$ with $\gamma = \operatorname{diag}(1, \dots, 1, -1) \in O(2m)$, $\det \gamma = -1$. The curvature matrix in the new frame is $\bar F = \gamma^{-1} F \gamma = \gamma^t F \gamma$, and by part (a) of [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian|the Pfaffian properties]] — *$\operatorname{Pf}(B^t A B) = \det(B)\,\operatorname{Pf}(A)$ for $A \in \mathfrak{so}(2m)$, $B \in \operatorname{Mat}(2m; \mathbb{R})$* — we get $\operatorname{Pf}(\bar F) = \det(\gamma)\operatorname{Pf}(F) = -\operatorname{Pf}(F)$. Hence $e(\overline{E}) = -e(E)$. This confirms that the Euler class genuinely records the orientation, not just the underlying real bundle.

**Corollary — $e(E) = c_1(E)$ for an oriented rank-$2$ bundle.** An oriented Euclidean rank-$2$ bundle $E$ is canonically a Hermitian complex line bundle: the fibrewise rotation by $+90^\circ$ in the positive sense, $I : E \to E$ with $I e_1 = e_2$ for every oriented orthonormal frame $(e_1, e_2)$, satisfies $I^2 = -\mathrm{id}$ and makes each fibre a one-dimensional complex vector space (see [[Def - Complex Vector Bundle and Hermitian Structure]]). We show its Euler class equals the [[Def - Chern Classes|first Chern class]] of this line bundle.

> [!note]- Proof that $e(E) = c_1(E)$ for an oriented rank-2 bundle
> **Goal.** Compute both classes from one metric connection and show the representative forms coincide.
>
> **Step 0 — the algebra $\mathfrak{so}(2) \cong \mathfrak{u}(1)$.** The isomorphism $SO(2) \cong U(1)$, $\begin{pmatrix} \cos\varphi & -\sin\varphi \\ \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{pmatrix} \mapsto e^{i\varphi}$ (proved on [[Thm - SO(2) is Isomorphic to U(1)|the SO(2)–U(1) page]]), differentiates at $\varphi = 0$ to a Lie algebra isomorphism $\phi : \mathfrak{so}(2) \to \mathfrak{u}(1)$ sending $J := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \mapsto i$, and hence $a J \mapsto a\, i$ for $a \in \mathbb{R}$. Under this identification the complex structure $I$ acts on an oriented orthonormal frame as multiplication by $i$: $e_2 = I e_1 = i\, e_1$.
>
> **Step 1 — the two representative forms.** Take a metric connection $\nabla$ on $E$ (automatically unitary for the induced Hermitian structure, since it preserves $g$ and commutes with $I$ when chosen compatibly). In an oriented orthonormal frame $(e_1, e_2)$ the connection matrix is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & A_{12} \\ -A_{12} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (skew) with $A_{12} \in \Omega^1$, and $A \wedge A = 0$ because its $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$ entries are $\pm\, A_{12} \wedge A_{12} = 0$; hence the curvature matrix is $F = dA = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \Omega_{12} \\ -\Omega_{12} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ with $\Omega_{12} = dA_{12} \in \Omega^2$ a real $2$-form.
>
> **Step 2 — the Euler representative.** The matrix $F = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \Omega_{12} \\ -\Omega_{12} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has the shape $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & a \\ -a & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ with $a = \Omega_{12}$, and for such a matrix $\operatorname{Pf} = a$ (the $m = 1$ value on [[Def - Pfaffian]]). Therefore $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{Pf}(F) = \frac{\Omega_{12}}{2\pi}$, and $e(E) = \big[\Omega_{12}/2\pi\big]$.
>
> **Step 3 — the Chern representative.** As a complex endomorphism-valued form, $F$ acts on $e_1$ by $F e_1 = \sum_i e_i F_{i1} = e_2 F_{21} = -\Omega_{12}\, e_2 = -\Omega_{12}\,(i\, e_1) = (-i\,\Omega_{12})\, e_1$, so the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$-valued curvature is $F^{\mathbb{C}} = -i\,\Omega_{12}$ (a $1\times 1$ skew-Hermitian matrix, that is, imaginary). By the series normalisation of the [[Def - Chern Classes|first Chern class]], $c_1(E) = \big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{tr} F^{\mathbb{C}}\big] = \big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi}(-i\,\Omega_{12})\big] = \big[\Omega_{12}/2\pi\big]$.
>
> **Step 4 — comparison and the role of the orientation.** The two representatives are the identical form $\Omega_{12}/2\pi$, so $e(E) = c_1(E)$ in $H^2_{dR}(M)$. The orientation entered in exactly one place: the choice $e_2 = I e_1 = i\, e_1$ (rather than $e_2 = -I e_1$), which is the requirement that $(e_1, I e_1)$ be positively oriented — the complex orientation. Reversing the orientation of $E$ replaces $I$ by $-I$ (the conjugate line bundle) and, by the previous corollary, negates $e$; correspondingly $c_1$ of the conjugate bundle is $-c_1$, and the two sides flip together, so the identity $e(E) = c_1(E)$ is stable. This matches Bär's Example 2.5.15, which records $e(P) = \big[\operatorname{tr}(\bar\Omega)/(2\pi i)\big] = c_1(P)$ for $G = SO(2) = U(1)$; the sign bookkeeping above is what makes the bare identity precise. $\blacksquare$

**Is an instance — the round two-sphere, $e(TS^2)[S^2] = 2$.** The tangent bundle $TS^2$ of the unit round sphere is oriented of rank $2$; we compute its Euler number directly from the curvature, using only the round curvature $K = 1$ and the area of $S^2$, without invoking Gauss–Bonnet.

> [!note]- Verification that $\int_{S^2} e(TS^2) = 2$
> **Goal.** Compute $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ for the Levi-Civita connection of the round metric and integrate.
>
> **Step 1 — the curvature in an oriented orthonormal frame.** In an oriented orthonormal coframe $(\theta^1, \theta^2)$ for the round metric, the Levi-Civita connection has a single independent connection $1$-form $\omega^1{}_2 = -\omega^2{}_1$, and the second Cartan structural equation gives the curvature $2$-form $\Omega^1{}_2 = d\omega^1{}_2 = K\, \theta^1 \wedge \theta^2 = K\, dA$, where $K$ is the Gauss curvature and $dA = \theta^1 \wedge \theta^2$ is the area form (this is worked on [[Ex - The Levi-Civita Connection of the Round Sphere as a Connection Matrix]]; for the unit sphere $K = 1$). The $\mathfrak{so}(2)$-valued curvature matrix is therefore $F = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \Omega^1{}_2 \\ \Omega^2{}_1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & K\, dA \\ -K\, dA & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
>
> **Step 2 — the Pfaffian.** This matrix has the shape $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & a \\ -a & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ with $a = K\, dA$, so $\operatorname{Pf}(F) = K\, dA$, and $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = \frac{K\, dA}{2\pi}$. Thus the Euler class of $TS^2$ is represented by $\frac{K\, dA}{2\pi} = \frac{dA}{2\pi}$ on the unit sphere ($K = 1$).
>
> **Step 3 — integrate.** Using the area of the unit two-sphere, $\int_{S^2} dA = \operatorname{area}(S^2) = 4\pi$ (see [[Ex - Volume of the n-Sphere via the Volume Form]]),
> $$\int_{S^2} e(TS^2) = \int_{S^2} \frac{dA}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot 4\pi = 2.$$
>
> **Conclusion.** The Euler number of the tangent bundle of the round sphere is $2$, matching the convention $e(TS^2)[S^2] = 2$ and the Euler characteristic $\chi(S^2) = 2$. Because $e(TS^2) = c_1(TS^2)$ by the rank-$2$ corollary, this equals the Chern-number computation on [[Ex - The Tangent Bundle of S^2 is Nontrivial via Chern Classes]], and in particular $TS^2$ is non-trivial (a non-zero Euler class obstructs a nowhere-vanishing vector field — the hairy-ball phenomenon). The dedicated drill is [[Ex - The Euler Class of the Tangent Bundle of the Round Sphere Integrates to Two]]. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary — Whitney product formula for Euler classes.** For oriented Euclidean bundles $E_1, E_2$ of even ranks $2m_1, 2m_2$ with the direct-sum orientation, $e(E_1 \oplus E_2) = e(E_1) \smile e(E_2)$. Choose metric connections $\nabla_1, \nabla_2$ and take the direct-sum connection on $E_1 \oplus E_2$; in oriented orthonormal frames the curvature matrix is block-diagonal, $F = \begin{pmatrix} F_1 & 0 \\ 0 & F_2 \end{pmatrix}$. By part (d) of [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian|the Pfaffian properties]] — *the Pfaffian of a block-diagonal skew matrix is the product of the Pfaffians of the blocks* — we get $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = \operatorname{Pf}(F_1/2\pi)\wedge \operatorname{Pf}(F_2/2\pi)$ as forms, and passing to classes, $e(E_1 \oplus E_2) = e(E_1)\smile e(E_2)$, since the cup product of de Rham classes is the class of the wedge of representatives.

**Corollary — $e(E)^2 = p_m(E)$.** For an oriented rank-$2m$ bundle, the square of the Euler class is the top [[Def - Pontryagin Classes|Pontryagin class]]. By part (b) of [[Thm - Properties of the Pfaffian|the Pfaffian properties]] — *$\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = \det A$ for $A \in \mathfrak{so}(2m)$* — applied entrywise to the curvature form, $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)\wedge \operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi) = \operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)^2 = \det(F/2\pi)$. The top Pontryagin form is the degree-$4m$ part of $\det(1 - F/2\pi)$, namely $\det(-F/2\pi) = (-1)^{2m}\det(F/2\pi) = \det(F/2\pi)$ because $2m$ is even. Hence $e(E)^2 = [\det(F/2\pi)] = p_m(E)$. (The Pontryagin normalisation and the vanishing of the odd terms are established on [[Def - Pontryagin Classes]]; this corollary uses only the top term.)

**Is NOT an instance — a non-orientable bundle.** The tautological rank-$2$ real bundle $E \to \mathbb{RP}^2$ (whose fibre over a line $\ell \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$ is $\ell^\perp$, say) admits no orientation, so its structure group cannot be reduced below $O(2)$; the local expressions $\operatorname{Pf}(F/2\pi)$ pick up a sign under the orientation-reversing transition functions and do not glue into a global form. Consequently $E$ has no Euler class in the sense of this definition. This is not a failure to compute but a genuine restriction: the definition presupposes an orientation, and there is no canonical real-cohomology class playing the Euler role for a non-orientable bundle (its topological Euler class is $2$-torsion and dies in $H^\bullet(\cdot; \mathbb{R})$).

**Calibration check.** First, the Euler class of a rank-$2m$ bundle over a base of dimension $\dim M < 2m$ is zero: $e(E) \in H^{2m}_{dR}(M)$ and any $2m$-form on an $n$-manifold with $2m > n$ vanishes identically, so $H^{2m}_{dR}(M) = 0$ and $e(E) = 0$. This is why the Euler class of a high-rank bundle over a low-dimensional base carries no information, and why $e(TS^2)$ (rank $2$, base dimension $2$) sits exactly at the top. Second, verify the Whitney formula on a trivial summand: if $E = E' \oplus (M \times \mathbb{R}^2)$ then $e(E) = e(E')\smile e(M\times \mathbb{R}^2) = e(E')\smile 0 = 0$, consistent with the nowhere-vanishing-section corollary, since a trivial summand supplies a nowhere-vanishing section of $E$. Third, check the orientation sign against the rank-$2$ identity: reversing the orientation of $TS^2$ turns $I$ into $-I$, the conjugate line bundle, with $c_1 \mapsto -c_1$, so $e(\overline{TS^2})[S^2] = -2$; both the Pfaffian sign rule and the conjugate-bundle Chern computation give the same $-2$.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Pontryagin classes and the signature *(from this chapter)*
> The identity $e(E)^2 = p_m(E)$ ties the Euler class to the [[Def - Pontryagin Classes|Pontryagin classes]], and for $E = TX$ over a closed oriented $4$-manifold the numbers $\langle e(TX), [X]\rangle = \chi(X)$ and $\langle p_1(TX), [X]\rangle = 3\sigma(X)$ (the signature theorem, later in the series) are the two characteristic numbers that drive four-manifold topology.

> [!tip] The Gauss–Bonnet and Euler characteristic story *(from Riemannian Geometry IV and Algebraic Topology)*
> The computation $\int_{S^2} e(TS^2) = 2$ is the tip of the general **Gauss–Bonnet–Chern** theorem $\int_M e(TM) = \chi(M)$ for closed even-dimensional oriented $M$, whose surface case is [[Thm - Gauss-Bonnet Theorem for Surfaces|Gauss–Bonnet]]. The Euler class is the de Rham incarnation of the Euler characteristic.

> [!tip] The Euler class as an obstruction and the Thom class *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> The vanishing $e(E) = 0$ for a bundle with a nowhere-vanishing section is the top of the tower of **primary obstructions to sections**; the Euler class is the pullback of the **Thom class** by the zero section, and $\langle e(E), [M]\rangle$ counts zeros of a generic section with sign (Poincaré–Hopf).
