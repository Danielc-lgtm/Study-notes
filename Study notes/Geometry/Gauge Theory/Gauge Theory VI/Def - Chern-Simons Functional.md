# Notation

Throughout this page $M$ is a **closed** (compact, without boundary) **oriented** smooth three-manifold, and $P\to M$ is a principal $SU(2)$-bundle. By the classification of principal $SU(2)$-bundles over manifolds of dimension at most three every such $P$ is trivial (this is the input we lean on; see [[Thm - Classification of Principal SU(2)-Bundles over Four-Manifolds|the classification theorem]], whose three-dimensional case gives triviality because $SU(2)\cong S^3$ is two-connected). We therefore fix, once and for all, a trivialisation $P\cong M\times SU(2)$; relative to it a [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle|principal connection]] is recorded by its **gauge potential**, a Lie-algebra-valued one-form
$$A\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2)),$$
where $\mathfrak{su}(2)=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{gl}_2(\mathbb C):\xi^*=-\xi,\ \operatorname{tr}\xi=0\}$ is the Lie algebra of $SU(2)$, that is, the traceless skew-Hermitian $2\times2$ complex matrices, and $\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))=\Gamma(T^*M\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is the space of one-forms on $M$ valued in that algebra. The set of all connections is written $\mathcal A(P)$; in the trivialisation it is the affine space modelled on $\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

The symbol $\operatorname{tr}$ always denotes the ordinary matrix [[Def - Trace|trace]] in the defining two-dimensional representation, applied to the matrix part of a Lie-algebra-valued form; on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ the symmetric bilinear form $(\xi,\eta)\mapsto-\operatorname{tr}(\xi\eta)$ is positive definite (the negative of the Killing-type form), a fact we use in the examples. For $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued forms the wedge combines the exterior product of the form parts with **matrix multiplication** of the algebra parts: for $\alpha\in\Omega^p(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$ and $\beta\in\Omega^q(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$ written locally $\alpha=\alpha^a\otimes E_a$, $\beta=\beta^b\otimes E_b$ (with $\alpha^a,\beta^b$ scalar forms and $E_a,E_b$ matrices), $\alpha\wedge\beta:=(\alpha^a\wedge\beta^b)\otimes(E_aE_b)$; taking $\operatorname{tr}$ afterwards gives a scalar form. The [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection|curvature]] of $A$ in the trivialisation is $F_A=dA+A\wedge A\in\Omega^2(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$ (for the matrix group $SU(2)$ the bracket term $\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$ equals $A\wedge A$; see the series convention on the [[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket|bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms]]). We abbreviate $A\wedge A\wedge A$ by $A^{\wedge3}$.

A [[Def - Gauge Transformation|gauge transformation]] of the trivialised bundle is a smooth map $g\colon M\to SU(2)$; it acts on gauge potentials on the right by
$$A\cdot g:=A^g:=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}\,dg,$$
the standard transformation law of a connection under a change of frame. Since $SU(2)$ is diffeomorphic to the three-sphere $S^3$, and $M$ is a closed oriented three-manifold, such a map $g$ has a well-defined integer **degree** $\deg g\in\mathbb Z$ (see [[Def - Brouwer Degree of a Map|the degree]]); this integer is the whole source of the modular ambiguity below. We write $\mathcal G(P)$ for the group of gauge transformations and $\mathcal A(P)/\mathcal G(P)$ for the quotient of connections modulo gauge.

> [!warning] Convention: this is a compound definition page.
> It defines two interlocking objects and records a third characterisation of one of them. The two definienda are (i) the **Chern-Simons three-form** $\operatorname{cs}(A)\in\Omega^3(M)$, an algebraic expression in a single gauge potential, and (ii) the **Chern-Simons functional** $\vartheta(A)\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, its integral over $M$ normalised so that the modular ambiguity is exactly $\mathbb Z$. The third item is the **four-dimensional formula** $(95)$ of the source, which is not a separate definition but a *characterisation* of the same $\vartheta$; it is stated here and proved on [[Thm - The Four-Dimensional Formula for the Chern-Simons Functional]]. They are treated together because the functional is literally the integral of the form, and the form is the object on which every computation (gauge variation, critical points, transgression) is actually carried out.

> [!warning] Convention: a source typo.
> Haydys's Exercise 96(a), in its second displayed line for the special manifold $X=M\times\mathbb R$, omits the normalising factor $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ (recorded as item 6 of the source's typo list). The corrected identity, used throughout the series, is $\vartheta(A_{t_1})-\vartheta(A_{t_0})=\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_{M\times[t_0,t_1]}\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)$; the factor is restored on [[Thm - The Four-Dimensional Formula for the Chern-Simons Functional|the four-dimensional formula page]].

# Axiom Motivation

We want to attach a number to a connection $A$ on a closed oriented three-manifold in a way that is intrinsic to the geometry and useful as an action. Three demands shape the construction, and each fixes one feature of the final formula; a reader who takes the three demands seriously is led to the definition with no freedom left except the overall constant, which the last demand pins down as well.

The first demand is that the invariant be the **primitive of the second Chern form**. In four dimensions the natural gauge-invariant density is $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)$, whose integral over a closed four-manifold is $8\pi^2$ times the second Chern number of the bundle (this is the content of [[Thm - Second Chern Number of an SU(2)-Bundle over a Closed Four-Manifold is the Clutching Degree|the second-Chern-number theorem]]: $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_X\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)=k(P)\in\mathbb Z$). This four-form is closed but not zero, so it has no global primitive on a general four-manifold; locally, however, and globally in a fixed trivialisation, it is exact, and the [[Thm - Transgression Formula and the Chern-Simons Form|transgression formula]] names the primitive explicitly:
$$d\,\operatorname{tr}\!\Big(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23A^{\wedge3}\Big)=\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A).$$
The three-form inside the exterior derivative is exactly the object we shall call $\operatorname{cs}(A)$. Integrating a primitive of the four-dimensional density over a bounding three-manifold is precisely how one manufactures a *secondary* invariant — one that remembers the connection itself, not merely the topology of the bundle. This is what forces the cubic combination $A\wedge dA+\tfrac23A^{\wedge3}$: no other polynomial in $A$ of degree at most three has $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)$ as its exterior derivative.

The second demand is that the **critical points be the flat connections**. A three-dimensional gauge theory should have as its "vacua" the connections with vanishing curvature, because those are the connections determined by a monodromy representation of $\pi_1(M)$ and carry the topological information the theory is meant to detect. Computing the first variation of $\vartheta$ (carried out in full on [[Thm - Critical Points of the Chern-Simons Functional are the Flat Connections|the critical-point theorem]]) gives $\tfrac{d}{ds}\big|_0\vartheta(A+sa)=\tfrac1{4\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$, so the differential vanishes for all variations $a$ exactly when $F_A=0$. This is only possible because of the coefficient $\tfrac23$ on the cubic term: it is the value that makes the two cross terms of the variation combine into $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$ rather than $\operatorname{tr}(dA\wedge a)$. Drop the cubic term entirely and the critical points become the connections with $dA=0$, which are not gauge-invariantly meaningful; use any coefficient other than $\tfrac23$ and the variation fails to close up into the curvature.

The third demand is that $\vartheta$ be **as gauge-invariant as possible**, so that it descends to the quotient $\mathcal A(P)/\mathcal G(P)$ where the geometry actually lives. Here we meet the obstruction that gives the functional its character. The three-form $\operatorname{cs}(A)$ is *not* gauge invariant: under $A\mapsto A^g=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg$ it changes by an exact term plus the pull-back under $g$ of the bi-invariant three-form of $SU(2)$. The [[Thm - Gauge Variation of the Chern-Simons Functional|gauge-variation theorem]] makes this exact:
$$\operatorname{cs}(A^g)=\operatorname{cs}(A)-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\!\big((g^{-1}dg)^3\big)-d\,\operatorname{tr}(dg\,g^{-1}\wedge A).$$
Integrating over the closed $M$, the exact term dies by [[Thm - Stokes' Theorem on Manifolds|Stokes' theorem]] (no boundary), and $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}((g^{-1}dg)^3)$ equals an integer multiple of $\deg g$; the whole point of the normalising constant $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ is that this integer multiple is exactly $\deg g$, because $\int_{SU(2)}\operatorname{tr}(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta)=\pm24\pi^2$ for the Maurer-Cartan form $\theta$ and $-\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\cdot24\pi^2=\mp1$. The consequence is that $\vartheta$ is invariant not in $\mathbb R$ but in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$: gauge transformations shift it by integers, so the class $\vartheta(A)\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ is genuinely gauge invariant and descends to the quotient. This is why the codomain is the circle $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ and not the line.

The three demands together dictate everything: the transgression demand fixes the cubic three-form up to a constant; the critical-point demand fixes the coefficient $\tfrac23$; the gauge-invariance demand fixes the normalising constant $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ and forces the codomain to be $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. We now examine what breaks if each ingredient is dropped, because the failures are exactly the theorems the definition is designed to make true.

**Per-clause failure analysis.** If the **cubic term** $\tfrac23A^{\wedge3}$ is dropped, the remaining form $\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA)$ no longer transgresses $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)$: one computes $d\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA)=\operatorname{tr}(dA\wedge dA)$, which differs from $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge F_A)=\operatorname{tr}((dA+A^{\wedge2})\wedge(dA+A^{\wedge2}))$ by the terms involving $A^{\wedge2}$; and the critical points become the connections with $dA=0$ in the chosen trivialisation, a condition that is not gauge invariant (a gauge transform of such a connection generally has $dA\neq0$). The strengthening that repairs it is precisely to restore $\tfrac23A^{\wedge3}$.

If the **normalising constant** $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ is replaced by any other positive constant $c$, the functional is still defined but the gauge shift becomes $c\cdot(\mp24\pi^2)\cdot\tfrac{\deg g}{-3}$, an integer multiple of $\deg g$ only when $c=\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ (up to sign). For any other $c$ the shift is an irrational or non-integer multiple of $\deg g$, the modular ambiguity is a dense subgroup of $\mathbb R$ rather than $\mathbb Z$, and $\vartheta$ carries no information at all: every value is identified with every other. The concrete counterexample is $c=1$: then a pure-gauge connection has $\vartheta=8\pi^2\deg g$, and since $\deg g$ ranges over $\mathbb Z$ the well-defined value modulo the ambiguity is a class in $\mathbb R/(8\pi^2\mathbb Z)$, a different circle, and the two-different-trivialisations comparison no longer lands in $\mathbb Z$.

If we insist the functional be **$\mathbb R$-valued** rather than $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$-valued, it is simply not well defined: it depends on the trivialisation of $P$, and two trivialisations differ by a gauge transformation $g$, changing the value by $\deg g\in\mathbb Z$. There is no canonical trivialisation to break the tie, so an honest real number cannot be extracted; the circle is the smallest quotient on which the trivialisation-dependence disappears. This is the exact analogue of the failure that a phase, not a number, is what a wavefunction carries.

If $M$ is **not closed** (has boundary) the integral $\int_M\operatorname{cs}(A)$ still makes sense, but the gauge-invariance argument breaks: the exact term $d\operatorname{tr}(dg\,g^{-1}\wedge A)$ no longer integrates to zero, contributing a boundary term $\int_{\partial M}\operatorname{tr}(dg\,g^{-1}\wedge A)$, so $\vartheta$ is not even invariant modulo $\mathbb Z$. Closedness of $M$ is exactly what Stokes' theorem needs to kill that term. If $M$ is **not oriented**, the integral of a three-form is not defined at all, since integration on manifolds requires a choice of orientation to fix the sign of the volume.

Finally, if the structure group is changed from $SU(2)$ to a group with different low-dimensional homotopy — the abelian $U(1)$ is the instructive case — the shift produced by a gauge transformation is governed by $\pi_3$ of the group rather than by $\deg g$, and for $U(1)$ one has $\pi_3(U(1))=0$; the same normalisation then fails to produce an $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$-valued invariant without an additional integer "level". We return to this as the non-example below.

# The Definition

Let $M$ be a closed oriented three-manifold, $P\to M$ a principal $SU(2)$-bundle, and fix a trivialisation, so that a connection is represented by a gauge potential $A\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$.

**The Chern-Simons three-form** of $A$ is
$$\operatorname{cs}(A):=\operatorname{tr}\!\Big(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23\,A\wedge A\wedge A\Big)\in\Omega^3(M),$$
an ordinary real-valued three-form on $M$ (the matrix trace has been taken, so no Lie-algebra indices remain). Because $M$ is three-dimensional, $\operatorname{cs}(A)$ is a top-degree form and may be integrated.

**The Chern-Simons functional** of $A$ is the residue modulo one of its integral,
$$\boxed{\ \vartheta(A):=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{cs}(A)=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}\!\Big(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23\,A\wedge A\wedge A\Big)\ \in\ \mathbb R/\mathbb Z.\ }$$
The real number $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{cs}(A)$ depends on the chosen trivialisation, but its class in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ does not, and it is this class that is called $\vartheta(A)$.

**Well-definedness modulo $\mathbb Z$ (a corollary of the gauge-variation theorem).** Two trivialisations of $P$ differ by a gauge transformation $g\colon M\to SU(2)$: if $A$ and $A'$ are the gauge potentials of the same connection in the two trivialisations, then $A'=A^g=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg$ for that $g$. By [[Thm - Gauge Variation of the Chern-Simons Functional|the gauge-variation theorem]] — which states precisely that for $A\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$ on a closed oriented three-manifold and $g\colon M\to SU(2)$ smooth,
$$\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{cs}(A^g)=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{cs}(A)+\deg g,\qquad \deg g\in\mathbb Z,$$
the two real numbers differ by the integer $\deg g$. Hence their classes in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ coincide, and $\vartheta(A)$ is independent of the trivialisation. In particular $\vartheta$ is invariant under all gauge transformations modulo $\mathbb Z$, so it descends to a well-defined map on the quotient of connections by the gauge group,
$$\vartheta\colon\mathcal A(P)/\mathcal G(P)\longrightarrow\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.$$

**Equivalent characterisation: the four-dimensional formula.** Suppose $X$ is a compact oriented four-manifold with $\partial X=M$ (with the induced boundary orientation), $P_X\to X$ a principal $SU(2)$-bundle with $P_X|_M\cong P$, and $A_X$ a connection on $P_X$ restricting to $A$ on the boundary. Then, as proved on [[Thm - The Four-Dimensional Formula for the Chern-Simons Functional|the four-dimensional-formula page]],
$$\vartheta(A)\equiv\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_X\operatorname{tr}(F_{A_X}\wedge F_{A_X})\pmod{\mathbb Z}.\qquad(95)$$
This is Haydys's original definition; it makes the relation to Chern-Weil theory transparent (the right-hand side is the integral of the second Chern form of the extended bundle) but requires a bounding four-manifold and an extension to exist, whereas the three-dimensional formula boxed above is computable directly from $A$ alone. In this series the **three-dimensional formula is the primary definition** and $(95)$ is a theorem about it. Its well-definedness modulo $\mathbb Z$ across different choices of $(X,P_X,A_X)$ is part of that theorem, and rests on the integrality $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_{\widehat X}\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)\in\mathbb Z$ for the closed four-manifold $\widehat X=X\cup_M\overline{X'}$ obtained by gluing two extensions along $M$ with the orientation of the second reversed.

**Scope remark.** Haydys's four-dimensional definition needs the topological fact that *every closed oriented three-manifold bounds a compact oriented four-manifold* (a theorem of Thom and Rokhlin; a constructive proof follows from the Lickorish–Wallace surgery presentation of three-manifolds). This fact is **not proved and not used anywhere in this series**: the primary definition is the three-dimensional one, which needs no bounding manifold, and the four-dimensional formula is invoked only when a bounding $X$ is explicitly supplied.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Operationally, $\vartheta$ is *the integral over $M$ of a local primitive of the second Chern form, read modulo the integer ambiguity of the primitive*. The second Chern form $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)$ is a closed four-form whose de Rham class is the characteristic class $c_2$; it is exact on any trivialised bundle, with primitive the Chern-Simons three-form; integrating that primitive over the three-manifold produces a *secondary* characteristic number, well defined only modulo the periods of $c_2$, which are the integers. In this sense $\vartheta$ is to $c_2$ as a potential is to a field strength: it is the antiderivative you can integrate over a cycle one dimension lower, at the cost of a residual ambiguity. Every occurrence of the phrase "Chern-Simons invariant" in the literature is this construction, run with a chosen invariant polynomial in place of $\operatorname{tr}(\xi^2)$.

The construction sits inside a general machine, **Chern-Simons transgression**, which the series builds on [[Thm - Transgression Formula and the Chern-Simons Form|the transgression page]]: to every $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d$ and every pair of connections it assigns a $(2d-1)$-form whose exterior derivative is the difference of the two Chern-Weil forms $p(F_1)-p(F_0)$. Taking $p(\xi)=\operatorname{tr}(\xi^2)$, degree two, and taking the second connection to be the product connection ($A=0$) recovers exactly $\operatorname{cs}(A)$ as the transgression from the trivial connection to $A$. The same machine with the first Pontryagin polynomial gives the gravitational Chern-Simons term, and with higher polynomials the higher secondary classes; the object on this page is the lowest and most-used instance.

In **theoretical physics** $\vartheta$ is the action of three-dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theory (the topological field theory of Witten, whose partition functions are knot invariants); the requirement that $e^{2\pi ik\vartheta}$ be well defined for a coupling constant $k$ is exactly the requirement that $k$ be an integer, the "quantisation of the Chern-Simons level", and it is nothing but the $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$-valuedness proved here read multiplicatively. In **low-dimensional topology** the critical values of $\vartheta$ on the flat connections are the Chern-Simons invariants of flat $SU(2)$-bundles, real-number invariants of the pair (three-manifold, representation of its fundamental group), and the gradient flow of $\vartheta$ between flat connections is the instanton equation whose count defines instanton Floer homology. Each of these is the same functional viewed through a different lens.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the trivial connection.** For $A=0$ every term of the integrand vanishes: $\operatorname{cs}(0)=\operatorname{tr}(0\wedge d0+\tfrac230\wedge0\wedge0)=0$, so
$$\vartheta(0)=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M 0=0\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.$$
Thus the product connection is a zero of the functional, consistent with its being flat ($F_0=d0+0\wedge0=0$) and hence a critical point.

**Is an instance — a pure-gauge connection.** Let $g\colon M\to SU(2)$ be any smooth map and consider the pure-gauge connection $A=g^{-1}dg$, which is the trivial connection transformed by $g$: indeed $0\cdot g=g^{-1}0g+g^{-1}dg=g^{-1}dg$. Applying the gauge-variation theorem with $A=0$,
$$\vartheta(g^{-1}dg)=\vartheta(0\cdot g)=\vartheta(0)+\deg g=0+\deg g\equiv0\pmod{\mathbb Z}\qquad\text{(gauge-variation theorem; }\vartheta(0)=0\text{; }\deg g\in\mathbb Z\text{).}$$
So every pure-gauge connection is a zero of $\vartheta$ in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. This is the calibration that the normalisation is correct: the integer produced is precisely $\deg g$, no other multiple. It also confirms flatness is not sufficient for the value to be zero as a real number before reduction — the *real* number $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{cs}(g^{-1}dg)=\deg g$ is a nonzero integer whenever $\deg g\neq0$, and only its class modulo $\mathbb Z$ is zero.

**Is an instance — an abelian connection.** Suppose $A$ takes values in a one-dimensional subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, that is, $A=\alpha\otimes\xi$ for a fixed matrix $\xi\in\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and a real-valued one-form $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$. Then the cubic term vanishes clause by clause: $A\wedge A=(\alpha\wedge\alpha)\otimes\xi^2=0$ because $\alpha$ is a one-form and $\alpha\wedge\alpha=0$ for any one-form, so a fortiori $A^{\wedge3}=0$. The functional therefore reduces to its quadratic part,
$$\vartheta(A)=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\xi^2)}{8\pi^2}\int_M\alpha\wedge d\alpha\qquad\text{(cubic term }=0\text{; }A\wedge dA=(\alpha\wedge d\alpha)\otimes\xi^2\text{).}$$
Here $\int_M\alpha\wedge d\alpha$ is the ordinary abelian Chern-Simons integral of the scalar one-form $\alpha$; for instance with $\xi=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ one has $\operatorname{tr}(\xi^2)=-2$, giving $\vartheta(A)=-\tfrac1{4\pi^2}\int_M\alpha\wedge d\alpha\pmod{\mathbb Z}$. The verification of each clause — that $\alpha\wedge\alpha=0$, that the tensor factor pulls out of the trace, that no other term survives — is exactly what makes this the tractable subcase used for hand computations, for example on the three-torus.

**Is NOT an instance — the naive $U(1)$ Chern-Simons functional over a three-manifold.** Replace the structure group $SU(2)$ by $U(1)$, so that a gauge potential is a one-form $A\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak u(1))$ valued in the abelian algebra $\mathfrak u(1)=i\mathbb R$, and consider the *same* expression $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23A^{\wedge3})$. This is **not** a well-defined $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$-valued invariant without a further normalisation, and the reason is instructive rather than a matter of a wrong constant. Because $\mathfrak u(1)$ is abelian and $A$ is a one-form, $A^{\wedge3}=0$, so the candidate is $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA)$. A gauge transformation is now a map $g=e^{i\varphi}\colon M\to U(1)$, and its "size" is measured not by a degree $\deg g\in\pi_3(U(1))=0$ — which is trivial, since $U(1)\cong S^1$ has no three-dimensional homotopy — but by the pull-back $g^{-1}dg=i\,d\varphi$, whose periods lie in $2\pi i\,H^1(M;\mathbb Z)$. Under $A\mapsto A+i\,d\varphi$ the integral changes by $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M i\,d\varphi\wedge dA$ plus a term, and this shift is an integer multiple of $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\cdot(2\pi)^2=\tfrac1{2}$ only for a particular normalisation, not for the $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$ inherited from $SU(2)$. The correct $U(1)$ Chern-Simons functional carries an integer level $k$ and the normalisation $\tfrac{k}{8\pi^2}$ (or $\tfrac{k}{4\pi}$ with the imaginary convention absorbed), tuned so that the shift is an integer; with the bare $SU(2)$ constant the ambiguity is not $\mathbb Z$ and the expression does not descend to $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. We record this as a caution — the formula does not transplant across structure groups by keeping the constant — rather than as a proven quantitative claim, since the exact $U(1)$ level requires the winding data of $H^1(M;\mathbb Z)$ that is outside this page's scope.

**Corollary — $\vartheta$ descends to the moduli space and its critical set is the flat connections.** Because $\vartheta$ is gauge invariant modulo $\mathbb Z$, it is a function on $\mathcal A(P)/\mathcal G(P)$. Its differential $d\vartheta_A(a)=\tfrac1{4\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$ is genuinely $\mathbb R$-valued (the ambiguity is locally constant, so it drops out of the derivative) and gauge invariant, and it vanishes for all $a$ exactly when $F_A=0$; the complete argument is on [[Thm - Critical Points of the Chern-Simons Functional are the Flat Connections|the critical-point theorem]]. Hence the critical points of $\vartheta$ on the moduli space are precisely the gauge classes of [[Def - Flat Connection|flat connections]], the same objects that the monodromy correspondence identifies with representations $\pi_1(M)\to SU(2)$ up to conjugacy.

**Calibration check.** First, $\vartheta$ is a **cubic polynomial on the affine space of connections**: writing $A_s=A+sa$ and expanding, $\operatorname{cs}(A+sa)$ is a polynomial of degree three in $s$ (the top term $\tfrac23s^3\operatorname{tr}(a^{\wedge3})$ integrates to zero on the closed $M$ but the degree-two-in-$s$ term does not), so $s\mapsto\vartheta(A+sa)$ is a cubic; its $s^1$-coefficient is the differential computed above, and reading off that coefficient reproduces $\tfrac1{4\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$. Second, $\vartheta(0)=0$ and $\vartheta(g^{-1}dg)\equiv0$, verified above, together show the zero locus of $\vartheta$ contains the entire gauge orbit of the trivial connection — as it must, since that orbit consists of flat connections, hence critical points, and here happens to sit at the value zero. Third, a reader who has followed the abelian example can now predict that on a three-manifold with $H^1(M;\mathbb R)=0$ (a rational homology sphere) the abelian contribution $\int_M\alpha\wedge d\alpha$ is controlled by the linking form, a check that connects the definition to genuinely topological data.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Critical Points and the Flat Moduli Space *(from this chapter)*
> With $\vartheta$ in hand, the [[Thm - Critical Points of the Chern-Simons Functional are the Flat Connections|critical-point theorem]] identifies its critical set with the space of flat connections modulo gauge, tying the analytic functional to the representation variety $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M),SU(2))/\text{conj}$ studied in Chapter V.

> [!tip] The Yang–Mills Boundary Term *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> On a four-manifold with boundary, $\vartheta$ of the boundary connection is the term that must be added to the Yang–Mills action to make its variational problem well posed; the four-dimensional formula on this page is the identity that expresses the bulk instanton number as the difference of the boundary Chern-Simons invariants.

> [!tip] Instanton Floer Homology *(from later gauge theory)*
> **Instanton Floer homology** is built as the Morse homology of $\vartheta$ on the space of $SU(2)$-connections modulo gauge, with the flat connections as generators and the anti-self-dual instantons on $M\times\mathbb R$ as the gradient trajectories; the $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$-valuedness proved here is what makes the relative gradings well defined.
