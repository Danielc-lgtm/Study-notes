# Notation

Throughout, $G$ is a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$, where $e \in G$ is the identity; $B$ is a smooth manifold, taken as the base of the bundles below; and $\mathbb{K}$ is the coefficient field, either $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. A principal $G$-bundle $\pi \colon P \to B$ carries a **right** action $R_g(p) = p \cdot g$ of $G$, following the standing convention of the series. For $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ the **fundamental vector field** $X_P$ is the vector field on $P$ generated by the action,
$$X_P(p) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\, p \cdot \exp(tX),$$
written $\bar{X}$ in Bär's lecture notes; we keep $X_P$ and record $\bar X$ where it is quoted. See [[Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action|fundamental vector field]].

A **connection on the principal bundle** is a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form $\omega \in \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g})$ satisfying the two axioms $\omega(X_P) = X$ for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $R_g^* \omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \omega$ for every $g \in G$ (see [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle|connection on a principal bundle]]). Its **curvature** is the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued two-form
$$\Omega \;=\; d\omega + \tfrac{1}{2}[\omega \wedge \omega] \;\in\; \Omega^2(P; \mathfrak{g})$$
(the structure equation; see [[Thm - Structure Equation for the Curvature|structure equation for the curvature]] and [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection|curvature of a principal connection]]). Here the bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms follows the convention of [[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket|the series]]: for one-forms $\alpha, \beta \in \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g})$,
$$[\alpha \wedge \beta](X, Y) = [\alpha(X), \beta(Y)] - [\alpha(Y), \beta(X)], \qquad\text{so}\qquad [\alpha \wedge \alpha](X, Y) = 2\,[\alpha(X), \alpha(Y)],$$
which is why the structure equation carries the factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$. A connection is **flat** when its curvature vanishes identically, $\Omega = 0$; see [[Def - Flat Connection|flat connection]].

An **$\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial** $p \colon \mathfrak{g} \to \mathbb{K}$ of degree $d$ is a homogeneous degree-$d$ polynomial with $p(\operatorname{Ad}_g X) = p(X)$ for all $g \in G$ and $X \in \mathfrak{g}$; its full polarisation is the symmetric $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant $d$-linear form $\lambda \colon \mathfrak{g}^{\times d} \to \mathbb{K}$ with $p(X) = \lambda(X, \dots, X)$ (see [[Def - Ad-Invariant Polynomial|Ad-invariant polynomial]]). The **Chern–Weil form** $p(F_\omega) \in \Omega^{2d}(B; \mathbb{K})$ is the closed $2d$-form obtained by evaluating $p$ (equivalently $\lambda$) on the curvature $\Omega$ and descending to the base $B$; Bär writes it $\lambda \circ \bar\Omega$, where $\bar\Omega$ is the curvature regarded through the adjoint bundle. The **characteristic class** of $P$ associated with $p$ is its de Rham class
$$c_p(P) \;:=\; \big[\, p(F_\omega) \,\big] \;\in\; H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K}),$$
which is independent of the connection $\omega$ by [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|the Chern–Weil theorem]] (see [[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial|Chern–Weil form of an invariant polynomial]]). We write $\deg p = d$ for the degree, so $2d$ is the form-degree of $p(F_\omega)$.

The **left Maurer–Cartan form** of $G$ is the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form $\theta \in \Omega^1(G; \mathfrak{g})$ defined by $\theta_{g}(v) = (dL_{g^{-1}})_g(v)$ for $v \in T_gG$, where $L_h(g) = hg$ is left translation; for matrix groups $\theta = g^{-1}\, dg$. See [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|the Maurer–Cartan form]]. The **product bundle** over $B$ is $B \times G$ with the right action $R_g(b, g') = (b, g'g)$ and projection $\operatorname{pr}_2 \colon B \times G \to G$, $(b, g') \mapsto g'$. A principal $G$-bundle is **trivial** when it is isomorphic, as a principal $G$-bundle over $B$, to the product $B \times G$; see [[Def - Principal G-Bundle|principal $G$-bundle]] and [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|sections of a principal bundle and triviality]].

> [!warning] Convention: bracket and exterior-derivative notation differ from Bär
> Bär (B §2.5) writes the curvature bracket as $[\omega, \omega]$ where the series writes $[\omega \wedge \omega]$; the two denote the same $\mathfrak{g}$-valued two-form, and the conversion is purely notational. Bär also uses the exterior-derivative convention $d\alpha(X, Y) = \partial_X\alpha(Y) - \partial_Y\alpha(X) - \alpha([X, Y])$ for a one-form $\alpha$ (no combinatorial prefactor), which the series adopts as well; every displayed use below is written in it.

> [!warning] Convention: the sign slip in Bär's computation of $d\phi(X, Y)$
> In Bär's proof (Remark 2.5.9, PDF p. 64) the intermediate line reads
> $$d\phi(X, Y) = \partial_X(Y(e)) - \partial_Y(X(e)) - [X, Y](e) = [X, Y](e) \qquad \text{(as printed).}$$
> The first two terms are the directional derivatives of the *constant* $\mathfrak{g}$-valued functions $X(e)$ and $Y(e)$ and are therefore zero, so the correct value is $d\phi(X, Y) = -[X, Y](e)$, not $+[X, Y](e)$. With the printed sign the sum $d\phi + \tfrac12[\phi \wedge \phi]$ would equal $2[X,Y](e) \neq 0$ and the argument would collapse; with the corrected sign it equals $0$, which is exactly the Maurer–Cartan equation. The proof below uses the corrected form, obtained cleanly from [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|the Maurer–Cartan form]]. This is Appendix B of the content map, item for B-R2.5.9.

# Statement

> **Theorem (characteristic classes vanish on flat and trivial bundles).** Let $G$ be a Lie group, $B$ a smooth manifold, and $p \colon \mathfrak{g} \to \mathbb{K}$ an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial of degree $d = \deg p \geq 1$. Let $\pi \colon P \to B$ be a principal $G$-bundle.
> 1. **(Flat case.)** If $P$ admits a flat connection — a connection $\omega$ with $\Omega = 0$ — then $c_p(P) = 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$.
> 2. **(Trivial case.)** If $P$ is trivial, then $c_p(P) = 0$. In particular every product bundle $B \times G$ has $c_p(B \times G) = 0$.
> 3. **(Obstruction to triviality, contrapositive.)** If $c_p(P) \neq 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$ for some $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d \geq 1$, then $P$ is not trivial; indeed $P$ admits no flat connection.

The three parts are ordered by logical strength: part 1 is the engine, part 2 is its specialisation to trivial bundles (a trivial bundle carries a flat connection), and part 3 is the contrapositive of the first two, which is the form in which the theorem is used as an obstruction. The hypothesis $d \geq 1$ is essential and is discussed in the Motivation: for a constant polynomial ($d = 0$) the class $c_p(P)$ is a nonzero multiple of the class of the constant function $1$ and never vanishes.

Bär records part 2 as Remark 2.5.9 (B-T2.5.6) and part 3 as Corollary 2.5.10 (B-T2.5.7); the flat mechanism behind them is Remark 2.5.9's construction (B-R2.5.3), which the series isolates as part 1.

# Motivation

A characteristic class is a cohomological fingerprint of a bundle. The Chern–Weil construction manufactures it out of curvature: pick any connection $\omega$, form the closed $2d$-form $p(F_\omega)$ out of the curvature by an invariant polynomial, and take its de Rham class. [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|The Chern–Weil theorem]] guarantees that the resulting class $c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)]$ does not depend on the connection, so it is a genuine invariant of the bundle and its base. The natural question is then: what does this invariant *see*? A first answer, and the cleanest one, is negative — it tells us exactly which bundles it cannot distinguish from the boring one.

The boring bundle is the product $B \times G$. It has a canonical connection, the one that declares the copies of $G$ over different points of $B$ to be "the same": parallel transport along any path in $B$ carries $(b_0, g)$ to $(b_1, g)$ without twisting. Such a connection has nothing to curve, and its curvature is zero. Since the characteristic class is built entirely from curvature, and this connection has none, the class computed from it is the class of the zero form, namely zero. The Chern–Weil theorem then upgrades this from a statement about one connection to a statement about the bundle: because $c_p$ is connection-independent, and one connection gives a zero representative, *every* connection gives a class of zero. The trivial bundle is invisible to every characteristic class of positive degree.

This is the observation the theorem records, and it is worth stating why it matters even though it sounds like a triviality. Its contrapositive is a working tool. If we can compute a single characteristic class of a bundle and find it nonzero, we have proved — without ever trying to construct a global trivialisation or a global section, and without a homotopy-theoretic classification — that the bundle is *not* trivial. This is how one proves the tangent bundle of the two-sphere admits no nowhere-vanishing section: its first Chern class (viewing $TS^2$ as a complex line bundle) integrates to $2 \neq 0$, so $TS^2 \not\cong S^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2$. It is how one distinguishes the infinitely many principal $SU(2)$-bundles over the four-sphere: their second Chern numbers are the distinct integers. Curvature is local and computable; triviality is global and a priori hard to disprove; the theorem trades the hard global question for the easy local computation.

The refinement to *flat* bundles, part 1, widens the reach. Flatness — zero curvature — is much weaker than triviality: a flat bundle can have interesting global monodromy and be far from a product, as the flat line bundles over a torus already show. Yet the same mechanism applies verbatim, because the argument never used triviality, only the vanishing of curvature. So a nonzero characteristic class obstructs not merely triviality but the far weaker property of admitting *any* flat connection. This is the seed of Chern–Weil theory as an obstruction theory: characteristic classes are precisely the shadow of curvature that survives in cohomology, and they must vanish wherever curvature can be made to vanish.

Finally, the degree hypothesis $d \geq 1$ is not a technicality but the boundary of the phenomenon. The class $c_p$ lives in $H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}$; for $d = 0$ it lives in $H^0$, which measures connected components, and there the "curvature-free" reasoning fails because a degree-zero polynomial is a constant that does not vanish when evaluated on a zero curvature. The theorem is exactly a statement about the positive-degree part of the Chern–Weil homomorphism.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The literal hypothesis of the main engine (part 1) is "$P$ admits a flat connection", and of part 2 "$P$ is trivial". These are strong-sounding conditions, so the skill is to recognise the many weaker or disguised situations that secretly supply them.

The first disguised source is **a global section of a principal bundle**. A principal $G$-bundle need not be handed to us as a product, but if it possesses a single global smooth section $s \colon B \to P$ then it is automatically isomorphic to the product $B \times G$ by the map $(b, g) \mapsto s(b) \cdot g$, so it is trivial and part 2 applies. The bridge — a principal bundle with a global section is trivial — is [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the section-triviality theorem]], and it is non-obvious precisely because for a *vector* bundle a single section says almost nothing, whereas for a *principal* bundle one section trivialises everything (the fibre is a torsor, and one point pins down the whole coordinate system). *Example problem:* show that the frame bundle of a parallelisable manifold — one with a global frame — has all characteristic classes zero, so that a manifold with a nonzero Pontryagin or Euler class cannot be parallelisable.

The second disguised source is **a contractible or homotopically trivial base**. If $B$ is contractible, or more generally if the classifying map of $P$ is null-homotopic, then $P$ is trivial by the homotopy classification of bundles, and part 2 applies. In cohomological terms one can even shortcut the classification: over a contractible $B$ every closed positive-degree form is exact ([[Thm - The Poincaré Lemma on a Star-Shaped Region|the Poincaré lemma]]), so $c_p(P) = 0$ for free; the theorem then says this cohomological vanishing is the reflection of an honest triviality. The bridge is homotopy invariance of pullback bundles, and the non-obvious step is that a global topological condition on the base (contractibility) forces a global condition on every bundle over it. *Example problem:* explain why characteristic classes are only ever interesting over bases with nontrivial higher cohomology, and why every bundle over a Euclidean chart is trivial.

The third disguised source is **a connection with trivial holonomy, or a flat connection with trivial monodromy**. A connection whose parallel transport around every loop is the identity is flat, and part 1 applies directly to give $c_p(P) = 0$; one does not need triviality of $P$ itself. More subtly, a flat connection on a bundle over a simply connected base has trivial monodromy and forces the bundle to be trivial by [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|the monodromy theorem]], so over a simply connected base "flat" and "trivial" coincide as sources. The bridge is that curvature is the infinitesimal holonomy, so vanishing holonomy implies vanishing curvature. *Example problem:* given a bundle carrying a connection whose curvature two-form is identically zero, conclude that all its Pontryagin numbers vanish, without deciding whether the bundle is trivial.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The bare conclusion is "$c_p = 0$" or its contrapositive "$c_p \neq 0 \Rightarrow$ nontrivial". Combined with a computed class it becomes a detection theorem.

Combine the contrapositive (part 3) with **an explicit nonzero curvature integral over a closed surface or four-manifold**. If one computes, by Stokes' theorem and a local connection, that $\int_\Sigma c_1(L) \neq 0$ for a line bundle $L$ over a closed surface $\Sigma$, or $\int_X c_2(P) \neq 0$ for an $SU(2)$-bundle over a closed four-manifold $X$, then the theorem yields nontriviality at once. The extra ingredient is the period computation; the payoff is a rigorous nontriviality proof with no global data. This is the route of [[Ex - The Tangent Bundle of S^2 is Nontrivial via Chern Classes|the nontriviality of $TS^2$]] and of [[Ex - The Chern Number of the Hopf Line Bundle over CP^1 is Minus One|the Hopf bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^1$]].

Combine part 3 with **an infinite family of distinct class values**. If a family of bundles $\{P_k\}$ has characteristic numbers taking every integer value $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, then part 3 shows all the $P_k$ with $k \neq 0$ are nontrivial, and [[Thm - Naturality and Isomorphism Invariance of Characteristic Classes|isomorphism invariance]] shows moreover that distinct values give non-isomorphic bundles, so there are infinitely many. The extra ingredient is isomorphism invariance; the payoff is a lower bound on the number of bundles. This is how the principal $SU(2)$-bundles over $S^4$ are seen to be infinite in number, indexed by the instanton number.

Combine part 1 with **the search for flat connections**. Read forwards, part 1 says a nonzero characteristic class is an obstruction to flatness: a bundle with $c_p(P) \neq 0$ admits no flat connection whatsoever, hence no connection with trivial holonomy, hence (over a suitable base) supports genuinely curved gauge fields only. The extra ingredient is the target class computation; the payoff is a no-go theorem for flat gauge fields, which in physics is the statement that certain topological sectors force a nonzero field strength. *Example:* on a bundle with nonzero second Chern number there is no pure-gauge (flat) configuration, so the Yang–Mills energy is bounded below by a positive topological constant.

# Why Is It True

Strip away the formalism. A characteristic class is assembled by a fixed recipe out of one ingredient — the curvature of a connection — and the [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|Chern–Weil theorem]] promises that the finished class does not remember which connection we started from. Now suppose the bundle is generous enough to carry a connection with *no* curvature at all. Feed that connection into the recipe. Every ingredient is zero, and a recipe of positive order (degree $d \geq 1$) applied to zero returns zero: the output form $p(F_\omega)$ is literally the zero $2d$-form, whose cohomology class is zero. Because the class is connection-independent, this zero is the value of $c_p(P)$ computed by any connection at all.

> **Mechanism, in one sentence:** a characteristic class is made of curvature and nothing else, so on a bundle whose curvature can be switched off — a flat, in particular a trivial, bundle — the class is forced to be the class of zero.

The only genuine content beyond this slogan is the existence of a curvature-free connection on the product bundle. Here the mental picture is that the product $B \times G$ has an obvious notion of "no twisting": declare the horizontal directions to be the copies of $B$ sitting at fixed $g \in G$. Parallel transport then just slides along $B$ without moving in the fibre. Concretely this connection is the pullback $\operatorname{pr}_2^* \theta$ of the Maurer–Cartan form, and its flatness is nothing but the Maurer–Cartan equation $d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0$, which itself is the coordinate-free statement that the group multiplication is associative — the left-invariant vector fields close under bracket with the structure constants and no extra term. So the flatness of the product connection is the geometric shadow of the group being a group.

For a general trivial bundle one adds one line: triviality means an isomorphism to $B \times G$, and an isomorphism carries the flat product connection to a flat connection on $P$ (curvature is natural under bundle maps), or equivalently carries $c_p(B \times G) = 0$ to $c_p(P)$ by [[Thm - Naturality and Isomorphism Invariance of Characteristic Classes|isomorphism invariance]]. Either way the zero is transported across the isomorphism.

# What Makes This Hard

The conceptual step — "no curvature, so no class" — is a single line, and the temptation is to think there is nothing to prove. The two places where care is genuinely required are, first, verifying that the pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form actually *is* a principal connection (both connection axioms have to be checked, and the equivariance axiom $R_g^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega$ is where Bär's exposition garbles the group-element bookkeeping); and second, the Maurer–Cartan equation itself, where the printed source carries a sign error that, taken at face value, makes the curvature nonzero. The common error is to quote "$d\theta(X,Y) = [X,Y](e)$" from a careless source; the correct value is $-[X,Y](e)$, because the terms $\partial_X(\theta(Y))$ vanish (the Maurer–Cartan form takes a left-invariant field to a *constant*), leaving only $-\theta([X,Y])$. Getting this sign right is the whole difference between $\Omega = 0$ and $\Omega \neq 0$.

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:** Show that the product bundle carries a flat connection, namely the pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form; deduce flatness from the Maurer–Cartan equation; observe that a positive-degree invariant polynomial applied to zero curvature gives the zero form, so its class is zero; then transport this to any flat or trivial bundle by connection-independence and isomorphism invariance.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Maurer–Cartan equation.** Prove $d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0$ for the left Maurer–Cartan form $\theta$ on $G$.
   - *Hint:* Evaluate on left-invariant vector fields $X, Y$; use that $\theta(X) = X(e)$ is constant so its directional derivatives vanish, and that $\theta([X, Y]) = [X, Y](e) = [X(e), Y(e)]$ by the definition of the Lie bracket.
   - *Why needed:* It is exactly the flatness of the product connection.

2. **The product connection.** Show $\omega := \operatorname{pr}_2^* \theta$ is a principal connection on $B \times G$: verify $\omega(X_P) = X$ and $R_g^* \omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \omega$.
   - *Hint:* For the first axiom, push $X_P$ forward by $\operatorname{pr}_2$ to a left-translate of $X$, then apply $\theta$. For the second, use $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ R_g = R_g^G \circ \operatorname{pr}_2$ and the right-translation law $(R_g^G)^* \theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \theta$.
   - *Why needed:* Only a genuine connection has a curvature to which Chern–Weil applies.

3. **Flatness of the product connection.** Show its curvature $\Omega$ is zero.
   - *Hint:* $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega \wedge \omega] = \operatorname{pr}_2^*(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta \wedge \theta])$; apply subgoal 1.
   - *Why needed:* Zero curvature is the input to the vanishing of the Chern–Weil form.

4. **Positive-degree polynomial kills zero curvature.** Show that if a connection has $\Omega = 0$ and $\deg p = d \geq 1$, then the Chern–Weil form $p(F_\omega)$ is the zero $2d$-form, hence $c_p = [0] = 0$.
   - *Hint:* In a local frame $p(F_\omega) = \sum \Omega^{j_1} \wedge \dots \wedge \Omega^{j_d}\, \lambda(Y_{j_1}, \dots, Y_{j_d})$; each summand carries at least one factor $\Omega^{j} = 0$ since $d \geq 1$.
   - *Why needed:* This is the arithmetic heart; note where $d \geq 1$ enters.

5. **Assembly.** Deduce parts 1, 2, 3 of the theorem from subgoals 1–4 together with connection-independence and isomorphism invariance.
   - *Hint:* Part 1 is subgoal 4 applied to the given flat connection; part 2 reduces a trivial $P$ to $B \times G$ by isomorphism invariance and applies subgoals 2–4; part 3 is the contrapositive.
   - *Why needed:* It packages the mechanism into the three usable statements.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: The Maurer–Cartan equation
> **Statement:** Let $G$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$ and left Maurer–Cartan form $\theta \in \Omega^1(G; \mathfrak{g})$, $\theta_g(v) = (dL_{g^{-1}})_g(v)$. Then
> $$d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta] = 0 \in \Omega^2(G; \mathfrak{g}).$$
>
> **Hint:** Both sides are two-forms, so it suffices to check equality on a frame; the left-invariant vector fields furnish one, and on them $\theta$ takes constant values, killing the derivative terms.
>
> **Why needed:** It is precisely the flatness of the pullback connection $\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$ used to trivialise the Chern–Weil form; it also supplies the corrected sign for Bär's computation.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > **Setup.** Let $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$ and denote by the same letters the associated left-invariant vector fields on $G$, so $X(g) = (dL_g)_e X(e)$ and $X(e) = X$; likewise for $Y$. A two-form on $G$ is determined by its values on all pairs of left-invariant fields, because these span $T_gG$ at every $g \in G$ (left translation is a diffeomorphism, so $\{X(g) : X \in \mathfrak{g}\} = T_gG$). We compute both sides on the pair $(X, Y)$.
> >
> > **The Maurer–Cartan form is constant on a left-invariant field.** By definition, for each $g \in G$,
> > $$\theta(X)(g) = \theta_g(X(g)) = (dL_{g^{-1}})_g (dL_g)_e X(e) = (d(L_{g^{-1}} \circ L_g))_e X(e) = X(e) \qquad \text{(since } L_{g^{-1}} \circ L_g = \operatorname{id}_G\text{)}.$$
> > Hence $\theta(X) \equiv X(e)$ is the *constant* $\mathfrak{g}$-valued function on $G$; likewise $\theta(Y) \equiv Y(e)$.
> >
> > **The derivative terms vanish.** Bär's exterior-derivative convention gives, for the one-form $\theta$,
> > $$d\theta(X, Y) = \partial_X\big(\theta(Y)\big) - \partial_Y\big(\theta(X)\big) - \theta([X, Y]) \qquad \text{(definition of } d \text{ on one-forms).}$$
> > The first two terms are directional derivatives of the constant functions $\theta(Y) \equiv Y(e)$ and $\theta(X) \equiv X(e)$, hence both are $0$. This is exactly the point Bär's printed intermediate line mishandles.
> >
> > **The bracket term.** The bracket $[X, Y]$ of two left-invariant fields is again left-invariant, and by the definition of the Lie algebra bracket its value at the identity is the algebra bracket of the values: $[X, Y](e) = [X(e), Y(e)]$. Therefore $\theta([X, Y]) \equiv [X, Y](e) = [X(e), Y(e)]$ is again constant, and
> > $$d\theta(X, Y) = -\,\theta([X, Y]) = -[X(e), Y(e)] = -[\theta(X)(g), \theta(Y)(g)] \qquad \text{(all evaluated at any } g\text{; the values are constant).}$$
> >
> > **The wedge-bracket term.** By the series bracket convention for $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-forms,
> > $$[\theta \wedge \theta](X, Y) = [\theta(X), \theta(Y)] - [\theta(Y), \theta(X)] = 2\,[\theta(X), \theta(Y)] \qquad \text{(antisymmetry of the Lie bracket).}$$
> > Hence $\tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta](X, Y) = [\theta(X), \theta(Y)] = [X(e), Y(e)]$.
> >
> > **Combine.** Adding the two computed values,
> > $$\Big(d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta]\Big)(X, Y) = -[X(e), Y(e)] + [X(e), Y(e)] = 0.$$
> > Since $(X, Y)$ ranges over a spanning set of pairs of tangent vectors at every point, the two-form $d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta]$ vanishes identically. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Lemma 2: The pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form is a principal connection on $B \times G$
> **Statement:** On the product bundle $\pi = \operatorname{pr}_1 \colon B \times G \to B$ with right action $R_g(b, g') = (b, g'g)$, the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form $\omega := \operatorname{pr}_2^* \theta \in \Omega^1(B \times G; \mathfrak{g})$ satisfies the two connection axioms: (i) $\omega(X_P) = X$ for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}$, where $X_P$ is the fundamental vector field; (ii) $R_g^* \omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \omega$ for every $g \in G$. Hence $\omega$ is a connection.
>
> **Hint:** For (i) compute $d\operatorname{pr}_2(X_P) = (dL_{g'})_e X$ and apply $\theta$. For (ii) use that $\operatorname{pr}_2$ intertwines the right action on $B \times G$ with right translation on $G$, and the equivariance $(R_g^G)^* \theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \theta$ of the Maurer–Cartan form.
>
> **Why needed:** Chern–Weil theory applies to the curvature of a *connection*; without both axioms $\omega$ has no curvature two-form in the required sense.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Write points of $P := B \times G$ as $(b, g')$ with $b \in B$, $g' \in G$. Denote by $R_g^G \colon G \to G$, $R_g^G(g') = g'g$, right translation on $G$; then $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ R_g = R_g^G \circ \operatorname{pr}_2$, since $\operatorname{pr}_2(R_g(b, g')) = \operatorname{pr}_2(b, g'g) = g'g = R_g^G(\operatorname{pr}_2(b, g'))$.
> >
> > **Axiom (i): the fundamental-field normalisation.** Fix $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. The fundamental vector field at $(b, g')$ is
> > $$X_P(b, g') = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0} (b, g') \cdot \exp(tX) = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0} \big(b,\, g'\exp(tX)\big) = \Big(0,\; \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0\, g'\exp(tX)\Big) = \big(0,\, (dL_{g'})_e X\big),$$
> > using that the $B$-component is constant and that $t \mapsto g'\exp(tX) = L_{g'}(\exp(tX))$ has velocity $(dL_{g'})_e \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \exp(tX) = (dL_{g'})_e X$ at $t = 0$. Pushing forward by $\operatorname{pr}_2$ discards the (zero) $B$-component: $d\operatorname{pr}_2(X_P(b,g')) = (dL_{g'})_e X$. Therefore
> > $$\omega(X_P)(b, g') = (\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta)(X_P) = \theta_{g'}\big(d\operatorname{pr}_2(X_P)\big) = \theta_{g'}\big((dL_{g'})_e X\big) = (dL_{g'^{-1}})_{g'}(dL_{g'})_e X = X,$$
> > where the last two equalities are the definition of $\theta$ and $L_{g'^{-1}} \circ L_{g'} = \operatorname{id}$. So $\omega(X_P) = X$ identically.
> >
> > **Axiom (ii): equivariance.** We first record the transformation law of the Maurer–Cartan form under right translation. For $g \in G$, $g' \in G$, and $v \in T_{g'}G$,
> > $$\big((R_g^G)^*\theta\big)_{g'}(v) = \theta_{g'g}\big((dR_g^G)_{g'} v\big) = (dL_{(g'g)^{-1}})_{g'g}(dR_g^G)_{g'} v = (dL_{g^{-1}})(dL_{g'^{-1}})(dR_g^G)\, v,$$
> > using $(g'g)^{-1} = g^{-1}g'^{-1}$ and the chain rule $dL_{g^{-1}g'^{-1}} = dL_{g^{-1}} \circ dL_{g'^{-1}}$. Left and right translations commute, $L_{g'^{-1}} \circ R_g^G = R_g^G \circ L_{g'^{-1}}$, hence $dL_{g'^{-1}} \circ dR_g^G = dR_g^G \circ dL_{g'^{-1}}$, so the expression becomes
> > $$(dL_{g^{-1}})(dR_g^G)\big((dL_{g'^{-1}}) v\big) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\big((dL_{g'^{-1}}) v\big) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\big(\theta_{g'}(v)\big),$$
> > where we used that $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} = d(L_{g^{-1}} \circ R_g^G)_e$ acting on $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$ — the differential at $e$ of conjugation $h \mapsto g^{-1} h g = L_{g^{-1}}(R_g^G(h))$ — and that $(dL_{g'^{-1}})v = \theta_{g'}(v) \in \mathfrak{g}$. Thus $(R_g^G)^*\theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \theta$.
> >
> > Now pull back. Using $\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ R_g = R_g^G \circ \operatorname{pr}_2$ and naturality of pullback,
> > $$R_g^*\omega = R_g^*\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta = (\operatorname{pr}_2 \circ R_g)^*\theta = (R_g^G \circ \operatorname{pr}_2)^*\theta = \operatorname{pr}_2^*\big((R_g^G)^*\theta\big) = \operatorname{pr}_2^*\big(\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\theta\big) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega,$$
> > where the penultimate equality holds because $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$ is a fixed linear endomorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$ and therefore commutes with pullback of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms. Both axioms hold, so $\omega$ is a connection on $B \times G$. $\blacksquare$
> >
> > *(This corrects the two bookkeeping slips flagged in the content map: Bär's chain for property 1 mislabels the group element as $g' \cdot g^{-1}$, and writes $\phi^*\omega$ for what should be $\operatorname{pr}_2^*$; the corrected law is $(R_g^G)^*\theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\theta$ as derived above.)*

> [!note]- Lemma 3: The product connection is flat
> **Statement:** The connection $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^* \theta$ on $B \times G$ of Lemma 2 has curvature $\Omega = 0$; it is a flat connection.
>
> **Hint:** Pullback commutes with $d$ and with the bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms, so the curvature is $\operatorname{pr}_2^*$ of the left-hand side of the Maurer–Cartan equation.
>
> **Why needed:** It supplies the flat connection whose Chern–Weil form is manifestly zero.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > By the structure equation ([[Thm - Structure Equation for the Curvature|structure equation for the curvature]]), the curvature of $\omega$ is $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac{1}{2}[\omega \wedge \omega]$. Pullback of forms commutes with the exterior derivative, $d(\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta) = \operatorname{pr}_2^*(d\theta)$, and with the pointwise Lie bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms, $[\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta \wedge \operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta] = \operatorname{pr}_2^*[\theta \wedge \theta]$ (the bracket is applied fibrewise to the $\mathfrak{g}$-values while the form parts are pulled back). Therefore
> > $$\Omega = d(\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta) + \tfrac{1}{2}[\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta \wedge \operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta] = \operatorname{pr}_2^*\Big(d\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\theta \wedge \theta]\Big) = \operatorname{pr}_2^*(0) = 0,$$
> > where the third equality is the Maurer–Cartan equation (Lemma 1). Hence $\Omega = 0$ and $\omega$ is flat. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Lemma 4: A positive-degree invariant polynomial applied to zero curvature gives the zero form
> **Statement:** Let $\omega$ be a connection on a principal $G$-bundle $P \to B$ with curvature $\Omega = 0$, and let $p$ be an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial of degree $d \geq 1$ with polarisation $\lambda$. Then the Chern–Weil form $p(F_\omega) \in \Omega^{2d}(B; \mathbb{K})$ is identically zero, and consequently $c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)] = 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$.
>
> **Hint:** Write the Chern–Weil form in a local frame of $\mathfrak{g}$; every monomial carries $d \geq 1$ curvature factors, each of which is zero.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the arithmetic core: it converts "no curvature" into "zero form", and it is exactly where the degree hypothesis $d \geq 1$ is used.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Fix a basis $(Y_1, \dots, Y_r)$ of $\mathfrak{g}$, where $r = \dim \mathfrak{g}$. Over a trivialising open set $U \subseteq B$ choose a local section $s \colon U \to P$; the local curvature form is $\Omega_U := s^* \Omega \in \Omega^2(U; \mathfrak{g})$, and since $\Omega = 0$ on $P$ we have $\Omega_U = s^*0 = 0$. Expand $\Omega_U$ in the basis: $\Omega_U = \sum_{j=1}^{r} \Omega_U^{\,j}\, Y_j$ with scalar two-forms $\Omega_U^{\,j} \in \Omega^2(U; \mathbb{K})$; because $\Omega_U = 0$ and the $Y_j$ are linearly independent, each component vanishes, $\Omega_U^{\,j} = 0$ for all $j$.
> >
> > By the basis formula for the Chern–Weil form ([[Def - Chern-Weil Form of an Invariant Polynomial|Chern–Weil form of an invariant polynomial]]), on $U$
> > $$p(F_\omega)\big|_U = \lambda(\Omega_U, \dots, \Omega_U) = \sum_{j_1, \dots, j_d = 1}^{r} \Omega_U^{\,j_1} \wedge \dots \wedge \Omega_U^{\,j_d}\; \lambda(Y_{j_1}, \dots, Y_{j_d}),$$
> > a sum of $2d$-forms, each summand a product of exactly $d$ scalar curvature two-forms $\Omega_U^{\,j_i}$ times the constant $\lambda(Y_{j_1}, \dots, Y_{j_d}) \in \mathbb{K}$. Since $d \geq 1$, every summand contains at least one factor $\Omega_U^{\,j_1} = 0$, so every summand is the zero form; hence $p(F_\omega)|_U = 0$. As $U$ ranges over a trivialising cover of $B$ and the local expressions agree on overlaps (they descend from the globally defined basic form on $P$), $p(F_\omega) = 0$ on all of $B$.
> >
> > Therefore $c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)] = [0] = 0$ in $H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$. (The class is independent of the connection by [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|the Chern–Weil theorem]]; here we computed it with the given connection $\omega$ and obtained the zero representative, so the class is zero for every connection.) The degree hypothesis is essential: for $d = 0$ the "sum" is the single constant $p = \lambda(\,) \in \mathbb{K}$, with no curvature factors, and $p(F_\omega) = p \cdot 1$ is the constant function, whose class in $H^0$ is nonzero when $p \neq 0$. $\blacksquare$

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Let $G$ be a Lie group, $B$ a smooth manifold, $p$ an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial of degree $d = \deg p \geq 1$, and $P \to B$ a principal $G$-bundle.
>
> **Step 0 — the ingredients are well-posed.** The characteristic class $c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)] \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$ is defined for any connection $\omega$ on $P$ and is independent of that choice by [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|the Chern–Weil theorem]] — *for a principal $G$-bundle $P \to B$, a connection $\omega$, and $p \in I(G)$ of degree $d$, the form $p(F_\omega)$ is closed, and for two connections $\omega_0, \omega_1$ the difference $p(F_{\omega_1}) - p(F_{\omega_0})$ is exact; hence $[p(F_\omega)]$ is independent of $\omega$*. This independence is what licenses computing $c_p(P)$ from any single convenient connection below.
>
> **Part I — a flat connection forces $c_p(P) = 0$.** Suppose $P$ admits a flat connection $\omega$, that is, a connection with curvature $\Omega = 0$ (see [[Def - Flat Connection|flat connection]]). By **Lemma 4**, applied to this connection and to the polynomial $p$ of degree $d \geq 1$, the Chern–Weil form is the zero form, $p(F_\omega) = 0$, and therefore
> $$c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)] = [0] = 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K}).$$
> This proves part 1 of the theorem.
>
> **Part II — a trivial bundle has $c_p(P) = 0$.**
>
> *Step II.0 — the product bundle carries a flat connection.* On the product bundle $B \times G$ set $\omega_0 := \operatorname{pr}_2^* \theta$, the pullback of the left Maurer–Cartan form $\theta$ of $G$. By **Lemma 2**, $\omega_0$ is a principal connection: it satisfies $\omega_0(X_P) = X$ for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $R_g^*\omega_0 = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega_0$ for every $g \in G$. By **Lemma 3**, its curvature is $\Omega_0 = \operatorname{pr}_2^*(d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]) = 0$, using the Maurer–Cartan equation (**Lemma 1**); so $\omega_0$ is flat.
>
> *Step II.1 — the product bundle has vanishing class.* Applying Part I to $B \times G$ with the flat connection $\omega_0$ gives
> $$c_p(B \times G) = 0.$$
>
> *Step II.2 — transport across the trivialisation.* Suppose now $P$ is trivial, i.e. there is an isomorphism $\phi \colon P \to B \times G$ of principal $G$-bundles over $B$ (this is the definition of triviality; equivalently, by [[Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality|the section-triviality theorem]], $P$ has a global section). By [[Thm - Naturality and Isomorphism Invariance of Characteristic Classes|isomorphism invariance of characteristic classes]] — *if $\phi \colon P \to P'$ is an isomorphism of principal $G$-bundles over $B$ then $c_p(P) = c_p(P')$ for every $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$* — we conclude
> $$c_p(P) = c_p(B \times G) = 0.$$
> This proves part 2 of the theorem. (Equivalently, one may pull the flat connection $\omega_0$ back through $\phi$ to a flat connection on $P$ and apply Part I directly; the two routes agree because curvature is natural under bundle isomorphisms.)
>
> **Part III — the contrapositive obstruction.** Suppose $c_p(P) \neq 0 \in H^{2d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(B; \mathbb{K})$ for some $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of degree $d \geq 1$. If $P$ were trivial, Part II would force $c_p(P) = 0$, a contradiction with the hypothesis $c_p(P) \neq 0$; hence $P$ is not trivial. If $P$ admitted a flat connection, Part I would force $c_p(P) = 0$, again contradicting $c_p(P) \neq 0$; hence $P$ admits no flat connection. This proves part 3.
>
> **Conclusion.** A principal $G$-bundle that admits a flat connection — in particular a trivial one — has vanishing characteristic class $c_p(P) = 0$ for every $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $p$ of positive degree; contrapositively, a nonzero such class certifies both non-triviality and the non-existence of any flat connection. $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Riemannian geometry: parallelisable manifolds have vanishing Pontryagin numbers.** A smooth manifold $M$ is parallelisable when its tangent bundle $TM$ is trivial, equivalently when its frame bundle $\operatorname{Fr}(M)$ admits a global section. The theorem then says every characteristic number of $TM$ — in particular every Pontryagin number and the Euler number — vanishes. This applies to every Lie group (left translations trivialise the tangent bundle) and to every orientable three-manifold. The exercise is non-obvious because parallelisability is a statement about vector fields while the conclusion is about curvature integrals; the bridge is that a global frame is a section of the frame bundle, hence a trivialisation, hence a flat connection exists. The reader should reconcile this with the fact that $S^2$ is *not* parallelisable, which is consistent because $\int_{S^2} e(TS^2) = 2 \neq 0$.

**Algebraic topology: flat vector bundles have rational characteristic classes zero.** A flat vector bundle is one whose structure group can be reduced to a discrete group, equivalently one carrying a connection with zero curvature. The theorem shows all its Chern (or Pontryagin) classes vanish in de Rham (real) cohomology, even though the bundle can be highly nontrivial — flat line bundles over a torus are classified by their holonomy character and are generically nonisomorphic. The exercise is to see that characteristic classes in *real* cohomology cannot detect flat bundles at all, so that the interesting invariants of flat bundles (the Cheeger–Simons or the torsion invariants) must live in finer groups; the theorem marks the exact boundary of what Chern–Weil forms can see.

**Mathematical physics: topological sectors forbid pure-gauge fields.** In a gauge theory a "pure gauge" or vacuum configuration is a flat connection, one with zero field strength $F = 0$. On a principal $SU(2)$-bundle $P \to X$ over a closed four-manifold with instanton number $k(P) = \int_X c_2(P) \neq 0$, the theorem shows no flat connection exists, so the field strength cannot be gauged away: the topological sector is genuinely non-vacuum, and the Yang–Mills energy is bounded below by a positive multiple of $|k|$. The exercise is to combine the theorem with the energy identity to derive this bound, and to explain physically why a nonzero winding number obstructs the vacuum.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|The Chern–Weil theorem]]** — the indispensable input. It is the theorem that $c_p(P) = [p(F_\omega)]$ is a well-defined bundle invariant, independent of the connection. The present theorem is its first non-trivial consequence: it computes that invariant to be zero on the class of bundles whose curvature can be switched off, by exhibiting one connection (the flat one) whose Chern–Weil representative is literally zero and invoking connection-independence to spread the answer to all connections.

- **[[Thm - Naturality and Isomorphism Invariance of Characteristic Classes|Naturality and isomorphism invariance]]** — the transport mechanism. It reduces a general trivial bundle to the concrete product $B \times G$: since $c_p$ depends only on the isomorphism class, and a trivial bundle is by definition isomorphic to the product, computing on the product suffices. Naturality under pullback additionally gives a second proof of the trivial case: a trivial bundle is the pullback of a bundle over a point, and $c_p$ of any bundle over a point is zero for degree reasons.

- **[[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|The Maurer–Cartan form]] and the Maurer–Cartan equation** — the source of the flat connection. The construction $\omega = \operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$ is the canonical flat connection on the product bundle, and its flatness is the Maurer–Cartan equation $d\theta + \tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta] = 0$, itself the infinitesimal form of the associativity of group multiplication. The bridge to remember is that the "no-twisting" connection on a product exists because the group supplies a canonical identification of nearby fibres, encoded in $\theta$.

- **[[Def - Flat Connection|Flat connections]] and [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|monodromy]]** — the sharper geometric picture. A flat connection has holonomy factoring through a representation $\pi_1(B) \to G$; over a simply connected base this representation is trivial and the bundle is forced to be trivial, so "flat" and "trivial" coincide there. Over a base with fundamental group the two diverge, and the theorem's part 1 is the strictly stronger statement, valid for all flat bundles regardless of monodromy.

- **[[Ex - The Tangent Bundle of S^2 is Nontrivial via Chern Classes|Nontriviality of $TS^2$]] and [[Ex - The Chern Number of the Hopf Line Bundle over CP^1 is Minus One|the Hopf bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^1$]]** — the first applications of the contrapositive. Each computes a single nonzero characteristic number by Stokes' theorem from a local connection and then invokes part 3 of this theorem to conclude non-triviality; the tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^1$ with $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1 \neq 0$ and the tangent bundle $TS^2$ with $\int_{S^2} c_1(TS^2) = 2 \neq 0$ are the model cases.

- **[[Thm - Axioms and Properties of Chern Classes|Axioms and properties of Chern classes]]** — the axiomatic home. The vanishing on trivial bundles is one of the standard Chern-class axioms (normalisation of the trivial bundle); the present theorem proves that axiom for the Chern–Weil construction, and its companion — additivity under Whitney sums — combines with it to give the general statement that $c_j(E) = 0$ above the rank of the non-trivial part of $E$.
