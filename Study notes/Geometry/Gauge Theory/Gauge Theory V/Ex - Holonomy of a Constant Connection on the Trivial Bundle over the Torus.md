# Problem Statement

Let $T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^2$ be the flat two-torus, with angular coordinates $x,y\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ inherited from $\mathbb{R}^2$, and let $P=T^2\times U(1)$ be the trivial principal $U(1)$-bundle over it, with its global section $s_0(m)=(m,1)$. Fix two real constants $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and consider the connection whose local connection form in the global section $s_0$ is the constant-coefficient one-form
$$A=i\,(a\,dx+b\,dy)\in\Omega^1\!\big(T^2;\,i\mathbb{R}\big),\qquad i\mathbb{R}=\mathfrak{u}(1).$$
(Here $dx$ and $dy$ are the global closed one-forms on $T^2$ descending from the coordinate differentials on $\mathbb{R}^2$; they are well defined because $a,b$ are constant.)

Let $c_1$ and $c_2$ be the two **generating loops** of the torus based at the image $m_0$ of the origin,
$$c_1(s)=(s,0),\qquad c_2(s)=(0,s),\qquad s\in[0,2\pi],$$
the first winding once in the $x$-direction and the second once in the $y$-direction.

**Establish the following.**

1. The curvature of the connection vanishes: $F_A=0$, so the connection is flat.
2. The holonomies around the generating loops are
$$\operatorname{hol}(c_1)=e^{-2\pi i a},\qquad \operatorname{hol}(c_2)=e^{-2\pi i b}.$$
3. The holonomy around a loop in the homotopy class $(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2=\pi_1(T^2,m_0)$ is $\operatorname{hol}=e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}$, depending only on the class; consequently the **holonomy group** is
$$\operatorname{Hol}(A)=\big\langle\, e^{-2\pi i a},\ e^{-2\pi i b}\,\big\rangle=\big\{\,e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\big\}\ \le\ U(1),$$
the subgroup of $U(1)$ generated by the two boundary holonomies.
4. Determine the structure of $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$: it is a finite cyclic group precisely when $a$ and $b$ are both rational (of order $\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$ when $a=p_1/q_1$, $b=p_2/q_2$ in lowest terms), and otherwise it is an infinite subgroup dense in $U(1)$ — in particular not closed.

**Recall:**

![[Def - Holonomy Group of a Connection#The Definition]]

The computation is routed entirely through the abelian holonomy formula, whose statement is:

![[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral#Statement]]

The point we exploit is its *line-integral* form: for an abelian group and **any** loop $c$ lying in a single trivialising set $U_\alpha$ (no bounding surface required), $\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\big(-\oint_c A_\alpha\big)$. For the trivial bundle here the whole torus is one trivialising set, so the formula applies to every loop, including the non-contractible generators that bound nothing.

> [!warning] Convention: the period of the torus and the sign
> We take $T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^2$, so each generating loop has length $2\pi$; this is the normalisation under which the boundary holonomies come out as $e^{-2\pi ia}$, $e^{-2\pi ib}$ for the connection form $A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$. Had we used $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ instead, the same holonomies would require $A=2\pi i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$. The minus sign in $\operatorname{hol}=\exp(-\oint A)$ is the series convention, inherited from the transport equation $\dot v=-A(\dot c)v$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *compute-the-holonomy-of-a-flat-abelian-connection* problem on a space with interesting fundamental group. The bundle is trivial and the connection form is a constant, so nothing local happens — the curvature is zero and every contractible loop has trivial holonomy. All of the content lives in the *global* topology: the non-contractible loops of the torus, around which the flat connection can still register a nontrivial phase. The end product is not a single number but a *subgroup* of $U(1)$, and characterising that subgroup is a small piece of the arithmetic of the circle.

**Assumption pattern.** Three features are used in turn. The group $U(1)$ is **abelian**, so [[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral|the holonomy formula]] applies and there is no ordering to track. The connection form $A$ is **closed** ($dA=0$, because its coefficients are constant), so the line integral $\oint_c A$ depends only on the homology class of $c$; on the torus homology and homotopy coincide ($\pi_1$ is already abelian), so the holonomy is a genuine function of the homotopy class. And the bundle is **globally trivial**, so the whole torus is a single trivialising set and the line-integral form of the formula is available for the non-bounding generators.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: (i) compute $F_A=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A]=0$ directly, using constancy of the coefficients and abelianness; (ii) apply the line-integral abelian holonomy formula to $c_1,c_2$ to get $e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib}$; (iii) extend to a general class $(m,n)$ by additivity of the line integral of a closed form over cycles, using $\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ from [[Ex - Pi_1 of the Torus is Z Squared|the torus fundamental-group exercise]]; (iv) analyse the subgroup $\langle e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib}\rangle$ of $U(1)$ by the rational/irrational dichotomy for cyclic subgroups of the circle.

**Key decision point.** The one genuine decision is to compute the holonomy of the generators through the *line-integral* form of the formula rather than the surface form. The generators do not bound any surface in $T^2$ — that is exactly what makes them non-contractible — so the familiar "flux through the enclosed cap" reading is unavailable; the phase they carry is not curvature (which is zero) but the period of the flat potential $A$ around a non-trivial cycle. Recognising that a flat connection can nonetheless have nontrivial holonomy, and that the invariant recording it is the collection of periods $\oint_{c}A$, is the conceptual crux and the doorway to the classification of flat bundles by representations of $\pi_1$.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Compute curvature of an abelian connection as $dA$ alone.** For an abelian structure group the bracket term $\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$ vanishes, so $F_A=dA$; with constant coefficients $dA=0$. This is the operation "reduce the structure equation in the abelian case", applied on [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection|the curvature page]].

2. **Apply the line-integral form of the abelian holonomy formula to a non-bounding loop.** Since the whole torus trivialises the bundle, $\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\big(-\oint_c A\big)$ for every loop $c$, whether or not it bounds; this is the operation from [[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral|the abelian holonomy page]].

3. **Use closedness to make the period a homology invariant.** Because $dA=0$, if $c$ and $c'$ are homologous then $\oint_c A=\oint_{c'}A$ (their difference bounds and $\int dA=0$); hence the holonomy descends to a homomorphism $\pi_1(T^2)\to U(1)$, evaluated on a class $(m,n)$ by $m\oint_{c_1}A+n\oint_{c_2}A$.

4. **Classify a two-generator subgroup of $U(1)$ by the rational/irrational dichotomy.** A subgroup of the circle is finite cyclic or dense; deciding which for $\langle e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib}\rangle$ reduces to whether $a,b$ are rational, using the closed-subgroups-of-$\mathbb{R}$ lemma.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> The structure group is abelian and the coefficients of $A$ are constants. What is the structure equation $F=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$ in this case? Both terms should be easy to kill.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> The bundle is trivial, so the *entire* torus is a single trivialising set. This means you may use the line-integral form $\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\big(-\oint_c A\big)$ for *any* loop, even the non-contractible generators. Integrate $A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$ along $c_1(s)=(s,0)$ and $c_2(s)=(0,s)$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For a general loop, note $A$ is closed. The integral of a closed one-form over a cycle depends only on the homology class, and equals a $\mathbb{Z}$-combination of its integrals over the generators. Since $\pi_1(T^2)=H_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ (see [[Ex - Pi_1 of the Torus is Z Squared]]), a class $(m,n)$ gives $\oint A=2\pi i(ma+nb)$.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> The holonomy group is $\{e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$. Identify $U(1)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and ask which subgroup of $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by $a$ and $b$ modulo $1$. A subgroup of the circle is either finite cyclic or dense. Which happens depends on whether $a$ and $b$ are rational.

# Solution

The plan is to dispatch the local content first — the curvature is zero because the coefficients are constant and the group is abelian — and then to extract all the information from the global periods of the flat potential $A$. Because the bundle is trivial the abelian holonomy formula applies to every loop in its line-integral form, so the holonomy of the generators is read off immediately; closedness of $A$ upgrades this to a homomorphism on $\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$, and the image is the subgroup of $U(1)$ generated by the two boundary phases, whose structure is settled by a rational/irrational dichotomy.

**Step 1: The connection is flat.**

The curvature $F_A=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$ vanishes because $[A\wedge A]=0$ (abelian) and $dA=0$ (constant coefficients).

> [!note]- Derivation
> By the structure equation on [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection|the curvature page]], the local curvature form of the connection in the section $s_0$ is
> $$F_A=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A].$$
> The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{u}(1)=i\mathbb{R}$ is abelian, so the bracket of any two elements is zero; hence $[A\wedge A]=0$ identically (concretely, $\tfrac12[A\wedge A](X,Y)=[A(X),A(Y)]=0$). For the exterior derivative,
> $$dA=d\big(i\,a\,dx+i\,b\,dy\big)=i\,a\,d(dx)+i\,b\,d(dy)+i\,(da)\wedge dx+i\,(db)\wedge dy=0\qquad(\text{since }d(dx)=d(dy)=0\text{ and }a,b\text{ are constants, so }da=db=0).$$
> Therefore $F_A=0$: the connection is flat, and it descends to a well-defined flat connection on $P=T^2\times U(1)$ (the global form $A$ is smooth and closed on all of $T^2$).

**Step 2: Holonomy around the generating loops.**

Applying the line-integral abelian holonomy formula on the global trivialisation gives $\operatorname{hol}(c_1)=e^{-2\pi ia}$ and $\operatorname{hol}(c_2)=e^{-2\pi ib}$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> The bundle $P=T^2\times U(1)$ is globally trivial, so the whole torus is a single trivialising set $U_\alpha=T^2$ with local connection form $A_\alpha=A$. The group $U(1)$ is abelian, so by [[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral|the abelian holonomy formula]] (line-integral form, valid for any loop in $U_\alpha$),
> $$\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\!\Big(-\oint_c A\Big)\qquad\text{for every loop }c\subset T^2.$$
> Along $c_1(s)=(s,0)$, $s\in[0,2\pi]$, we have $dy=0$ and $dx=ds$, so
> $$\oint_{c_1}A=\int_0^{2\pi}i\big(a\cdot 1+b\cdot 0\big)\,ds=2\pi i a\qquad(\text{pulling back }A\text{ along }c_1),$$
> hence $\operatorname{hol}(c_1)=\exp(-2\pi i a)=e^{-2\pi i a}$. Along $c_2(s)=(0,s)$, symmetrically $dx=0$, $dy=ds$, so $\oint_{c_2}A=2\pi i b$ and $\operatorname{hol}(c_2)=e^{-2\pi i b}$. Since $U(1)$ is abelian the base point is immaterial: by the conjugation corollary on [[Def - Holonomy Group of a Connection|the holonomy page]], $\operatorname{hol}_{pg}(c)=g^{-1}\operatorname{hol}_p(c)g=\operatorname{hol}_p(c)$.

**Step 3: Holonomy of a general class, and the holonomy group.**

Closedness of $A$ makes the holonomy a homomorphism on $\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$; on the class $(m,n)$ it is $e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}$, so $\operatorname{Hol}(A)=\langle e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib}\rangle$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> By [[Ex - Pi_1 of the Torus is Z Squared|the torus fundamental-group exercise]], $\pi_1(T^2,m_0)\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by the classes $[c_1]$ and $[c_2]$; and since $\mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian it coincides with $H_1(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$. A loop $c$ in the class $(m,n)$ is homologous to the cycle $m[c_1]+n[c_2]$, so there is a smooth singular chain $\Sigma$ (a $2$-chain) with $\partial\Sigma=c-(m\,c_1+n\,c_2)$ up to reparametrisation. Because $A$ is **closed** ($dA=0$ from Step 1), [[Thm - Stokes' Theorem on Manifolds|Stokes' theorem]] — for a smooth $1$-form $\eta$ and a compact oriented $2$-chain $\Sigma$, $\int_\Sigma d\eta=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\eta$ — gives
> $$\oint_c A-\Big(m\oint_{c_1}A+n\oint_{c_2}A\Big)=\int_\Sigma dA=0\qquad(\text{Stokes, and }dA=0),$$
> so $\oint_c A=m\oint_{c_1}A+n\oint_{c_2}A=2\pi i(ma+nb)$ by Step 2. Therefore
> $$\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\!\big(-2\pi i(ma+nb)\big)=\big(e^{-2\pi ia}\big)^m\big(e^{-2\pi ib}\big)^n\qquad(\text{Step 2 and additivity of the period}).$$
> In particular the assignment $[c]\mapsto\operatorname{hol}(c)$ is a well-defined group homomorphism $\rho\colon\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2\to U(1)$, $\rho(m,n)=e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}$ — the monodromy representation of the flat connection. Its image is exactly the holonomy group,
> $$\operatorname{Hol}(A)=\rho(\mathbb{Z}^2)=\big\{\,e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\big\}=\big\langle\,e^{-2\pi ia},\ e^{-2\pi ib}\,\big\rangle,$$
> the subgroup of $U(1)$ generated by the two boundary holonomies. Every contractible loop, being the class $(0,0)$, has holonomy $e^0=1$, as flatness demands.

**Step 4: Structure of the holonomy group — rational versus irrational.**

$\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is finite cyclic if and only if $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}$ (of order $\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$ for $a=p_1/q_1$, $b=p_2/q_2$ in lowest terms); otherwise it is infinite and dense in $U(1)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Identify $U(1)$ with $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ through $e^{2\pi i\theta}\leftrightarrow\theta+\mathbb{Z}$. Under this isomorphism, $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ corresponds to the subgroup
> $$H:=\{\,-(ma+nb)+\mathbb{Z}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\}=\big(\mathbb{Z}a+\mathbb{Z}b+\mathbb{Z}\big)/\mathbb{Z}\ \le\ \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$$
> (the overall sign is irrelevant to the subgroup). Write $\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}a+\mathbb{Z}b+\mathbb{Z}\le\mathbb{R}$, so $H=\Lambda/\mathbb{Z}$.
>
> **A dichotomy for subgroups of $\mathbb{R}$.** *A subgroup $K\le\mathbb{R}$ is either dense in $\mathbb{R}$ or of the form $\alpha\mathbb{Z}$ for a unique $\alpha\ge 0$.* Indeed, if $K\ne\{0\}$ set $\alpha:=\inf\{x\in K:x>0\}$. If $\alpha=0$ then $K$ contains positive elements arbitrarily close to $0$, and their integer multiples then meet every interval, so $K$ is dense. If $\alpha>0$ then $\alpha\in K$: otherwise there would be two elements of $K$ in $(\alpha,2\alpha)$ whose positive difference lies in $(0,\alpha)\cap K$, contradicting the infimum; and then $K=\alpha\mathbb{Z}$, for any $x\in K$ has $x-\lfloor x/\alpha\rfloor\alpha\in[0,\alpha)\cap K=\{0\}$.
>
> **Apply it to $\Lambda\supseteq\mathbb{Z}$.** Since $1\in\Lambda$, $\Lambda\ne\{0\}$, so either $\Lambda$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$ or $\Lambda=\alpha\mathbb{Z}$ with $\alpha>0$. In the second case $1\in\alpha\mathbb{Z}$ forces $\alpha=1/N$ for a positive integer $N$, so $\Lambda=\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}$ and
> $$H=\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N,$$
> the group of $N$-th roots of unity — finite cyclic of order $N$, hence a closed subgroup. In the first case $\Lambda$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so its image $H$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=U(1)$; being dense and proper (a countable set) it is infinite and **not closed**, with closure all of $U(1)$.
>
> **Which case holds.** $\Lambda=\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}$ is contained in $\mathbb{Q}$, and then $a,b\in\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$; conversely if $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}$ then $\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$ is not dense (it misses every irrational), so it is of the form $\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is finite (cyclic) **iff** both $a$ and $b$ are rational. In that case, with $a=p_1/q_1$ and $b=p_2/q_2$ in lowest terms, $\mathbb{Z}\tfrac{p_1}{q_1}+\mathbb{Z}=\tfrac1{q_1}\mathbb{Z}$ (because $\gcd(p_1,q_1)=1$ gives $\mathbb{Z}p_1+\mathbb{Z}q_1=\mathbb{Z}$), and likewise for $b$, so $\Lambda=\tfrac1{q_1}\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac1{q_2}\mathbb{Z}=\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}$ with $N=\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$; thus $\operatorname{Hol}(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}/\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$. If at least one of $a,b$ is irrational, $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is dense in $U(1)$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> Let $P=T^2\times U(1)$ over $T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^2$, with connection form $A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$ in the global section, $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ constant.
>
> **Flatness.** By the structure equation, $F_A=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$. The bracket vanishes as $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ is abelian, and $dA=0$ since $a,b$ are constant. Hence $F_A=0$.
>
> **Boundary holonomies.** The bundle is trivial, so $T^2$ is a single trivialising set; as $U(1)$ is abelian, [[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral|the abelian holonomy formula]] gives $\operatorname{hol}(c)=\exp\big(-\oint_c A\big)$ for every loop. Along $c_1(s)=(s,0)$, $\oint_{c_1}A=\int_0^{2\pi}ia\,ds=2\pi ia$, so $\operatorname{hol}(c_1)=e^{-2\pi ia}$; likewise $\operatorname{hol}(c_2)=e^{-2\pi ib}$.
>
> **General class.** Since $dA=0$, the period $\oint_c A$ depends only on the homology class of $c$, and $H_1(T^2)=\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ (from [[Ex - Pi_1 of the Torus is Z Squared]]). For a class $(m,n)$, Stokes' theorem gives $\oint_c A=2\pi i(ma+nb)$, so $\operatorname{hol}(c)=e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}=(e^{-2\pi ia})^m(e^{-2\pi ib})^n$. The map $\rho\colon\mathbb{Z}^2\to U(1)$, $\rho(m,n)=e^{-2\pi i(ma+nb)}$, is a homomorphism, and
> $$\operatorname{Hol}(A)=\rho(\mathbb{Z}^2)=\big\langle e^{-2\pi ia},\,e^{-2\pi ib}\big\rangle.$$
>
> **Structure.** Under $U(1)\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is $\Lambda/\mathbb{Z}$ with $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}a+\mathbb{Z}b+\mathbb{Z}$. A subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ is dense or discrete ($=\alpha\mathbb{Z}$); as $\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\Lambda$, either $\Lambda$ is dense (so $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is dense in $U(1)$ and not closed) or $\Lambda=\tfrac1N\mathbb{Z}$ (so $\operatorname{Hol}(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}/N$ is finite cyclic). The discrete case holds iff $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}$, and then $N=\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$ for $a=p_1/q_1$, $b=p_2/q_2$ in lowest terms. Otherwise $\operatorname{Hol}(A)$ is dense. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting route: concluding trivial holonomy from flatness
> Because $F_A=0$ one is tempted to declare all holonomy trivial, "since holonomy is the flux of the curvature". This is false, and the error is to apply the *surface* form of the abelian holonomy formula, $\operatorname{hol}=\exp(-\int_S F)$, to a loop that does not bound. The generators $c_1,c_2$ are non-contractible: they bound no surface in $T^2$, so there is no $S$ to integrate over. The correct statement is that flat connections have holonomy depending only on the homotopy class (trivial on contractible loops), *not* that they have trivial holonomy; the invariant surviving on the non-contractible cycles is the period $\oint_c A$ of the flat potential, and it is generally nonzero modulo $2\pi i\mathbb{Z}$. The extra condition that *would* make the tempting conclusion correct is simple connectivity of the base: over a simply connected manifold every loop is contractible, and a flat connection is then gauge-trivial with trivial holonomy.

> [!tip] Foreshadowing: the moduli space of flat connections on the torus
> The holonomy computed here is exactly the data that classifies the flat connection up to gauge. Two constant connections $A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$ and $A'=i(a'\,dx+b'\,dy)$ are gauge equivalent precisely when $(a',b')\equiv(a,b)\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^2}$, because a gauge transformation $g=e^{i\theta}\colon T^2\to U(1)$ shifts $A$ by $g^{-1}dg=i\,d\theta$, and the globally defined shifts $d\theta$ have integer periods $\tfrac1{2\pi}\oint d\theta\in\mathbb{Z}$ around each generator. Thus the pair of boundary holonomies $(e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib})\in U(1)^2$ is a complete gauge invariant, and the moduli space of flat $U(1)$-connections on the torus is $U(1)^2\cong T^2$. This is the monodromy correspondence $\mathcal{M}^\flat_{U(1)}(T^2)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(T^2),U(1))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^2,U(1))=U(1)^2$, worked out in full in [[Ex - Flat U(1)-Connections on the Torus]].

# Key Takeaways

**A flat connection can have nontrivial holonomy; the invariant is the period, not the curvature.** The instinct that "zero curvature means trivial parallel transport" is correct only for contractible loops. On a space with nontrivial fundamental group a flat connection stores its content in the holonomies of the non-contractible cycles, and these are computed not as fluxes — there is nothing to integrate over — but as the periods $\oint_c A$ of the flat potential around the generators. The trigger for this pattern is a flat connection on a base that is not simply connected; the diagnostic is that the holonomy factors through $\pi_1$ (indeed through $H_1$, when the group is abelian) as the monodromy representation. This is the seed of the entire correspondence between flat connections and representations of the fundamental group, and of gauge-theoretic quantities like the Aharonov–Bohm phase, which is precisely such a period around a solenoid the field cannot penetrate.

**The whole-space trivialisation is what lets the line-integral formula reach non-bounding loops.** The surface form $\exp(-\int_S F)$ of the abelian holonomy formula is the one usually quoted, but it silently assumes the loop bounds a surface inside a trivialising set. On the trivial bundle the trivialising set is everything, so the line-integral form $\exp(-\oint_c A)$ is available for *every* loop, and it is the only form that survives for the generators of the torus. The reusable principle: when a bundle is globally trivial, holonomy is just the exponential of the potential's period, and topology enters only through which loops are available; when the bundle is nontrivial one must patch potentials across trivialising sets and the mismatch is a characteristic number, as in the companion Hopf computation [[Ex - Holonomy of the Hopf Connection around a Latitude Circle]].

**Whether the holonomy group is closed is an arithmetic accident of the connection.** The holonomy group of this flat connection is finite cyclic or dense in $U(1)$ according as $a,b$ are rational or not — the same dichotomy that governs whether a line of irrational slope wraps the torus into a closed curve or an everywhere-dense winding. This is the concrete example behind the scope remark on [[Def - Holonomy Group of a Connection|the holonomy page]] that the holonomy group is *not* in general a closed (Lie) subgroup: for irrational $a$ it is a countable dense subgroup, genuinely not a submanifold. The transferable diagnostic is that dense holonomy is the norm, not the exception, for flat connections with irrational periods; taking the closure recovers a compact subgroup (here all of $U(1)$), which is the restricted-holonomy or Zariski-closure object that the structure theorems actually control. The rationality threshold — cyclic of order $\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,q_2)$ on one side, dense on the other — is a clean rehearsal of Kronecker's theorem on the equidistribution of $\{n\alpha\}$ for irrational $\alpha$.

**Companion exercises.** This flat, non-bounding computation is the natural foil to [[Ex - Holonomy of the Hopf Connection around a Latitude Circle]], where curvature is nonzero and the loop bounds a cap, and it is completed by [[Ex - Flat U(1)-Connections on the Torus]], which uses the same boundary holonomies to identify the moduli space of flat connections with $U(1)^2$. Together they show the two ways a $U(1)$-holonomy can be nontrivial: through curvature enclosed by a bounding loop, and through the period of a flat potential around a non-bounding cycle.
