# Problem Statement

Let $P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with a connection $\omega \in \mathcal{A}(P)$, where $\mathcal{A}(P) \subset \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g})$ is the space of principal connection forms and $\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$ is the Lie algebra of $G$. The gauge group $\mathcal{G}(P)$ acts on $\mathcal{A}(P)$ on the right by pullback, $\omega \cdot f := f^* \omega$. Fix a section $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ of the adjoint bundle $\operatorname{ad} P = P \times_{\operatorname{Ad}} \mathfrak{g}$, and let $\hat{\xi} \colon P \to \mathfrak{g}$ be the corresponding $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant function, so that $\hat{\xi}(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \hat{\xi}(p)$ for all $p \in P$, $g \in G$. Let
$$f_t(p) := p \cdot \exp\!\big(t\,\hat{\xi}(p)\big), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R},$$
be the one-parameter family of gauge transformations generated by $\xi$.

Show that the infinitesimal gauge action is the exterior covariant derivative:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f_t^* \omega \;=\; \widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega} \xi} \;\in\; \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g}), \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \xi \;\longmapsto\; d^{\nabla_\omega} \xi \in \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P),$$
where $d^{\nabla_\omega}$ is the exterior covariant derivative induced on $\operatorname{ad} P$ by $\omega$, and $\widehat{(\cdot)}$ is the equivariant-function representative on $P$. Then verify the result on the trivial $U(1)$-bundle, where it reduces to $\xi \mapsto d\xi$.

This is Haydys, Exercise 63 (item A-X2.2.11). The source prints the codomain of the map as $\Omega^0(\operatorname{ad} P)$; this is a typo, corrected here to $\Omega^1(\operatorname{ad} P)$, since $d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$ is a one-form. The source also records the map with a minus sign, $(\omega, \xi) \mapsto -d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$; the sign is a convention, discussed in the callout below, and the series fixes the $+$ sign that goes with the right action $\omega \cdot f = f^* \omega$ and the generator $f_t(p) = p\exp(t\hat\xi(p))$.

> [!warning] Convention: the sign of the infinitesimal action
> Haydys states the infinitesimal action as $(\omega, \xi) \mapsto -d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$. The discrepancy with the $+$ sign proved here is not a computational error but a bookkeeping choice about how a Lie algebra is attached to a **right** action. For the right action $\omega \cdot f = f^*\omega$, the assignment $\xi \mapsto \frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (\omega \cdot \exp(t\xi))$ is a Lie algebra **anti**-homomorphism (the order of a product reverses under pullback). To obtain an honest Lie algebra **homomorphism** $\Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P) \to T_\omega \mathcal{A}(P)$ one composes with $\xi \mapsto -\xi$, which flips the sign of the result to $-d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$. The series works throughout with the raw derivative of the one-parameter subgroup $f_t(p) = p\exp(t\hat\xi(p))$, which is $+d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$; this is the quantity that appears when one linearises the affine formula $A_{f^*\omega} = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} A_\omega + g^*\theta$ of the gauge-transformation theorem. Whenever the sign matters (for example in identifying the tangent space to a gauge orbit, or the linearised gauge-fixing operator $d^{\nabla_\omega,*}$), state which of the two you mean; the two differ only by an overall sign and the recipe above converts one to the other.

**Recall.** The objects in play are the finite gauge-transformation formula, the exterior covariant derivative on the adjoint bundle, the Maurer–Cartan form, and the adjoint bundle together with the equivariant-function picture of its sections.

![[Thm - Gauge Transformations Act on Connections and Curvature#Statement]]

The single piece of that theorem we differentiate is its finite formula for the transformed connection. Writing $f \in \mathcal{G}(P)$ as $f(p) = p \cdot \hat{g}(p)$ with $\hat{g} \colon P \to G$ satisfying $\hat{g}(pg) = g^{-1}\hat{g}(p)g$, the theorem proves
$$f^* \omega \;=\; \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat{g}^{-1}} \omega \;+\; \hat{g}^* \theta,$$
where $\theta \in \Omega^1(G; \mathfrak{g})$ is the left Maurer–Cartan form; in a local section this reads $A_{f^*\omega} = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} A_\omega + g^* \theta$, and for a matrix group $\operatorname{Ad}_g X = gXg^{-1}$ and $\theta = g^{-1}\,dg$, so the formula is $\hat{g}^{-1} \omega \hat{g} + \hat{g}^{-1} d\hat{g}$.

![[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form#The Definition]]

![[Def - Adjoint Bundles ad P and Ad P#The Definition]]

A section $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ is the same datum as an $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant function $\hat{\xi} \colon P \to \mathfrak{g}$, $\hat{\xi}(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat{\xi}(p)$; this is the equivariant-function description of sections of an associated bundle, and the correspondence $\xi \leftrightarrow \hat{\xi}$ is the infinitesimal shadow of the group isomorphism $\mathcal{G}(P) \cong \Gamma(\operatorname{Ad} P)$ established in [[Thm - The Gauge Group is the Space of Sections of the Adjoint Group Bundle]]. The exterior covariant derivative on the adjoint bundle, in this picture, is given on $P$ by
$$\widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega} \xi} \;=\; d\hat{\xi} \;+\; [\omega, \hat{\xi}], \qquad [\omega, \hat{\xi}](X) := [\omega(X), \hat{\xi}],$$
the horizontal, equivariant $\mathfrak{g}$-valued one-form representing $d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi \in \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ (see [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Principal Bundle]]).

Finally we use, from Lie theory, the derivative of the adjoint representation: for $X \in \mathfrak{g}$,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(tX)} \;=\; \operatorname{ad}_X \;=\; [X, \,\cdot\,],$$
proved in [[Thm - Ad is a Smooth Representation and its Differential is ad]].

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *linearisation* problem: we are handed a nonlinear group action (pullback of connection forms by gauge transformations) and asked for its derivative at the identity along a prescribed one-parameter subgroup. The universal method for such problems is to write the action in closed form, substitute the one-parameter subgroup, and differentiate term by term at $t = 0$, keeping only first-order data. The closed form is already available: it is the finite gauge-transformation law $f^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g^{-1}}\omega + \hat g^*\theta$. So the whole exercise is the differentiation of two terms, an $\operatorname{Ad}$-conjugation term and a Maurer–Cartan term, followed by recognising the sum as the exterior covariant derivative.

**Assumption pattern.** The hypotheses do exactly two jobs. First, $f_t(p) = p\exp(t\hat\xi(p))$ is the *equivariant* lift of the section $\xi$: the equivariance $\hat\xi(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat\xi(p)$ is what makes each $f_t$ a genuine gauge transformation (its $\hat g_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$ satisfies the conjugation-equivariance $\hat g_t(pg) = g^{-1}\hat g_t(p) g$, because the exponential intertwines $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$ on $\mathfrak g$ with conjugation by $g$ on $G$). Second, $\hat g_0 = \exp(0) = e$ is the identity gauge transformation, so we are differentiating at the identity of the group, which is where the finite formula becomes tractable: the conjugation factor is trivial to first order and the Maurer–Cartan factor collapses because $\hat g_0 \equiv e$ kills the zeroth-order piece.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: take the finite formula from [[Thm - Gauge Transformations Act on Connections and Curvature]]; substitute $\hat g_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$; differentiate the conjugation term using $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)} = -\operatorname{ad}_{\hat\xi}$ from [[Thm - Ad is a Smooth Representation and its Differential is ad]]; differentiate the Maurer–Cartan term $\hat g_t^*\theta$ using $\hat g_0 \equiv e$ and the definition of the Maurer–Cartan form to get $d\hat\xi$; add the two and recognise $d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi]$ as $\widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi}$ from [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Principal Bundle]].

**Key decision point.** The one genuinely non-obvious move is the differentiation of the Maurer–Cartan term. It is tempting to expand $\hat g_t^*\theta = \hat g_t^{-1}\,d\hat g_t$ (matrix case) and apply the product rule to two $t$-dependent factors; the shortcut is to notice that at $t = 0$ the factor $\hat g_0^{-1} = e$ is the identity and the factor $d\hat g_0 = d(\text{const}) = 0$ vanishes, so the product rule leaves only the term in which the derivative lands on $d\hat g_t$, giving $d\hat\xi$ and nothing else. The same collapse happens invariantly, without matrices, straight from the definition of the Maurer–Cartan form and the interchange of $d$ (on $P$) with $\frac{d}{dt}$; recognising that these two derivatives commute is the second small decision.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations, in the sense of the chapter's topic-page Legal Operations (to be reconciled by number when that page is written):

1. **Write a group action in closed form before linearising.** Do not differentiate the abstract map $f \mapsto f^*\omega$; first replace it by the explicit formula $f^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g^{-1}}\omega + \hat g^*\theta$ supplied by the gauge-transformation theorem, so that the derivative becomes an ordinary calculus computation on two named terms.

2. **Lift a section of an associated bundle to an equivariant function.** Replace the section $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ by its equivariant representative $\hat\xi \colon P \to \mathfrak g$ and the gauge transformation $f_t$ by its $G$-valued function $\hat g_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$; the computation is cleanest upstairs on $P$, and the descent to $M$ is automatic because every object produced is horizontal and equivariant.

3. **Differentiate the adjoint representation by its infinitesimal generator.** Use $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(tX)} = \operatorname{ad}_X$ to convert the conjugation term into a bracket, the operation that produces the $[\omega, \hat\xi]$ summand.

4. **Exploit the vanishing of the zeroth order at the identity.** Because $\hat g_0 \equiv e$, the product rule applied to $\hat g_t^*\theta$ retains only one term; this is the standard "linearise at the identity, where the constant part drops out" move.

5. **Interchange the base exterior derivative with the parameter derivative.** Justify $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 d\hat g_t = d\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \hat g_t\big) = d\hat\xi$ by smoothness of $(t, p) \mapsto \hat g_t(p)$, so that mixed partials commute.

6. **Recognise a horizontal equivariant form as a covariant derivative.** Identify the sum $d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi]$ with the equivariant representative of $d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$, reading the formula on the exterior-covariant-derivative page backwards.

# Solution

The plan is to substitute the one-parameter subgroup $\hat g_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$ into the finite gauge-transformation formula and differentiate at $t = 0$. The conjugation term contributes a bracket $[\omega, \hat\xi]$, by the definition of $\operatorname{ad}$ as the derivative of $\operatorname{Ad}$; the Maurer–Cartan term contributes $d\hat\xi$, because at the identity the only surviving piece of the product rule is the one differentiating the group element itself. Adding the two and matching against the exterior-covariant-derivative formula gives the claim, and the abelian $U(1)$ case is the special case in which the bracket disappears.

**Step 0: The family $f_t$ is a well-defined one-parameter subgroup of gauge transformations.**

Before differentiating we check that each $f_t$ is a gauge transformation, so that $f_t^*\omega$ genuinely lies in $\mathcal{A}(P)$ and the derivative is taken inside the correct space.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Write $\hat g_t := \exp(t\hat\xi) \colon P \to G$, so $f_t(p) = p\,\hat g_t(p)$. By [[Thm - The Gauge Group is the Space of Sections of the Adjoint Group Bundle]], a fibre-preserving map $f(p) = p\,\hat g(p)$ is a gauge transformation if and only if $\hat g \colon P \to G$ satisfies the conjugation-equivariance $\hat g(pg) = g^{-1}\hat g(p)\,g$ for all $p \in P$, $g \in G$. We verify this for $\hat g_t$.
>
> **Use equivariance of $\hat\xi$ and the intertwining of $\exp$ with $\operatorname{Ad}$.** By hypothesis $\hat\xi(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat\xi(p)$ (the equivariance defining a section of $\operatorname{ad} P$). For any $X \in \mathfrak g$ and $g \in G$ the exponential intertwines the adjoint action on the algebra with conjugation on the group,
> $$\exp\!\big(\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} X\big) \;=\; g^{-1}\,\exp(X)\,g \qquad \text{(naturality of }\exp\text{ under the conjugation automorphism } c_{g^{-1}}\text{, whose differential at } e \text{ is } \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\text{)}.$$
> Applying this with $X = t\hat\xi(p)$,
> $$\hat g_t(pg) = \exp\!\big(t\,\hat\xi(pg)\big) = \exp\!\big(t\,\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat\xi(p)\big) = \exp\!\big(\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(t\hat\xi(p))\big) = g^{-1}\exp\!\big(t\hat\xi(p)\big)\,g = g^{-1}\hat g_t(p)\,g,$$
> which is exactly the conjugation-equivariance. Hence each $f_t \in \mathcal{G}(P)$. Moreover $f_0(p) = p\exp(0) = p$, so $f_0 = \operatorname{id}$, and $(t, p) \mapsto \hat g_t(p) = \exp(t\hat\xi(p))$ is smooth in $(t, p)$ because $\hat\xi$ is smooth and $\exp$ is smooth. Therefore $f_t^*\omega \in \mathcal{A}(P)$ for every $t$, the curve $t \mapsto f_t^*\omega$ is smooth, and its derivative at $t = 0$ is a tangent vector to $\mathcal{A}(P)$ at $\omega$, that is, an element of the model space $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ lifted to a horizontal equivariant one-form on $P$.

**Step 1: Substitute the one-parameter subgroup into the finite gauge-transformation formula.**

The transformed connection is $f_t^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g_t^{-1}}\omega + \hat g_t^*\theta$, so the derivative splits as the sum of the derivatives of the two summands.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Apply the gauge-transformation theorem.** By part (c) of [[Thm - Gauge Transformations Act on Connections and Curvature]], for any $f \in \mathcal{G}(P)$ with $f(p) = p\,\hat g(p)$,
> $$f^*\omega \;=\; \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g^{-1}}\omega \;+\; \hat g^*\theta \qquad \text{(finite gauge-transformation law; } \theta \text{ the left Maurer--Cartan form of } G\text{)}.$$
> Substituting $f = f_t$, $\hat g = \hat g_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$, and using $\hat g_t^{-1} = \exp(-t\hat\xi)$,
> $$f_t^*\omega \;=\; \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\,\omega \;+\; \hat g_t^*\theta.$$
> **Split the derivative.** Both summands are smooth in $t$ (the first because $\operatorname{Ad}$ and $\exp$ are smooth; the second by Step 0), so by linearity of $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0$,
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} f_t^*\omega \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\,\omega}_{\text{(I) conjugation term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \hat g_t^*\theta}_{\text{(II) Maurer--Cartan term}}.$$
> We compute (I) in Step 2 and (II) in Step 3.

**Step 2: The conjugation term contributes the bracket $[\omega, \hat\xi]$.**

Differentiating $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\omega$ at $t = 0$ produces $-\operatorname{ad}_{\hat\xi}\omega = -[\hat\xi, \omega]$, which equals $[\omega, \hat\xi]$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> The form $\omega$ is fixed (independent of $t$), so the $t$-derivative acts only on the coefficient $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}$, a $t$-dependent linear endomorphism of $\mathfrak g$ applied pointwise to the value $\omega(X) \in \mathfrak g$ for each tangent vector $X$. Fix $p \in P$ and $X \in T_p P$; then
> $$\left(\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\,\omega\right)(X) \;=\; \left(\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi(p))}\right)\!\big(\omega(X)\big).$$
> **Invoke the differential of $\operatorname{Ad}$.** By [[Thm - Ad is a Smooth Representation and its Differential is ad]], $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(tY)} = \operatorname{ad}_Y = [Y, \,\cdot\,]$ for every $Y \in \mathfrak g$. Applying this with $Y = -\hat\xi(p)$ (the generator of the curve $t \mapsto \exp(-t\hat\xi(p))$),
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi(p))} \;=\; \operatorname{ad}_{-\hat\xi(p)} \;=\; -[\hat\xi(p), \,\cdot\,] \qquad \text{(differential of } \operatorname{Ad}\text{, chain rule for the curve } -t\hat\xi(p)\text{)}.$$
> Therefore
> $$\left(\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\,\omega\right)(X) \;=\; -[\hat\xi(p), \omega(X)] \;=\; [\omega(X), \hat\xi(p)] \qquad \text{(antisymmetry of the Lie bracket)}.$$
> By the definition of the bracket $[\omega, \hat\xi]$ of the $\mathfrak g$-valued one-form $\omega$ with the $\mathfrak g$-valued function $\hat\xi$, namely $[\omega, \hat\xi](X) = [\omega(X), \hat\xi]$, this says
> $$\text{(I)} \;=\; \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\,\omega \;=\; [\omega, \hat\xi].$$

**Step 3: The Maurer–Cartan term contributes $d\hat\xi$.**

Because $\hat g_0 \equiv e$, differentiating $\hat g_t^*\theta$ at $t = 0$ leaves only $d\hat\xi$, the exterior derivative on $P$ of the generator $\hat\xi = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \hat g_t$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Recall the left Maurer–Cartan form: for $g \in G$ and $w \in T_g G$, $\theta_g(w) = (dL_{g^{-1}})_g(w) \in \mathfrak g$, where $L_{g^{-1}}$ is left translation by $g^{-1}$ (see [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form]]). For the smooth map $\hat g_t \colon P \to G$ and a tangent vector $X \in T_p P$,
> $$(\hat g_t^*\theta)_p(X) \;=\; \theta_{\hat g_t(p)}\big((d\hat g_t)_p(X)\big) \;=\; \big(dL_{\hat g_t(p)^{-1}}\big)\big((d\hat g_t)_p(X)\big).$$
> **Evaluate the zeroth order.** At $t = 0$ we have $\hat g_0(p) = e$ for all $p$, so $\hat g_0 \colon P \to G$ is the constant map, whence $(d\hat g_0)_p = 0$ and $(\hat g_0^*\theta)_p(X) = 0$. Thus the curve $t \mapsto (\hat g_t^*\theta)_p(X)$ starts at $0$.
> **Differentiate, using $\hat g_0 = e$.** Write $F(t) := \big(dL_{\hat g_t(p)^{-1}}\big)\big((d\hat g_t)_p(X)\big)$. This is a product of two $t$-dependent quantities: the linear map $dL_{\hat g_t(p)^{-1}} \colon T_{\hat g_t(p)}G \to \mathfrak g$ and the vector $(d\hat g_t)_p(X) \in T_{\hat g_t(p)}G$. Since $(d\hat g_0)_p(X) = 0$, the product rule kills the term in which the derivative falls on the left factor:
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} F(t) \;=\; \underbrace{\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 dL_{\hat g_t(p)^{-1}}\Big)\big((d\hat g_0)_p(X)\big)}_{=\,0 \text{ since } (d\hat g_0)_p(X) = 0} \;+\; \big(dL_{\hat g_0(p)^{-1}}\big)\!\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (d\hat g_t)_p(X)\Big).$$
> Now $\hat g_0(p) = e$, so $dL_{\hat g_0(p)^{-1}} = dL_e = \operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak g}$, and the surviving term is $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (d\hat g_t)_p(X)$.
> **Interchange the two derivatives.** The map $(t, q) \mapsto \hat g_t(q) = \exp(t\hat\xi(q))$ is smooth in both variables (Step 0), so the mixed partials commute: differentiating first in the base directions (the exterior derivative $d$ on $P$) and then in $t$ gives the same result as the other order. Hence
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} (d\hat g_t)_p(X) \;=\; d\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \hat g_t\Big)_p(X) \;=\; (d\hat\xi)_p(X) \qquad \Big(\text{since } \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \hat g_t = \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \exp(t\hat\xi) = \hat\xi\Big).$$
> Combining, $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (\hat g_t^*\theta)_p(X) = (d\hat\xi)_p(X)$ for every $p$ and $X$, that is,
> $$\text{(II)} \;=\; \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} \hat g_t^*\theta \;=\; d\hat\xi.$$
> (In the matrix picture the same conclusion is immediate: $\hat g_t^*\theta = \hat g_t^{-1}\,d\hat g_t$, and at $t = 0$ the product rule gives $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0(\hat g_t^{-1}\,d\hat g_t) = (\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0\hat g_t^{-1})\,d\hat g_0 + \hat g_0^{-1}\,d(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0\hat g_t) = 0 + d\hat\xi$, using $d\hat g_0 = 0$ and $\hat g_0^{-1} = e$.)

**Step 4: Add the two terms and identify the exterior covariant derivative.**

Summing (I) and (II) gives $d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi]$, which is precisely the equivariant representative of $d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> **Combine Steps 2 and 3.** From Step 1,
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} f_t^*\omega \;=\; \text{(I)} + \text{(II)} \;=\; [\omega, \hat\xi] + d\hat\xi \;=\; d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi].$$
> **Match against the covariant derivative.** By [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Principal Bundle]], the exterior covariant derivative $d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi \in \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$ of a section $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ is represented on $P$ by the horizontal, $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant $\mathfrak g$-valued one-form
> $$\widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi} \;=\; d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi].$$
> The two expressions are literally equal, so
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} f_t^*\omega \;=\; \widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi},$$
> and descending to the base along the equivariant-function correspondence, the infinitesimal gauge action is
> $$\xi \;\longmapsto\; d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi \;\in\; \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P).$$
> This is the corrected statement of A-X2.2.11: the codomain is $\Omega^1(\operatorname{ad} P)$ (not $\Omega^0$), and the sign is $+$ under the series convention (see the Convention callout for the $-$ sign of the source).

**Step 5: Verify on the trivial $U(1)$-bundle.**

For $G = U(1)$ the adjoint action is trivial and every bracket vanishes, so the infinitesimal action collapses to $\xi \mapsto d\xi$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Take $G = U(1)$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak{u}(1) = i\mathbb R$, and the trivial bundle $P = M \times U(1)$. Because $U(1)$ is abelian, $\operatorname{Ad}_g = \operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak g}$ for every $g$, and hence:
> - the adjoint bundle is trivial, $\operatorname{ad} P = M \times i\mathbb R$, and a section $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ is just a smooth function $\xi \colon M \to i\mathbb R$; its equivariant representative $\hat\xi$ is $U(1)$-invariant, hence $\hat\xi = \xi \circ \pi$;
> - the Lie bracket on $i\mathbb R$ is identically zero, so $[\omega, \hat\xi] = 0$.
>
> Substituting into the result of Step 4,
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} f_t^*\omega \;=\; d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi] \;=\; d\hat\xi + 0 \;=\; d\hat\xi \qquad (\text{abelian: bracket vanishes}),$$
> which descends to $\xi \mapsto d\xi \in \Omega^1(M; i\mathbb R) = \Omega^1(M) \otimes i\mathbb R$. Concretely, if we write a $U(1)$-connection on the trivial bundle as $\omega = \theta + \pi^* a$ with $a \in \Omega^1(M; i\mathbb R)$ the gauge potential and $\theta$ the Maurer–Cartan form of $U(1)$, and gauge-transform by $f_t$ corresponding to $u_t = \exp(t\xi) \colon M \to U(1)$, the potential transforms by $a \mapsto a + u_t^{-1} du_t = a + t\,d\xi + O(t^2)$ (the $\operatorname{Ad}$-conjugation term is absent because $U(1)$ is abelian), so $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 a = d\xi$. This recovers the familiar electromagnetic gauge transformation $a \mapsto a + d\lambda$ in its infinitesimal form, confirming the general formula in the simplest case.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** Let $P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with connection $\omega \in \mathcal{A}(P)$, let $\xi \in \Gamma(\operatorname{ad} P)$ with equivariant representative $\hat\xi \colon P \to \mathfrak g$, and let $f_t(p) = p\exp(t\hat\xi(p))$. Then $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 f_t^*\omega = \widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi} = d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi]$; equivalently the infinitesimal gauge action is $\xi \mapsto d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi \in \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$.
>
> *Proof.* Write $\hat g_t := \exp(t\hat\xi) \colon P \to G$, so $f_t(p) = p\,\hat g_t(p)$.
>
> **Well-posedness.** For $g \in G$ the exponential satisfies $\exp(\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}X) = g^{-1}\exp(X)g$; with $X = t\hat\xi(p)$ and the equivariance $\hat\xi(pg) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\hat\xi(p)$ this gives $\hat g_t(pg) = g^{-1}\hat g_t(p)g$, so by [[Thm - The Gauge Group is the Space of Sections of the Adjoint Group Bundle]] each $f_t$ is a gauge transformation, $f_0 = \operatorname{id}$, and $(t,p) \mapsto \hat g_t(p)$ is smooth; hence $t \mapsto f_t^*\omega$ is a smooth curve in $\mathcal{A}(P)$.
>
> **The finite law.** By part (c) of [[Thm - Gauge Transformations Act on Connections and Curvature]], $f_t^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g_t^{-1}}\omega + \hat g_t^*\theta = \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\omega + \hat g_t^*\theta$, where $\theta$ is the left Maurer–Cartan form. Differentiating at $t = 0$ splits into two terms.
>
> **Conjugation term.** For $X \in T_pP$, $\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi)}\omega\big)(X) = \big(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-t\hat\xi(p))}\big)(\omega(X)) = \operatorname{ad}_{-\hat\xi(p)}(\omega(X)) = -[\hat\xi(p), \omega(X)] = [\omega(X), \hat\xi(p)]$, using [[Thm - Ad is a Smooth Representation and its Differential is ad]] and antisymmetry. Hence this term is $[\omega, \hat\xi]$.
>
> **Maurer–Cartan term.** With $(\hat g_t^*\theta)_p(X) = (dL_{\hat g_t(p)^{-1}})((d\hat g_t)_p(X))$: since $\hat g_0 \equiv e$ we have $(d\hat g_0)_p(X) = 0$ and $dL_{\hat g_0(p)^{-1}} = \operatorname{id}$, so the product rule leaves $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (\hat g_t^*\theta)_p(X) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 (d\hat g_t)_p(X) = d\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 \hat g_t\big)_p(X) = (d\hat\xi)_p(X)$, the interchange of $d$ and $\frac{d}{dt}$ being valid by smoothness of $(t,p) \mapsto \hat g_t(p)$. Hence this term is $d\hat\xi$.
>
> **Conclusion.** Adding, $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 f_t^*\omega = d\hat\xi + [\omega, \hat\xi]$, which by [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Principal Bundle]] equals $\widehat{d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi}$. Descending to $M$, the infinitesimal action is $\xi \mapsto d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$. For $G = U(1)$, abelianness forces $[\omega, \hat\xi] = 0$, giving $\xi \mapsto d\xi$. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting: differentiating the local matrix formula and forgetting equivariance
> One is tempted to run the whole computation in a single local trivialisation, using $A_{f^*\omega} = g^{-1}Ag + g^{-1}dg$ with $g = \exp(t\eta)$ for an arbitrary $\mathfrak g$-valued function $\eta \colon U \to \mathfrak g$, obtaining $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0 A_{f^*\omega} = dA\text{-piece} = d\eta + [A, \eta] = d^{\nabla_A}\eta$ on $U$. This local computation is correct, but it does **not** by itself prove the global statement: an arbitrary local $\eta$ need not glue to a global section of $\operatorname{ad} P$, and the result $d^{\nabla_A}\eta$ is a local $\operatorname{ad} P$-valued one-form only when $\eta$ transforms as a section, that is, when the various $\eta$'s on overlaps are related by $\eta' = \operatorname{Ad}_{g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}}\eta$. The global gauge transformation $f_t \in \mathcal{G}(P)$ automatically supplies a globally defined equivariant $\hat\xi$, which is why the invariant computation on $P$ (Steps 1–4) is the honest proof; the local formula is legitimate only as its coordinate shadow, valid on each patch and consistent across overlaps precisely because $\hat\xi$ is global.

# Key Takeaways

**Linearising a group action at the identity means substituting a one-parameter subgroup into the finite action law and reading off the first-order term.** The entire exercise is an instance of one reusable move: given a nonlinear action of a group on a space, its infinitesimal action (the fundamental vector field, or the derivative of the orbit map at the identity) is computed by writing the action in closed form, plugging in $g_t = \exp(t\xi)$, and differentiating at $t = 0$. Here the closed form is the gauge-transformation law $f^*\omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat g^{-1}}\omega + \hat g^*\theta$, and the two summands linearise to a bracket and an exterior derivative. The trigger for this method is any phrase of the form "infinitesimal action", "linearisation at a fixed point", or "tangent space to an orbit": whenever you see it, look for the finite formula and differentiate a one-parameter subgroup through it. The transferable diagnostic is that the answer is always first order in the generator and lands in the tangent space of the acted-on manifold; here that tangent space is $\Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$, the model space of the affine space of connections, which is exactly where a covariant derivative of a section of $\operatorname{ad} P$ lives.

**The exterior covariant derivative $d^{\nabla_\omega} = d + [\omega, \,\cdot\,]$ is the infinitesimal generator of gauge orbits, and this is why gauge theory is a theory of that operator.** The identity proved here says that the tangent space to the gauge orbit through $\omega$ is exactly the image of $d^{\nabla_\omega} \colon \Omega^0(M; \operatorname{ad} P) \to \Omega^1(M; \operatorname{ad} P)$. Everything downstream in the geometry of the space $\mathcal{A}(P)/\mathcal{G}(P)$ of connections modulo gauge inherits from this one fact: the normal space to the orbit (the "gauge slice") is the kernel of the formal adjoint $d^{\nabla_\omega, *}$, the Coulomb gauge-fixing condition $d^{\nabla_\omega, *}(\omega' - \omega) = 0$ is the statement of transversality to the orbit, and the linearised operator whose cohomology computes the local structure of instanton and Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces is a deformation complex built from $d^{\nabla_\omega}$ at one end. The recurring pattern to store: *orbit directions are exact* (they are $d^{\nabla_\omega}$ of something), so *gauge-invariant transverse directions are co-closed*; this dichotomy, exact versus co-closed, is the Hodge-theoretic backbone of every moduli-space calculation in the later chapters.

**Signs in a right action carry information; keep the action convention and the generator convention welded together.** The exercise's one genuine subtlety is the sign, and its resolution is a lesson that recurs whenever a group acts on the right. The raw derivative of the right action $\omega \cdot f = f^*\omega$ along $f_t = \exp(t\hat\xi)$ is $+d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$; the source's $-d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$ is the value one gets after inserting the sign flip that turns the anti-homomorphism $\xi \mapsto \frac{d}{dt}\big|_0(\omega \cdot \exp(t\xi))$ into a homomorphism. Neither is wrong; they answer subtly different questions. The diagnostic for spaced practice is: whenever a formula for an infinitesimal action disagrees with a source by a sign, check three things in order — is the action left or right, is the generator $\exp(t\xi)$ or $\exp(-t\xi)$, and is the Lie-algebra map required to be a homomorphism or merely a linear map. Fixing all three (right action, $+\exp(t\xi)$, raw derivative) pins the sign to $+d^{\nabla_\omega}\xi$, and every later page that needs the tangent-to-orbit map should quote that choice rather than re-derive it. This is a companion computation to [[Ex - The Gauge Group Homomorphism from a Representation and its Infinitesimal Version]], where the same $\exp$-and-differentiate technique produces the infinitesimal version of the representation-induced homomorphism $\gamma$.
