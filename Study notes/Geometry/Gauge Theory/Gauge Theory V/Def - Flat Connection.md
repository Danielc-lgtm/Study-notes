# Notation

Throughout, $G$ is a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=T_eG$, and $\pi\colon P\to M$ is a smooth principal $G$-bundle over a smooth manifold $M$; $G$ acts on $P$ on the right, $R_g(p)=p\cdot g$. A **connection** on $P$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $1$-form $\omega\in\Omega^1(P;\mathfrak{g})$ with $\omega(\xi_P)=\xi$ for the fundamental vector field $\xi_P$ of $\xi\in\mathfrak{g}$ and $R_g^*\omega=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega$; equivalently a $G$-invariant [[Def - Horizontal Subspace and Horizontal Lift|horizontal distribution]] $H=\ker\omega\subset TP$, so that $T_pP=H_p\oplus V_p$ with $V_p=\ker d_p\pi$ the vertical subspace (see [[Def - Connection on a Principal Bundle]]). The set of all connections on $P$ is $\mathcal{A}(P)$. Its **curvature** is $\Omega=\Omega_\omega\in\Omega^2(P;\mathfrak{g})$, given by the structure equation $\Omega=d\omega+\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]$; it is horizontal and $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant (see [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection]]). Here $[\alpha\wedge\beta]$ is the bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms, normalised so that $[\alpha\wedge\beta](X,Y)=[\alpha(X),\beta(Y)]-[\alpha(Y),\beta(X)]$ on $1$-forms, whence $\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega](X,Y)=[\omega(X),\omega(Y)]$ (see [[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket]]); for a matrix group $\tfrac12[A\wedge A]=A\wedge A$. A **local section** $s\colon U\to P$ over an open $U\subseteq M$ produces the local connection form $A_s=s^*\omega\in\Omega^1(U;\mathfrak{g})$ (the gauge potential) and the local curvature form $F_s=s^*\Omega\in\Omega^2(U;\mathfrak{g})$, related by $F_s=dA_s+\tfrac12[A_s\wedge A_s]$ (see [[Thm - Structure Equation for the Curvature]]).

For a vector bundle $E\to M$ of rank $k$ with a [[Def - Connection on a Vector Bundle|covariant derivative]] $\nabla\colon\Gamma(E)\to\Omega^1(M;E)$, the **curvature** is $F_\nabla=d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla\in\Omega^2(M;\operatorname{End}E)$, satisfying $F_\nabla(X,Y)s=\nabla_X\nabla_Y s-\nabla_Y\nabla_X s-\nabla_{[X,Y]}s$; in a local frame $e=(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ with connection matrix $A\in\Omega^1(U;\mathfrak{gl}_k)$ (so $\nabla e=e\cdot A$), one has $F=dA+A\wedge A$ (see [[Def - Curvature of a Vector-Bundle Connection]]). We write $\theta\in\Omega^1(G;\mathfrak{g})$ for the left [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|Maurer–Cartan form]], $\theta_g=d_gL_{g^{-1}}$, which for a matrix group is $\theta=g^{-1}\,dg$. The [[Def - Gauge Transformation|gauge group]] $\mathcal{G}(P)$ of $P$ is the group of $G$-equivariant diffeomorphisms of $P$ covering the identity of $M$; it acts on $\mathcal{A}(P)$ on the right by $\omega\cdot f=f^*\omega$.

This is a compound page: it fixes one notion — **vanishing curvature** — in three equivalent guises ($\Omega=0$ on a principal bundle; $F_\nabla=0$ on a vector bundle; $F_s=0$ in every local gauge), and then names the two objects the notion is invented for: the space $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ of flat connections, and the moduli space $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)=\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)/\mathcal{G}(P)$. We collect them on one page because they are inseparable: the moduli space is well posed exactly because flatness is a gauge-invariant condition, and that invariance is a one-line corollary of the definition, proved below.

# Axiom Motivation

The curvature $\Omega$ of a connection measures the failure of horizontal transport to be path-independent: to first order, carrying a fibre around a small loop and comparing with the identity produces exactly the flux of $\Omega$ through the loop (this is the content of [[Thm - Curvature is the Infinitesimal Holonomy|the infinitesimal-holonomy theorem]]). The single most natural question one can ask of a connection is therefore the extreme case: **what happens when the curvature vanishes identically?** A definition is worth making only if the class it isolates is both special enough to be rigid and common enough to be interesting, and vanishing curvature is the rare condition that is both. It is what we will call flatness, and everything in this section — parallel transport that sees only the homotopy class of a path, the identification of connections with representations of the fundamental group, the first compactness theorem of the series — flows from it.

The desideratum is that a flat connection be, locally, indistinguishable from the most trivial connection there is: the product connection $d$ (or $\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$ on a principal bundle), whose parallel transport in a fixed trivialisation is the identity. We want a pointwise, checkable, coordinate-free condition on $\omega$ that captures "no local twisting", and we want it to be exactly the obstruction to writing the connection as $d$ after a change of gauge. The infinitesimal-holonomy picture tells us what that condition must be: if the holonomy of every small loop is trivial to leading order, the curvature must vanish, and conversely. So the condition is $\Omega=0$. It is pointwise (a statement about a $2$-form), it is coordinate-free (curvature is a tensor), and — as the companion theorem [[Thm - Local Triviality of Flat Connections|on local triviality]] shows — it is precisely equivalent to the connection being the product connection in a suitable local gauge. The definition is not an arbitrary normalisation; it is forced by the requirement that "flat" mean "locally the product connection".

Why does the *condition* have to be exactly $\Omega=0$, and not something weaker or stronger? Consider dropping it to a proper subclass, for instance requiring only that the *trace* of the curvature vanish, $\operatorname{tr}F=0$ (for a $U(n)$-connection, the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$-part). This is the natural condition for an $SU(n)$-structure and it is genuinely weaker: the standard connection on the Hopf bundle, pulled into a trace-free setting, still has nonzero curvature in its $\mathfrak{su}$-part, so its holonomy is nontrivial and it is not the product connection in any gauge — the local-triviality conclusion fails. Requiring the full tensor $\Omega$ to vanish is the minimal strengthening that restores it: only when every component of the curvature dies does the horizontal distribution close up into a foliation whose leaves are local flat sections. Strengthening the condition instead — demanding, say, that $\omega$ literally equal $\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$ on all of $P$, not merely locally — would exclude most of the interesting examples: the constant connection $i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$ on the trivial $U(1)$-bundle over the torus has $\Omega=0$ everywhere yet is *not* the product connection (its holonomies $e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib}$ around the two generating loops are generically nontrivial), and the whole point of the moduli space $\mathcal{M}^\flat$ is that flat connections which look identical locally can differ globally. Flatness is the Goldilocks condition: weaker, and it no longer forces local triviality; stronger, and it collapses the global information that makes $\mathcal{M}^\flat$ worth studying.

This last observation is the real motivation for packaging $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ and $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$ into the definition. The equation $\Omega=0$ is a nonlinear first-order partial differential equation on the connection (nonlinear because of the $[\omega\wedge\omega]$ term whenever $G$ is non-abelian), and its space of solutions modulo gauge is the prototype of every moduli space that appears later in gauge theory. As Haydys puts it, $\mathcal{M}^\flat$ "represents the space of all solutions of a non-linear PDE modulo an equivalence relation, so that in essence the question is to describe topological properties of the space of all solutions of a non-linear PDE". Two features make it worth the trouble. First, $\mathcal{M}^\flat$ often encodes subtle information about the base manifold $M$ and the bundle $P$ — indeed [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|it is exactly the space of representations of $\pi_1(M)$]], a purely topological object, dressed as a space of solutions of a differential equation. Second, moduli spaces of this kind carry extra geometric structure (symplectic in the surface case, for instance) of independent interest. The definition below is the entry point to both.

# The Definition

Let $\omega\in\mathcal{A}(P)$ be a connection on a principal $G$-bundle $P\to M$, with curvature $\Omega=d\omega+\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]\in\Omega^2(P;\mathfrak{g})$.

> **Definition (flat connection).** The connection $\omega$ is **flat** if its curvature vanishes identically,
> $$\Omega=0.$$
> A covariant derivative $\nabla$ on a vector bundle $E\to M$ is **flat** if $F_\nabla=0$. The **space of flat connections** on $P$ is
> $$\mathcal{A}^\flat(P):=\{\omega\in\mathcal{A}(P):\Omega_\omega=0\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}(P).$$

The following three conditions on $\omega$ are equivalent, and any one may be taken as the definition:

1. **Global (total-space) form.** $\Omega=0$ in $\Omega^2(P;\mathfrak{g})$.
2. **Local (base) form.** For every local section $s\colon U\to P$ of a trivialising atlas, the local curvature form vanishes, $F_s=dA_s+\tfrac12[A_s\wedge A_s]=0$ in $\Omega^2(U;\mathfrak{g})$ (equivalently, $F_s=0$ for a single trivialisation covering $M$).
3. **Involutive-distribution form.** The horizontal distribution $H=\ker\omega$ is involutive: for any two horizontal vector fields $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$ on $P$, the bracket $[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]$ is again horizontal.

**Equivalence of (1) and (2).** By [[Thm - Structure Equation for the Curvature|the structure equation]], the local curvature form of a section $s$ is the pull-back of the total-space curvature, $F_s=s^*\Omega$; explicitly $F_s=dA_s+\tfrac12[A_s\wedge A_s]$ (for matrix groups, $dA_s+A_s\wedge A_s$). Hence if $\Omega=0$ then $F_s=s^*0=0$ for every section, giving (1)$\Rightarrow$(2). Conversely, suppose $F_s=0$ for every section of a trivialising atlas $\{U_\alpha\}$ covering $M$. Fix $p\in P$ and put $m=\pi(p)$; choose a section $s$ of the atlas near $m$ and write $p=s(m)\cdot g$ for a unique $g\in G$. Since $\Omega$ is horizontal — it vanishes as soon as one argument is vertical (see [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection]]) — and $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant, $R_g^*\Omega=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\Omega$, we may compute $\Omega_p$ from its value at $s(m)$: for horizontal tangent vectors it is determined by $F_s=s^*\Omega$ through the isomorphism $d_ms\colon T_mM\to H_{s(m)}$ (the horizontal lift), and $F_s=0$ forces $\Omega=0$ on horizontal pairs at $s(m)$, hence (by equivariance) at $p$, and hence everywhere by horizontality. Thus (2)$\Rightarrow$(1). The equivalence (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(3) is proved as Corollary 2 below. $\square$

Because flatness is preserved by the gauge group — this is Corollary 1 below — the group $\mathcal{G}(P)$ acts on $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$, and we may form the quotient.

> **Definition (moduli space of flat connections).** The **moduli space of flat connections** on $P$ is the orbit space
> $$\mathcal{M}^\flat(P):=\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)/\mathcal{G}(P).$$

We follow Haydys' notation $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$, $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$ throughout; some authors write $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{flat}}$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{flat}}$. The definition of $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$ as a set is unconditional; that it is well posed as "the flat connections up to gauge" — that the $\mathcal{G}(P)$-action does not leave $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ — is exactly Corollary 1.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

There is a structural reading of flatness that makes the local-triviality theorem almost tautological and explains why flat bundles are "combinatorial" objects. A connection is a $G$-invariant splitting $TP=H\oplus V$ of the tangent bundle into horizontal and vertical parts. The vertical distribution $V=\ker d\pi$ is always involutive — its leaves are the fibres — so a connection is the choice of a *complementary* distribution $H$. The curvature, read through Corollary 2, is precisely the obstruction to $H$ being involutive in its own right: $\Omega$ measures how far the horizontal distribution is from closing up under the Lie bracket. A **flat** connection is therefore exactly one whose horizontal distribution is a foliation of $P$.

By the [[Thm - The Frobenius Theorem|Frobenius theorem]] — an involutive smooth distribution is integrable, so through each point passes a unique maximal [[Def - Integral Manifold of a Distribution|integral manifold]] tangent to it — an involutive $H$ integrates to a foliation of $P$ by leaves transverse to the fibres. Each leaf projects locally diffeomorphically to the base, and its local inverse is a horizontal section, i.e. a local section $s$ with $s^*\omega=0$: a **flat local gauge** in which $\omega$ is literally the product connection. This is the content of [[Thm - Local Triviality of Flat Connections|the local-triviality theorem]], which upgrades the involutivity of Corollary 2 to a genuine local product structure and shows further that a flat bundle admits a trivialising atlas whose transition functions are locally constant — equivalently, that its structure group reduces to the discrete group $G^\delta$ ($G$ with the discrete topology). In this reading a flat $G$-bundle is a bundle with discrete structure group: a purely combinatorial gluing datum, whose only invariant is the conjugacy class of the resulting representation of $\pi_1(M)$. That statement is [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|the monodromy correspondence]], and it is the reason this page exists.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** The official definition, $\Omega=0$, is a statement about a $2$-form; its operational meaning is transport-theoretic. A flat connection is exactly *a rule for transporting fibres along paths that sees only the homotopy class of the path*. This is the honest name for the object: on a flat bundle, parallel transport $\Gamma(c)$ along a path $c$ from $m$ to $m'$ is unchanged when $c$ is deformed rel endpoints (proved in [[Thm - Holonomy of a Flat Connection Depends Only on the Homotopy Class of the Loop|the homotopy-invariance theorem]]), so a flat connection is the same data as a homomorphism $\pi_1(M,m)\to G$ up to conjugacy. Every equivalent form of the definition is a different angle on this one fact: "$\Omega=0$" is its infinitesimal shadow (holonomy of every small loop is trivial to leading order); "$H$ involutive" is its integrated form (horizontal planes fit together into leaves, along which transport is canonical); "locally the product connection" is what a single leaf looks like in coordinates; "locally constant transition functions" is the combinatorial residue. When you meet a flat connection, the useful thing to remember is not the vanishing $2$-form but the representation of the fundamental group it encodes.

The pattern "curvature is the obstruction to integrability" is one of the most reused in geometry, and flatness is its zero locus in each incarnation. On the tangent bundle with the Levi-Civita connection, flatness of $\nabla$ is exactly local isometry with Euclidean space (the Riemann curvature tensor vanishes), so a Riemannian manifold is flat in the metric sense precisely when it is flat in the connection sense — the torus $\mathbb{R}^n/\mathbb{Z}^n$ with its flat metric is the model, and its canonical flat connection is the constant one. In complex geometry, a holomorphic vector bundle with a Hermitian connection is flat when its Chern connection has vanishing curvature, and by the same monodromy correspondence such bundles are the unitary local systems, the objects of the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence between flat connections and representations of $\pi_1$. In the theory of foliations, an involutive distribution *is* a foliation, and flat connections are the special foliations transverse to a fibration; the Bott vanishing theorem lives one step beyond. And in physics, a flat $U(1)$-connection with nontrivial holonomy is the mathematics of the Aharonov–Bohm effect: a vector potential with $F=0$ that is nonetheless not pure gauge, detected only by the phase a charged particle picks up going around a solenoid — precisely the failure of a flat connection on a non-simply-connected space to be gauge-trivial.

A second compression worth carrying: flatness makes the exterior covariant derivative into a genuine differential. On a vector bundle, $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=F_\nabla\wedge(\cdot)$ acting on $E$-valued forms (see [[Def - Curvature of a Vector-Bundle Connection]] and [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Vector Bundle]]), so the sequence $\Omega^0(M;E)\xrightarrow{d^\nabla}\Omega^1(M;E)\xrightarrow{d^\nabla}\Omega^2(M;E)\xrightarrow{d^\nabla}\cdots$ is a cochain complex — that is, $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=0$ — **if and only if** $\nabla$ is flat. A flat bundle thus carries its own de Rham-type cohomology, the cohomology of $M$ with coefficients in the local system, and this is where the invariants of flat bundles (twisted Betti numbers, Reidemeister torsion) come from.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the product connection.** Let $P=M\times G$ be the trivial bundle and take the **product connection** (the canonical flat connection) $\omega=\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$, where $\operatorname{pr}_2\colon M\times G\to G$ is the projection and $\theta$ is the [[Def - The Maurer-Cartan Form|Maurer–Cartan form]]. We verify $\Omega=0$ clause by clause. By [[Thm - Structure Equation for the Curvature|the structure equation]] and the naturality of the exterior derivative and the bracket under pull-back,
$$\Omega=d\omega+\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]=d(\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta)+\tfrac12[\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta\wedge\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta]=\operatorname{pr}_2^*\!\big(d\theta+\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]\big)\qquad(\text{since }d\circ\operatorname{pr}_2^*=\operatorname{pr}_2^*\circ d\text{ and }\operatorname{pr}_2^*\text{ commutes with the bracket}).$$
By [[Thm - The Maurer-Cartan Equation|the Maurer–Cartan equation]], the term in brackets vanishes: $d\theta+\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta]=0$. Therefore $\Omega=\operatorname{pr}_2^*0=0$, and $\omega$ is flat. (This is the mechanism behind Bär's Remark 2.5.9, that trivial bundles have vanishing characteristic classes: the canonical connection they carry is flat. Bär's printed computation contains a sign slip — for left-invariant fields $X,Y$ it prints $d\theta(X,Y)=[X,Y](e)$, whereas the correct value, needed for the two terms to cancel against $\tfrac12[\theta\wedge\theta](X,Y)=[X,Y]$, is $d\theta(X,Y)=-[X,Y](e)$; we use the corrected form.) The corresponding vector-bundle statement: on the trivial bundle $\underline{V}=M\times V$ the connection $\nabla=d$ has local connection matrix $A=0$, so $F=dA+A\wedge A=0$, and $d=\nabla$ is flat. Its parallel transport in this trivialisation is the identity map on $V$ for every path — the archetype of "flat means transport is trivial".

**Is an instance — the constant connection on $T^2\times U(1)$, flat but not gauge-trivial.** Let $M=T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ with coordinates $x,y$, let $P=T^2\times U(1)$, and take
$$\omega=\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta+\pi^*A,\qquad A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)\in\Omega^1(T^2;\mathfrak{u}(1)),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R}\text{ constants}.$$
In the global section $s=(\operatorname{id},1)$ the gauge potential is $A_s=A=i(a\,dx+b\,dy)$. Since $U(1)$ is abelian, $\mathfrak{u}(1)=i\mathbb{R}$ has trivial bracket, so $\tfrac12[A_s\wedge A_s]=0$, and because $a,b$ are constant, $dA_s=i(a\,d(dx)+b\,d(dy))=0$. Hence $F_s=dA_s+\tfrac12[A_s\wedge A_s]=0$, so by equivalence (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2) the connection is flat. It is nonetheless generically **not** gauge-trivial: by [[Thm - Holonomy of an Abelian Connection is the Exponential of the Curvature Integral|the abelian-holonomy theorem]] its holonomies around the two generating loops $x\mapsto(x,0)$ and $y\mapsto(0,y)$ are $\exp(-\oint A_s)=e^{-2\pi ia}$ and $e^{-2\pi ib}$, which are nontrivial unless $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$. A gauge transformation is a smooth map $g\colon T^2\to U(1)$ (Corollary 3 below), and it shifts $(a,b)$ by the pair of winding numbers of $g$; so the connection is gauge-equivalent to the product connection precisely when $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$, and the moduli space is $\mathcal{M}^\flat_{U(1)}(T^2)\cong(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^2=U(1)^2$. This is worked out in full in [[Ex - Holonomy of a Constant Connection on the Trivial Bundle over the Torus]] and [[Ex - Flat U(1)-Connections on the Torus]]; it is the smallest example in which flatness fails to imply global triviality, and the mechanism — nontrivial $\pi_1$ — is exactly the one the monodromy correspondence organises.

**Is an instance — the canonical flat connection on a Lie group.** The tangent bundle $TG$ of a Lie group $G$ carries a canonical flat connection, obtained by declaring the left-invariant vector fields to be parallel; its curvature vanishes although its torsion need not. This is [[Ex - The Tangent Bundle of a Lie Group has a Canonical Flat Connection]] from Riemannian Geometry I, and it shows that flatness of a connection is genuinely weaker than flatness of a Riemannian metric — a non-abelian Lie group with a bi-invariant metric has flat *connection* in this sense but nonzero *Riemannian* curvature for the Levi-Civita connection.

**Is NOT an instance — the standard connection on the Hopf bundle.** The Hopf bundle $S^3\to S^2$ with its standard $U(1)$-connection $a$ (the one whose horizontal spaces are the orthogonal complements of the fibre directions) is the canonical *non-flat* connection. Its curvature was computed in [[Ex - Curvature of the Standard Hopf Connection]]: $\Omega_p(X,Y)=2i\langle iX,Y\rangle$, which under the projection descends to $F=2i\,\mathrm{vol}_{S^2_{1/2}}$, a nonzero $2$-form on $S^2$. Concretely, in the local trivialisation over the upper hemisphere the gauge potential is $A_1=(-x_1\,dx_0+x_0\,dx_1-x_3\,dx_2+x_2\,dx_3)\,i$ pulled back, and $dA_1=2i(dx_0\wedge dx_1+dx_2\wedge dx_3)\ne0$. Since $F\ne0$, condition (2) fails, so $\omega$ is not flat. This is exactly as it must be: $\int_{S^2}\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F=-1\ne0$ is the first Chern number of the Hopf bundle, and a bundle with a nonzero characteristic number can carry no flat connection at all (a flat connection would have $F=0$, hence $c_1=[\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F]=0$). Flatness is obstructed by topology.

**Corollary 1 (flatness is gauge-invariant; $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$ is well posed).** *If $\omega\in\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ and $f\in\mathcal{G}(P)$, then $f^*\omega\in\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$. Hence $\mathcal{G}(P)$ acts on $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ and $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)=\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)/\mathcal{G}(P)$ is defined.*

> [!note]- Proof of Corollary 1
> **What is assumed and what is shown.** We assume $\Omega_\omega=0$ and $f\in\mathcal{G}(P)$, and must show $\Omega_{f^*\omega}=0$.
>
> **Invoke the transformation law for curvature under gauge.** By [[Thm - Gauge Transformations Act on Connections and Curvature|the gauge-action theorem]] — which states that for a connection $\omega\in\mathcal{A}(P)$ and $f\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ the pull-back $f^*\omega$ is again a connection and its curvature satisfies $\Omega_{f^*\omega}=f^*\Omega_\omega$, and that in a local section $s$ with $g=\hat{g}\circ s\colon U\to G$ (where $f(p)=p\cdot\hat{g}(p)$) the local curvature transforms by conjugation, $F_{f^*\omega,s}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}F_{\omega,s}$ — we have
> $$\Omega_{f^*\omega}=f^*\Omega_\omega\qquad(\text{by the gauge-action theorem, part (c)}).$$
> **Apply the hypothesis.** Since $\Omega_\omega=0$ (flatness of $\omega$), $\Omega_{f^*\omega}=f^*0=0$. Equivalently, in any local gauge, $F_{f^*\omega,s}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}F_{\omega,s}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}0=0$, using that $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$ is a linear isomorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$ for each point.
> **Conclusion.** Therefore $f^*\omega$ is flat, so $\mathcal{G}(P)$ maps $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ into itself; as $\mathcal{G}(P)$ is a group acting on $\mathcal{A}(P)$ (part (b) of the same theorem), its restriction to the invariant subset $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)$ is a group action, and the quotient $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)=\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)/\mathcal{G}(P)$ is a well-defined set. Because curvature transforms by conjugation, the gauge orbit of a flat connection consists entirely of flat connections — flatness is a property of the whole orbit, hence a property of a point of $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 2 (flat $\Leftrightarrow$ horizontal distribution involutive).** *A connection $\omega$ is flat if and only if its horizontal distribution $H=\ker\omega$ is involutive.*

> [!note]- Proof of Corollary 2
> **What is assumed and what is shown.** We prove the two implications separately. Write $H=\ker\omega$ for the horizontal distribution and recall that a vector field $\tilde{X}$ on $P$ is *horizontal* when $\omega(\tilde{X})\equiv0$. Involutivity of $H$ means: for all horizontal $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$, the bracket $[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]$ is horizontal, i.e. $\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])\equiv0$.
>
> **Step 0 — the key identity.** Let $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$ be horizontal vector fields on $P$. We compute $\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})$ from the structure equation $\Omega=d\omega+\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega]$. First, the bracket term: by the normalisation of the bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms (see [[Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket]]),
> $$\tfrac12[\omega\wedge\omega](\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=[\omega(\tilde{X}),\omega(\tilde{Y})]=[0,0]=0\qquad(\text{since }\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}\text{ are horizontal}).$$
> Second, the $d\omega$ term: by [[Thm - Coordinate Expression for the Exterior Derivative|the invariant formula for the exterior derivative]] of a $1$-form, $d\eta(U,W)=U(\eta(W))-W(\eta(U))-\eta([U,W])$, applied componentwise to the $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $1$-form $\omega$,
> $$d\omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=\tilde{X}\big(\omega(\tilde{Y})\big)-\tilde{Y}\big(\omega(\tilde{X})\big)-\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])=-\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])\qquad(\text{since }\omega(\tilde{X})\equiv0\text{ and }\omega(\tilde{Y})\equiv0\text{ as functions, so their derivatives vanish}).$$
> Adding the two,
> $$\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=-\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])\qquad\text{for all horizontal }\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}.\tag{$\ast$}$$
>
> **Direction 1 ($\Rightarrow$): flat implies involutive.** Assume $\Omega=0$. Then for any horizontal $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$, identity $(\ast)$ gives $\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])=-\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=0$, so $[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]$ is horizontal. Hence $H$ is involutive.
>
> **Direction 2 ($\Leftarrow$): involutive implies flat.** Assume $H$ is involutive. By $(\ast)$, $\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=-\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])=0$ for all horizontal $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$, because $[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]$ is horizontal. It remains to see that $\Omega$ vanishes on *all* pairs of tangent vectors, not only horizontal ones. **Use horizontality of the curvature.** By [[Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection]], $\Omega$ is a *horizontal* $2$-form: $\Omega(u,w)=0$ whenever $u$ or $w$ is vertical. Fix $p\in P$ and arbitrary $u,w\in T_pP$; using the splitting $T_pP=H_p\oplus V_p$ write $u=u_H+u_V$, $w=w_H+w_V$ with $u_H,w_H\in H_p$ and $u_V,w_V\in V_p$. Then by bilinearity and horizontality of $\Omega$,
> $$\Omega_p(u,w)=\Omega_p(u_H,w_H)\qquad(\text{every term with a vertical argument vanishes}).$$
> Extend $u_H,w_H$ to horizontal vector fields $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$ near $p$ (possible since $H$ is a smooth distribution). Then $\Omega_p(u_H,w_H)=\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})|_p=0$ by the previous paragraph. Hence $\Omega_p=0$, and as $p$ was arbitrary, $\Omega=0$: the connection is flat.
>
> **Conclusion.** The connection $\omega$ is flat if and only if $H=\ker\omega$ is involutive; the curvature is, up to sign, exactly the vertical part of the bracket of horizontal fields, $\Omega(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})=-\omega([\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}])$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 3 (the abelian gauge group, used above).** *If $G$ is abelian, every gauge transformation of $P=M\times G$ has the form $f(m,h)=(m,g(m)\,h)$ for a smooth $g\colon M\to G$, and $\mathcal{G}(P)\cong C^\infty(M;G)$.* This is proved in full in [[Thm - The Gauge Group is the Space of Sections of the Adjoint Group Bundle]] (part (b)); we quote it only to identify the gauge freedom in the torus example, where it gives $\mathcal{G}(T^2\times U(1))\cong C^\infty(T^2;U(1))$, whose components are labelled by the winding numbers around the two generating loops.

**Calibration check.** Three quick verifications the reader can run from the material on this page. First, *flatness makes the exterior covariant derivative a complex.* On a vector bundle, $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=F_\nabla\wedge(\cdot)$; so $\nabla$ flat ($F_\nabla=0$) gives $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=0$, and the sequence $\Omega^\bullet(M;E)$ becomes a cochain complex — conversely, if $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=0$ on $\Omega^0(M;E)=\Gamma(E)$ then $F_\nabla s=0$ for every section $s$, forcing $F_\nabla=0$ (see [[Def - Curvature of a Vector-Bundle Connection]] and [[Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Vector Bundle]]). Second, *the constant connection $i\,a\,dx$ on the trivial $U(1)$-bundle over $\mathbb{R}$ is flat and gauge-trivial, but over $S^1$ it is flat and gauge-trivial only when $a\in\mathbb{Z}$* — the difference is entirely $\pi_1$, and it is a good instant test of whether one has internalised that flat does not mean trivial. Third — a forward calibration — *a flat $U(1)$-connection over a simply connected base is gauge-trivial*: with $\pi_1(M)=0$ (see [[Def - Simply Connected Space]]) the monodromy representation $\rho_\omega\colon\pi_1(M)\to U(1)$ is trivial, so by [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|the monodromy correspondence]] the connection is gauge-equivalent to the product connection; the obstruction to trivialising a flat connection lives entirely in $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M),G)/\text{conjugation}$.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Local triviality of flat connections *(from Gauge Theory V)*
> The equivalence "flat $\Leftrightarrow$ horizontal distribution involutive" (Corollary 2) is the hinge on which [[Thm - Local Triviality of Flat Connections|the local-triviality theorem]] turns: applying the [[Thm - The Frobenius Theorem|Frobenius theorem]] to the now-integrable $H$ produces, around every point, a flat local gauge $s$ with $s^*\omega=0$, and a trivialising atlas with locally constant transition functions. Flat means "locally the product connection".

> [!tip] The monodromy correspondence *(from Gauge Theory V)*
> Once parallel transport of a flat connection is known to depend only on homotopy classes ([[Thm - Holonomy of a Flat Connection Depends Only on the Homotopy Class of the Loop]]), the moduli space $\mathcal{M}^\flat(P)$ defined here is identified with a space of representations of the fundamental group by [[Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group|the flat-connections/representations correspondence]]: $\mathcal{M}^\flat_G(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M),G)/\text{conjugation}$. The differential-geometric object $\mathcal{A}^\flat(P)/\mathcal{G}(P)$ becomes a topological invariant of $M$.

> [!tip] Compactness of the moduli space *(from Gauge Theory V)*
> When $G$ is compact, the representation variety, hence $\mathcal{M}^\flat$, is compact — this is the first "compactness theorem" of the series, proved in **Thm - The Moduli Space of Flat Connections with Compact Structure Group is Compact**, and it is the flat prototype for the far harder compactness theorems of Seiberg–Witten and Donaldson theory.

> [!tip] Flat connections as critical points of Chern–Simons *(from Gauge Theory VI)*
> On a closed oriented $3$-manifold, the flat connections are exactly the critical points of the **Chern–Simons functional** $\vartheta\colon\mathcal{A}(P)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$: the differential is $d\vartheta_A(a)=\tfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$, which vanishes for all variations $a$ if and only if $F_A=0$. The definition of flatness on this page is therefore the Euler–Lagrange equation of the most basic topological action in gauge theory.

> [!tip] The Aharonov–Bohm effect and flat $U(1)$-connections *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> A flat $U(1)$-connection with nontrivial holonomy — the torus example above — is the mathematics of the Aharonov–Bohm effect: a vector potential with vanishing field strength $F=0$ that is not pure gauge, detected only through the phase acquired by transport around a non-contractible loop. Flatness that fails to be triviality is a measurable physical phenomenon.
