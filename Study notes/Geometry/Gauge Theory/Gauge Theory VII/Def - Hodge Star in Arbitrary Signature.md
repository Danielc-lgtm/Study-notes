# Notation

Throughout, $V$ is a real vector space of finite dimension $n$, carrying a **non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form** $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ that need not be positive definite; we call it the inner product on $V$ even in the indefinite case. Its **index** $p$ is the number of negative diagonal entries in any diagonalisation — equivalently, the number of $j$ with $\langle e_j,e_j\rangle=-1$ for a generalized orthonormal basis — and this number is independent of the basis by Sylvester's law of inertia (see [[Def - Generalized Orthonormal Basis, Index, and the Induced Inner Product on Forms]] and [[Thm - Sylvester's Law of Inertia]]). We assume $V$ is **oriented**: an orientation is a choice of one of the two components of the set of ordered bases, made precise in [[Def - Orientation of a Vector Space]].

A **generalized orthonormal basis** $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $V$ is one with $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=0$ for $i\ne j$ and $\langle e_j,e_j\rangle=\epsilon_j\in\{+1,-1\}$; it is **positively oriented** when it lies in the chosen orientation class. We write $e_1^*,\dots,e_n^*$ for the dual basis of $V^*$ and $\Lambda^kV^*$ for the space of alternating $k$-linear forms on $V$ ([[Def - Alternating Tensor and Lambda k V Dual]]). For an increasing multi-index $I=(i_1<\dots<i_k)$ we abbreviate $e_I^*:=e_{i_1}^*\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_k}^*$ and $\epsilon_I:=\epsilon_{i_1}\cdots\epsilon_{i_k}$.

The **induced inner product on $\Lambda^kV^*$** is the unique symmetric bilinear form for which the monomials $\{e_I^*\}_{|I|=k}$ of any generalized orthonormal basis are orthogonal with $\langle e_I^*,e_I^*\rangle=\epsilon_I$; concretely
$$\langle\omega,\eta\rangle=\sum_{i_1<\dots<i_k}\epsilon_{i_1}\cdots\epsilon_{i_k}\,\omega(e_{i_1},\dots,e_{i_k})\,\eta(e_{i_1},\dots,e_{i_k}),$$
and it is basis-independent and non-degenerate by [[Thm - The Induced Inner Product and Volume Form are Well-Defined]]. The **volume form** is $\mathrm{vol}:=e_1^*\wedge\dots\wedge e_n^*$ for any positively oriented generalized orthonormal basis; it is well-defined by the same theorem, spans the one-dimensional space $\Lambda^nV^*$, and satisfies $\langle\mathrm{vol},\mathrm{vol}\rangle=(-1)^p$. All of these are the standing objects of §7.1; this page defines the operator that ties the wedge product, the induced inner product, and the volume form together.

> [!warning] Convention: this series uses Bär's Hodge star $\star_B$, not the vault's Riemannian $\star_V$
> Three conventions for the Hodge star circulate, all agreeing in Riemannian signature but differing by signs once the form is indefinite. Let $\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*$ and $\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$, and let $\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda^kV^*$.
> - **Bär's $\star_B$** (adopted for the whole gauge-theory series, and the operator defined below): $\omega\wedge\eta=\langle\star_B\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$.
> - **The vault's $\star_V$** ([[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]], written for Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian manifolds): $\alpha\wedge\star_V\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$.
> - **The reversed-order variant $\star'$** (common in physics texts): $\star'\beta\wedge\alpha=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$.
>
> The exact relations, proved in the Compression section below, are
> $$\star_B=(-1)^p\,\star_V\quad\text{on }\Lambda^kV^*,\qquad \star'=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\,\star_V\quad\text{on }\Lambda^kV^*.$$
> Two consequences matter for later pages. First, in Riemannian signature ($p=0$) all three agree on the sign carried by $p$, so $\star_B=\star_V$ there; the vault's Riemannian Hodge pages transfer to this series verbatim. Second, the phrase "$(-1)^{k(n-k)}$" in `conventions.md` refers to $\star'$ versus $\star_V$ — the reversal of the wedge order — **not** to Bär versus the vault; the Bär-versus-vault sign is $(-1)^p$. In dimension four on $2$-forms both correction signs are trivial ($k(n-k)=4$ is even and, for a Euclidean $V$, $p=0$), which is why the self-dual/anti-self-dual splitting of §7.4 is convention-free; in Lorentzian signature ($p=1$) the two operators $\star_B$ and $\star_V$ differ by an overall minus sign, and every chapter VII page that quotes a Minkowski star value states which operator it means.

# Axiom Motivation

We already have three separate pieces of structure on the oriented space $(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$: the wedge product $\Lambda^kV^*\times\Lambda^{n-k}V^*\to\Lambda^nV^*$, the induced inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on each $\Lambda^jV^*$, and the volume form $\mathrm{vol}$ trivialising the line $\Lambda^nV^*$. The wedge product measures how two forms of complementary degree combine into a top form; the inner product measures how two forms of the *same* degree overlap. These live on different spaces and cannot be compared directly. What we want is a single linear map that translates the first pairing into the second, so that "wedging $\omega$ against something" and "taking the inner product of a fixed dual partner against that something" become one and the same operation. That translator is the Hodge star, and asking for it to exist is exactly asking for a canonical isomorphism $\Lambda^kV^*\cong\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ compatible with all three structures at once.

The desideratum can be written as one equation. We want $\star\omega\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ to encode $\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*$ so that testing $\star\omega$ against an arbitrary $\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ by the inner product reproduces the top form $\omega\wedge\eta$:
$$\omega\wedge\eta=\langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{for all }\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*.$$
Two independent facts make this determine $\star\omega$ uniquely. First, the wedge pairing $\Lambda^k\times\Lambda^{n-k}\to\Lambda^n\cong\mathbb{R}$ is **perfect**: dividing the top form $\omega\wedge\eta$ by $\mathrm{vol}$ gives a genuine linear functional of $\eta$, and no non-zero $\omega$ pairs to zero with every $\eta$. Second, the induced inner product on $\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ is **non-degenerate** ([[Thm - The Induced Inner Product and Volume Form are Well-Defined]], part (ii)), so every linear functional on $\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ is represented by a unique vector. Composing "represent the functional $\eta\mapsto\omega\wedge\eta/\mathrm{vol}$" with the perfectness of the wedge pairing produces exactly one $\star\omega$, and the assignment $\omega\mapsto\star\omega$ is linear. This is proved in full on the next page, [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Hodge Star]], which we quote below at the point of use.

Now the per-clause failure analysis, because the definition presupposes three properties and each is load-bearing. **If we drop non-degeneracy of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$** — allow a genuinely degenerate form — then there is no generalized orthonormal basis at all (some direction would need $\langle e_j,e_j\rangle=0$), the volume form is not normalisable, and, most concretely, the induced pairing on forms acquires a radical: some non-zero $\eta_0$ has $\langle x,\eta_0\rangle=0$ for every $x$. Choosing $\omega$ with $\omega\wedge\eta_0\ne0$ then makes the defining equation demand $\langle\star\omega,\eta_0\rangle=(\text{non-zero})$, which no $\star\omega$ can satisfy; the operator simply does not exist. The explicit witness is worked out among the non-examples below. **If we drop the orientation** — refuse to single out $\mathrm{vol}$ up to positive scale — then $\mathrm{vol}$ is defined only up to sign, and by the defining relation $\star$ flips sign with it; the operator exists on each oriented component but is not a function of the unoriented space. This is not a technicality: it is the reason self-dual and anti-self-dual $2$-forms swap roles under a change of orientation, which in turn is why an anti-self-dual instanton on $X$ is a self-dual instanton on the orientation-reversed $\overline{X}$ (chapter XIII). **If we ignore the index $p$** — pretend the ambient signature is Riemannian when it is not — every sign that distinguishes $\star_B$ from $\star_V$, and every occurrence of $(-1)^p$ in the properties $\star\star=(-1)^{k(n-k)+p}$ and $\langle\star\omega,\star\eta\rangle=(-1)^p\langle\omega,\eta\rangle$, is lost, and the Lorentzian identities of electrodynamics come out with the wrong signs. In particular $\star$ is an isometry only up to the factor $(-1)^p$, so in Minkowski signature $\star$ is *not* an isometry, a distinction the exercises of §7.1 drill directly.

A reader handed only these desiderata — a linear translator between the wedge pairing and the inner pairing, forced to exist by perfectness and non-degeneracy, sign-tied to the orientation, and index-sensitive — could reconstruct the definition and anticipate its main properties without being told them.

# The Definition

Let $(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ be an oriented $n$-dimensional real vector space with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form of index $p$, and let $0\le k\le n$.

> [!note] Definition (Hodge star operator, Bär's convention)
> The **Hodge star operator** on $k$-forms is the linear map
> $$\star:\Lambda^kV^*\longrightarrow\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$$
> uniquely determined by the requirement
> $$\omega\wedge\eta=\langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{for all }\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*,\ \eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*.\tag{$\ast$}$$
> Here $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on the right is the induced inner product on $\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$, and $\mathrm{vol}=e_1^*\wedge\dots\wedge e_n^*$ is the volume form of the orientation. The operator $\star$ associated with $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is called the Hodge star of the inner product.

That there is exactly one such map, and that it is linear, is the content of the next page, which we restate here since $(\ast)$ presupposes it:

> **Theorem (existence and uniqueness of the Hodge star; [[Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Hodge Star]]).** For every $\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*$ there is a unique $\star\omega\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ satisfying $(\ast)$ for all $\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$, and $\omega\mapsto\star\omega$ is linear.

The proof there fixes $\omega$, observes that $\eta\mapsto(\omega\wedge\eta)/\mathrm{vol}$ (division being the identification $\Lambda^nV^*\cong\mathbb{R}$ via $\mathrm{vol}$) is a linear functional on $\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$, and uses non-degeneracy of the induced inner product to represent it by a unique $\star\omega$; linearity in $\omega$ then follows from uniqueness. We take that result as given on this page.

**Dependence on the inner product (Remark 3.1.7).** The symbol $\star$ suppresses its dependence on the data. The wedge product and the space $\Lambda^kV^*$ are purely linear-algebraic, but $(\ast)$ involves both $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ (through the pairing on the right) and $\mathrm{vol}$ (through the orientation and the normalisation), and $\mathrm{vol}$ is itself built from $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. Consequently **the Hodge star depends on the inner product**, and a different inner product on the same oriented $V$ generally gives a different operator. This is exactly what makes $\star$ the carrier of geometric information rather than of merely topological information, and it is the reason the metric can be varied against the electromagnetic action in §7.3: the variation of the action is, up to the calculus of variations, the variation of $\star$.

**Pointwise extension to manifolds.** Let $(M,g)$ be an oriented $n$-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold, so that each tangent space $T_xM$ carries a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form $g_x$ of a fixed index $p$ ([[Def - Semi-Riemannian Metric and Signature]]), and let the orientation of $M$ ([[Def - Orientation of a Smooth Manifold]]) orient each $T_xM$. Then $g_x$ induces an inner product and, with the orientation, a volume element on each $\Lambda^kT_x^*M$, and the construction above applies fibrewise. The resulting bundle map extends to a $C^\infty(M)$-linear operator
$$\star:\Omega^k(M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{n-k}(M),\qquad (\star\omega)_x:=\star(\omega_x),$$
on differential forms ([[Def - Differential k-Form on a Manifold]]); smoothness of $\star\omega$ follows because in any oriented local orthonormal coframe the coefficients of $\star\omega$ are, by property (1) on [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature]], constant-sign permutations of the coefficients of $\omega$, hence smooth. On a Riemannian volume form the normalisation matches [[Def - Riemannian Volume Form]] and [[Thm - Existence of the Riemannian Volume Form]].

**Extension to vector-valued forms (Definition 3.1.6 / Remark 3.1.11).** Let $W$ be a real vector space with its own inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_W$. The tensor product $V\otimes W$ ([[Def - Tensor Product of Vector Spaces]]) carries the **product inner product**, defined on decomposables by
$$\langle v\otimes w,\,v'\otimes w'\rangle:=\langle v,v'\rangle\,\langle w,w'\rangle_W$$
and extended bilinearly; it is non-degenerate whenever both factors are. Correspondingly, a $W$-valued $k$-form is an element of $\Lambda^kV^*\otimes W$, and the Hodge star is extended to $W$-valued forms by acting on the form factor only and leaving the coefficient vector untouched:
$$\star:\Lambda^kV^*\otimes W\longrightarrow\Lambda^{n-k}V^*\otimes W,\qquad \star(\omega\otimes w):=(\star\omega)\otimes w,$$
extended linearly. This is the version used in §7.4, where $W=\mathfrak{g}$ is the Lie algebra of the structure group with an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant inner product and the curvature $F_A\in\Omega^2(M;\mathfrak{g})$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued $2$-form; the Yang–Mills density $F_A\wedge\star F_A$ combines the wedge of forms with the inner product of the $\mathfrak{g}$-parts, precisely the two structures the product inner product records.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The operational content of $(\ast)$ is that $\star$ is a composite of two canonical isomorphisms, and reading it this way removes any sense that the defining equation is an unmotivated normalisation.

Fix the degree $k$ and abbreviate $A:=\Lambda^kV^*$ and $B:=\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$. There are two structure maps out of $B$ into its dual $B^*$. The first is the **wedge-pairing map**
$$\Phi:A\longrightarrow B^*,\qquad \Phi(\omega):=\Big(\eta\mapsto \frac{\omega\wedge\eta}{\mathrm{vol}}\Big),$$
which is an isomorphism because the wedge pairing $A\times B\to\Lambda^nV^*\cong\mathbb{R}$ is perfect: it is well known that for complementary degrees the pairing $(\omega,\eta)\mapsto\omega\wedge\eta$ is non-degenerate, so $\Phi$ is injective, and $\dim A=\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}=\dim B=\dim B^*$ forces it to be an isomorphism. The second is the **musical isomorphism of the inner product on $B$**,
$$\flat_B:B\longrightarrow B^*,\qquad \flat_B(\zeta):=\langle\zeta,\cdot\rangle,$$
which is an isomorphism precisely because $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is non-degenerate on $B$ ([[Thm - The Induced Inner Product and Volume Form are Well-Defined]], part (ii)). The Hodge star is the unique map making the triangle commute, that is,
$$\star:=\flat_B^{-1}\circ\Phi:A\longrightarrow B,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \flat_B(\star\omega)=\Phi(\omega),$$
and unwinding $\flat_B(\star\omega)=\Phi(\omega)$ on an arbitrary $\eta$ recovers $\langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle=(\omega\wedge\eta)/\mathrm{vol}$, which is $(\ast)$. In one sentence: **the Hodge star is the perfect wedge pairing read through the non-degenerate inner product**. This structural description also makes the dependence on data transparent — $\Phi$ needs the orientation (to fix $\mathrm{vol}$), $\flat_B$ needs the inner product, and $\star$ inherits both dependencies — and it is the natural home for the convention comparisons, since the three conventions differ only in which slot of $\Phi$ or of $\flat_B$ one uses.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** Operationally, $\star$ is "complementation of an orthonormal monomial, with a bookkeeping sign." On a positively oriented generalized orthonormal basis, property (1) of [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature]] reads
$$\star(e_{i_1}^*\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_k}^*)=\epsilon_{j_1}\cdots\epsilon_{j_{n-k}}\,\operatorname{sign}(IJ)\,e_{j_1}^*\wedge\dots\wedge e_{j_{n-k}}^*,$$
where $(j_1,\dots,j_{n-k})$ is the complementary index set and $(IJ)=(i_1,\dots,i_k,j_1,\dots,j_{n-k})$ is the permutation of $(1,\dots,n)$ appearing. So to apply $\star$ to a basis $k$-form one writes down the complementary basis $(n-k)$-form, multiplies by the product of the $\epsilon$'s over the *complementary* indices, and multiplies by the sign of the shuffle that interleaves the two index blocks back into standard order. Every value on this page is computed this way once, and cross-checked against the defining relation $(\ast)$.

**The convention signs, derived.** Recall the vault's operator $\star_V$ is defined by $\alpha\wedge\star_V\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for $\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda^kV^*$ ([[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]]). We show $\star_B=(-1)^p\star_V$. Property (4) of [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature]] states $\omega\wedge\star_B\eta=(-1)^p\langle\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for $\omega,\eta\in\Lambda^kV^*$; comparing this with the vault relation $\omega\wedge\star_V\eta=\langle\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ gives $\omega\wedge(\star_B\eta-(-1)^p\star_V\eta)=0$ for every $\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*$ (by property (4), then the vault definition). Since the wedge pairing $\Lambda^kV^*\times\Lambda^{n-k}V^*\to\Lambda^nV^*$ is perfect, the only $(n-k)$-form annihilating every $\omega$ is zero, so $\star_B\eta=(-1)^p\star_V\eta$; as $\eta$ was arbitrary, $\star_B=(-1)^p\star_V$. For the reversed-order variant, $\star'$ is defined by $\star'\beta\wedge\alpha=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$; since $\star'\beta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ and $\alpha\in\Lambda^kV^*$, graded-commutativity of the wedge ([[Thm - Wedge Product Properties]]) gives $\star'\beta\wedge\alpha=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\alpha\wedge\star'\beta$, so $\alpha\wedge\star'\beta=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ (rearranging), and comparison with the vault relation yields $\star'\beta=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\star_V\beta$, i.e. $\star'=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\star_V$. This is the precise sense in which `conventions.md`'s "$(-1)^{k(n-k)}$" is the $\star'$-versus-$\star_V$ sign, not the Bär-versus-vault sign.

**Where the star appears elsewhere.** In three-dimensional Euclidean space the Hodge star is the reason vector calculus works: $\star$ turns a $1$-form into a $2$-form, and the composite of $d$ with $\star$ reproduces gradient, curl, and divergence, with the cross product $\vec u\times\vec v$ appearing as $\star(u^\flat\wedge v^\flat)$ and the scalar triple product as $\star(u^\flat\wedge v^\flat\wedge w^\flat)$ ([[Ex - Hodge Star on R^3 Recovers Cross Product and Scalar Triple Product]]). In electromagnetism ([[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor]]) the star swaps the electric and magnetic blocks of the field strength $F$ and packages the two inhomogeneous Maxwell equations into $d\star F+J=0$. In Hodge theory ([[Def - The Codifferential]]) it defines the codifferential $\delta=\pm\star d\star$ and hence the Laplacian, and it makes Poincaré duality a metric statement. What unifies these is a single fact: **middle- and complementary-degree forms are canonically paired once a non-degenerate inner product and an orientation are fixed, and $\star$ is that pairing made into a map**. The dependence on the metric is what separates this from the purely topological duality of de Rham theory, and it is exactly what makes $\star$ the natural object to vary when one varies the geometry.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Euclidean three-space (Riemannian, $p=0$), recovering vector calculus.** Take $V=\mathbb{R}^3$ with the standard positive-definite inner product, positively oriented generalized orthonormal basis giving the coframe $dx,dy,dz$ (all $\epsilon=+1$), and $\mathrm{vol}=dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$. Here $p=0$, so $\star_B=\star_V$ and there is nothing to reconcile. We compute $\star\,dx$ directly from $(\ast)$: for a $1$-form $\omega=dx$ we need $\star\,dx\in\Lambda^2$ with $dx\wedge\eta=\langle\star\,dx,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for all $\eta\in\Lambda^2$. Testing against the orthonormal $2$-form basis $\{dy\wedge dz,\ dz\wedge dx,\ dx\wedge dy\}$, only $\eta=dy\wedge dz$ gives a non-zero wedge, $dx\wedge dy\wedge dz=\mathrm{vol}$ (all others contain a repeated factor, hence vanish), so we need $\langle\star\,dx,dy\wedge dz\rangle=1$ and $\langle\star\,dx,\eta\rangle=0$ for the other two; since the basis $2$-forms are orthonormal with $\langle dy\wedge dz,dy\wedge dz\rangle=(+1)(+1)=1$ (by the induced-inner-product formula), the unique solution is $\star\,dx=dy\wedge dz$. The same computation with $dy$ gives $dy\wedge dz\wedge dx=\mathrm{vol}$ — the permutation $(y,z,x)=(2,3,1)$ has two inversions, hence sign $+1$ — so $\star\,dy=dz\wedge dx$; and $\star\,dz=dx\wedge dy$, from $dz\wedge dx\wedge dy=\mathrm{vol}$ (the permutation $(3,1,2)$ again has sign $+1$). These are the classical identities behind $\vec u\times\vec v=\big(\star(u^\flat\wedge v^\flat)\big)^\sharp$, matching [[Ex - Hodge Star on R^3 Recovers Cross Product and Scalar Triple Product]] clause for clause.

**Minkowski $2$-forms (Lorentzian, $p=1$): Bär's star table, each entry verified from $(\ast)$.** Take $V=\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$ with coordinates $(t,x,y,z)$, signature $(-,+,+,+)$, so $\epsilon_t=-1$ and $\epsilon_x=\epsilon_y=\epsilon_z=+1$, index $p=1$, and $\mathrm{vol}=dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$. We compute $\star$ on the six basis $2$-forms. Here $k=2$, $n-k=2$, and for any $2$-form $\omega$ the defining relation $(\ast)$ says $\star\omega$ is the unique $2$-form with $\langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}=\omega\wedge\eta$ for every basis $2$-form $\eta$. Because the six basis $2$-forms are orthogonal with $\langle e_I^*,e_I^*\rangle=\epsilon_I$, once we know which $\eta$ makes $\omega\wedge\eta$ a non-zero multiple $c\,\mathrm{vol}$, the coefficient of that $\eta$ in $\star\omega$ is $c/\epsilon_\eta$ where $\epsilon_\eta=\langle\eta,\eta\rangle$.

> [!note]- Verification of all six Minkowski star values
> In each line, $\omega\wedge\eta$ is computed by reordering the four factors into $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz=\mathrm{vol}$ and recording the sign of the reordering permutation; then the coefficient in $\star\omega$ is that sign divided by $\langle\eta,\eta\rangle=\epsilon_\eta$.
>
> **$\star(dt\wedge dx)$.** The only basis $2$-form $\eta$ with $(dt\wedge dx)\wedge\eta\ne0$ is $\eta=dy\wedge dz$, and $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz=\mathrm{vol}$ (identity permutation, sign $+1$). Here $\epsilon_\eta=\langle dy\wedge dz,dy\wedge dz\rangle=\epsilon_y\epsilon_z=+1$, so the coefficient is $(+1)/(+1)=+1$ and
> $$\star(dt\wedge dx)=dy\wedge dz.$$
> **$\star(dy\wedge dz)$.** Only $\eta=dt\wedge dx$ contributes: $dy\wedge dz\wedge dt\wedge dx$ reorders to $\mathrm{vol}$ by the permutation $(y,z,t,x)=(3,4,1,2)$, which has four inversions, hence sign $+1$; so $dy\wedge dz\wedge dt\wedge dx=+\mathrm{vol}$. Now $\epsilon_\eta=\langle dt\wedge dx,dt\wedge dx\rangle=\epsilon_t\epsilon_x=-1$, so the coefficient is $(+1)/(-1)=-1$ and
> $$\star(dy\wedge dz)=-\,dt\wedge dx.$$
> **$\star(dt\wedge dy)$.** Only $\eta=dz\wedge dx$ contributes: $dt\wedge dy\wedge dz\wedge dx$ reorders by $(t,y,z,x)=(1,3,4,2)$, two inversions, sign $+1$; $\epsilon_\eta=\epsilon_z\epsilon_x=+1$, coefficient $+1$, so
> $$\star(dt\wedge dy)=dz\wedge dx.$$
> **$\star(dz\wedge dx)$.** Only $\eta=dt\wedge dy$ contributes: $dz\wedge dx\wedge dt\wedge dy$ reorders by $(z,x,t,y)=(4,2,1,3)$, four inversions, sign $+1$; $\epsilon_\eta=\epsilon_t\epsilon_y=-1$, coefficient $(+1)/(-1)=-1$, so
> $$\star(dz\wedge dx)=-\,dt\wedge dy.$$
> **$\star(dt\wedge dz)$.** Only $\eta=dx\wedge dy$ contributes: $dt\wedge dz\wedge dx\wedge dy$ reorders by $(t,z,x,y)=(1,4,2,3)$, two inversions, sign $+1$; $\epsilon_\eta=\epsilon_x\epsilon_y=+1$, coefficient $+1$, so
> $$\star(dt\wedge dz)=dx\wedge dy.$$
> **$\star(dx\wedge dy)$.** Only $\eta=dt\wedge dz$ contributes: $dx\wedge dy\wedge dt\wedge dz$ reorders by $(x,y,t,z)=(2,3,1,4)$, two inversions, sign $+1$; $\epsilon_\eta=\epsilon_t\epsilon_z=-1$, coefficient $(+1)/(-1)=-1$, so
> $$\star(dx\wedge dy)=-\,dt\wedge dz.$$

Collecting the six values gives Bär's table:
$$\star(dt\wedge dx)=dy\wedge dz,\quad \star(dy\wedge dz)=-\,dt\wedge dx,\quad \star(dt\wedge dy)=dz\wedge dx,$$
$$\star(dz\wedge dx)=-\,dt\wedge dy,\quad \star(dt\wedge dz)=dx\wedge dy,\quad \star(dx\wedge dy)=-\,dt\wedge dz.$$
The pattern is transparent: $\star$ pairs each "electric" plane $dt\wedge d(\text{space})$ with the complementary "magnetic" plane $d(\text{space})\wedge d(\text{space})$, with a plus sign going from electric to magnetic and a minus sign going from magnetic to electric — the minus being exactly the factor $\epsilon_t=-1$ that the timelike direction contributes.

**Non-example: a degenerate form admits no Hodge star.** Let $V=\mathbb{R}^2$ with basis $e_1,e_2$ and the *degenerate* symmetric bilinear form with $\langle e_1,e_1\rangle=1$, $\langle e_2,e_2\rangle=0$, $\langle e_1,e_2\rangle=0$; the vector $e_2$ lies in the radical. There is no generalized orthonormal basis (a basis vector would need $\langle e_j,e_j\rangle=\pm1$ in every direction, impossible along the radical), so the machinery of $(\ast)$ never starts; but we can see the failure directly. Fix any candidate top form $\mu=e_1^*\wedge e_2^*$ to play the role of $\mathrm{vol}$ and ask for $\star:\Lambda^1V^*\to\Lambda^1V^*$ with $\omega\wedge\eta=\langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mu$ for all $\eta\in\Lambda^1V^*$. The induced pairing on $\Lambda^1V^*$ inherits the radical: $\langle x,e_2^*\rangle=0$ for every $x\in\Lambda^1V^*$, since $e_2^*$ is dual to the null direction. Take $\omega=e_1^*$ and $\eta=e_2^*$: the left side is $e_1^*\wedge e_2^*=\mu\ne0$, while the right side is $\langle\star\omega,e_2^*\rangle\,\mu=0\cdot\mu=0$, a contradiction. No linear map $\star$ can satisfy the defining relation, confirming that non-degeneracy is not decoration but the precise hypothesis that makes the operator exist.

**Calibration check.** First, verify $\star\star=-1$ on Minkowski $2$-forms directly from the table just proved, so that property (2) of [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature]] — which states $\star\star\omega=(-1)^{k(n-k)+p}\omega$, here $(-1)^{2\cdot2+1}=(-1)^5=-1$ — is confirmed on an example rather than merely quoted: $\star\star(dt\wedge dx)=\star(dy\wedge dz)=-\,dt\wedge dx$, and $\star\star(dy\wedge dz)=\star(-\,dt\wedge dx)=-\,dy\wedge dz$, both equal to $-1$ times the input; the remaining four basis forms check the same way by the electric–magnetic pairing above. Second, verify the convention sign $\star_B(dt\wedge dx)=-\star_V(dt\wedge dx)$: from [[Def - The Hodge Star Operator]] the vault's coordinate formula gives $\star_V(dt\wedge dx)=\operatorname{sign}(I,J)\,\epsilon_I\,dy\wedge dz$ with $I=(t,x)$, $J=(y,z)$, $\operatorname{sign}(I,J)=+1$ (identity permutation) and $\epsilon_I=\epsilon_t\epsilon_x=(-1)(+1)=-1$, so $\star_V(dt\wedge dx)=-\,dy\wedge dz$, whereas we proved $\star_B(dt\wedge dx)=+\,dy\wedge dz$; the two differ by the overall sign $(-1)^p=(-1)^1=-1$, exactly as $\star_B=(-1)^p\star_V$ predicts. If you can also see why the Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^3$ values above carry no such sign discrepancy — because there $p=0$ — you have understood the role of the index in this definition.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Properties of the Hodge star in arbitrary signature *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> With the definition in hand, the five sign identities — the monomial formula, $\star\star=(-1)^{k(n-k)+p}$, the isometry-up-to-$(-1)^p$ property $\langle\star\omega,\star\eta\rangle=(-1)^p\langle\omega,\eta\rangle$, the symmetric pairing $\omega\wedge\star\eta=\eta\wedge\star\omega=(-1)^p\langle\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$, and the mixed-degree relation — are all forced by $(\ast)$; they are proved on [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature]] and are the workhorses of the rest of the chapter.

> [!tip] Self-dual and anti-self-dual $2$-forms *(from Gauge Theory VII / XIII)*
> In dimension four on $2$-forms in Riemannian signature, $\star$ is an involution ($\star\star=1$) and an isometry, so $\Lambda^2V^*$ splits orthogonally into the $\pm1$ eigenspaces $\Lambda^2_\pm$ ([[Thm - Self-Dual Decomposition of 2-Forms in Four Dimensions]], [[Def - Self-Dual and Anti-Self-Dual Forms]]). This splitting is the algebraic seed of instantons and of Donaldson theory.

> [!tip] The electromagnetic and Yang–Mills actions *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> The star turns the wedge $F\wedge F$ (a topological, connection-independent quantity) into the energy density $F\wedge\star F$ (a metric quantity), and the $W$-valued extension does the same for $\mathfrak{g}$-valued curvature; the Maxwell law $d\star F+J=0$ and the Yang–Mills equation $d^A\star F_A=0$ both live on $\star$.
