# Notation

Throughout, $M$ is an oriented [[Def - Lorentzian Manifold|Lorentzian]] four-manifold, the mathematical model of spacetime, with metric $g = \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ of signature $(-,+,+,+)$; in the language of the Hodge-star convention its **index** — the number of negative directions of $g$ — is $p = 1$. We work in units with the speed of light $c = 1$ and with a test particle of unit mass and unit charge, the normalisation that removes the vacuum constants $\mu_0$ and $c$ from every formula below. In **standard local coordinates** $(t,x,y,z)$ adapted to the signature, $\langle\partial_t,\partial_t\rangle < 0$ and $\langle\partial_x,\partial_x\rangle,\langle\partial_y,\partial_y\rangle,\langle\partial_z,\partial_z\rangle > 0$; on Minkowski space $M = \mathbb{R}^4$ these are inertial coordinates with $\langle\partial_t,\partial_t\rangle = -1$, $\langle\partial_x,\partial_x\rangle = \langle\partial_y,\partial_y\rangle = \langle\partial_z,\partial_z\rangle = +1$, and oriented volume form $\mathrm{vol} = dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$.

We carry over the objects of the two preceding definition pages. Let $\pi\colon P\to M$ be a principal $U(1)$-bundle with space of connections $\mathcal{C}(P)$. For $\omega\in\mathcal{C}(P)$ the descended curvature is $\bar\Omega\in\Omega^2(M;i\mathbb{R})$, and the **electromagnetic field strength** is the real two-form $F = F(\omega)\in\Omega^2(M;\mathbb{R})$ defined by $\bar\Omega = iF$ ([[Def - U(1) Gauge Field and Electromagnetic Connection|the $U(1)$ gauge field]]); it satisfies the homogeneous law $dF = 0$ by the Bianchi identity, and in standard coordinates
$$F = E_x\,dx\wedge dt + E_y\,dy\wedge dt + E_z\,dz\wedge dt + B_x\,dy\wedge dz + B_y\,dz\wedge dx + B_z\,dx\wedge dy,$$
with $\vec E = (E_x,E_y,E_z)$ the electric and $\vec B = (B_x,B_y,B_z)$ the magnetic field. We fix once and for all a **background connection** (reference connection) $\omega_0\in\mathcal{C}(P)$, with field strength $F_0 = F(\omega_0)$, and write $A = A(\omega,\omega_0)\in\Omega^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ for [[Def - U(1) Gauge Field and Electromagnetic Connection|the potential relative to $\omega_0$]], the global one-form determined by $iA = s^*(\omega - \omega_0)$ for any local section $s$; it satisfies $dA = F - F_0$. The source is the [[Def - Charge-Current 3-Form|charge-current three-form]] $J\in\Omega^3(M;\mathbb{R})$, whose coefficients in standard coordinates are the charge density $\varrho$ and the current density $\vec\jmath$. The [[Def - Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature|Hodge star]] $\star$ is Bär's star, characterised by $\omega\wedge\eta = \langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$; on Minkowski two-forms it acts by
$$\star(dt\wedge dx) = dy\wedge dz,\quad \star(dy\wedge dz) = -dt\wedge dx,\quad \star(dt\wedge dy) = dz\wedge dx,$$
$$\star(dz\wedge dx) = -dt\wedge dy,\quad \star(dt\wedge dz) = dx\wedge dy,\quad \star(dx\wedge dy) = -dt\wedge dz.$$
The notation $U\Subset M$ means $U$ is an open set whose closure $\bar U$ is compact and contained in $M$ (a relatively compact open set), and $\Omega^k_c$ denotes compactly supported $k$-forms. The full symbol registry for the chapter is on [[Gauge Theory VII — The Hodge Star, Electrodynamics, and Yang–Mills Theory]].

> [!warning] Convention: Bär's Hodge star versus the other common star
> The series uses **Bär's** Hodge-star convention throughout, defined by the relation $\omega\wedge\eta = \langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for $\omega\in\Lambda^k$, $\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}$, from which $\star\star = (-1)^{k(n-k)+p}$ on $k$-forms ([[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature|properties of the star]]). The other convention widely used in the literature defines the star by $\alpha\wedge\star\beta = \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$; the two differ by the factor $(-1)^{k(n-k)}$, which equals $+1$ in dimension four on two-forms, so on our two-forms $F$ the two stars agree and the formulae below are convention-independent in that degree. Every metric-dependent sign on this page is computed inside Bär's convention.

> [!warning] Convention: the special-relativity action and units
> The special-relativity chapter writes the free electromagnetic action as $S = -\tfrac{1}{4\mu_0}\int F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{vol}$ in signature $(+,-,-,-)$ with the vacuum permeability $\mu_0$ and the speed of light restored. This is the same object as the free Lagrangian $L_1$ below: in an orthonormal frame $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F = -\tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ (Lorentzian, $p=1$) and $\langle F,F\rangle = \tfrac12 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$, so $L_1 = -\tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{vol}$; Bär's normalisation $c=1$, unit charge, and $\mu_0 = 1$ is what removes the constant $\tfrac{1}{\mu_0}$. The metric signatures are related by $g_B = -g_{\mathrm{SR}}$ and the Minkowski stars by $\star_B = -\star_V^{\mathrm{SR}}$, but as noted above these signs cancel in degree two.

# Axiom Motivation

We have on the table one field equation and are missing another. The field strength $F$ of a $U(1)$-connection satisfies $dF = 0$ automatically, and in coordinates this single identity unpacks into the two *homogeneous* Maxwell equations, $\operatorname{div}\vec B = 0$ and $\partial_t\vec B + \operatorname{rot}\vec E = 0$. But the two *inhomogeneous* equations — Coulomb's law $\operatorname{div}\vec E = \varrho$ and Ampère's law $\operatorname{rot}\vec B - \partial_t\vec E = \vec\jmath$, the equations in which the sources $\varrho,\vec\jmath$ actually appear — cannot be identities, because they involve data ($J$) that the connection alone does not determine. They must instead be *equations of motion*: the conditions that single out the physically realised connections among all connections. The question this definition answers is therefore the standard one of classical field theory: what is the action functional whose stationary points are exactly the physical field configurations? Answering it turns the search for a field equation into the search for a single scalar quantity to extremise, and that quantity is the Lagrangian.

The desiderata for such a functional are fixed by what we require of the resulting equation of motion. We want a map $L\colon\mathcal{C}(P)\to\Omega^4(M;\mathbb{R})$ assigning to each connection a **real four-form**, because a four-form is exactly the object one integrates over the four-dimensional spacetime to obtain an action $\int L$, and reality is demanded of any physical action. The functional should be **local** and **first order** in the connection, built pointwise from $F$ (and possibly the potential $A$ and the source $J$) with no derivatives of $F$, so that the Euler–Lagrange equation is *second* order in the potential — first order in $F$ — matching Maxwell's equations, which relate $F$ to its first derivatives. The free part, the part present even when $\varrho = \vec\jmath = 0$, should be **quadratic** in $F$, so that its variation is *linear* in $F$ and the vacuum equations are linear, as Maxwell's are. It should be built **invariantly** from $F$, using only the metric and orientation, so that the theory does not secretly depend on a choice of coordinates. And it should be **gauge-invariant** in its dynamical content, since $F$ is the gauge-invariant datum and $\omega$ and any gauge transform of it describe the same physics.

Run these requirements to ground and the free Lagrangian is essentially forced. A real four-form built pointwise, quadratically, and $\mathrm{SO}$-invariantly from a two-form $F$ on an oriented semi-Riemannian four-manifold has only two independent forms available: $F\wedge\star F$ and $F\wedge F$. The first is metric-dependent (the star carries the metric) and equals $(-1)^p\langle F,F\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$, a genuine scalar density built from the norm of $F$; the second is metric-free and topological. **The metric-free candidate $F\wedge F$ has no equation of motion at all**: it is closed and locally exact, and (as verified below in the Examples) its integral is stationary at *every* connection, so it contributes nothing to the Euler–Lagrange equation. If one drops the metric — that is, tries to build the free Lagrangian from $F\wedge F$ alone — one loses all dynamics: there is no field equation, only a topological number. Hence the free Lagrangian must be the metric-dependent $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$; the factor $\tfrac12$ is a normalisation chosen so that the coupled equation comes out as $d\star F + J = 0$ with unit coefficients. If instead one *strengthens* the quadratic requirement to a higher power, say $\langle F,F\rangle^2\,\mathrm{vol}$, the vacuum equation becomes nonlinear; such nonlinear electrodynamics (Born–Infeld, Euler–Heisenberg) exists and is interesting, but it is not classical Maxwell theory, whose defining feature is the superposition principle and hence linearity.

The source coupling is fixed by a parallel analysis. We need a term linear in the sources $J$ that, upon variation, deposits $J$ into the field equation. The natural pairing of the three-form $J$ with the field is through the potential one-form $A$: the four-form $A\wedge J$ is the unique local, first-order term linear in both the potential and the source. It has a feature that at first looks like a defect and turns out to be exactly right. **The term $A\wedge J$ is gauge-dependent and depends on the choice of background $\omega_0$, yet its variation is neither.** It is gauge-dependent because $A$ is only defined relative to $\omega_0$ and changes under gauge transformations; it is $\omega_0$-dependent because replacing $\omega_0$ shifts $A$ by a fixed one-form. But under the variation $\omega\mapsto\omega + t\eta$ of the connection with the background held fixed, $A$ changes by $t\eta$, so the variation of $A\wedge J$ is $\int\eta\wedge J$, a quantity that involves neither $\omega_0$ nor any gauge choice. This is the phenomenon that makes the coupling legitimate: the action is not gauge-invariant on the nose, but its critical-point condition is. If one drops the coupling term entirely, one recovers only the vacuum equation $d\star F = 0$ and can never see sources — Coulomb's law and Ampère's law disappear, and the theory describes light in empty space only. There is no strengthening that improves matters: a term quadratic in $A$ would break gauge invariance of the equation of motion (it is the photon-mass term of Proca theory, a different theory), and a term with $dA$ in place of $A$ merely reshuffles the free Lagrangian.

A reader handed only these desiderata — a real four-form, local, first order, quadratic in $F$ for the free part and linear in the sources for the coupling, with gauge-invariant dynamics on a four-manifold — could reconstruct $L(\omega) = \tfrac12 F\wedge\star F + A\wedge J$ line for line: the free part is the only quadratic invariant with an equation of motion, and the coupling is the only linear source term, with the $\tfrac12$ fixed by demanding unit-coefficient Maxwell equations. That is the test of a good definition, and this one passes it.

# The Definition

Fix a background connection $\omega_0\in\mathcal{C}(P)$ and a charge-current three-form $J\in\Omega^3(M;\mathbb{R})$. The **electromagnetic Lagrangian** (also called the electrodynamics Lagrangian) is the map
$$L\colon\mathcal{C}(P)\longrightarrow\Omega^4(M;\mathbb{R}),\qquad L(\omega) := \tfrac12\,F\wedge\star F + A\wedge J,$$
where $F = F(\omega)$ is the field strength of $\omega$ and $A = A(\omega,\omega_0)$ is its potential relative to $\omega_0$. We write $L = L_1 + L_2$ for its two pieces,
$$L_1(\omega) := \tfrac12\,F\wedge\star F\qquad\text{(the free field Lagrangian)},\qquad L_2(\omega) := A\wedge J\qquad\text{(the source coupling)}.$$

On a Lorentzian four-manifold the free Lagrangian simplifies to a scalar density. By property (4) of the Hodge star in arbitrary signature — [[Thm - Properties of the Hodge Star in Arbitrary Signature|the identity]] $\omega\wedge\star\eta = (-1)^p\langle\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for $\omega,\eta$ of equal degree — applied with $\omega = \eta = F$ and index $p = 1$,
$$L_1 = \tfrac12\,F\wedge\star F = \tfrac12(-1)^p\langle F,F\rangle\,\mathrm{vol} = -\tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(Lorentzian signature, }p = 1\text{)},$$
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the induced inner product on $\Lambda^2 T^*M$. The minus sign is genuine and is the source of the well-known fact that the Lagrangian is the difference, not the sum, of the electric and magnetic energy densities (verified in the Examples).

The **action** of $\omega$ over a relatively compact open set $U\Subset M$ is the integral $S_U(\omega) := \int_{\bar U} L(\omega)$. Because $M$ need not be compact, and because $L_2$ is only defined once a background is chosen, criticality is defined not by a single global integral but by the vanishing of the first variation under all compactly supported perturbations, as follows.

> **Definition (critical connection).** A connection $\omega\in\mathcal{C}(P)$ is **critical** for $L$ if for every relatively compact open set $U\Subset M$ and every real one-form $\eta\in\Omega^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ with $\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset U$,
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\int_{\bar U} L(\omega_{t,\eta}) = 0,$$
> where $\omega_{t,\eta}\in\mathcal{C}(P)$ is the connection determined by the requirement $A(\omega_{t,\eta},\omega_0) = A(\omega,\omega_0) + t\eta$.

The connection $\omega_{t,\eta}$ exists and is unique, and its curvature is $F(\omega_{t,\eta}) = F + t\,d\eta$. This is where the affine structure of the space of connections is used, and it is worth restating the fact being invoked. By [[Thm - The Space of Connections is an Affine Space|the affine-space theorem]] — the space $\mathcal{C}(P)$ of principal connections is a nonempty affine space modelled on the vector space $\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$ of one-forms valued in the adjoint bundle, meaning the difference of any two connections descends to such a one-form and any connection may be shifted by any such one-form — and because for the abelian group $U(1)$ the adjoint bundle is the trivial bundle $M\times i\mathbb{R}$, the model space is $\Omega^1(M;i\mathbb{R})$. Hence we may set
$$\omega_{t,\eta} := \omega + it\,\eta,$$
the connection obtained by adding the $i\mathbb{R}$-valued one-form $it\eta$ (pulled back to $P$) to $\omega$. Its potential relative to $\omega_0$ is $iA(\omega_{t,\eta},\omega_0) = s^*(\omega_{t,\eta}-\omega_0) = s^*(\omega-\omega_0) + it\eta = i(A + t\eta)$, so $A(\omega_{t,\eta},\omega_0) = A + t\eta$ as required, and its curvature satisfies $\bar\Omega(\omega_{t,\eta}) = \bar\Omega(\omega) + d(it\eta) = i(F + t\,d\eta)$, so $F(\omega_{t,\eta}) = F + t\,d\eta$. The map $t\mapsto\omega_{t,\eta}$ is an affine line in $\mathcal{C}(P)$, and criticality asks that the action be stationary along every such line generated by a compactly supported $\eta$.

> [!warning] Convention: source typo in the target of $L$
> Bär (p. 85) writes the Lagrangian as a map $L\colon\mathcal{C}(P)\to\Omega^4(M;i\mathbb{R})$, into the *imaginary*-valued four-forms. This is a typographical slip: $F$ is a real two-form, $\star F$ is a real two-form, $A$ is a real one-form, and $J$ is a real three-form, so both $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$ and $A\wedge J$ are real four-forms and $L$ takes values in $\Omega^4(M;\mathbb{R})$. We use the corrected target throughout. (The imaginary unit lives one level down, in $\bar\Omega = iF$ and $iA = s^*(\omega-\omega_0)$, which are the honestly $i\mathbb{R}$-valued objects; $L$ is built from the real avatars $F$ and $A$.)

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

**True name.** The official definition presents $L$ as a pair of four-forms, but its operational meaning is a scalar energy expression. On Minkowski space $L_1 = \tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2)\,\mathrm{vol}$ (verified below), which is the field's "kinetic minus potential" energy density in the sense of Lagrangian mechanics: the electric field plays the role of a velocity and the magnetic field the role of a potential, and extremising the time integral of kinetic minus potential energy is exactly Hamilton's principle. The reason the Lagrangian is the *difference* and the energy density (the $T^{00}$ component of the stress tensor) is the *sum* $\tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 + |\vec B|^2)$ is the standard Legendre-transform relationship between a Lagrangian and its Hamiltonian; the sign flip on the magnetic term is the imprint of the Lorentzian signature, which weights the time direction oppositely to the space directions.

This definition is one instance of a pattern that organises the whole chapter and much of mathematical physics. The free Lagrangian $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$ is the **abelian, degree-two case of the Yang–Mills Lagrangian**. Replacing the scalar $U(1)$ field strength $F$ by the adjoint-bundle-valued curvature $\bar\Omega$ of a connection on a bundle with non-abelian structure group $G$, and replacing the pointwise product of real numbers by an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant inner product $\lambda$ on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, gives the [[Def - Yang-Mills Lagrangian and Action Functional|Yang–Mills Lagrangian]] $\tfrac12\lambda(\bar\Omega\wedge\star\bar\Omega)$; when $G = U(1)$ and $\lambda(ia,ib) = ab$, the two coincide and Maxwell theory is recovered as abelian Yang–Mills theory. The source coupling $A\wedge J$ has no direct non-abelian analogue precisely because the potential $A$ is not gauge-invariant there in a usable way; couplings to matter in the non-abelian case are built from matter fields rather than from a fixed external current.

The metric-free companion $F\wedge F$, which we exclude from the Lagrangian, is not useless — it is the **Chern–Weil representative of a characteristic number**. For a $U(1)$-bundle, $\tfrac{1}{8\pi^2}F\wedge F$ (up to normalisation) represents the square of the first Chern class, and its integral over a closed four-manifold is a topological invariant computed by [[Thm - Chern-Weil Theorem|the Chern–Weil theorem]]; in the non-abelian case the corresponding trace form $\operatorname{tr}(\bar\Omega\wedge\bar\Omega)$ represents a [[Def - Pontryagin Classes|Pontryagin number]] and controls the instanton number. The fact that this term is variationally inert — which disqualifies it as a Lagrangian — is the very fact that makes it a topological invariant: a quantity that does not change under any variation of the connection can only depend on the bundle, not on the connection. This is the same object seen from two sides, and the split $\|\bar\Omega\|^2 = \|\bar\Omega^+\|^2 + \|\bar\Omega^-\|^2$ of the Yang–Mills energy into self-dual and anti-self-dual parts, whose *difference* is topological, is exactly the interplay of $F\wedge\star F$ and $F\wedge F$ (see [[Thm - Energy Identity and the Topological Bound for Yang-Mills|the energy identity]]).

Finally, adjoining the [[Def - Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian and the Coupled Action|Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian]] $-\tfrac12\operatorname{scal}_g\,\mathrm{vol}_g$ to $L$ and varying the *metric* rather than the connection turns this definition into the Einstein–Maxwell system: the metric variation of $L_1$ produces the electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor $T$, and stationarity in $g$ is Einstein's equation $\operatorname{ric}_g - \tfrac12\operatorname{scal}_g\,g = T$. Thus the single expression $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F + A\wedge J$ is simultaneously the input to Maxwell's equations (vary $\omega$) and the source of Einstein's equations (vary $g$).

# Examples / Corollaries

**Verified computation — the Minkowski free Lagrangian $L_1 = \tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2)\,\mathrm{vol}$.** We compute $F\wedge\star F$ in standard Minkowski coordinates and confirm the sign. From the star table in the Notation, applied term by term to $F$,
$$\star F = -E_x\,dy\wedge dz - E_y\,dz\wedge dx - E_z\,dx\wedge dy + B_x\,dx\wedge dt + B_y\,dy\wedge dt + B_z\,dz\wedge dt.$$

(This uses $\star(dx\wedge dt) = \star(-dt\wedge dx) = -dy\wedge dz$ and the five corresponding entries; note in particular $\star(dx\wedge dy) = -dt\wedge dz$ gives the coefficient $+B_z\,dz\wedge dt$, correcting Bär's printed $-B_z\,dz\wedge dt$ on p. 86, which is a sign typo — the star table on the same page forces the plus sign, and it is verified below by the positivity of the resulting energy.)

Now $F\wedge\star F$ is a four-form, and a wedge $\mu\wedge\nu$ of two of the basis two-forms is nonzero only when $\mu$ and $\nu$ involve complementary pairs of coordinates. Inspecting the six terms of $F$ against the six terms of $\star F$, the only surviving pairings are the six in which an $F$-monomial meets the $\star F$-monomial built on the complementary index pair; every other product repeats a coordinate one-form and vanishes. Computing each survivor and reducing to $\mathrm{vol} = dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$:
$$(E_x\,dx\wedge dt)\wedge(-E_x\,dy\wedge dz) = -E_x^2\,(dx\wedge dt\wedge dy\wedge dz) = +E_x^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(since }dx\wedge dt\wedge dy\wedge dz = -\mathrm{vol}\text{, one transposition)},$$
$$(E_y\,dy\wedge dt)\wedge(-E_y\,dz\wedge dx) = -E_y^2\,(dy\wedge dt\wedge dz\wedge dx) = +E_y^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(the permutation }(y,t,z,x)\text{ of }(t,x,y,z)\text{ has 3 inversions, sign }-1),$$
$$(E_z\,dz\wedge dt)\wedge(-E_z\,dx\wedge dy) = -E_z^2\,(dz\wedge dt\wedge dx\wedge dy) = +E_z^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(the permutation }(z,t,x,y)\text{ has 3 inversions, sign }-1),$$
$$(B_x\,dy\wedge dz)\wedge(B_x\,dx\wedge dt) = B_x^2\,(dy\wedge dz\wedge dx\wedge dt) = -B_x^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(the permutation }(y,z,x,t)\text{ has 5 inversions, sign }-1),$$
$$(B_y\,dz\wedge dx)\wedge(B_y\,dy\wedge dt) = B_y^2\,(dz\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dt) = -B_y^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(the permutation }(z,x,y,t)\text{ has 5 inversions, sign }-1),$$
$$(B_z\,dx\wedge dy)\wedge(B_z\,dz\wedge dt) = B_z^2\,(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\wedge dt) = -B_z^2\,\mathrm{vol}\qquad\text{(the permutation }(x,y,z,t)\text{ has 3 inversions, sign }-1).$$
Summing the six lines,
$$F\wedge\star F = (E_x^2 + E_y^2 + E_z^2 - B_x^2 - B_y^2 - B_z^2)\,\mathrm{vol} = (|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2)\,\mathrm{vol},$$
so $L_1 = \tfrac12 F\wedge\star F = \tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2)\,\mathrm{vol}$, as claimed. This is consistent with the invariant formula $L_1 = -\tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$: reading off from the above, $\langle F,F\rangle = -F\wedge\star F/\mathrm{vol} = -(|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2) = -|\vec E|^2 + |\vec B|^2$, the correct Lorentzian norm of a two-form whose electric part sits in the time-space blocks (negative-normed) and whose magnetic part sits in the space-space blocks (positive-normed).

**Corollary — the Lagrangian is a real four-form (degree and reality check).** We verify that $L(\omega)\in\Omega^4(M;\mathbb{R})$ clause by clause. The piece $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$ is the wedge of a two-form $F\in\Omega^2$ with the two-form $\star F\in\Omega^{4-2} = \Omega^2$, hence a $2+2 = 4$-form; it is real because $F$ is real and the star maps real forms to real forms. The piece $A\wedge J$ is the wedge of a one-form $A\in\Omega^1$ with the three-form $J\in\Omega^3$, hence a $1+3 = 4$-form; it is real because $A$ and $J$ are real. So $L = L_1 + L_2\in\Omega^4(M;\mathbb{R})$, confirming the corrected target of the previous Convention callout.

**Corollary — the Euler–Lagrange equation, and independence of the background connection (R3.2.4).** The criticality condition above is the input to the variational calculation carried out in full on [[Thm - Euler-Lagrange Equation of the Electromagnetic Action|the Euler–Lagrange page]], whose result we restate: *a connection $\omega$ is critical for $L$ if and only if $d\star F + J = 0$.* We do not repeat that derivation here; instead we prove the corollary that makes the definition well-posed despite its dependence on the arbitrary background $\omega_0$: **criticality is independent of the choice of background connection.** Let $\tilde\omega_0\in\mathcal{C}(P)$ be a second background, and let $\tilde L$ be the Lagrangian built with $\tilde\omega_0$ in place of $\omega_0$; write $\tilde A = A(\omega,\tilde\omega_0)$ for the corresponding potential. The two potentials differ by a fixed one-form independent of $\omega$: from the defining relation,
$$i\tilde A = s^*(\omega - \tilde\omega_0) = s^*(\omega - \omega_0) + s^*(\omega_0 - \tilde\omega_0) = iA + iA(\omega_0,\tilde\omega_0),$$
so $\tilde A = A + A(\omega_0,\tilde\omega_0)$, where $A(\omega_0,\tilde\omega_0)\in\Omega^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ is the (fixed, $\omega$-independent) potential of the reference $\omega_0$ relative to $\tilde\omega_0$. Since the free part $L_1$ depends only on $F$ and not on the background, $L_1 = \tilde L_1$, and the two Lagrangians differ only through their coupling terms:
$$L(\omega) - \tilde L(\omega) = A\wedge J - \tilde A\wedge J = -A(\omega_0,\tilde\omega_0)\wedge J = A(\tilde\omega_0,\omega_0)\wedge J\qquad\text{(using }A(\tilde\omega_0,\omega_0) = -A(\omega_0,\tilde\omega_0)\text{)}.$$
The right-hand side does not depend on $\omega$. Therefore, along any variation $\omega_{t,\eta}$ with $A(\omega_{t,\eta},\omega_0) = A + t\eta$, the difference $L(\omega_{t,\eta}) - \tilde L(\omega_{t,\eta}) = A(\tilde\omega_0,\omega_0)\wedge J$ is constant in $t$, and hence
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0}\int_{\bar U}\big(L - \tilde L\big)(\omega_{t,\eta}) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0}\int_{\bar U}A(\tilde\omega_0,\omega_0)\wedge J = 0\qquad\text{(the integrand is independent of }t\text{)}.$$
Consequently $\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0\int_{\bar U}L(\omega_{t,\eta}) = \tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0\int_{\bar U}\tilde L(\omega_{t,\eta})$ for every $U$ and every compactly supported $\eta$, so $\omega$ is critical for $L$ if and only if it is critical for $\tilde L$. Therefore the set of critical connections — and, in agreement with this, the Euler–Lagrange equation $d\star F + J = 0$, which contains no reference to $\omega_0$ — is background-independent. The background is genuine scaffolding: it is needed to write down a global potential $A$ and hence the coupling term at all, but it cancels out of every physical conclusion.

**Non-example — $F\wedge F$ alone has no Euler–Lagrange content.** We show that the metric-free four-form $F\wedge F$, if used as a Lagrangian, is stationary at *every* connection, and therefore carries no field equation. Take any $\omega$, any $U\Subset M$, and any $\eta\in\Omega^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ with $\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset U$; along the variation $F(\omega_{t,\eta}) = F + t\,d\eta$ we compute
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0}\int_{\bar U}(F + t\,d\eta)\wedge(F + t\,d\eta) = \int_{\bar U}\big(d\eta\wedge F + F\wedge d\eta\big) = 2\int_{\bar U}d\eta\wedge F\qquad\text{(since }F\wedge d\eta = (-1)^{2\cdot2}\,d\eta\wedge F = d\eta\wedge F\text{, both two-forms)}.$$
Now $d(\eta\wedge F) = d\eta\wedge F + (-1)^{1}\eta\wedge dF = d\eta\wedge F$ (Leibniz rule for the exterior derivative; $\deg\eta = 1$; and $dF = 0$ by the Bianchi identity), so the integrand is exact, and because $\eta\wedge F$ is compactly supported in $U$,
$$2\int_{\bar U}d\eta\wedge F = 2\int_{\bar U}d(\eta\wedge F) = 2\int_M d(\eta\wedge F) = 0\qquad\text{(by [[Thm - Stokes' Theorem on Manifolds|Stokes' theorem]] for the compactly supported form }\eta\wedge F\text{ on }M\text{, which has no boundary term)}.$$
Thus the first variation of $\int F\wedge F$ vanishes identically, for every connection and every perturbation: the functional is *everywhere* critical, so its "Euler–Lagrange equation" is the empty condition $0 = 0$ and it distinguishes no connection from any other. This is the precise sense in which $F\wedge F$ has no dynamical content — its integral over a closed manifold is a fixed topological number, not a functional to be extremised — and it is why the free Lagrangian must be $F\wedge\star F$, which involves the metric through the star and does yield the equation $d\star F = 0$.

**Calibration check.** Three verifications the reader can carry out from the page alone. First, in the purely electrostatic case $\vec B = 0$, $\vec E = \nabla\phi$ constant in time, the Minkowski formula gives $L_1 = \tfrac12|\vec E|^2\,\mathrm{vol} > 0$, whereas a purely magnetic field $\vec E = 0$ gives $L_1 = -\tfrac12|\vec B|^2\,\mathrm{vol} < 0$ — the difference-of-energies structure, not a positivity. Second, the energy density $T^{00} = \tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 + |\vec B|^2) > 0$ is the *sum*; that the Lagrangian is the difference while the energy is the sum is the Legendre-transform signature, and the reader should confirm that the sign of the corrected $+B_z\,dz\wedge dt$ term in $\star F$ is exactly what makes $T^{00}$ positive rather than $\tfrac12(|\vec E|^2 - |\vec B|^2)$. Third, re-run the background-independence argument replacing $J$ by a source that is *not* closed ($dJ\neq0$): the cancellation of $A(\tilde\omega_0,\omega_0)\wedge J$ still goes through because it uses only that the difference term is $\omega$-independent, not that $J$ is closed — confirming that background-independence of *criticality* holds for any $J$, while charge conservation $dJ = 0$ (needed elsewhere) is a separate matter.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Maxwell's equations from a variational principle *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> With the Lagrangian in hand, the two inhomogeneous Maxwell equations become the Euler–Lagrange equation of $L$. [[Thm - Euler-Lagrange Equation of the Electromagnetic Action|The Euler–Lagrange page]] proves that $\omega$ is critical if and only if $d\star F + J = 0$, and [[Def - Maxwell Equations in Form Language|the Maxwell equations in form language]] and their coordinate form unpack this into Coulomb's law $\operatorname{div}\vec E = \varrho$ and Ampère's law $\operatorname{rot}\vec B - \partial_t\vec E = \vec\jmath$. Together with the Bianchi identity $dF = 0$ (the homogeneous pair), the full Maxwell system is now derived, half as an identity and half as an equation of motion.

> [!tip] The Yang–Mills Lagrangian and action *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> Replacing the scalar field strength by an adjoint-bundle-valued curvature and the pointwise product by an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant inner product turns $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$ into the [[Def - Yang-Mills Lagrangian and Action Functional|Yang–Mills Lagrangian]] $\tfrac12\lambda(\bar\Omega\wedge\star\bar\Omega)$, whose critical points are the Yang–Mills connections. Maxwell theory is the abelian case, and the [[Thm - Yang-Mills Equation from the Action Principle|Yang–Mills equation]] $d^\omega\star\bar\Omega = 0$ has the electromagnetic $d\star F = 0$ as its $U(1)$ shadow.

> [!tip] The energy–momentum tensor and the Einstein–Maxwell equations *(from Gauge Theory VII)*
> Varying the *metric* in $L_1$ rather than the connection produces the electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor $T$; adjoining the [[Def - Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian and the Coupled Action|Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian]] and demanding stationarity in $g$ yields Einstein's field equation $\operatorname{ric}_g - \tfrac12\operatorname{scal}_g\,g = T$ with $T$ as the source. The same expression $\tfrac12 F\wedge\star F$ thus feeds both Maxwell's and Einstein's equations, depending on which argument is varied.
