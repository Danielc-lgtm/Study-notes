## §1.4 Parallel Transport and Holonomy — Exercises

The exercises of §1.4 drill [[Def - Parallel Transport|parallel transport]] along curves, the [[Thm - Parallel Transport is an Isometry for Metric-Compatible Connections|isometry property]] for metric-compatible connections, and the **holonomy** — the rotation of a tangent vector after parallel transport around a closed loop. The exercise here gives the prototypical 2D illustration on the round sphere, where the holonomy equals the integrated Gaussian curvature inside the loop (the 2D Gauss-Bonnet identity). This is the cleanest geometric demonstration that the curvature of a connection has direct physical / geometric content — and it is the basis of Foucault's pendulum experiment, **Berry's phase** in quantum mechanics, and the **Aharonov-Bohm effect**. Each entry below names the exercise, summarises the technique it practices in one line, and lists in parentheses every definition and theorem invoked in that exercise's solution.

- [[Ex - Parallel Transport around a Geodesic Triangle on the Sphere]] (⭐⭐) — parallel-transport a tangent vector around a geodesic right triangle on the unit sphere (vertices at the north pole and two equatorial points), get holonomy rotation by $\pi/2$; verify via the 2D Gauss-Bonnet formula that the holonomy equals the integrated Gaussian curvature = area of the triangle = $\pi/2$ ([[Def - Parallel Transport]], [[Def - Covariant Derivative along a Curve]], [[Thm - Parallel Transport is an Isometry for Metric-Compatible Connections]])
