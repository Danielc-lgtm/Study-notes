## §3.2 Sectional, Ricci, and Scalar Curvature — Exercises

This section drills the hierarchy of curvature invariants extracted from the Riemann tensor by tracing: the sectional curvature on $2$-planes, the Ricci tensor as a single trace, the scalar curvature as the double trace. The principal techniques are **trace contraction in an orthonormal frame** (Ricci = sum of sectional curvatures in transverse $2$-planes; scalar = sum over all unordered pairs) and the **interpretation of constant-sectional-curvature manifolds** via the simplest possible algebraic form of the Riemann tensor. The exercises range from quick scalar identities (the Ricci tensor of a $2$-manifold) to computations on specific manifolds (the round sphere, the hyperbolic plane), illustrating how the same algebraic machinery applies across very different geometries.

- [[Ex - Sectional Curvature of the Round Sphere is 1]] (⭐⭐) — Verify $K \equiv 1$ on $S^n$ via the Gauss equation for a hypersurface, recognising the second fundamental form $B = -g$ as the source of constant positive curvature ([[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - Riemann Curvature Tensor]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
- [[Ex - Sectional Curvature of the Hyperbolic Plane is -1]] (⭐⭐) — Verify $K \equiv -1$ on $H^2$ via Cartan's method, exhibiting negative curvature as the source of exponential geodesic divergence ([[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - The Hyperbolic Space H^n]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
- [[Ex - The Ricci Tensor of a 2-Manifold is g times K]] (⭐) — Show $\mathrm{Ric} = K g$ on every $2$-manifold; conclude that the Einstein condition is trivially satisfied (with constant $\lambda$ only forced in dim $\ge 3$ by Schur's lemma) ([[Def - Ricci Tensor]], [[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Def - Einstein Manifold]]).
