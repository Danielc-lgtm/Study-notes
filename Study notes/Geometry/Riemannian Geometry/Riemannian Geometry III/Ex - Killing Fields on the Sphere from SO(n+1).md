# Problem Statement

Let $S^n \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ be the unit $n$-sphere with the round metric induced from $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. The isometry group of $S^n$ is $\mathrm{O}(n+1)$, with the rotation subgroup $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ acting transitively and orientation-preservingly.

(a) For each skew-symmetric matrix $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ (i.e., $A^T = -A$, the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$), define the vector field $X_A$ on $S^n$ by
$$X_A(p) := \text{component of } Ap \text{ tangent to } S^n.$$
Show that $X_A$ is a [[Def - Killing Vector Field|Killing vector field]] on $S^n$.

(b) Show that the assignment $A \mapsto X_A$ is a linear bijection $\mathfrak{so}(n+1) \to \mathfrak{iso}(S^n, g)$ between the Lie algebra of skew matrices and the Lie algebra of Killing fields on $S^n$.

(c) Conclude that the Killing algebra of $S^n$ has dimension $n(n+1)/2$ — the maximum possible by [[Thm - Killing Equation]] — and that this maximum is attained because $S^n$ has constant sectional curvature.

**Recall:**

A [[Def - Killing Vector Field|Killing vector field]] $X$ on $(M, g)$ satisfies $\mathcal{L}_X g = 0$, equivalently $\nabla_a X_b + \nabla_b X_a = 0$ (the [[Thm - Killing Equation|Killing equation]]).

The **Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$** is $\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = \{A \in \mathrm{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R}) : A^T = -A\}$, with $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = \binom{n+1}{2} = n(n+1)/2$.

Every Killing field on $S^n$ generates a $1$-parameter group of isometries (a $1$-parameter subgroup of $\mathrm{O}(n+1)$); the converse is also true by the Lie-algebra-of-Lie-group correspondence.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class:** Identify the Killing fields on a specific Riemannian manifold (the round sphere) with the elements of a specific Lie algebra ($\mathfrak{so}(n+1)$). The map $A \mapsto X_A$ is a Lie-algebra isomorphism realising the Myers–Steenrod identification $\mathfrak{iso}(M, g) = \mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Iso}(M, g))$.

**Assumption pattern:** $S^n$ has the round metric (constant sectional curvature $K = 1$), so it has the maximum possible Killing algebra of dimension $n(n+1)/2$. The natural source of isometries is the $\mathrm{O}(n+1)$-action on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ restricted to the unit sphere; each $1$-parameter subgroup of $\mathrm{O}(n+1)$ — generated by an $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ via $\exp(tA)$ — gives a flow on $S^n$, whose generator is a Killing field.

**Theorem routing:** (a) Verify directly that $X_A$ satisfies the Killing equation, or equivalently that its flow is by isometries. The flow $\phi_t(p) = e^{tA}p$ is a rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, which preserves the Euclidean inner product, hence preserves the induced metric on $S^n$. (b) For linearity, observe $X_{A+B} = X_A + X_B$ and $X_{cA} = cX_A$. For bijectivity, count dimensions: $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = n(n+1)/2 = $ max dim of Killing algebra (by Killing equation theory). So the injective $A \mapsto X_A$ is also surjective. (c) Killing equation gives the dimension bound, and the maximum is attained on constant-sectional-curvature manifolds.

**Key decision point:** The geometric identification "the flow of $X_A$ on $S^n$ is the rotation $\exp(tA)$." This is what makes the calculation work without invoking the Killing equation directly. The standard pitfall: forgetting that the *component of $Ap$ tangent to $S^n$* is automatically tangent (since $\langle Ap, p\rangle = -\langle p, Ap\rangle$ by skew-symmetry, so $Ap$ is automatically in $p^\perp = T_pS^n$). So actually $X_A(p) = Ap$ directly, no projection needed.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> Observe: if $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ (skew matrix) and $p \in S^n$ (unit vector), then $\langle Ap, p\rangle = -\langle p, Ap\rangle = -\langle Ap, p\rangle$, so $\langle Ap, p\rangle = 0$. Therefore $Ap \perp p$, i.e., $Ap \in T_pS^n$ automatically. So $X_A(p) = Ap$ directly, no projection needed.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> The flow of $X_A$ is $\phi_t(p) = e^{tA}p$. Check: $(d/dt)|_{t=0}\phi_t(p) = Ap = X_A(p)$. ✓ Also: $\phi_t$ maps $S^n$ to $S^n$ since $|e^{tA}p| = |p| = 1$ (because $e^{tA} \in \mathrm{O}(n+1)$ — actually $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ for the path-component connected to the identity).

> [!note]- Hint 3
> $\phi_t = e^{tA} : \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is a rotation of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ for each $t$. Rotations preserve the Euclidean inner product. The restriction $\phi_t|_{S^n}$ is an isometry of $S^n$ (with the induced metric). So the flow of $X_A$ is by isometries, hence $X_A$ is Killing.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> Linearity: $X_{A+B} = (A+B)p = Ap + Bp = X_A + X_B$. $X_{cA} = (cA)p = cAp = cX_A$. So $A \mapsto X_A$ is linear.

> [!note]- Hint 5
> Injectivity: $X_A = 0$ everywhere means $Ap = 0$ for every $p \in S^n$. Since the unit vectors span $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, $Av = 0$ for all $v$, i.e., $A = 0$.
> Dimension count: $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = n(n+1)/2$. The Killing algebra of any Riemannian $n$-manifold has dimension $\le n(n+1)/2$ ([[Thm - Killing Equation]]). So the injective map $A \mapsto X_A$ has image of dimension exactly $n(n+1)/2$, which is the entire Killing algebra. Hence bijective.

# Solution

**Plan:** Verify (a) directly that $X_A(p) = Ap$ defines a Killing field by checking the flow $e^{tA}$ is by isometries of $S^n$. (b) Linearity is trivial; bijectivity follows from injectivity (clear) + dimension count $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = n(n+1)/2$ = max possible Killing algebra dim. (c) The dimension matches because $S^n$ has constant sectional curvature, so its isometry group is maximally large.

**Step 1: $X_A(p) = Ap$ is automatically tangent to $S^n$.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> For $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ skew and $p \in S^n$ (unit vector), $\langle Ap, p\rangle = -\langle p, Ap\rangle = -\langle Ap, p\rangle$ (using skew-symmetry $\langle Ax, y\rangle = -\langle x, Ay\rangle$ and symmetry of the inner product). So $\langle Ap, p\rangle = 0$, i.e., $Ap \in p^\perp = T_pS^n$.
>
> The "projection onto $T_pS^n$" in the problem statement is therefore trivial: $X_A(p) = Ap$ (no projection needed). So $X_A$ is a well-defined vector field on $S^n$.

**Step 2: The flow of $X_A$ is the rotation $e^{tA}$ restricted to $S^n$.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Define $\phi_t : S^n \to S^n$ by $\phi_t(p) = e^{tA}p$. Two checks:
> 
> *Well-definedness:* $e^{tA} \in \mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ (since $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ generates a $1$-parameter subgroup of $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$). Hence $|e^{tA}p| = |p| = 1$, and $\phi_t$ maps $S^n$ to $S^n$.
>
> *Initial velocity:* $(d/dt)|_{t=0}\phi_t(p) = (d/dt)|_{t=0}e^{tA}p = Ap = X_A(p)$.
>
> So $\phi_t$ is the flow of $X_A$.

**Step 3: $\phi_t$ is an isometry of $S^n$, hence $X_A$ is Killing.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> $\phi_t = e^{tA} \in \mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ preserves the Euclidean inner product on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$: $\langle e^{tA}v, e^{tA}w\rangle = \langle v, w\rangle$. The round metric on $S^n$ is the *restriction* of the Euclidean inner product to tangent vectors of $S^n$. So $\phi_t$ preserves the round metric: $\phi_t$ is an isometry of $(S^n, g)$.
>
> Since the flow of $X_A$ consists of isometries, $X_A$ is a Killing field (by the equivalence (1) ⟺ (2) of [[Thm - Killing Equation]]).

**Step 4: Linearity of $A \mapsto X_A$.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> $X_{A+B}(p) = (A + B)p = Ap + Bp = X_A(p) + X_B(p)$, and $X_{cA}(p) = (cA)p = cAp = cX_A(p)$. Linearity is immediate.

**Step 5: Bijectivity via dimension count.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Injectivity:* if $X_A = 0$ identically, then $Ap = 0$ for every unit vector $p \in S^n$. The unit vectors span $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, so $Av = 0$ for every $v$, hence $A = 0$.
>
> *Surjectivity (via dimension count):* $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = n(n+1)/2$. By [[Thm - Killing Equation]] (the dimension bound for Killing fields on an $n$-manifold), the Killing algebra $\mathfrak{iso}(S^n, g)$ has dimension at most $n(n+1)/2$. So the injective linear map $A \mapsto X_A : \mathfrak{so}(n+1) \to \mathfrak{iso}(S^n, g)$ between spaces of the same dimension is bijective. (To be careful: we need $\dim\mathfrak{iso}(S^n) \ge n(n+1)/2$ to argue this way. This is automatic because the map is injective into a space of dim $\le n(n+1)/2$, with the domain of dim exactly $n(n+1)/2$.) So $\dim\mathfrak{iso}(S^n) = n(n+1)/2$, achieving the maximum.

**Step 6: The maximum is achieved because $S^n$ has constant sectional curvature.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> By [[Thm - Killing Equation]] (Lemma 5), the Killing algebra has dimension $n(n+1)/2$ iff $(M, g)$ has constant sectional curvature. $S^n$ has $K \equiv 1$ (constant), so the maximum is achieved. The three model spaces — $S^n, \mathbb{R}^n, H^n$ — all have Killing algebras of this maximal dimension (with isometry groups $\mathrm{O}(n+1), \mathbb{R}^n \rtimes \mathrm{O}(n), \mathrm{O}(1, n)$ respectively).

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Part (a).** For $A \in \mathfrak{so}(n+1)$, $X_A(p) = Ap \in T_pS^n$ (since $\langle Ap, p\rangle = 0$ by skew-symmetry). The flow of $X_A$ is $\phi_t = e^{tA} : S^n \to S^n$ (rotation of ambient $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ restricted to $S^n$). Since $e^{tA} \in \mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ preserves the Euclidean inner product, $\phi_t$ is an isometry of $S^n$, so $X_A$ is Killing.
> **Part (b).** Linearity: $X_{A+B} = (A+B)p = X_A + X_B$, $X_{cA} = cX_A$. Injectivity: $X_A = 0 \Rightarrow Ap = 0 \forall p \in S^n \Rightarrow A = 0$ (unit vectors span). Dimension count: $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = n(n+1)/2$, and by Killing equation theory the Killing algebra of $S^n$ has dim $\le n(n+1)/2$, so the injective map is bijective.
> **Part (c).** $\dim\mathfrak{iso}(S^n, g) = n(n+1)/2$ — the maximum possible — because $S^n$ has constant sectional curvature $K = 1$, as forced by Lemma 5 of [[Thm - Killing Equation]].

# Key Takeaways

**The map $A \mapsto X_A$ realises the Myers–Steenrod identification.** The general fact that $\mathfrak{iso}(M, g) = \mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Iso}(M, g))$ becomes concrete on $S^n$: the Killing fields are exactly the infinitesimal generators of rotations of the ambient $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. This pattern generalises to any **homogeneous space** $M = G/H$: the Killing fields are generated by elements of $\mathfrak{g}$ acting on $M$ via the natural $G$-action. The trigger: recognise the underlying Lie-group action, then the Killing algebra is the Lie algebra of the group (modulo the stabiliser).

**Constant sectional curvature ⟺ maximum Killing-algebra dimension.** The condition "$\dim\mathfrak{iso}(M, g) = n(n+1)/2$" picks out exactly the model spaces $S^n$, $\mathbb{R}^n$, $H^n$ (and their space-form quotients, with Killing algebra of the same dim but the *isometry group* possibly smaller). This is **Lemma 5 of [[Thm - Killing Equation]]** and is one of the cleanest characterisations of the model spaces.

**Skew-symmetry of $A$ is exactly the infinitesimal version of orthogonality of $e^{tA}$.** The map $\mathfrak{so}(n+1) = $ Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ is the differential at the identity of $\exp : \mathfrak{so}(n+1) \to \mathrm{SO}(n+1)$. The condition $A^T = -A$ is equivalent (via $e^{tA}(e^{tA})^T = e^{tA}e^{tA^T} = e^{tA - tA} = e^0 = I$) to $e^{tA}$ being orthogonal for all $t$. Tracking which infinitesimal data $A$ corresponds to which $1$-parameter group $e^{tA}$ is the central computational fact of Lie group theory.

**The action of $\mathrm{SO}(n+1)$ on $S^n$ is the prototype of a transitive isometric Lie-group action.** Generalising: every Riemannian homogeneous space $M = G/H$ (where $G$ is a Lie group acting transitively isometrically and $H = \mathrm{Stab}(p_0)$) has Killing algebra $\mathfrak{g}/$(stabiliser) $= \mathfrak{g} \pmod{\mathfrak{h}}$, with dimension $\dim G - \dim H$. For $S^n = \mathrm{SO}(n+1)/\mathrm{SO}(n)$: $\dim\mathrm{SO}(n+1) - \dim\mathrm{SO}(n) = n(n+1)/2 - n(n-1)/2 = n$. Wait, but the Killing algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ itself with dim $n(n+1)/2$ — the discrepancy is because the formula "$\mathfrak{iso} = \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}$" is incorrect; the correct statement is $\mathfrak{iso} = \mathfrak{g}$ (the full Lie algebra of the isometry group). The stabiliser $H$ acts on $T_pM$ via its isotropy representation. For $S^n$: $H = \mathrm{SO}(n)$ acts on $T_pS^n \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ by the standard $\mathrm{SO}(n)$-rep. This is the **isotropic** structure that makes $S^n$ a constant-curvature manifold.
