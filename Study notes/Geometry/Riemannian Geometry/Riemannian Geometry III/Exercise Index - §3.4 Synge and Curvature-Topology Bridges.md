## §3.4 Synge and Curvature–Topology Bridges — Exercises

This section drills the application of **Synge's theorem** to identify simply-connected positively-curved manifolds, and the **Killing-field machinery** that connects continuous isometric symmetries to geodesic conservation laws. The principal techniques are: (a) verifying Synge's hypotheses (compact, orientable, even-real-dim, $K > 0$) and deducing $\pi_1 = 0$; (b) constructing Killing fields on a manifold from its isometric Lie-group action; (c) using a Killing field to extract conserved quantities along geodesics (Noether's theorem). The exercises also drill the parallel-transport eigenvalue argument that lies at the heart of Synge's proof — connecting linear algebra of orthogonal matrices to global topology of Riemannian manifolds.

- [[Ex - Bonnet-Myers Gives a Finite Fundamental Group of CP^n]] (⭐⭐) — Apply Bonnet–Myers + Synge to $\mathbb{CP}^n$ ($K \ge 1/4$, even real dim, orientable Kähler), deducing $\pi_1 = 0$ from positive sectional curvature ([[Thm - Synge's Theorem]], [[Thm - Bonnet-Myers Theorem]], [[Def - Sectional Curvature]]).
- [[Ex - Killing Fields on the Sphere from SO(n+1)]] (⭐⭐) — Identify the Killing algebra of $S^n$ with $\mathfrak{so}(n+1)$ via the explicit map $A \mapsto X_A(p) = Ap$; verify the dimension count $n(n+1)/2$ matches the maximum-symmetry condition ([[Def - Killing Vector Field]], [[Thm - Killing Equation]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
- [[Ex - Sectional Curvature of the Round Sphere is 1]] (⭐⭐) — The model calibration: $S^n$ has $K \equiv 1$, satisfies Synge's hypotheses in even dim ($S^{2n}$), and Synge correctly gives $\pi_1(S^{2n}) = 0$. The companion $\mathbb{RP}^{2n}$ shows orientability is essential ([[Def - Sectional Curvature]], [[Thm - Synge's Theorem]], [[Def - Constant Sectional Curvature]]).
