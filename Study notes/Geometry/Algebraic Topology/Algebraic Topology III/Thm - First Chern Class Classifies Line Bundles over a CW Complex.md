# Notation

$X$ is a CW complex (or more generally a paracompact Hausdorff space with the homotopy type of a CW complex). $L \to X$ is a complex line bundle (complex vector bundle of rank 1). $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ is the **Picard group** of $X$: the set of isomorphism classes of complex line bundles on $X$, with group operation tensor product. $c_1(L) \in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is the [[Def - First Chern Class|first Chern class]]. $BU(1) = \mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ is the classifying space of $U(1)$. See [[Algebraic Topology III — Higher Homotopy and Chern Forms]] for the full notation registry.

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (Classification of Line Bundles by $c_1$).** Let $X$ be a CW complex (or any space of CW homotopy type). The first Chern class map
> $$c_1 : \mathrm{Pic}(X) \to H^2(X; \mathbb{Z}), \qquad L \mapsto c_1(L),$$
> is a **group isomorphism** (where $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ has tensor product as group law and $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ has addition).

> **Equivalent formulations:**
>
> 1. **Classifying-space form:** $\mathrm{Pic}(X) \cong [X, \mathbb{CP}^\infty]$ (homotopy classes of based maps), and $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ represents $H^2(\cdot; \mathbb{Z})$, so $[X, \mathbb{CP}^\infty] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.
>
> 2. **Cohomological form:** Every $\alpha \in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is the first Chern class of a unique line bundle (up to isomorphism).
>
> 3. **Sheaf-cohomological form:** $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = H^1(X; \mathcal{O}^*) \xrightarrow{c_1} H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism (using $\mathcal{O}^* = $ sheaf of $\mathbb{C}^*$-valued functions; for smooth $X$, equivalently the sheaf of $S^1 = U(1)$-valued functions).

> **Corollary.** $\mathrm{Pic}(S^2) = H^2(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$. So *every* complex line bundle on $S^2$ is $\mathcal{O}(n) = \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes n}$ for some integer $n$, distinguished by $n = c_1$.

> **Caveat (algebraic vs topological).** For a complex *algebraic* variety $X$, the **algebraic Picard group** $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ classifies holomorphic line bundles. There is a map $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X) \to \mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{top}}(X) = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$, with kernel the **Picard variety** $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$ — typically a complex torus. The classification theorem above is for *topological* line bundles only.

# Motivation

This theorem is the cleanest classification result in characteristic class theory and the prototype for higher-rank statements. It says: *complex line bundles on a topological space are completely classified by a single cohomology class*. Two line bundles are isomorphic iff they have the same first Chern class; every integer cohomology class in degree 2 arises from some line bundle. The map $c_1$ is a perfect bijection.

The motivating question is: *what is the simplest topological invariant that distinguishes complex line bundles?* For very simple spaces — $S^2$, $\mathbb{CP}^n$, tori — there is a one-parameter family of "obviously different" line bundles, indexed by an integer (the "degree"). The theorem says this integer is in fact the *complete* invariant: there is nothing else to distinguish.

The structural reason is the classifying space. $U(1)$ acts on $S^\infty$ freely (by scalar multiplication on $\mathbb{C}^\infty$), and the quotient is $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$. The total space $S^\infty$ is contractible. So $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ is the classifying space $BU(1)$, and the natural map $BU(1) \to K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ is a *homotopy equivalence* because both have $\pi_2 = \mathbb{Z}$ and all other homotopy zero. So $BU(1) = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$, line bundles on $X$ are maps $X \to BU(1) = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$, and these are exactly cohomology classes in $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.

This identification is one of the great unifications in algebraic topology: it puts together
- the geometric notion of line bundle,
- the homotopy-theoretic notion of map to a classifying space,
- the cohomological notion of integer cohomology class,
- the differential-geometric notion of $c_1 = F/(2\pi)$ where $F$ is a $U(1)$ curvature,

into a single object: **the isomorphism class of a complex line bundle on $X$**.

The classification is *complete* for line bundles. For higher rank, $c$ classifies *stably* — two bundles with the same total Chern class are isomorphic up to stabilisation by trivial bundles — but not strictly. There can be inequivalent rank-2 bundles with the same Chern classes (distinguished by torsion in homotopy of $BU(2)$, the **Eilenberg–MacLane invariants**). The line-bundle case is the cleanest because $BU(1)$ has no torsion.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The theorem applies whenever a space is given as a CW complex (or has CW homotopy type, equivalently is paracompact Hausdorff and has CW model).

**Source 1: smooth manifold.** Every smooth manifold has CW homotopy type (by Morse theory or by triangulation). So the theorem applies to all smooth manifolds, including spheres, projective spaces, Lie groups, and Riemannian manifolds.

**Source 2: simplicial complex.** Simplicial complexes are CW complexes by construction. So the theorem applies to all polyhedra and triangulations.

**Source 3: a specific cohomology class.** Given $\alpha \in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$, the theorem promises a line bundle $L_\alpha$ with $c_1(L_\alpha) = \alpha$. So the source is "I have a 2-class and want a bundle", and the output is the bundle (constructed via the classifying map $X \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ representing $\alpha$).

**Source 4: a $U(1)$ connection with given curvature.** If $F$ is a closed 2-form on $X$ with integer periods $\int_z F \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ for all integer 2-cycles, then by the **Weil integrality criterion** there is a $U(1)$ bundle with $F$ as curvature, and $c_1 = [F/(2\pi)]$. So the source is "I have a 2-form with integer periods", and the output is the bundle.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The conclusion $c_1$ is a bijection unlocks:

**Target 1: line bundle classification on specific spaces.** For $S^2$: $\mathrm{Pic} = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by Hopf bundle. For $T^n$: $\mathrm{Pic} = H^2(T^n; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^{\binom{n}{2}}$. For $\mathbb{CP}^n$: $\mathrm{Pic} = \mathbb{Z}$. For Riemann surface $\Sigma_g$ of genus $g$: $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{top}} = \mathbb{Z}$, $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{alg}} = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{C}^g/\Lambda$ (Jacobian variety).

**Target 2: Dirac quantisation in physics.** The first Chern class on $S^2$ surrounding a magnetic monopole must be an integer, so monopole charge is quantised: $g = (\hbar c/2e) n$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Target 3: classification of $U(1)$ gauge configurations.** All inequivalent $U(1)$ gauge fields on a space are parameterised by $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ (modulo gauge transformations). Different sectors correspond to different first Chern classes — different topological charges.

**Target 4: TKNN integers in condensed matter.** The Hall conductivity of a 2D electron system is $\sigma_{xy} = (e^2/h) \int c_1$ for the Bloch bundle on the Brillouin torus. The classification of band structures by first Chern class is the topological classification of the integer quantum Hall effect.

**Target 5: divisors on Riemann surfaces.** Holomorphic line bundles on a Riemann surface $\Sigma$ correspond to divisors $\sum n_i p_i$ (modulo linear equivalence), and $c_1$ corresponds to the *degree* $\sum n_i$. Divisor theory is the algebraic-geometric realisation of $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{top}}$.

# Why Is It True

**The one-line mechanism:** *$U(1)$ acts freely on $S^\infty$ (contractible) with quotient $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$, so line bundles on $X$ are maps $X \to K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$, which are exactly elements of $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.*

The proof is the **classifying-space argument**, which has three steps:

**Step 1: $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ is the classifying space $BU(1)$.** $U(1)$ acts freely on $S^{2n+1} \subset \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ by scalar multiplication, with quotient $\mathbb{CP}^n$. As $n \to \infty$, we get $S^\infty$ (the unit sphere in $\mathbb{C}^\infty$, which is contractible) with quotient $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$. So $S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ is a principal $U(1)$-bundle with contractible total space — by definition, this is $EU(1) \to BU(1)$.

**Step 2: $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$.** From the long exact sequence of the fibration $U(1) \to S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$:
- $\pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \pi_{k-1}(U(1)) = \pi_{k-1}(S^1)$ for $k \geq 1$.
- $\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \pi_0(S^1) = 0$ (since $S^1$ is connected).
- $\pi_2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$.
- $\pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \pi_{k-1}(S^1) = 0$ for $k \geq 3$.

So $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ has $\pi_2 = \mathbb{Z}$ and all other homotopy zero — it is the Eilenberg–MacLane space $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$.

**Step 3: $[X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.** This is the **Brown representability** theorem (or the explicit construction: $K(\pi, n)$ represents $H^n(\cdot; \pi)$ for abelian $\pi$). The bijection is by associating to a map $f : X \to K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ the pullback $f^* \iota \in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$, where $\iota \in H^2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2); \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ is the canonical generator.

Combining:
$$\mathrm{Pic}(X) = [X, BU(1)] = [X, \mathbb{CP}^\infty] = [X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z}),$$
and the bijection sends a line bundle to its first Chern class.

For the **group structure**: tensor product of line bundles corresponds to the multiplication on $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ coming from its $H$-space structure, which in turn corresponds to addition in $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2) = \pi_2$. So tensor product becomes addition in $H^2$, recovering $c_1(L \otimes L') = c_1(L) + c_1(L')$.

# What Makes This Hard

The hardest step is **Step 3: $[X, K(\pi, n)] = H^n(X; \pi)$**, the Brown representability theorem. This requires a non-trivial spectral-sequence or homotopy-theoretic argument to identify homotopy classes of maps to an Eilenberg–MacLane space with cohomology classes. The construction is: given a class $\alpha \in H^n(X; \pi)$, build a map $X \to K(\pi, n)$ by extending cell by cell, using the universal property of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces ($\pi_n$ is the only non-vanishing homotopy group). The verification of bijectivity requires obstruction theory.

A second difficulty is the identification of $c_1$ with the pullback of the universal class $\iota \in H^2(BU(1); \mathbb{Z})$. The Chern–Weil construction gives $c_1$ as the curvature; the classifying-space construction gives $c_1$ as a pullback. The agreement of these is the **Chern–Weil = classifying-space** theorem, a non-trivial fact that requires showing the de Rham realisation of the universal class equals the curvature 2-form of the universal connection (the **Maurer–Cartan form** on $EU(1) = S^\infty$).

A third subtlety is the relationship with algebraic geometry. On a complex variety $X$, the **algebraic** Picard group classifies *holomorphic* line bundles, which is a strictly smaller class than topological line bundles in general (the discrepancy is the **Picard variety** $\mathrm{Pic}^0$). The theorem above is purely topological.

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:**
Show that $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ is both $BU(1)$ (the classifying space of complex line bundles) and $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ (the Eilenberg–MacLane space representing $H^2(\cdot; \mathbb{Z})$). These two identifications give $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = [X, BU(1)] = [X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **$\mathbb{CP}^\infty = BU(1)$.** Show $U(1)$ acts freely on $S^\infty$ with contractible total space and quotient $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$. This makes $S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ a universal $U(1)$-bundle.
   - *Hint:* $S^\infty$ is the unit sphere in $\mathbb{C}^\infty$, contractible by the linear homotopy $(1-t) v / |(1-t)v|$ slid to a fixed point at $t = 1$.
   - *Why needed:* Identifies $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ with the classifying space for line bundles.

2. **$\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$.** Compute $\pi_*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty)$ using the long exact sequence of the fibration $U(1) \to S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ and conclude $\pi_2 = \mathbb{Z}$, all others zero.

3. **$[X, K(\pi, n)] = H^n(X; \pi)$ (Brown representability).** Use the fact that $K(\pi, n)$ classifies cohomology: the universal class $\iota \in H^n(K(\pi, n); \pi)$ corresponds to the identity homomorphism via Hurewicz, and pullback by maps $X \to K(\pi, n)$ gives all cohomology classes.

4. **Identify $c_1$ with the pullback.** Show that the differential-geometric $c_1$ (curvature/2$\pi$) agrees with the classifying-space pullback $f_L^* \iota$. This is the Chern–Weil identification at the universal level.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = BU(1)$
> **Statement:** $U(1)$ acts freely on $S^\infty$ with quotient $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$, making $S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ a universal principal $U(1)$-bundle.
>
> **Hint:** $S^\infty \subset \mathbb{C}^\infty$ is contractible (linear-deformation argument). The $U(1)$ scalar action is free and the quotient is the union of $\mathbb{CP}^n$, giving $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$.
>
> **Why needed:** Identifies $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ with the classifying space.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > The unit sphere $S^{2n+1} \subset \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ has a free $U(1)$ action by scalar multiplication $\lambda \cdot v = \lambda v$ for $\lambda \in U(1)$. The quotient is $\mathbb{CP}^n$. As $n \to \infty$, $S^\infty = \bigcup_n S^{2n+1}$ is the unit sphere in $\mathbb{C}^\infty$, contractible by the linear homotopy $H(v, t) = ((1-t)v + t e_0)/\|((1-t)v + t e_0)\|$ where $e_0 = (1, 0, 0, \ldots)$ — this is well-defined for $v \neq -e_0$ and can be globalised. The quotient is $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$. By definition, a principal $G$-bundle with contractible total space is a universal bundle, so $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = BU(1)$.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$
> **Statement:** $\pi_2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = 0$ for $k \neq 2$.
>
> **Hint:** Use the long exact sequence of $U(1) \to S^\infty \to \mathbb{CP}^\infty$, with $S^\infty$ contractible (so $\pi_k(S^\infty) = 0$ for all $k$).
>
> **Why needed:** Identifies $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ as an Eilenberg–MacLane space.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > From the long exact sequence with $S^\infty$ contractible:
> > $$\cdots \to \pi_k(S^\infty) = 0 \to \pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) \to \pi_{k-1}(U(1)) \to \pi_{k-1}(S^\infty) = 0 \to \cdots$$
> >
> > So $\pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) \cong \pi_{k-1}(U(1)) = \pi_{k-1}(S^1)$ for $k \geq 1$.
> >
> > $\pi_0(S^1) = 0$ (connected), so $\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = 0$.
> > $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$, so $\pi_2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \mathbb{Z}$.
> > $\pi_{k-1}(S^1) = 0$ for $k \geq 3$, so $\pi_k(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = 0$.
> >
> > Therefore $\pi_*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty)$ is concentrated in degree 2, equal to $\mathbb{Z}$. This is the defining property of $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$.

> [!note]- Lemma 3: $[X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$
> **Statement:** For any CW complex $X$, homotopy classes of based maps $X \to K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ are in bijection with $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.
>
> **Hint:** This is the **Brown representability** theorem at degree 2. The bijection sends $f \mapsto f^* \iota$, where $\iota \in H^2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2); \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ is the fundamental class.
>
> **Why needed:** Connects classifying-space picture to cohomology.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof (sketch)
> > Both functors $[\cdot, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)]$ and $H^2(\cdot; \mathbb{Z})$ are *representable* and *contravariant*, sending CW-pair Mayer–Vietoris sequences to exact sequences. Both satisfy the wedge axiom: $[\bigvee X_i, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = \prod [X_i, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)]$ etc. By Brown representability, two contravariant functors satisfying these axioms and agreeing on a single point (both are $\mathbb{Z}$ on $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ itself, with the universal class $\iota$) must be naturally isomorphic. Constructively, the universal class $\iota \in H^2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2))$ is obtained by Hurewicz: $H_2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2); \mathbb{Z}) = \pi_2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)) = \mathbb{Z}$, dually $H^2(K(\mathbb{Z}, 2); \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, and $\iota$ is the canonical generator. The bijection is $f \mapsto f^*\iota$.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Combine Lemmas 1, 2, 3.
>
> By Lemma 1, $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = BU(1)$ is the classifying space for complex line bundles. So $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = [X, \mathbb{CP}^\infty]$ (homotopy classes of based maps).
>
> By Lemma 2, $\mathbb{CP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)$ as a homotopy type.
>
> By Lemma 3, $[X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.
>
> Composing: $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = [X, BU(1)] = [X, K(\mathbb{Z}, 2)] = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.
>
> The map $L \mapsto c_1(L)$ realises this bijection because (i) the classifying map $f_L : X \to BU(1)$ pulls back the universal line bundle to $L$, and (ii) the universal class $\iota \in H^2(BU(1); \mathbb{Z})$ is the first Chern class of the universal bundle, so $f_L^* \iota = c_1(L)$.
>
> The group structure: tensor product of line bundles corresponds to the H-space structure on $BU(1) = \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ (concatenation of classifying maps), which on the Eilenberg–MacLane side is addition. So $c_1(L \otimes L') = c_1(L) + c_1(L')$, confirming the isomorphism of groups.
>
> $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Picard group of $\mathbb{CP}^n$.** $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^n; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the hyperplane class $h$. So $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^n) = \mathbb{Z}$, with the bundles $\mathcal{O}(n) = \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes n}$ giving all isomorphism classes.

**Riemann surface.** For a Riemann surface $\Sigma_g$, $H^2(\Sigma_g; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, so $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{top}}(\Sigma_g) = \mathbb{Z}$ (classified by degree). The full algebraic $\mathrm{Pic}(\Sigma_g)$ includes the Jacobian variety as kernel — a complex torus of dimension $g$ — capturing the "moduli" of degree-zero line bundles.

**Magnetic monopole.** A unit-charge monopole at the origin of $\mathbb{R}^3$ defines a $U(1)$ bundle on $S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{0\}$. The bundle is classified by $c_1 \in H^2(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$, and the monopole charge is exactly this integer (Dirac quantisation).

**Topological insulator.** The Bloch bundle of a 2D electronic system on the Brillouin torus $T^2$ has filled-band sub-bundle classified by $c_1 \in H^2(T^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, the **TKNN integer**, which equals the Hall conductivity in units of $e^2/h$.

# Bridges

- **Eilenberg–MacLane spaces and cohomology.** The fundamental fact is $K(\pi, n)$ represents $H^n(\cdot; \pi)$. For $\pi = \mathbb{Z}$, $n = 2$: $K(\mathbb{Z}, 2) = \mathbb{CP}^\infty$. This identification turns "find a bundle with given $c_1$" into "find a map to $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ representing the given cohomology class" — a topological question with no analytic content. The miracle of identifying $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ with the classifying space of $U(1)$ is what makes the theorem work.

- **Sheaf-theoretic Picard.** In sheaf-theoretic language, $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = H^1(X; \mathcal{O}^*)$ (Čech cohomology of the sheaf of invertible elements). The exponential exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}^* \to 0$ gives a long exact sequence in cohomology, and the connecting map $H^1(X; \mathcal{O}^*) \to H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is precisely $c_1$. For a smooth manifold, $\mathcal{O}$ is fine (smooth functions, partition of unity) so $H^k(X; \mathcal{O}) = 0$ for $k \geq 1$, and the connecting map is an isomorphism. For a complex variety, $\mathcal{O}$ is the structure sheaf of holomorphic functions and is not fine; the kernel of $c_1$ is the Picard variety $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X) = H^1(X; \mathcal{O})/H^1(X; \mathbb{Z})$.

- **Divisor theory.** On a smooth complex projective variety $X$, divisors $\sum n_i Y_i$ (formal sums of codimension-1 subvarieties) modulo linear equivalence (rational functions) form the divisor class group $\mathrm{Cl}(X)$, isomorphic to $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ via the construction "the line bundle of meromorphic functions with prescribed zeros and poles". The first Chern class $c_1(L)$ corresponds to the *degree* of the divisor — the simplest invariant. Algebraic geometry studies the full $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$; algebraic topology only sees the topological quotient $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$.

- **Holomorphic vs topological.** The map $\mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X) \to \mathrm{Pic}^{\mathrm{top}}(X) = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is surjective for projective varieties with $H^2 = $ Néron–Severi rank (the **Lefschetz theorem on (1,1)-classes**), but the kernel is the **Picard variety** $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$, a complex abelian variety encoding the *complex* moduli of topologically trivial line bundles. So the theorem above classifies bundles only up to *continuous deformation*; for finer holomorphic classification, $\mathrm{Pic}^0$ enters.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Classifying Spaces for Higher Rank Bundles *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> The classification result generalises: complex rank-$n$ bundles on a CW complex $X$ are classified by $[X, BU(n)]$, where $BU(n)$ is the classifying space of $U(n)$ (the colimit of complex Grassmannians $\mathrm{Gr}(n, N)$ as $N \to \infty$). However, $BU(n)$ for $n \geq 2$ is *not* an Eilenberg–MacLane space — it has homotopy in multiple degrees — so the classification is *not* by a single cohomology class. The total Chern class $c \in H^{\mathrm{even}}(X; \mathbb{Z})$ is a *necessary* but not always *sufficient* invariant; finer invariants from $\pi_*(BU(n))$ enter. For *stable* bundles (after adding trivial factors), Chern classes are a complete invariant; this is the foundation of complex K-theory.

> [!tip] Universal Coefficient Theorem and Torsion *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ classifies line bundles, but $H^2$ can have torsion. A torsion class $\alpha$ of order $n$ corresponds to a line bundle $L$ with $L^{\otimes n}$ trivial — a **torsion line bundle**. These exist on spaces with torsion in $H^2$: $\mathbb{RP}^\infty$ has $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}/2$, giving a non-trivial 2-torsion line bundle (the real line bundle's complexification, although the real line bundle itself is classified by $w_1 \in H^1(\mathbb{RP}^\infty; \mathbb{Z}/2)$). Lens spaces $L(n; 1, \ldots, 1)$ have $\mathbb{Z}/n$ in $H^2$.

> [!tip] Gerbes and Higher Bundles *(from Higher Category Theory)*
> The classification $\mathrm{Pic}(X) = H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ generalises to higher gerbes: 2-gerbes are classified by $H^3(X; \mathbb{Z})$, $n$-gerbes by $H^{n+1}(X; \mathbb{Z})$. A gerbe is the higher-categorical analogue of a line bundle, and the theory is the foundation of **B-field** physics in string theory: the **NS-NS B-field** is a 2-gerbe with curvature $H \in H^3(M; \mathbb{Z})$. Higher gerbes appear in M-theory as **C-fields** ($H^4$) and so on.
