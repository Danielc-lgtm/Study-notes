## §3.2 Fibrations and the Long Exact Sequence — Exercises

This section drills the central computational technique of higher homotopy: the long exact sequence of a fibration. The Hopf fibration $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$ is the canonical example, producing the spectacular fact $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ and, more broadly, the identification $\pi_k(S^2) = \pi_k(S^3)$ for $k \geq 3$. The reusable skills are: **identifying a fibration relevant to a homotopy question**, **systematically writing out the long exact sequence at every degree**, and **recognising trivial-group sandwiches that produce isomorphisms**. The same patterns recur in $SU(n-1) \to SU(n) \to S^{2n-1}$ (Bott periodicity), $\Omega X \to PX \to X$ (loop-space adjunction), and every principal-bundle computation in gauge theory.

- [[Ex - Pi_3 of S^2 is Z via the Hopf Map]] (⭐⭐) — Use the long exact sequence of the Hopf fibration to compute $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Hopf map itself. The cleanest demonstration of the long-exact-sequence machinery. ([[Def - The Hopf Map]], [[Thm - Long Exact Sequence of a Fibration]], [[Def - Higher Homotopy Group]])

- [[Ex - Long Exact Sequence of the Hopf Fibration]] (⭐⭐⭐) — Write down the *full* long exact sequence at every degree, deduce $\pi_2(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_k(S^2) = \pi_k(S^3)$ for $k \geq 3$. The structural picture: the Hopf fibration identifies all higher homotopy of $S^2$ with that of $S^3$ — a striking and non-obvious equality. ([[Def - The Hopf Map]], [[Thm - Long Exact Sequence of a Fibration]], [[Def - Higher Homotopy Group]])

- [[Ex - Pi_n of S^n is Z]] (⭐⭐) — Although filed under §3.1, this exercise also illustrates fibration-style reasoning when $n = 1$: the long exact sequence of the covering $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to S^1$ gives $\pi_k(S^1) = 0$ for $k \geq 2$, the key input to all Hopf-fibration computations. ([[Def - Higher Homotopy Group]], [[Thm - Hurewicz Theorem (Statement)]])
