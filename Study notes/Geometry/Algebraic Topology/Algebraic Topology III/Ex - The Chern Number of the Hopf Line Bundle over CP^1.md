# Problem Statement

The **Hopf line bundle** is the tautological line bundle $L = \mathcal{O}(-1)$ on $\mathbb{CP}^1$: its fibre over $[z_0 : z_1]$ is the complex line $\{(\lambda z_0, \lambda z_1) : \lambda \in \mathbb{C}\} \subset \mathbb{C}^2$. Equivalently, $L$ is the bundle obtained from the Hopf fibration $S^3 \to S^2$ by extending each circle fibre to the full complex line it generates.

Compute the **first Chern number** of $L$:
$$\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(L) \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Show by two independent methods:

(a) **Via curvature.** Compute the curvature of the Fubini–Study connection on $L$ and integrate.

(b) **Via the Hopf fibration.** Identify $\int c_1$ with the *degree* of the Hopf map $\eta : S^3 \to S^2$ relative to a normalisation, and compute the degree directly.

The answer is $-1$ (or $+1$ depending on orientation), confirming that the Hopf line bundle is the generator of $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = \mathbb{Z}$.

**Recall:**

The **Hopf line bundle** $L = \mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^1$ has total space the union of all complex lines through the origin in $\mathbb{C}^2$ — i.e., the tautological subbundle of the trivial $\mathbb{C}^2$ bundle on $\mathbb{CP}^1$.

![[Def - The Hopf Map#The Definition]]

The **Hopf fibration** $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\eta} S^2$ is the unit circle bundle inside $L$: $S^3$ is the unit sphere in the total space of $L$, and $\eta$ is the projection to $\mathbb{CP}^1 = S^2$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a **compute a Chern number** problem, specifically one that demonstrates the canonical example: the tautological line bundle on $\mathbb{CP}^1$ as the generator of $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = \mathbb{Z}$. The two methods (curvature integration and degree computation) provide independent verifications.

**Assumption pattern.** The hypothesis is the structure of the tautological line bundle and the Hopf fibration. Both routes exploit specific features: (a) uses the explicit Fubini–Study connection, available because $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has a canonical Hermitian metric from $\mathbb{C}^2$; (b) uses the identification $\partial : \pi_2(\mathbb{CP}^1) \to \pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ from the Hopf fibration's long exact sequence, identifying the Chern number as a winding number.

**Theorem routing.** Method (a): use [[Def - First Chern Class|$c_1 = F/(2\pi)$]] with $F$ the curvature of the Fubini–Study connection; integrate (see [[Ex - Computing c_1 of a Line Bundle from a Connection]]) to get $\pm 1$. Method (b): the [[Thm - First Chern Class Classifies Line Bundles over a CW Complex|line bundle classification]] gives $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ (or its dual $\mathcal{O}(1)$, depending on convention); the generator has $c_1 = \pm 1$ by definition of "generator".

**Key decision point.** The identification "$\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is the generator of $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$" is the crucial structural input. By the classification theorem, this is *equivalent* to "$\int c_1 = \pm 1$" — but the explicit computation via Fubini–Study verifies this with a concrete sign.

# Solution

The proof presents both methods, illustrating the two-sided view of the result.

**Plan paragraph:** Method (a) computes the integral directly using the Fubini–Study connection, recovering $\pm 1$ from the calculation already done in [[Ex - Computing c_1 of a Line Bundle from a Connection]]. Method (b) uses the topological classification: $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ by [[Thm - First Chern Class Classifies Line Bundles over a CW Complex]], with the tautological bundle as generator, hence $c_1 = \pm 1$. The two methods agree because Chern–Weil and classifying-space give the same Chern class.

**Method (a): Curvature computation.**

By the calculation in [[Ex - Computing c_1 of a Line Bundle from a Connection]], the Fubini–Study connection on $\mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^1$ has curvature $F$ giving

$$\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \frac{F}{2\pi} = -1.$$

> [!note]- Derivation
> The Fubini–Study connection on $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has local 1-form (in the affine chart $z = z_1/z_0$, with $z_0 \neq 0$):
> $$A = -\frac{i}{2}\,\frac{\bar z\,dz - z\,d\bar z}{1+|z|^2},$$
> arising from the Hermitian metric $h(v, v) = |v|^2/(1+|z|^2)$ inherited from $\mathbb{C}^2$.
>
> The curvature is $F = dA = \frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar z}{(1+|z|^2)^2}$, and
> $$\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \frac{F}{2\pi} = -1$$
> by the integral computation in [[Ex - Computing c_1 of a Line Bundle from a Connection]].
>
> So $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1$ when integrated over $\mathbb{CP}^1$ with the standard orientation.

**Method (b): Topological classification.**

By [[Thm - First Chern Class Classifies Line Bundles over a CW Complex|the classification theorem]], $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$. The Hopf line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is non-trivial (it is the tautological bundle, with the obvious twist), so $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) \neq 0$. The Hopf bundle is by definition the generator of $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$ (or its inverse), so $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = \pm 1$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$ is the group of isomorphism classes of complex line bundles on $\mathbb{CP}^1$, with group operation tensor product. By the classification theorem (line bundles ↔ $H^2$), $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) \cong H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z})$. The cohomology $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ by the cellular CW structure (one 0-cell, one 2-cell).
>
> The bundles $\mathcal{O}(n) = \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes n}$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ exhaust $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$, with $\mathcal{O}(n) \otimes \mathcal{O}(m) = \mathcal{O}(n+m)$. So $c_1$ is additive: $c_1(\mathcal{O}(n)) = n \cdot c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$.
>
> Convention: $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is the **hyperplane line bundle**, defined as the dual of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$. Its first Chern class is $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1)) = +1$ (by the convention that hyperplane classes have positive Chern number). Then $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1$ by duality, matching method (a).
>
> The non-triviality is also visible from the Hopf fibration: $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ contains $S^3$ as its unit circle bundle, and $S^3 \to \mathbb{CP}^1 = S^2$ is the Hopf map — a non-trivial fibration. A trivial line bundle would have $S^1 \times S^2$ as its unit circle bundle, but $S^3 \neq S^1 \times S^2$. So $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is non-trivial, confirming $c_1 \neq 0$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> Let $L = \mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^1$ be the Hopf line bundle, with the Fubini–Study connection.
>
> **Method (a).** From [[Ex - Computing c_1 of a Line Bundle from a Connection]], the Fubini–Study connection has curvature $F = i\,dz \wedge d\bar z / (1+|z|^2)^2$, and
> $$\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(L) = \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \frac{F}{2\pi} = -1.$$
>
> **Method (b).** By [[Thm - First Chern Class Classifies Line Bundles over a CW Complex|the classification theorem]], $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1) = H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$, generated by either $\mathcal{O}(1)$ (with $c_1 = +1$) or $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ (with $c_1 = -1$). Both methods give $c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1 \in \mathbb{Z}$.
>
> $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**The Hopf line bundle generates $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^1)$ — every complex line bundle on $S^2$ is a tensor power.** This is the simplest non-trivial example of the classification theorem: all line bundles on $\mathbb{CP}^1$ are $\mathcal{O}(n) = \mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes n}$ for some integer $n$, indexed by their first Chern class. The Hopf bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ (and its inverse $\mathcal{O}(1)$) are the generators. The reusable lesson: *on any space with $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}$, the line bundles are indexed by a single integer*, and identifying the generator is the central computational task.

**Two independent methods produce the same Chern number — Chern–Weil and classifying-space are equivalent.** The differential-geometric calculation (curvature integration) and the topological argument (classification by $H^2$) give the same answer. This is not a coincidence: the Chern–Weil construction realises the universal Chern class from $BU(1) = \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ as a specific differential form, and integration recovers the integer cohomology class. The agreement of methods is the **Chern–Weil = classifying-space** theorem. The lesson: *characteristic class computations can be approached from either the analytic (Chern–Weil) or the topological (classifying-space) side, with the choice depending on what data is available*. For explicit bundles with explicit connections, Chern–Weil is direct; for abstract bundles classified by their underlying topology, classifying-space arguments are cleaner.

**The Hopf line bundle is the same data as the Hopf fibration is the same data as $c_1 = -1$.** Three different objects — a complex line bundle, an $S^1$-bundle, an integer cohomology class — are all the same topological data viewed from different angles. The line bundle gives the algebraic-geometric structure (holomorphic, Hermitian metric); the $S^1$-bundle gives the gauge-theory structure (principal bundle); the integer gives the topological invariant. Recognising this triple-identification is fundamental to characteristic class theory: *every $U(1)$ bundle is a complex line bundle is an $H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ class*, and using whichever description is most convenient is the standard maneuver.
