## §2.4 Applications: Orientability, Lie Groups, and Riemannian Geometry — Exercises

This section drills the *applications* of the fundamental group and covering-space theory to other parts of geometry and topology: orientability obstructions, universal covering groups of Lie groups, fixed-point theorems, and the curvature-controls-topology principle (Myers' theorem). The recurring techniques: using the orientation character to detect non-orientability via $\pi_1$, using the universal-covering-group construction for Lie groups, using functoriality of $\pi_1$ as an obstruction tool (no-retraction → Brouwer fixed-point), and translating geometric data (curvature bounds) into algebraic constraints on $\pi_1$. These exercises connect $\pi_1$ to differential geometry, Lie theory, and fixed-point theorems, and prepare the reader for the curvature-and-topology theorems of [[Riemannian Geometry III — Riemann Curvature and Topology]].

- [[Ex - The Orientable Double Cover of the Möbius Strip]] (⭐) — explicit construction of the orientable double cover of a non-orientable surface; orientation character $w_1$ as the index-2 subgroup ([[Def - Orientable Double Cover]], [[Def - Covering Space]])
- [[Ex - Pi_1 of RP^n is Z over 2 for n at least 2]] (⭐⭐) — $\mathbb{RP}^n$ as $S^n / \{\pm 1\}$, with the universal cover the simply-connected sphere, giving $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}/2$ for $n \geq 2$ ([[Def - Universal Cover]], [[Def - Simply Connected Space]], [[Thm - Galois Correspondence for Covering Spaces]])
- [[Ex - The Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem in Dimension 2 via Pi_1]] (⭐⭐) — functoriality of $\pi_1$ applied to a hypothetical retraction $D^2 \to S^1$; the trivial $\pi_1$ of $D^2$ vs. $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ gives the contradiction ([[Thm - Pi_1 of S^1 is Z]], [[Def - Continuous Map]])
- [[Ex - Pi_1 of a Topological Group is Abelian]] (⭐⭐) — Eckmann-Hilton argument: $\pi_1$ of any topological group (in particular every Lie group) is abelian; consequence: $\pi_1$ of $\mathrm{SO}(n), \mathrm{U}(n)$, tori are all abelian ([[Def - Path-Product and the Fundamental Group]], [[Def - Group]])
