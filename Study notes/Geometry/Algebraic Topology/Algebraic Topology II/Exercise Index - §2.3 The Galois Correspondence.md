## §2.3 The Galois Correspondence — Exercises

This section drills the heart of the chapter: the bijection between connected covers of $X$ and subgroups of $\pi_1(X)$. The exercises here focus on the **constructive direction** (build covers from subgroups, and recognise universal covers from explicit constructions) and on showing how non-trivial $\pi_1$ structure manifests geometrically. The flagship is the figure-eight, whose universal cover is the infinite 4-valent tree — the Cayley graph of $F_2$ — making the algebra and geometry of free groups visible simultaneously. The recurring techniques: identifying universal covers via simply-connectedness, computing deck groups from the cover's symmetries, and using the correspondence to switch between algebraic (subgroup) and geometric (cover) sides.

- [[Ex - The Universal Cover of the Figure-Eight is the Cayley Graph of F_2]] (⭐⭐⭐) — flagship: construct the infinite 4-valent tree as the universal cover of $S^1 \vee S^1$ and identify it with the Cayley graph of $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$ ([[Def - Universal Cover]], [[Def - Deck Transformation Group]], [[Def - Free Group and Free Product]], [[Thm - Seifert-van Kampen Theorem (Statement)]])
- [[Ex - SU(2) is the Universal Cover of SO(3)]] (⭐⭐) — explicit Lie-group example of the Galois correspondence: simply-connected $\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3$ covers $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ with deck group $\mathbb{Z}/2$, giving $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(3)) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ ([[Def - Universal Cover]], [[Thm - Galois Correspondence for Covering Spaces]], [[Ex - SU(2) is Diffeomorphic to S^3]])
- [[Ex - Pi_1 of the Torus is Z Squared]] (⭐⭐) — universal cover $\mathbb{R}^n \to T^n$ with deck group $\mathbb{Z}^n$ acting by translations; recovers $\pi_1(T^n) = \mathbb{Z}^n$ from the correspondence ([[Def - Universal Cover]], [[Def - Deck Transformation Group]], [[Thm - Galois Correspondence for Covering Spaces]])
