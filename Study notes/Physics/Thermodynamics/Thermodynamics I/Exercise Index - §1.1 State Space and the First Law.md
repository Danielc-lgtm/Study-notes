## §1.1 State Space and the First Law — Exercises

The exercises of this section drill the basic machinery of the topic: viewing thermodynamic states as points of a smooth manifold, recognising the heat and work 1-forms as Pfaffian (non-exact) 1-forms, and applying the first law $dU = \delta Q - \delta W$ to compute heat, work, and energy changes along specific quasistatic processes. The recurring theme is the distinction between *state functions* (whose differentials are exact and whose line integrals depend only on endpoints) and *transit quantities* (whose differentials are non-exact and whose integrals depend on the path). Once this distinction is internalised, the algebraic apparatus of thermodynamic potentials in §1.4 becomes natural.

- [[Ex - The Heat 1-Form for an Ideal Gas]] (⭐) — Compute $\delta Q$ in coordinates, verify it is non-closed, find the integrating factor $\lambda = T$ by inspection. ([[Def - Heat 1-Form and Work 1-Form]], [[Def - The First Law of Thermodynamics]], [[Def - Closed and Exact Forms]])

- [[Ex - Verifying dU is Exact for an Ideal Gas]] (⭐) — Direct verification of $d^2 U = 0$ via cross-partial test, illustrating that $U$ is a state function for an ideal gas. ([[Def - The First Law of Thermodynamics]], [[Def - Closed and Exact Forms]])

- [[Ex - Work Done in Isothermal vs Isobaric Expansion]] (⭐) — Compute $\int p\, dV$ along three different paths between the same endpoints, showing that work is path-dependent while $\Delta U$ is path-independent. ([[Def - Heat 1-Form and Work 1-Form]], [[Def - The First Law of Thermodynamics]])
