## §1.2 Caratheodory's Principle and the Second Law — Exercises

The exercises of this section drill the geometric content of the second law: the equivalence of Kelvin's, Clausius's, and Caratheodory's statements; the role of the Frobenius integrability condition $\theta \wedge d\theta = 0$ in distinguishing physical heat 1-forms from arbitrary ones; the explicit construction of the adiabatic equation of state for ideal gases. The recurring theme is the **bracket-generating vs involutive dichotomy** for codimension-one distributions — bracket-generating gives horizontal connectivity (Chow), involutive gives integrability and entropy (Frobenius). Caratheodory's principle is the physical selector that places real $\delta Q$ on the involutive side.

- [[Ex - Non-Integrability of a Hypothetical Adiabatic Distribution (Counterexample)]] (⭐⭐) — Compute the Frobenius obstruction for the standard contact form $\theta = y\, dx - x\, dy + dz$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, find it nonzero, and verify directly via the commutator-flow construction that every point of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is horizontally accessible — illustrating what Caratheodory's principle rules out. ([[Def - Caratheodory's Principle (Inaccessibility)]], [[Def - Adiabatic Process and Adiabatic Distribution]], [[Thm - Caratheodory's Theorem on the Second Law]], [[Thm - Chow's Connectivity Theorem (Statement)]], [[Def - Closed and Exact Forms]])

- [[Ex - Kelvin Implies Caratheodory]] (⭐⭐) — Prove Kelvin's statement of the second law implies Caratheodory's, by constructing a cycle whose existence would violate Kelvin's prohibition. Illustrates the logical equivalence (under suitable connectivity) of the various second-law formulations. ([[Def - Caratheodory's Principle (Inaccessibility)]], [[Def - Heat 1-Form and Work 1-Form]], [[Def - The First Law of Thermodynamics]], [[Def - Adiabatic Process and Adiabatic Distribution]])

- [[Ex - Adiabatic Equation of State for Ideal Gas]] (⭐⭐) — Derive $pV^\gamma = \text{const}$ for an ideal gas adiabatic process by setting $\delta Q = 0$ and integrating. Illustrates how the adiabatic distribution's integral curves are computed in the simplest 2D case, where integrability is automatic.
