## §7.1 The Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity — Exercises

The exercises of §7.1 drill the foundational move of the whole chapter: reading every kinematic quantity as a scalar product or projection of the two four-velocities. The Lorentz factor is the scalar product $\Gamma = U \cdot U_0$, which in the observer's own frame is just the time component $u^0$; the relative velocity is the rest-space part $V = (1/\Gamma)\perp_{U_0}U$, with the $1/\Gamma$ converting the particle's clock to the observer's; and the unit-norm constraint $U \cdot U = 1$ ties the two together as $\Gamma^2(1-|\mathbf V|^2) = 1$. The physical payoff is time dilation — the muon that reaches the ground because its clock runs slow by $\Gamma$ — and the unifying observation is that the relativity of these quantities is exactly their dependence on the observer's four-velocity $U_0$.

- [[Ex - Lorentz factor as the scalar product of four-velocities]] (⭐) — compute the Lorentz factor two ways (scalar product $U\cdot U_0$ and time component $u^0$) and confirm they agree, extract the relative velocity from the orthogonal decomposition, and derive the reciprocity of the Lorentz factor from the symmetry of the inner product ([[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space]]).

- [[Ex - The cosmic-ray muon reaches the ground]] (⭐⭐) — explain why muons survive the descent through the atmosphere using time dilation (lab frame: lifetime $\Gamma\tau_0$) and equivalently length contraction (muon frame: distance $L/\Gamma$), show the two accounts agree with one factor of $\Gamma$ each, and confirm consistency with the Frisch–Smith measurement $\Gamma = 8.4 \pm 2.0$ at $V \approx 0.995c$ ([[Thm - Time Dilation (General Observer)]], [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity]], [[Def - Proper Time]], [[Thm - Length Contraction]]).

- [[Ex - Components of the relative velocity and the four-velocity]] (⭐⭐) — for uniform circular motion, compute the relative velocity and constant speed, assemble the four-velocity as $(\Gamma, \Gamma V^i)$ and check its unit norm, show the four-velocity is nonetheless non-constant (the particle is accelerated despite constant speed), and find the sub-light condition $R\Omega < 1$ with $\Gamma \to \infty$ at the rim ([[Def - Velocity Relative to an Observer]], [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Thm - Maximum Relative Velocity is c]]).
