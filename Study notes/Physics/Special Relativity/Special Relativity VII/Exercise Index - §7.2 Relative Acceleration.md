## §7.2 Relative Acceleration — Exercises

The exercises of §7.2 drill the distinction between the two accelerations of relativistic kinematics — the observer-independent four-acceleration $A = \mathrm{d}U/\mathrm{d}\tau'$ and the observer-dependent relative acceleration $\boldsymbol\gamma = \mathrm{d}\mathbf V/\mathrm{d}t$ — and the bound that separates massive from massless and forbidden motion. The central identity is that the four-acceleration equals the relative acceleration at momentary rest, $A = \boldsymbol\gamma$, which is the operational definition of proper acceleration (the accelerometer reading). The norm formula weights parallel acceleration by $\Gamma^3$ and transverse by $\Gamma^2$, distinguishing uniformly accelerated motion (hyperbolic worldline, Rindler horizon) from circular motion (synchrotron radiation). The speed ceiling $|\mathbf V| < c$ is the reality condition on the Lorentz factor, with the three causal classes mapping onto the three velocity regimes.

- [[Ex - Maximum relative velocity and why tachyons exceed c]] (⭐⭐) — derive the speed bound $|\mathbf V| < c$ as the reality condition on $\Gamma$, show a spacelike-worldline tachyon would have $|\mathbf V| > c$, distinguish a closing speed (a coordinate difference, allowed to exceed $c$) from a relative speed (a four-velocity quantity, bounded), and explain via rapidity why no finite chain of boosts reaches $c$ ([[Thm - Maximum Relative Velocity is c]], [[Def - Velocity Relative to an Observer]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition]], [[Def - Rapidity]]).

- [[Ex - The four-acceleration at momentary rest is the proper acceleration]] (⭐⭐) — compute the collinear proper acceleration $\|A\|_g = \Gamma^3|\dot V|$ and its momentary-rest value $|\boldsymbol\gamma|$, integrate the constant-proper-acceleration condition $\Gamma V = at$ to get $V(t)$, derive the hyperbolic worldline $x^2 - t^2 = 1/a^2$ and the Rindler horizon, and explain why the relative acceleration falls to zero as the speed asymptotes to $c$ ([[Thm - Expression of the Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Acceleration Relative to an Observer]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Thm - Maximum Relative Velocity is c]]).

- [[Ex - Proper acceleration of circular motion and its norm]] (⭐⭐⭐) — compute the centripetal relative acceleration $\boldsymbol\gamma = -\Omega^2\overrightarrow{OM}$, apply the transverse-case norm formula $\|A\|_g = \Gamma^2 R\Omega^2$, re-derive it independently from the raw four-acceleration components as a sign check, and explain via the $\Gamma^4$ scaling of radiated power why synchrotrons radiate so fiercely ([[Thm - Expression of the Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Acceleration Relative to an Observer]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]).
