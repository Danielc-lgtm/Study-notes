# Notation Registry

A standing convention for this whole topic: **we set $c = 1$**, restoring $c$ only where a formula is more recognisable with it (the Larmor and synchrotron formulas, in particular, are usually quoted with $c$). The signature is **mostly minus**, $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$: a timelike vector has $X\cdot X > 0$, a four-velocity satisfies $U\cdot U = 1$ (with $c$: $U\cdot U = c^2$), and a four-acceleration, being spacelike, has $A\cdot A < 0$ with magnitude $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A \ge 0$. To restore $c$: replace $t$ by $ct$, every velocity $v$ by $v/c$, and insert factors of $c$ by dimensional analysis.

> [!warning] Convention
> Gourgoulhon, the principal source, uses the opposite signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ ("mostly plus"), in which a four-velocity has $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u = -1$ and a four-acceleration has $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a > 0$. Every formula here has been translated to mostly-minus by flipping the overall metric sign. The component matrix of energy density / momentum density / stress is unchanged by the flip (it is a table of measured quantities), but the *trace* term $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ in any metric-built tensor (notably the electromagnetic $T^{\mu\nu}$) flips with the metric, and the sign of $A\cdot A$ flips — so watch $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$ in the radiation formulas.

- $T$, $T_{\mu\nu}$, $T^{\mu\nu}$ — the **energy-momentum tensor**, a symmetric type-$(0,2)$ tensor field; its covariant, and fully raised, components
- $\varepsilon = T(U_0, U_0)$ — **energy density** measured by an observer of four-velocity $U_0$
- $\varpi$, $\varpi_i = -\tfrac1c T(\mathbf e_i, U_0)$ — **momentum density** ($1$-form in the rest space)
- $\varphi$, $\varphi_i$ — **energy-flux density** ($1$-form); for the electromagnetic field, the **Poynting vector**; always $\varphi = c^2\varpi$
- $S$, $S_{ij} = T(\mathbf e_i, \mathbf e_j)$ — **stress tensor** relative to the observer; for the electromagnetic field, the **Maxwell stress tensor**
- $\vec\nabla\cdot T$, $(\vec\nabla\cdot T)_\alpha = \nabla^\mu T_{\alpha\mu}$ — the **divergence** of $T$, a $1$-form, built with the flat-spacetime [[Def - The Covariant Derivative|covariant derivative]] $\nabla$
- $\mathcal F$, $\mathcal F_\alpha = F_{\alpha\mu}J^\mu$ — the **four-force density**; $\vec\nabla\cdot T = \mathcal F$ for a non-isolated system
- $U_0$ — observer four-velocity; $(U_0, \mathbf e_i)$ an orthonormal local frame; $\perp_{U_0}$ the orthogonal projector onto the rest space
- $F$, $F_{\mu\nu}$ — the [[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor|electromagnetic field tensor]]; $\mathbf E, \mathbf B$ the electric and magnetic fields relative to $U_0$
- $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = 2(B^2 - E^2)$ — the field invariant (with $c=1$); $T^{\text{em}}$ is traceless because of its $-\tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2$ term
- $q$, $m$ — charge and mass of a radiating particle; $A = \mathrm dU/\mathrm d\tau$ its four-acceleration, $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$
- $\mathbf V$, $\boldsymbol\gamma$, $\Gamma$ — three-velocity, three-acceleration, and Lorentz factor of a particle relative to an inertial observer
- $\mathcal P = \mathrm dE/\mathrm dt$ — radiated **power**; $\omega_B = |q|B/m$ the **cyclotron frequency**; $f_c$ the synchrotron **characteristic frequency**
- $\varepsilon_0, \mu_0$ — vacuum permittivity and permeability, $\varepsilon_0\mu_0 c^2 = 1$
- Einstein summation: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0,1,2,3$ (Greek) or $1,2,3$ (Latin)

# Motivation

Here is the whole chapter in one sentence: the energy-momentum tensor is the object whose flux through a surface is the four-momentum crossing it, and its vanishing divergence is the conservation of energy and momentum for fields and continuous matter. Everything else — the energy of the electromagnetic field, the Larmor formula, synchrotron radiation — is what you get by applying that one object to electromagnetism.

Up to now relativistic dynamics has tracked particles: each carries a [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|four-momentum]] $P = mU$, and the total $P$ of an isolated collection is conserved. But most of physics is not a handful of particles. A gas is a continuum of $10^{23}$ molecules; a star is a ball of fluid; an electromagnetic field fills a region with energy and momentum but contains no particles at all. We need an object that says, region by region, how much energy is present, how much momentum, and how that momentum flows — the last being *stress*, the pressure and shear one part of a medium exerts on the next, which has no analogue in single-particle dynamics. Counting the data — one energy density, three momentum densities, nine stresses — and demanding that it transform correctly under a change of observer forces a rank-two symmetric tensor, the [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor|energy-momentum tensor]] $T^{\mu\nu}$. The deepest characterisation is through a flux: the conserved four-momentum is a *vector*, so it cannot be the flux of a vector (that gives a scalar, like charge) — it must be the flux of a *field of bilinear forms*. That is why $T$ has the rank it has.

The structural backbone of the chapter is a single equation and its meaning:
$$
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \begin{cases}\ \partial_t\varepsilon + c^2\,\vec\nabla\cdot\varpi = 0 & \text{(energy)}\\[2pt]\ \partial_t\varpi + \vec\nabla\cdot S = 0 & \text{(momentum)}\end{cases}
$$
One tensor equation, four conservation laws — one for energy, three for momentum — bundled together by the symmetry of $T$. This is [[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]], and it is the Noether current of spacetime translations: time-translation symmetry gives energy conservation, space-translation symmetry gives momentum conservation. When the divergence does *not* vanish, $\vec\nabla\cdot T = \mathcal F$, the right-hand side is the four-force density pushing on the system — and this is exactly the lever that lets us assign an energy-momentum tensor to the electromagnetic field. A system of charges is not isolated (the field pushes on them), but charges-plus-field *is*, so demanding total conservation $\vec\nabla\cdot(T_{\text{mat}} + T_{\text{em}}) = 0$ *defines* the field's tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{em}} = \varepsilon_0(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2)$, whose $00$ component is the familiar field energy density $\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}{2}(E^2 + c^2B^2)$ and whose $0i$ block is the Poynting momentum.

The chapter then cashes this out in its most spectacular application: the radiation of an accelerated charge. The energy a charge loses to radiation is the flux of $T_{\text{em}}$ through a sphere surrounding it, and computing that flux gives the [[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)|Larmor formula]] $\mathcal P = q^2 a^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3$ and its relativistic generalisation, the Liénard formula $\mathcal P = \tfrac{q^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}\Gamma^6[a^2 - (\mathbf v\times\mathbf a)^2/c^2]$. Specialised to a charge bent into a helix by a magnetic field, this becomes [[Def - Synchrotron Radiation|synchrotron radiation]] — beamed into a forward cone of half-angle $1/\Gamma$, broadband from radio to X-rays, the dominant non-thermal emission of the universe and the reason high-energy electron colliders must be linear.

This chapter is where special relativity stops being kinematics and becomes the launch pad for gravitation. The energy-momentum tensor is the *source* of gravity: in general relativity the Einstein equation sets the curvature of spacetime proportional to exactly this $T_{\mu\nu}$, and its conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is the consistency condition that makes the coupling possible. The forward road runs through [[Special Relativity XXIV — Relativistic Hydrodynamics|relativistic hydrodynamics]] (the perfect-fluid $T$) and [[Special Relativity XXV — Toward Relativistic Gravitation|relativistic gravitation]], and connects to the existing vault treatment of [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]].

The reader is assumed to be fluent with [[Def - Tensors on Minkowski Space|tensors on Minkowski space]], the [[Def - The Covariant Derivative|covariant derivative]] and divergence of a tensor field, the [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes/Gauss theorem on spacetime]], and the [[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor|electromagnetic field tensor]] $F$ together with [[Thm - Maxwell Equations|Maxwell's equations]] $\mathrm dF = 0$, $\mathrm d{\star}F = \mu_0{\star}J$. The genuinely new ingredient is small in volume but large in consequence: a single symmetric tensor that packages all the energy and momentum in a region, obeys one conservation law, and turns out to be the source of gravity.

# Concept Map

## §23.1 The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Its Conservation

- **[[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor]]**
	- The energy-momentum tensor $T$ is the symmetric type-$(0,2)$ tensor field whose flux through a hypersurface is the four-momentum crossing it: $\mathbf p|_{\mathscr V} = \pm\tfrac1c\int_{\mathscr V}T(\,\cdot\,,\vec n)\,\mathrm dV$. Relative to an observer $U_0$ it delivers the energy density $\varepsilon = T(U_0,U_0)$, the momentum density $\varpi = -\tfrac1c T(\perp_{U_0},U_0)$, the energy flux $\varphi = c^2\varpi$, and the stress $S = T(\perp_{U_0},\perp_{U_0})$, assembled into the component matrix $T_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag\text{-}block}(\varepsilon; -c\varpi_i; S_{ij})$. For pressureless dust $T^{\mu\nu} = \varepsilon_0 U^\mu U^\nu$; it generalises mass density, momentum density, and Cauchy stress into one object, and it is the source term of the Einstein equation. Unlike the metric it may vanish (in vacuum $T = 0$) or be degenerate.

- **[[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]]**
	- For an isolated system the divergence of $T$ vanishes everywhere, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — a single tensor equation that splits, in any inertial frame, into the energy continuity equation $\partial_t\varepsilon + c^2\vec\nabla\cdot\varpi = 0$ and the momentum continuity equation $\partial_t\varpi + \vec\nabla\cdot S = 0$. It is the Noether current of spacetime translations: time-translation invariance gives energy conservation, space-translation invariance gives momentum conservation. For a non-isolated system $\vec\nabla\cdot T = \mathcal F$, the four-force density; for two interacting systems the total is conserved, $\vec\nabla\cdot(T_1 + T_2) = 0$ with $\mathcal F_1 = -\mathcal F_2$. The proof: flux of $T$ through a closed surface vanishes (isolated) $\Rightarrow$ Gauss gives $\int\vec\nabla\cdot T = 0$ over every region $\Rightarrow$ pointwise vanishing.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Einstein's Field Equation *(from General Relativity)*
> The conservation law $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is the consistency condition that lets $T$ source gravity. The **Einstein equation** $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$ sets the curvature tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$ proportional to the energy-momentum tensor; since $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically (the contracted Bianchi identity), the equation is solvable only because $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Energy and momentum tell spacetime how to curve — see [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: Conserved Charges from Killing Vectors *(from General Relativity)*
> Once $T$ is symmetric and divergence-free, every spacetime symmetry — every **Killing vector** $\xi$ — yields a conserved current $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu$. Time-translation symmetry gives conserved energy, rotational symmetry conserved angular momentum, boost symmetry the centre-of-energy theorem. The symmetric divergence-free $T$ secretly contains every conserved quantity of the system, each unlocked by a different symmetry.

## §23.2 Energy-Momentum of the Electromagnetic Field

- **[[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]**
	- The electromagnetic field carries energy and momentum through the symmetric, traceless tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{em}} = \varepsilon_0(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta})$ (in Heaviside–Lorentz units, $F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2$). It is *constructed* to make the divergence equal the negative Lorentz four-force density, $\nabla^\beta T^{\text{em}}_{\alpha\beta} = -F_{\alpha\mu}J^\mu$, so that field-plus-matter energy-momentum is conserved. Relative to an observer it gives energy density $\rho_{\text{em}} = \tfrac{\varepsilon_0}{2}(E^2 + c^2B^2)$, Poynting vector $\vec\varphi = \tfrac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$, momentum density $\varepsilon_0\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$, and the Maxwell stress tensor. Its tracelessness is the statement that the photon is massless and the field conformally invariant; its $\tfrac14$ coefficient is fixed independently by conservation and by tracelessness.

- **[[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation|The Poynting theorem (special case)]]**
	- Applying the splitting of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -\mathcal F^\nu$ to the electromagnetic field gives Poynting's theorem $\partial_t\rho_{\text{em}} + \vec\nabla\cdot\mathbf S = -\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf E$: the field energy density changes as the Poynting vector carries energy away and as the field does work on charges. The momentum component gives the Maxwell-stress force balance. These three nineteenth-century theorems (energy density, energy flux, stress) are the time and space components of one covariant law.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Stress-Energy of Yang–Mills Fields *(from Gauge Theory)*
> The structure $F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2$ recurs verbatim for non-abelian gauge fields, with a colour trace inserted: $T^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{tr}(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha) - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathrm{tr}(F^2)$. The gluon field of **quantum chromodynamics** carries energy and momentum by exactly this expression, and most of the proton's mass is this tensor's $00$ component integrated over its interior. See [[Gauge Theory VII — The Hodge Star, Electrodynamics, and Yang–Mills Theory]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Casimir Effect and the Trace Anomaly *(from Quantum Field Theory)*
> Promoting the fields to operators makes $\langle 0|T_{\mu\nu}|0\rangle$ the vacuum stress-energy, whose configuration-dependence is the measurable **Casimir effect**. The classically vanishing trace $T^\mu{}_\mu$ acquires a quantum **trace anomaly**, a fingerprint of the breaking of conformal symmetry by renormalisation.

## §23.3 Radiation by Accelerated Charges

- **[[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]]**
	- An accelerated charge radiates power $\mathcal P = q^2 a^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3$ (Larmor), where $a$ is the three-acceleration in the instantaneous rest frame; equivalently $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$ with $|A|^2$ the invariant squared four-acceleration. The relativistic generalisation is the **Liénard formula** $\mathcal P = \tfrac{q^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}\Gamma^6[\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\boldsymbol\gamma - (\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mathbf V)^2/c^2] = \tfrac{q^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}\Gamma^4(\Gamma^2\gamma_\parallel^2 + \gamma_\perp^2)$. The radiated power is a Lorentz scalar (the radiated four-momentum $\mathrm dp^{\text{rad}} = \tfrac{q^2|A|^2\mathrm d\tau}{6\pi\varepsilon_0}U$ is observer-independent), computed as the flux of $T_{\text{em}}$ through a sphere; the $\Gamma^6$ is just the invariant $|A|^2$ in laboratory variables. Only acceleration radiates — a uniformly moving charge does not.

- **[[Thm - Angular Distribution of Radiation]]**
	- The power radiated per unit solid angle is the directional Poynting flux. For a charge at rest it is the dipole donut $\mathrm d\mathcal P/\mathrm d\Omega \propto \gamma^2\sin^2\theta$; relativistically it is multiplied by a power of the Doppler factor $(1 - \tfrac Vc\cos\theta)^{-n}$ ($n = 6$ for collinear acceleration, a forward cone for orthogonal), sweeping the donut into a forward cone of half-angle $\sim 1/\Gamma$. This is **relativistic beaming** (Doppler boosting): the radiation of an ultrarelativistic charge is a knife-edge searchlight, explaining one-sided astrophysical jets and the collimation of synchrotron light. The distortion is aberration of the emitted radiation.

- **[[Def - Synchrotron Radiation]]**
	- Synchrotron radiation is the radiation of an ultrarelativistic charge bent into a helix by a magnetic field — the Liénard formula evaluated on circular motion. Its power is $\mathcal P = q^4 B^2\Gamma^2 V^2\sin^2\alpha/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3 m^2$, with energy lost per turn scaling as $\Gamma^4 \propto m^{-4}$. Beaming makes a fixed observer see short pulses, so the spectrum is broad — extending to the characteristic frequency $f_c = \Gamma^3(c/R)\sin^2\alpha$, far above the orbital frequency, from radio to X-rays. At the non-relativistic limit it degenerates to monochromatic **cyclotron radiation** at $\omega_B = qB/m$. It limits circular electron accelerators (LEP, not the LHC), powers synchrotron X-ray sources, and dominates cosmic non-thermal emission (Crab Nebula, AGN jets).

> [!tip] Unlocked: Radiation Reaction and the Abraham–Lorentz Force *(from Classical Electrodynamics)*
> The charge loses exactly the four-momentum it radiates, so there is a back-reaction — the **Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac force**, proportional to $\dot{\mathbf a}$. It is famously pathological (runaway and pre-accelerating solutions), exposing the limits of the classical point charge and pointing toward quantum electrodynamics. The Larmor power is the energy budget the radiation-reaction force must balance.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Synchrotron Astrophysics and Cosmic Magnetometry *(from High-Energy Astrophysics)*
> Because $f_c \propto \Gamma^2 B$, reading a cosmic source's synchrotron spectrum measures *both* its relativistic-electron energies and its magnetic-field strength across light-years — how we know the Crab's field is $\sim10^{-8}$ T and its electrons reach $\Gamma\sim10^6$. Synchrotron emission powers radio galaxies, supernova remnants, pulsar wind nebulae, and gamma-ray-burst afterglows.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Free-Electron Lasers *(from Accelerator Physics)*
> Turned around, synchrotron radiation in a long undulator, made coherent, is the **free-electron laser** — X-ray pulses billions of times brighter than a storage ring (LCLS, European XFEL). The radiation that limits accelerators is, inverted, an unrivalled scientific instrument.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The exercises of this chapter chase a recognisable set of goals. The most common is to **compute an energy-momentum tensor**: given a medium or field — dust, a perfect fluid, a Coulomb field, a plane wave, a radiating charge — write down its $T^{\mu\nu}$ and read off the energy density, momentum density, and stress relative to a chosen observer. This is the chapter's basic literacy, and the entire skill is bookkeeping the contractions with $U_0$ and the frame vectors. A second target is to **establish a conservation law**: to show $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ for an isolated system, or to extract from it the separate energy and momentum balance equations (Poynting's theorem, Euler's equation), or to derive a conserved scalar from a symmetry. A third is to **prove a structural property of $T$** — its symmetry (from angular-momentum conservation), its tracelessness (for the electromagnetic field, from masslessness), the equality $\varphi = c^2\varpi$ of energy flux and momentum density. A fourth, more computational, is to **find a radiated power or spectrum**: apply Larmor or Liénard to a given trajectory, integrate the angular distribution, or estimate a synchrotron characteristic frequency. A fifth is to **decide a physical question by scaling** — will a circular machine radiate too much, will a classical atom survive, is a reaction energetically allowed — settled by the $\Gamma$- and $m$-dependence of the formulas. Across all five the unifying remark is that you understand a system's energetics when you know its $T^{\mu\nu}$, because the energy density, the momentum, the stress, the conservation laws, and (for charges) the radiation all flow from that one object.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A medium or field is specified**, which immediately supplies its $T^{\mu\nu}$ — dust gives $\varepsilon_0 U^\mu U^\nu$, a fluid gives $(\rho+p)U^\mu U^\nu - p\eta^{\mu\nu}$, a field gives the quadratic-in-$F$ tensor. **An observer's four-velocity is given**, which lets every measured quantity be extracted by contraction: energy density is $T(U_0,U_0)$, and the rest follow by projecting with $\perp_{U_0}$. **The system is isolated** (or a closed system is identified by adding the interacting pieces), which unlocks $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ — the single most-used source in the chapter, and the lever that defines $T_{\text{em}}$. **Maxwell's equations hold**, both the inhomogeneous $J^\mu = \varepsilon_0\nabla_\beta F^{\mu\beta}$ (used to convert the Lorentz force into a divergence) and the homogeneous $\mathrm dF = 0$ (used to absorb the leftover term), which together construct the electromagnetic tensor. **A trajectory is given** — a helix in a magnetic field, a Coulomb-bound orbit, a deceleration — which supplies the four-acceleration that the radiation formulas need. The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a specified medium routes through contraction to its measured energetics; isolation routes through Gauss to a conservation law; Maxwell's equations route through the force-density construction to $T_{\text{em}}$; a trajectory routes through Liénard to a radiated power. The [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves almost every problem in the chapter is assembled from. When stuck, scan the list and try each.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Read off measured quantities by contracting $T$ with the observer's frame.** Given $T^{\mu\nu}$ and an observer $U_0$, the energy density is $T(U_0,U_0)$, the momentum density $-\tfrac1c T(\perp_{U_0},U_0)$, the energy flux $c^2$ times that, and the stress $T(\perp_{U_0},\perp_{U_0})$; see [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor]]. *Trigger:* a tensor and an observer are both in hand and you want a physical number. *Pattern:* "set both slots to $U_0$ for energy density; project with $\perp_{U_0}$ for the rest." This is the chapter's basic literacy.

2. **Build the dust/fluid tensor from the four-velocity field.** For pressureless matter, $T^{\mu\nu} = \varepsilon_0 U^\mu U^\nu$; for a [[Def - Perfect Fluid|perfect fluid]] add the pressure term, $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho+p)U^\mu U^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$; see [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor]]. *Trigger:* a continuous medium with a flow field is described. *Pattern:* "rest-frame $\mathrm{diag}(\rho,p,p,p)$, then boost by writing it covariantly with $U^\mu$." The rest frame is where the tensor is simplest.

3. **Take the divergence and set it to zero (or to $\mathcal F$).** For an isolated system $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$; for a forced one $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \mathcal F^\nu$; see [[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]]. *Trigger:* a conservation law or equation of motion is wanted. *Pattern:* "isolated $\Rightarrow$ divergence-free; interacting pieces $\Rightarrow$ total divergence-free." This is the workhorse of the chapter.

4. **Project the conservation law along and orthogonal to $U$.** Contracting $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ with $U_\nu$ gives the energy (entropy) equation; projecting with $\perp_U$ gives the momentum (Euler) equation; see [[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]]. *Trigger:* you have a tensor conservation law and want recognisable scalar/vector balance laws. *Pattern:* "parallel projection $\to$ energy; orthogonal projection $\to$ momentum." This splits one tensor equation into the physical pair.

5. **Construct $T_{\text{em}}$ by demanding total conservation.** The Lorentz four-force density $\mathcal F_\alpha = F_{\alpha\mu}J^\mu$, rewritten with Maxwell's equations, is minus a divergence, and the tensor inside is $T_{\text{em}}$; see [[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]. *Trigger:* you need the field's energy-momentum tensor or the Poynting theorem. *Pattern:* "$\mathcal F = F(\,\cdot\,,J)$, eliminate $J$ via $\nabla\cdot F$, absorb the leftover via $\mathrm dF = 0$, read off $T_{\text{em}}$." Conservation is the construction principle.

6. **Exploit tracelessness of the electromagnetic tensor.** $T^\mu{}_{\text{em}\,\mu} = 0$ because the $\tfrac14$ coefficient is $1/(\text{dimension})$; this fixes the radiation equation of state $p = \rho/3$ and signals masslessness; see [[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]. *Trigger:* electromagnetism or massless fields appear, or a trace must be computed. *Pattern:* "trace of $T_{\text{em}}$ is zero — use it as a check and as a constraint on the pressure." A nonzero trace for a field means a sign error.

7. **Compute radiated power as a flux of $T_{\text{em}}$ through a sphere.** The energy a charge radiates is $\oint T_{\text{em}}(\,\cdot\,,\vec m)\,\mathrm dS$ over a sphere; for the null radiative field the trace term drops, and the angular integral $\int\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\Omega = 8\pi/3$ gives the Larmor coefficient; see [[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]]. *Trigger:* a charge accelerates and you want the energy it loses. *Pattern:* "radiative field $\to$ its $T_{\text{em}} \to$ sphere flux $\to$ $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$." The invariant $|A|^2$ does all the work.

8. **Express the four-acceleration invariant in laboratory variables.** The Lorentz-scalar power depends only on $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$; rewriting it through three-velocity and three-acceleration gives the Liénard $\Gamma^6$ form; see [[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]]. *Trigger:* you have the invariant power and want it in lab quantities, or vice versa. *Pattern:* "$|A|^2 = \Gamma^6[\boldsymbol\gamma^2 - (\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mathbf V)^2/c^2]$; the $\Gamma$-powers are kinematic, not new physics." The simple invariant is the true content.

9. **Read beaming from the Doppler factor.** A relativistic radiation pattern carries $(1 - \tfrac Vc\cos\theta)^{-n}$, which for $\Gamma\gg1$ collimates the emission into a forward cone of half-angle $\sim1/\Gamma$; see [[Thm - Angular Distribution of Radiation]]. *Trigger:* you need the direction or spectrum of relativistic radiation. *Pattern:* "expand $1 - \tfrac Vc\cos\theta \approx (1+\Gamma^2\theta^2)/2\Gamma^2$; significant only for $\Gamma\theta\lesssim1$." Beaming is the geometric heart of synchrotron radiation.

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Treating $T$ like a metric — assuming it is non-degenerate or defines lengths
> It is tempting, because both are symmetric $(0,2)$-tensors, to handle the [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor|energy-momentum tensor]] like the metric — to raise indices with it, invert it, or assume $T(X,X) \ne 0$ for $X\ne0$. This fails: the dust tensor $\varepsilon_0 U^\mu U^\nu$ has *rank one*, hence is wildly degenerate (it annihilates every vector orthogonal to $U$), and in vacuum $T = 0$ entirely. A zero or degenerate metric is meaningless, but a zero or degenerate $T$ is physically ordinary. The operation becomes legal only for the *metric itself* (or any non-degenerate tensor); for a general $T$, never invert it and never use it to define geometry. Raise and lower with $\eta$, always.

> [!warning] 2. Dropping the $-\tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2$ trace term from the electromagnetic tensor
> The trace term looks like a removable nuisance, and one is tempted to keep only the manifestly-symmetric $F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha$. But without it the divergence does *not* reproduce the Lorentz force — the leftover from the homogeneous Maxwell equation is exactly $\tfrac14\nabla^\nu(F^2)$, and only the trace term cancels it; see [[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]. Drop it and energy-momentum is not conserved, the energy density comes out wrong (you would get $\varepsilon_0 E^2$ instead of $\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}{2}(E^2+c^2B^2)$ in the wrong combination), and the tensor acquires a spurious nonzero trace. The term is legal to omit *only* for the null radiative field, where $F^2 = 0$ makes it vanish on its own — and even then it is the special value of the field invariant, not a free choice.

> [!warning] 3. Concluding the radiated power depends on velocity
> The Liénard formula's $\Gamma^6$ tempts the reader to say "fast charges radiate more". But the power depends only on the *invariant* four-acceleration $|A|^2$, and a charge moving arbitrarily fast in a *straight line* ($A = 0$) radiates *nothing*; see [[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]]. The $\Gamma^6$ is what the fixed invariant $|A|^2$ looks like when expressed in laboratory three-vectors — it appears because maintaining a given laboratory acceleration at high speed requires a large four-acceleration, not because velocity per se causes radiation. The correct statement: radiation depends on acceleration alone. The operation "scale radiated power with velocity" is legal only after you have fixed *what acceleration* is held constant (a force, a trajectory curvature), and then the velocity-dependence is an indirect consequence, not a primary law.

> [!warning] 4. Forgetting the sign of $A\cdot A$ under mostly-minus
> The four-acceleration is spacelike, so in our mostly-minus signature $A\cdot A < 0$, and the radiated power $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$ uses the *magnitude* $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A > 0$. Writing $\mathcal P \propto A\cdot A$ literally gives a *negative* power — physically absurd. The trap is transcribing Gourgoulhon's mostly-plus formulas (where $A\cdot A > 0$) without flipping the sign. The operation is legal only with the explicit magnitude $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$; the sign convention must be tracked through every contraction.

> [!warning] 5. Using the Poynting vector of a static field to claim energy flows
> A static configuration can have a nonzero Poynting vector $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/\mu_0$ — for instance a charge sitting in a magnet's field — and it is tempting to conclude energy is circulating. But in a *static* situation $\partial_t\rho_{\text{em}} = 0$ and $\vec\nabla\cdot\mathbf S = 0$, so although momentum density $\varepsilon_0\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ may be nonzero (it stores angular momentum, the "Feynman disk paradox"), no *net* energy crosses any closed surface and nothing actually radiates; see [[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]. The operation "nonzero Poynting vector $\Rightarrow$ radiation" is legal only when the field is *time-dependent and falls as $1/r$* (the radiative field); for static or $1/r^2$ fields the surface flux vanishes at infinity.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won by recognising that almost everything is one object — the [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor|energy-momentum tensor]] — and one law — its divergence. Begin every problem by asking "what is the $T^{\mu\nu}$ here, and is the system isolated?"

If the problem **asks for measured energetics** — the energy density, momentum, pressure, or stress that some observer sees — the decisive first move is to write down $T^{\mu\nu}$ for the medium or field, then contract with the observer's four-velocity and frame. For dust it is $\varepsilon_0 U^\mu U^\nu$; for a fluid, add the pressure; for a field, the quadratic-in-$F$ tensor. The reason this works is that the rest frame removes complexity (a fluid is isotropic there, a charge is momentarily still), and the contraction $T(U_0,U_0)$ for energy density, $T(\perp_{U_0},\perp_{U_0})$ for stress, mechanically extracts what any observer measures ([[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor]]). The single most common error is forgetting that energy density is observer-dependent — boosting dust multiplies its energy density by $\gamma^2$, one factor for the energy per particle and one for the length-contracted volume — so always track *which* observer is doing the measuring.

If the problem **asks for a conservation law or an equation of motion**, the instrument is the divergence. For an isolated system $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]]); if the system is not isolated, identify the interacting pieces and use that the *total* is conserved. The route to a recognisable balance law is to project: contract the conservation law with $U_\nu$ to get the energy (entropy) equation, project orthogonally with $\perp_U$ to get the momentum (Euler) equation. This is how the abstract tensor law becomes Poynting's theorem for the electromagnetic field or Euler's equation for a fluid. The trick to internalise is that one tensor equation is four scalar laws, and the projection selects which one you want.

If the problem **concerns the electromagnetic field's energy**, the construction principle is conservation again, now used backwards. The matter is not isolated — the field pushes on the charges — so its divergence is the Lorentz [[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force|four-force]] density $\mathcal F = F(\,\cdot\,,J)$; demanding that charges-plus-field be conserved *defines* $T_{\text{em}}$ as the tensor with $\vec\nabla\cdot T_{\text{em}} = -\mathcal F$ ([[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]]). To carry out the construction, eliminate the current with the inhomogeneous Maxwell equation and absorb the leftover with the homogeneous one ($\mathrm dF = 0$); the $\tfrac14$-trace term is forced by this cancellation. Use tracelessness as a constant sanity check.

If the problem **asks for radiated power**, the strategy is to recognise that radiation is the flux of $T_{\text{em}}$ through a sphere, but in practice you skip straight to the formula. Identify the four-acceleration of the trajectory; the Lorentz-scalar power is $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$, which in laboratory variables is the Liénard $\Gamma^6$ form ([[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]]). For circular motion this is synchrotron power; for the spectrum and direction, invoke beaming. The key decision is *what is held fixed* — a force, a trajectory curvature, an energy — because the velocity-dependence is entirely indirect: the power depends only on $|A|^2$, and the $\Gamma$-factors track how much four-acceleration a given laboratory situation demands. Never say "fast charges radiate more" without specifying the constraint.

If the problem **is a scaling or feasibility question** — will a circular accelerator radiate too much, will a classical atom collapse, is this reaction allowed — the strategy is dimensional and asymptotic. Extract the $\Gamma$- and $m$-dependence: synchrotron loss per turn scales as $\Gamma^4 \propto m^{-4}$, so protons radiate $\sim10^{13}$ times less than electrons at the same energy ([[Def - Synchrotron Radiation]]); the classical atom's Larmor loss gives a collapse time of $\sim10^{-11}$ s. The justification is that these questions are decided by a single power of $\Gamma$ or $m$, and getting that power right settles the verdict without a detailed calculation.

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **find the energy-momentum tensor first, then everything else is a contraction or a divergence.** Every question in this chapter is the question "what is $T^{\mu\nu}$, and what does $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$ tell me?" — measured energetics are contractions of $T$, conservation laws are its divergence, the field's energy is the $T$ that makes conservation hold, and radiation is the flux of that $T$ through a sphere. The tensor is to relativistic energetics what the free-body diagram is to Newtonian mechanics: the first thing on the page.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation|Local conservation]]** $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$: the most-used fact in the chapter, because it is the single law from which every balance equation flows. Reach for it whenever a system is isolated (or can be closed by adding interacting pieces), whenever you need an equation of motion for a continuum, and whenever a conserved quantity is wanted. Its most powerful disguised use is *backwards*: demanding total conservation for charges-plus-field is what *defines* the electromagnetic tensor. Combined with a Killing vector it yields a conserved scalar for every spacetime symmetry; combined with the projection technique it becomes Poynting's theorem and Euler's equation. Recognise its applicability the instant "isolated" or "closed system" appears, or the instant you need to convert a tensor into balance laws.

- **The energy-density contraction** $\varepsilon = T(U_0, U_0)$: the universal recipe for "how much energy does *this* observer see". It is reusable because it is mechanical — set both slots of $T$ to the observer's four-velocity — and because it makes the observer-dependence explicit (boosting multiplies dust's $\varepsilon$ by $\gamma^2$). Whenever a medium and an observer both appear and a number is wanted, this is the first computation; the momentum density, energy flux, and stress follow by projecting with $\perp_{U_0}$.

- **The electromagnetic tensor** $T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{em}} = \varepsilon_0(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2)$: the bridge from the field $F$ to its energetics. Its recognisable setup is any problem involving field energy, momentum, radiation pressure, or the Poynting theorem. The reusable content is twofold: its $00$ component is the field energy density $\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}{2}(E^2+c^2B^2)$, and its tracelessness ($T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$) is a free constraint and a sanity check. Combine it with the conservation law for Poynting's theorem; combine it with a sphere integral for the Larmor formula; combine its tracelessness with cosmology for the radiation equation of state $p = \rho/3$.

- **[[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)|The Larmor/Liénard formula]]** $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$: the universal radiated-power formula, whose true content is that the power is a *Lorentz scalar* depending only on the invariant four-acceleration. Its typical use is to compute energy loss for any accelerated charge — orbiting, oscillating, decelerating, spiralling. The reusable skill is reading the invariant $|A|^2$ first and only then expanding into the laboratory $\Gamma^6$ form; this keeps the physics (acceleration radiates) separate from the kinematics (the $\Gamma$-factors). Reach for it whenever a charge is non-inertial, and never confuse its velocity-dependence for a primary law.

- **The $\Gamma^4 \propto m^{-4}$ synchrotron scaling**: the single power law that decides accelerator design and dominates non-thermal astrophysics. Its recognisable setup is a charge bent by a magnetic field at fixed energy. The reusable content is that radiative loss per turn grows as the fourth power of the Lorentz factor and falls as the fourth power of the mass, so light particles at high energy radiate catastrophically — the reason the LHC (protons) tolerates what crippled LEP (electrons), and the reason cosmic synchrotron sources are powered by electrons. Reach for it whenever circular motion, a magnetic field, and a relativistic charge coincide.

# Bridges

1. **General relativity — the energy-momentum tensor is the source of gravity.** In [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild|general relativity]] the Einstein field equation reads $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$, where the left side is the **Einstein tensor** built from the curvature of spacetime and the right side is exactly the symmetric $T_{\mu\nu}$ of this chapter. The construction is forced: the Einstein tensor is symmetric, so $T$ must be symmetric (which is why angular-momentum conservation, forcing $T_{\mu\nu} = T_{\nu\mu}$, matters); and the contracted Bianchi identity gives $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically, so the field equation is consistent *only because* $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — precisely [[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation]]. The flat-spacetime $T$ here is the seed of the curved theory: it is what the source term reduces to in the local inertial frame at each event, and gravity is the story of letting this same tensor curve the metric against which it is measured. The Schwarzschild solution is the vacuum case $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$; a star is the perfect-fluid $T$ sourcing its own gravity.

2. **Continuum mechanics — the relativistic stress tensor unifies three Newtonian fields.** Classical continuum mechanics carries a mass density $\rho$, a momentum density $\rho\mathbf v$, and a Cauchy stress tensor $\sigma_{ij}$ as three logically separate fields obeying separate balance laws — mass continuity $\partial_t\rho + \vec\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v) = 0$ and momentum balance $\partial_t(\rho\mathbf v) + \vec\nabla\cdot\sigma = \mathbf f$. Special relativity fuses them: $\rho c^2$ is the $00$ component, $\rho\mathbf v$ the $0i$ block, and $\sigma_{ij}$ the $ij$ block of one tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$, and the two separate Newtonian balance laws are the time and space components of the single equation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$. The relativistic completion of the Cauchy stress is the spatial block of $T$, and the relativistic Euler equation of [[Special Relativity XXIV — Relativistic Hydrodynamics|hydrodynamics]] is the orthogonal projection of this one conservation law. What three nineteenth-century balance laws said, one tensor equation now says.

3. **Gauge theory — the field stress tensor is the same construction for every gauge field.** The electromagnetic tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{em}} = \varepsilon_0(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2)$ generalises verbatim to non-abelian [[Gauge Theory VII — The Hodge Star, Electrodynamics, and Yang–Mills Theory|Yang–Mills theory]]: the energy-momentum tensor of a gauge field with curvature $F$ is $T^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{tr}(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha) - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathrm{tr}(F^2)$, the abelian formula with a colour trace. The gluon field of quantum chromodynamics carries energy and momentum by exactly this expression, and the remarkable consequence is that most of the mass of the proton — about $99\%$ — is not the rest mass of its quarks but the energy of the confined gluon and quark fields, this tensor's $00$ component integrated over the proton's volume. The same tracelessness that makes the photon massless makes the *classical* gluon massless; the proton's mass comes from the quantum trace anomaly and confinement.

4. **Noether's theorem — $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the conserved current of spacetime translations.** The deepest reading of the conservation law is through [[Thm - Noether Theorem (Relativistic Particle)|Noether's theorem]]: to every continuous symmetry of the action corresponds a conserved current. Spacetime is homogeneous — there is no preferred origin in time or space — and the conserved current of that translation symmetry is exactly $T^{\mu\nu}$. Time-translation invariance ($x^0 \mapsto x^0 + a$) gives the conserved energy current $T^{0\nu}$; space-translation invariance gives the momentum currents. The canonical Noether tensor obtained this way is not automatically symmetric, and the *improvement* terms needed to symmetrise it (the Belinfante–Rosenfeld procedure) are precisely those forced by angular-momentum conservation — so the symmetry of $T$, its conservation, and its identity as the translation current are three faces of one Noether construction. This is why $T$ couples to gravity: gravity sees the conserved current of translations, and the symmetric (Hilbert) form is the one general relativity demands.

5. **Astrophysics — synchrotron radiation as a cosmic magnetometer.** The relativistic radiation formula, specialised to charges in magnetic fields, is the most important non-thermal emission mechanism in the universe, and it doubles as a remote-sensing instrument. Because the [[Def - Synchrotron Radiation|synchrotron]] characteristic frequency $f_c \propto \Gamma^2 B$ couples the relativistic-electron energy to the magnetic-field strength, observing a source's synchrotron spectrum — its turnover frequency, its spectral index — measures *both* across distances of light-years. This is how the Crab Nebula's field is known to be $\sim10^{-8}$ T and its electrons to reach $\Gamma\sim10^6$; how magnetic fields are mapped in radio galaxies and clusters; and how the energies of cosmic-ray electrons are inferred. The same energy-momentum tensor that limits a laboratory accelerator, read in the sky, measures the magnetism of the cosmos.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: one tensor holds all the energetics, one divergence holds all the conservation.** It is tempting to see this chapter as a collection of separate results — the energy-momentum tensor, then its conservation, then the field's energy, then Larmor, then synchrotron. The right picture is that there is *one object* and *one law*, and everything else is a contraction or a flux of them. The energy density, momentum density, and stress are contractions of $T^{\mu\nu}$ with an observer; the energy and momentum conservation laws are the time and space components of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$; the electromagnetic field's energy is the particular $T$ that makes conservation hold when charges and field interact; and the radiated power of a charge is the flux of that $T$ through a sphere. Once this is internalised, the chapter is predictable: every question is "what is $T$, and what does its divergence say?", and the answer is always a contraction (for measured quantities) or a divergence (for conservation) or a flux (for radiation). The energy-momentum tensor is to relativistic energetics what the wavefunction is to quantum mechanics — the single object that, once known, answers every question by a standard operation.

**The true name of the energy-momentum tensor is "the conserved Noether current of spacetime translations".** The component matrix — energy density here, stress there — is the right thing to *compute with* but the wrong thing to *think*. The operational characterisation is: $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the conserved current of the translation symmetry of spacetime, packaged as the object whose flux through a hypersurface is the four-momentum crossing it. This single sentence explains the three properties that otherwise seem to need separate justification. *Why two indices?* One labels which component of four-momentum, the other labels across which surface — a current ($\mu$) of a four-vector quantity ($\nu$). *Why conserved?* Because translations are a symmetry, by Noether. *Why symmetric, and why this is the source of gravity?* Because the gravitationally-coupled (Hilbert) tensor, obtained by varying the action with respect to the metric, is automatically symmetric and automatically the right thing for the Einstein equation. When you meet a new field and need its $T$, do not read off energy density and stress by hand — vary its action with respect to the metric, or apply Noether to translations, and the symmetric conserved tensor falls out.

**Conservation is a construction principle, not just a property.** The most-used move in the chapter is to deploy $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ *backwards*. One usually thinks of conservation as something you prove *about* a known tensor. But the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor is *defined by demanding* conservation: the matter system is not isolated, so its divergence is the Lorentz force density; insisting that matter-plus-field be conserved forces the existence of a field tensor with a prescribed divergence, and that requirement — together with Maxwell's equations — pins down $T_{\text{em}}$ uniquely, $\tfrac14$-trace term and all. The same logic recurs throughout physics: when energy seems not to be conserved, the resolution is to find the additional reservoir (a field, a potential, a thermal bath) whose energy-momentum tensor restores conservation. Energy-momentum conservation is so fundamental that it is more profitable to treat it as inviolable and *discover* the tensors that satisfy it than to treat it as a theorem to be checked.

**Only acceleration radiates, and the radiated power is an invariant — the $\Gamma^6$ is a kinematic illusion.** The Liénard formula's sixth power of the Lorentz factor is the most intimidating expression in the chapter, and it is the source of the most common misconception: that fast charges radiate copiously *because* they are fast. They do not. The radiated power is a Lorentz scalar, $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0 c$, depending only on the invariant magnitude of the four-acceleration; a charge coasting at $0.9999c$ in a straight line radiates *exactly nothing*. The $\Gamma^6$ appears only when you insist on expressing the frame-independent $|A|^2$ through laboratory three-velocity and three-acceleration: maintaining a given *laboratory* acceleration at high speed requires an enormous *four*-acceleration, and that is what the $\Gamma$-factors count. The lesson for a problem-solver is to always compute the invariant first and decompose into lab variables last — it separates the physics (acceleration radiates) from the bookkeeping (the kinematic enhancement), and it prevents the error of attributing radiation to velocity. This same discipline — find the invariant, then specialise to a frame — is the master technique of all of special relativity, and the radiation formula is its sharpest illustration.

**The minus signs in the metric resurface as a sign trap in the radiation formula.** A recurring theme of special relativity is that the indefiniteness of the metric is where the physics lives, and this chapter supplies a concrete operational reminder. The four-acceleration is spacelike — it is orthogonal to the timelike four-velocity — so in the mostly-minus signature $A\cdot A < 0$, and the radiated power, which must be positive, uses the *magnitude* $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$. A reader transcribing Gourgoulhon's mostly-plus formulas (where $A\cdot A > 0$) without flipping the sign would compute a negative radiated power, a flat contradiction. The general principle: whenever a physical quantity must be positive (an energy, a power, a squared mass) but is built from a contraction that the signature can make negative, the signature is telling you to take a magnitude, and tracking that sign is not pedantry — it is the difference between a charge that radiates and one that absorbs.
