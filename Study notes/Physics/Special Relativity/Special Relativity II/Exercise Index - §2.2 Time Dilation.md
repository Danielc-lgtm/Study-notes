## §2.2 Time Dilation — Exercises

The exercises of §2.2 drill the statement that a moving clock runs slow by $\gamma$, the recognition that a rest-frame duration is a proper time, and the two-frame consistency that reconciles dilation in one frame with contraction in another. The first is the cosmic-ray muon — the textbook demonstration that time dilation is real and that one observable (the muon reaching the ground) has two valid descriptions, the Earth dilating the lifetime and the muon contracting the atmosphere. The second triangulates the dilation factor by three independent derivations (light clock, Lorentz transformation, invariant interval), showing the light clock supplies the mechanism and the interval supplies the "proper time is shortest" invariant. The third estimates the Hafele–Keating flying-clock experiment quantitatively and confronts the general-relativistic gravitational correction that rides along, plus the eastward/westward asymmetry from the non-rotating inertial frame. The unifying technique: identify the proper-time frame (two events at the same place), apply $T = \gamma T_0$, and check the orthogonal frame for consistency.

- [[Ex - Time dilation and the cosmic-ray muon]] (⭐⭐) — show a muon with rest-frame half-life $\tau \approx 2\times10^{-6}\,\text{s}$ and $\gamma \approx 10$ reaches sea level although the Earth-frame transit exceeds several half-lives, by dilating the lifetime to $\gamma\tau$ (Earth frame) and equivalently contracting the $15\,\text{km}$ atmosphere to $H/\gamma$ (muon frame), and verify the two accounts agree on the observable ([[Thm - Time Dilation]], [[Thm - Length Contraction]], [[Def - Proper Time]]).

- [[Ex - The light clock and the interval derivation of time dilation]] (⭐) — derive $T = \gamma T_0$ three ways (a bouncing-light clock via Pythagoras on the slant path, the inverse Lorentz transformation with $\Delta x' = 0$, and the invariant interval), prove transverse lengths are unchanged by a two-rod symmetry argument, and show the interval route additionally proves the proper time is the shortest coordinate time ([[Thm - Time Dilation]], [[Def - The Spacetime Interval]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]]).

- [[Ex - Hafele–Keating and the flying-clock experiment]] (⭐⭐) — expand the dilation factor to leading order to get the kinematic time loss $\Delta\tau_{\text{SR}} \approx -\tfrac12(v^2/c^2)T \sim 10^{-7}\,\text{s}$ for a $48$-hour jet flight, explain why the opposite-signed gravitational gain $+gh\,T/c^2$ of comparable size partly cancels it, and account for the eastward/westward asymmetry via the non-rotating Earth-centred inertial frame ([[Thm - Time Dilation]], [[Def - Proper Time]]).
