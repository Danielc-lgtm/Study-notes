## §2.1 Proper Time and Four-Velocity — Exercises

The exercises of §2.1 drill proper time and the four-velocity. Proper time is maximised on inertial worldlines (the reversed triangle inequality, resolving the twin paradox); the relativistic Doppler effect derives in one stroke from $\omega = U \cdot K$ as the contraction of frequency four-vector with observer four-velocity; hyperbolic motion under constant proper acceleration corresponds to linear-in-proper-time rapidity; and the muon-storage-ring time dilation depends only on speed, not acceleration. The unifying observation: proper time is the worldline integral $\int d\tau = \int dt/\gamma$, a Lorentz invariant.

- [[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]] (⭐⭐) — compute the elapsed proper time for an inertial and an accelerated traveller, then prove by a pointwise bound on the integrand $\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}\le 1$ that the inertial worldline maximises proper time, resolving the twin paradox as a statement that proper time is a worldline invariant ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]]).

- [[Ex - The relativistic Doppler effect]] (⭐⭐) — derive the longitudinal blueshift and redshift, the transverse Doppler shift, and the general-angle formula in one stroke from the invariant $\omega_{\text{obs}} = U\cdot K$, recognising that an observer's measured frequency is the contraction of the frequency four-vector with their four-velocity ([[Def - The Frequency Four-Vector]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]]).

- [[Ex - Hyperbolic motion under constant proper acceleration]] (⭐⭐⭐) — solve the worldline of a particle with constant proper acceleration by parametrising the four-velocity with rapidity, $U^\mu = c(\cosh\phi,\sinh\phi)$, finding that rapidity grows linearly in proper time and the trajectory is a hyperbola asymptoting to but never reaching the speed of light ([[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Proper Time]], [[Def - Rapidity]], [[Def - The Spacetime Interval]]).

- [[Ex - Proper time of a clock in circular motion]] (⭐⭐) — integrate $d\tau = dt/\gamma$ along the curved worldline of a clock orbiting at constant speed $v$; since the speed is constant $\gamma$ leaves the integral and $\Delta\tau = T/\gamma$, so the orbiting clock ages less than an inertial one by exactly $1/\gamma$, and the result depends only on the speed — not the centripetal acceleration — as the CERN muon storage ring confirms ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]).
