# Notation Registry

This chapter uses **SI units** and keeps the speed of light $c$ explicit, restoring it wherever a formula is more recognisable that way (rather than setting $c=1$ as in the kinematics chapters), because the electromagnetic constants $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ carry dimensions that make $c$ informative here. The signature is **mostly minus**, $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike vector $X$ has $X\cdot X > 0$.

> [!warning] Convention
> The rigour source, Gourgoulhon, works in the opposite signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, where a timelike four-velocity has $(U_0)_\alpha = (-1,0,0,0)$. We have translated everything to $\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, so $(U_0)_\alpha = (+1,0,0,0)$ and the time rows and columns of the component matrices of $F$ carry the opposite overall sign from his Eq. (17.12). The 2-form $F$ itself and the physical fields $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ are convention-independent; both field invariants and every physical force are sign-robust. When comparing with a field-theory text in mostly-plus signature, flip the sign of $\eta$ and of every raised-lowered index pair.

- $F$, $F_{\mu\nu}$ — the **electromagnetic field tensor** (Faraday tensor), an antisymmetric $(0,2)$ tensor, i.e. a 2-form
- $F^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\alpha}\eta^{\nu\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}$ — its fully raised components; $F^\mu{}_\nu = \eta^{\mu\alpha}F_{\alpha\nu}$ the mixed form
- $\star F$, $(\star F)_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$ — the **Hodge dual** of $F$, another 2-form
- $F^\sharp$, $F^{\mu\nu} = g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}$ — the **metric dual** (both indices raised)
- $f$ (lowercase) — the **Lorentz four-force**, $f = qF(\cdot,U)$, $f_\alpha = qF_{\alpha\beta}U^\beta$ (lowercase reserves $F$ for the field tensor)
- $q$ — electric charge; $m$ — rest mass; $\mathcal{P}$ — a charged particle, four-velocity $U$, proper time $\tau$
- $\mathbf{E}$ — the **electric field** relative to an observer (a one-form / vector in the rest space): $\mathbf{E} = F(\cdot,U_0)$
- $\mathbf{B}$ — the **magnetic field** relative to an observer (a vector in the rest space): $c\mathbf{B} = \star F(U_0,\cdot)$
- $U_0$ — an observer's four-velocity; $\mathcal{E}_{U_0} = U_0^\perp$ its local rest space; $\Gamma = U\cdot U_0$ a Lorentz factor
- $\mathbf{V}$ — the velocity of a particle relative to an observer (in the rest space, $\mathbf{V}\cdot U_0 = 0$)
- $\mathbf{U} = U\mathbf{e}$ — the velocity of one observer relative to another (boost velocity); $\Gamma = (1-U^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$
- $I_1 = \tfrac12 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ — the first (scalar) **field invariant**
- $I_2 = \tfrac14(\star F)_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ — the second (pseudoscalar) **field invariant**
- $\omega_B = qB/m$ — the **cyclotron frequency**; $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma_0$ the **synchrotron (gyration) frequency**
- $R = P\sin\theta/(qB)$ — the **Larmor radius** ($P = \Gamma_0 mV_0$ the momentum, $\theta$ the pitch angle)
- $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{kin}} = (\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}-1)mc^2$ — relativistic kinetic energy; $\mathfrak{E} = \Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}mc^2$ the energy
- $\varepsilon_0$ — vacuum permittivity; $\mu_0 = 1/(\varepsilon_0 c^2)$ — vacuum permeability
- $\times$ or $\times_{U_0}$ — the spatial cross product in an observer's rest space (via the restricted Levi-Civita tensor)
- $\epsilon$, $\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}$ — the **Levi-Civita tensor**; $\wedge$ — the exterior (wedge) product; $\underline{V}$ — metric dual one-form of $V$
- Einstein summation: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0,1,2,3$

# Motivation

Special relativity was born from electromagnetism, and this chapter completes the circle by showing what electromagnetism looks like once spacetime is taken seriously: the electric and magnetic fields are not two things but one. The single object is the **electromagnetic field tensor** $F$, an antisymmetric $(0,2)$ tensor — a 2-form — and the familiar $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are the way one particular observer slices it. Change observer and the slices change, mixing electric into magnetic, but the underlying tensor does not. This is the central message, and everything else in the chapter is its elaboration.

The structural backbone is the defining relation, which says the field is whatever exerts the right force on a charge:
$$
f \;=\; q\,F(\,\cdot\,,U), \qquad f_\alpha = q\,F_{\alpha\beta}\,U^\beta.
$$
From this one equation the whole structure unfolds. Linearity in the four-velocity $U$ forces $F$ to be a bilinear form; the demand that the force conserve rest mass (be a *pure* four-force, $f\cdot U = 0$) forces $F$ to be *antisymmetric*, hence a 2-form with exactly six independent components — precisely the three of $\mathbf{E}$ and three of $\mathbf{B}$. The observer-dependence of the $\mathbf{E}/\mathbf{B}$ split is then unavoidable: dividing $F$ into "electric" and "magnetic" parts requires choosing a time direction, that is, an observer's $U_0$.

Section §21.1 builds the field tensor from the Lorentz force and reads off its component matrix and its Hodge dual; it establishes the observer's decomposition $F = \underline{U_0}\wedge\mathbf{E} + \star(\underline{U_0}\wedge c\mathbf{B})$ and recovers the elementary $\mathbf{f} = q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{B})$ as a projection. Section §21.2 asks what changes and what does not under a change of observer: the fields $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ transform by mixing (a pure electric field acquires a magnetic field in a moving frame), but the two scalars $I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ are invariant and classify the field; the headline application is the field of a uniformly moving charge — the boosted Coulomb field, which is the relativistic *origin* of magnetism. Section §21.3 solves the motion of a charge in a uniform field — helical in a magnetic field, hyperbolic in an electric field, trochoidal in crossed fields — and applies it to the design of every particle accelerator, resting on the single fact that *a magnetic field does no work*.

The reader is assumed to have worked through the tensor and exterior-calculus chapters: the [[Def - Alternate Forms and the Exterior Product|alternating forms and exterior product]], the [[Def - The Hodge Star|Hodge star]], and the [[Def - The Levi-Civita Tensor|Levi-Civita tensor]] of [[Special Relativity XVIII — Tensors, Alternate Forms and Hodge Duality]], and the [[Def - Four-Force|four-force]] of [[Special Relativity XIII — Energy and Momentum]]. The new ingredient is small — it is the recognition that the electromagnetic field is a 2-form and that $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ are its observer-relative shadows — but it reorganises all of electromagnetism. The next chapter, [[Special Relativity XXII — Maxwell's Equations]], writes $F = dA$ and collapses Maxwell's four equations to two.

# Concept Map

## §21.1 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor and the Lorentz Force

- **[[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor]]**
	- The electromagnetic field is the antisymmetric $(0,2)$ tensor $F$ (a 2-form) defined by the Lorentz force it exerts, $f = qF(\cdot,U)$. Linearity in the four-velocity makes it a bilinear form; the purity of the Lorentz force ($f\cdot U = 0$, conserving rest mass) makes it antisymmetric, hence a 2-form with six independent components. Relative to an observer $U_0$ it decomposes as $F = \underline{U_0}\wedge\mathbf{E} + \star(\underline{U_0}\wedge c\mathbf{B})$ with $\mathbf{E} = F(\cdot,U_0)$, $c\mathbf{B} = \star F(U_0,\cdot)$ lying in the rest space; the component matrix has $\mathbf{E}$ in the time-space block and $c\mathbf{B}$ in the space-space block. It is closed and exact, $F = dA$ (developed next chapter), and is the curvature of a $U(1)$ connection.

- **[[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force]]**
	- The four-force on a charge $q$ is $f = qF(\cdot,U)$, $f^\mu = qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$, a *pure* four-force ($f\cdot U = 0$ by antisymmetry, conserving rest mass). Projected onto an inertial observer it splits into the spatial Lorentz force $\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}} = q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{B})$ and the power $d\mathfrak{E}/dt = q\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{V}$ — so the magnetic field does no work, only $\mathbf{E}$ changes the energy. The equation of motion is $m\,dU^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$. Viewed with a raised index, $qF^\mu{}_\nu/m$ is an element of the Lorentz Lie algebra acting on the four-velocity.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Curvature of a U(1) Connection *(from Gauge Theory)*
> The relation $F = dA$ makes $F$ the **curvature** of a connection on a $U(1)$ principal bundle, with $A$ the connection one-form and $dF = 0$ the Bianchi identity. Electromagnetism is the abelian case of [[Gauge Theory I — Connections on Vector Bundles and the Electromagnetic Connection|Yang–Mills theory]]; the non-abelian generalisation $F = dA + A\wedge A$ (for $SU(2)$, $SU(3)$) underlies the electroweak and strong interactions.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The (1,0) ⊕ (0,1) Representation and the Photon *(from Quantum Field Theory)*
> The 2-form $F$ carries the antisymmetric tensor representation of the Lorentz group, which splits into self-dual and anti-self-dual parts (eigenspaces of $\star$, with $\star^2 = -1$ on 2-forms). The combinations $\mathbf{E}\pm ic\mathbf{B}$ live in the $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ pieces — the classical shadow of the **photon's two helicity states**, the organising principle of the quantised electromagnetic field.

## §21.2 Change of Observer and Field Invariants

- **[[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields]]**
	- Under a boost of velocity $\mathbf{U}$ between inertial observers, the field components *along* $\mathbf{U}$ are unchanged ($\mathbf{E}'_\parallel = \mathbf{E}_\parallel$, $\mathbf{B}'_\parallel = \mathbf{B}_\parallel$) while those *transverse* mix electric and magnetic, scaled by $\Gamma$: $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})_\perp$, $\mathbf{B}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{B}-\tfrac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E})_\perp$. This is just $F'_{\alpha\beta} = \Lambda_\alpha{}^\mu\Lambda_\beta{}^\nu F_{\mu\nu}$ read in blocks. A pure electric field acquires a magnetic field in any frame where $\mathbf{U}\not\parallel\mathbf{E}$; the non-relativistic limit is $\mathbf{E}' = \mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{B}' = \mathbf{B}$.

- **[[Thm - The Electromagnetic Field Invariants]]**
	- The two scalars $I_1 = \tfrac12 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = \tfrac14(\star F)_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ are Lorentz-invariant, the only two scalars built from $F$. Their signs classify the field independently of any observer: $I_1>0$ mostly magnetic ($cB>E$ always), $I_1<0$ mostly electric, $I_1=0$ equal amplitudes; $I_2=0$ means $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ always; $I_1=I_2=0$ is the **null** (radiative) field. They are the real and imaginary parts of the single complex invariant $(\mathbf{E}+ic\mathbf{B})^2 = -I_1 + 2iI_2$.

- **[[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields]]**
	- If the field is *not null* at an event, there is an observer for whom $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are parallel (or one vanishes). The proof boosts normal to the field plane and solves a quadratic in $U/c$ whose discriminant is $(I_1^2+4I_2^2)/(\text{positive})\ge0$; the admissible root $U<c$ exists unless $I_1=I_2=0$. For crossed fields ($I_2=0$, $I_1\ne0$) the reduction makes the field purely magnetic at $U=E/B$ (if $I_1>0$) or purely electric at $U=c^2B/E$ (if $I_1<0$) — the former is the $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$ drift velocity.

- **[[Def - Field of a Charge in Uniform Translation]]**
	- The field of a charge moving uniformly at $\mathbf{U}$ is the *boosted Coulomb field*: $\mathbf{E} = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0[\Gamma^2(x-Ut)^2+y^2+z^2]^{3/2}}[(x-Ut)e_x+ye_y+ze_z]$, pointing radially from the *present* position, with $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$. The field is weaker by $\Gamma^2$ ahead/behind and stronger by $\Gamma$ abreast (the "pancake"); the non-relativistic $\mathbf{B}$ is the Biot–Savart law. This is the relativistic *origin of magnetism*: a charge with no magnetic field in its own frame carries one in any frame where it moves.

- **[[Ex - Transforming electric and magnetic fields between frames]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Assemble $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ into $F_{\mu\nu}$ and boost it; verify the longitudinal components are unchanged and the transverse mix by $\Gamma$, and that a pure electric field acquires $\mathbf{B}' = -\frac{\gamma}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$ in a moving frame.

- **[[Ex - Field invariants classify the field]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Compute $I_1$ and $I_2$ for given fields, classify as mostly electric / mostly magnetic / null, and decide whether a frame exists making the field purely electric, purely magnetic, or unchanged in character.

- **[[Ex - The field of a uniformly moving charge]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Boost the Coulomb field to derive the pancaked electric field and the circulating magnetic field of a fast charge; recover the Biot–Savart law in the slow limit and verify $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ from $I_2=0$.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The E×B Drift and Magnetic Confinement *(from Plasma Physics)*
> The reducing velocity for crossed fields is the $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}/B^2$ **drift velocity**, the charge-independent flow at which a particle's gyration centre moves — the foundation of **guiding-centre theory** and magnetic-confinement fusion, and the dynamics of the Van Allen belts.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Theta Term and Electromagnetic Duality *(from Quantum Field Theory)*
> The pseudoscalar $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ is the natural object in theories with **magnetic monopoles** and electric–magnetic duality; added to the action as $\theta I_2$ it is a total derivative classically but physical quantum-mechanically (the **Witten effect** shifts a monopole's charge), the seed of the $\theta$-vacuum and the strong-CP problem.

## §21.3 Charged-Particle Motion and Accelerators

- **[[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field]]**
	- The equation of motion $m\,dU^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$ has solutions classified by the invariants. A **purely magnetic** field gives a helix at the cyclotron frequency $\omega_B = qB/m$, Larmor radius $R = P\sin\theta/(qB)$, constant speed (the magnetic field does no work). A **purely electric** field gives hyperbolic (uniformly accelerated) motion, proper acceleration $qE/m$, energy gain $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{kin}} = qEz = q\Delta V$. **Crossed** fields give a trochoid governed by $\ddot u^2 + (1-\beta^2)\omega_B^2 u^2 = 0$ with $\beta = E/(cB)$, $1-\beta^2 = I_1/(c^2B^2)$; the Wien condition $U = E/B$ selects undeflected particles. The actual orbital frequency is $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma_0$, which detunes a fixed-frequency cyclotron at high energy.

- **[[Ex - The cyclotron frequency and momentum from orbit radius]]** (⭐)
	- Derive $\omega_B = qB/m$ and $R = P\sin\theta/(qB)$ from the equation of motion; use the radius of a track in a magnetic field to read off a particle's momentum, and explain why $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma$ detunes the cyclotron.

- **[[Ex - The Wien filter velocity selector]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show that in crossed $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ a particle of speed $U = E/B$ passes undeflected (electric and magnetic forces cancel), that this is the boost to the purely-magnetic frame, and design a velocity selector for a given speed.

- **[[Ex - Energy gain in a linear accelerator]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Prove that only the electric field does work, $\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{kin}} = qEz = q\Delta V$, identical in form to the non-relativistic result; compute the Lorentz factor an electron and a proton reach across a given potential, and explain why a cyclotron cannot accelerate electrons.

- **[[Ex - Synchrotron field ramp and the LHC magnets]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Use $B = P/(qR)$ to find the magnetic field needed to hold an ultrarelativistic particle on a fixed-radius ring; compute the LHC's bending field for $7\,$TeV protons on a $4.3\,$km radius, and contrast the synchrotron with the cyclotron and synchrocyclotron.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Particle Accelerators *(from Accelerator Physics)*
> The three motion cases are the building blocks of every accelerator: the **linac** ($\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{kin}} = q\Delta V$), the **cyclotron** (constant $\omega_B$, $\Gamma\approx1$), the **synchrocyclotron** (ramp the frequency $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma$), and the **synchrotron** (ramp the field $B = P/(qR)$). **Storage rings** hold pre-accelerated beams for collisions or to harvest synchrotron radiation — the LHC, Tevatron, RHIC, and the light sources SOLEIL and ESRF.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Penning Trap and Precision QED *(from Atomic Physics)*
> A static magnetic field plus a quadrupole electric field — a **Penning trap** — confines a single charge whose cyclotron frequency $\omega_B = qB/m$ is measured to extraordinary precision, yielding the most accurate electron and proton magnetic moments and the cleanest tests of **quantum electrodynamics**.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The exercises of this chapter pursue a recognisable handful of goals. The most common is to **transform fields between frames**: given $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ in one frame and a relative velocity, find the fields in another — a direct application of the [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields|transformation law]], where the whole skill is tracking which components are longitudinal (unchanged) and which transverse (mixed by $\Gamma$). A second target is to **classify a field invariantly**: compute $I_1$ and $I_2$ and decide whether the field is mostly electric, mostly magnetic, or null, and what canonical form it can be boosted to. A third is to **find the field of a moving source**: boost the Coulomb (or static-magnetic) field of a source at rest to get its lab-frame field, the route to the [[Def - Field of a Charge in Uniform Translation|moving-charge field]] and the relativistic origin of magnetism. A fourth, the dynamical core, is to **solve a charged particle's trajectory** in a given field — helical, hyperbolic, or trochoidal — by writing the equation of motion in an adapted frame and decoupling it. A fifth, the most applied, is to **design or analyse an accelerator component**: compute the energy gain across a gap ($q\Delta V$), the cyclotron frequency, the field needed for a fixed-radius ring ($B = P/(qR)$), or the Wien-filter pass-velocity ($E/B$). Across all five, the unifying remark is that you understand an electromagnetic situation when you know which features are observer-dependent (the split into $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, the three-force, the energy) and which are invariant (the tensor $F$, the scalars $I_1$ and $I_2$, the rest mass).

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **Fields are given in some frame**, which immediately supplies the component matrix $F_{\mu\nu}$ and, through a boost, the fields in any other frame — the richest source, because it converts a field in one frame into the field, force, and trajectory in any other. **A source is at rest in some frame** — a charge, a current, a capacitor — where its field is elementary (Coulomb, static magnetic), and one boosts out; this is how the field of a moving charge, and the relativistic explanation of magnetism, are obtained. **The field invariants are computable** — $I_1$ and $I_2$ in any convenient frame fix the field's class everywhere, constraining the transformed fields and the trajectory without further work. **A particle's charge-to-mass ratio and the field are given** — then the cyclotron frequency $qB/m$, the Larmor radius, and the energy gain follow directly, the staples of accelerator and spectrometer problems. **The field is uniform** — a constant $F^\mu{}_\nu$, so the equation of motion is linear with constant coefficients and integrates to an exponential (rotation or boost). The recurring move is to route a source to a target: given fields route through the transformation law to fields in another frame; a rest-frame source routes through a boost to its moving field; computed invariants route through the classification to a canonical form; a uniform field routes through the equation of motion to a trajectory. The [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves almost every problem in the chapter is assembled from. Because the reader's difficulty is the physics, not the index algebra, each operation states explicitly *which observer measures which field* — that bookkeeping is where electromagnetic-relativity errors live. When stuck, scan the list and try each one.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Assemble the field tensor from $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ (and read it back).** Given the fields relative to an observer, build $F_{\mu\nu}$ with $\mathbf{E}$ in the time-space block ($F_{0i} = E_i$) and $c\mathbf{B}$ in the space-space block ($F_{ij} = -c\epsilon_{ijk}B^k$); see [[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor]]. *Trigger:* a problem gives $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ and asks for anything covariant (a boost, an invariant, a force). *Pattern:* "write $F_{\mu\nu}$, manipulate the tensor, read off the new $\mathbf{E}'$, $\mathbf{B}'$."

2. **Apply the field transformation law.** For a boost of velocity $\mathbf{U}$, keep the longitudinal components and mix the transverse ones by $\Gamma$: $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})_\perp$, $\mathbf{B}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{B}-\tfrac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E})_\perp$; see [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields]]. *Trigger:* fields are known to one observer and wanted by another. The sign of the cross term depends on whether $\mathbf{U}$ is the velocity of the new frame relative to the old or the reverse — fix it once and check against the non-relativistic limit $\mathbf{E}' = \mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}$.

3. **Work in the source's rest frame, then boost out.** Any charge, current, or field configuration has a frame in which it is simplest — a charge is static (Coulomb), a current is steady. Compute the field there, then boost to the frame the problem asks about; see [[Def - Field of a Charge in Uniform Translation]]. *Trigger:* the field is produced by a moving source. This is the relativistic analogue of choosing convenient coordinates, and it is *the* way to see that magnetism is electrostatics boosted.

4. **Compute the invariants in the most convenient frame.** $I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ are the same in every frame; see [[Thm - The Electromagnetic Field Invariants]]. *Trigger:* you want a frame-independent statement about the field, or you want to know what form it can be boosted to. Evaluate them where the fields are simplest (often a rest frame where one field vanishes), and the classification holds everywhere.

5. **Reduce to a pure or parallel field by a chosen boost.** For a non-null field, boost normal to the field plane to make $\mathbf{E}\parallel\mathbf{B}$; for crossed fields ($I_2=0$), boost at $U = E/B$ (if $cB>E$) or $U = c^2B/E$ (if $E>cB$) to kill one field; see [[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields]]. *Trigger:* a charged-particle motion problem in a general field, or a request to simplify a field. Solve the motion in the reduced frame and boost back.

6. **Write the equation of motion and decouple it.** Use $m\,dU^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$; in a frame adapted to the field it decouples into a hyperbolic (electric) pair and a circular (magnetic) pair; see [[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field]]. *Trigger:* a trajectory is wanted in a uniform field. *Pattern:* "adapt the frame so $\mathbf{E}\parallel\mathbf{B}\parallel e_3$, solve the two 2D systems, integrate to the worldline."

7. **Use that the magnetic field does no work.** Energy changes only through $d\mathfrak{E}/dt = q\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{V}$; in a purely magnetic field the speed and $\Gamma$ are constant; see [[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force]]. *Trigger:* any energy or speed question. The energy gained over a path is $q\!\int\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{x} = q\Delta V$, independent of any magnetic field present.

8. **Extract the cyclotron frequency and Larmor radius.** From the circular part of the motion, $\omega_B = qB/m$ (charge-to-mass ratio times field) and $R = P\sin\theta/(qB)$ (transverse momentum over $qB$); see [[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field]]. *Trigger:* a particle in a magnetic field, or a momentum measurement from a track. Remember the *orbital* frequency is the velocity-dependent $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma$, not $\omega_B$.

9. **Project the four-force onto an observer.** Decompose $f = qF(\cdot,U)$ into the spatial Lorentz force $q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{B})$ and the power $q\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{V}$; see [[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force]]. *Trigger:* you need the elementary three-force or the rate of working in a specific frame. The four-force is the invariant object; its projection is what an observer measures.

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Treating $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ as separate Lorentz vectors
> It is tempting to think of the electric and magnetic fields as two independent three-vectors that each transform on their own — $\mathbf{E}$ rotating as a vector, $\mathbf{B}$ likewise — and to boost them separately. They are *not* independent: they are the blocks of a single 2-form, and a boost *mixes* them ([[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields]]). The counterexample is a pure electric field, $\mathbf{B}=0$: boosting it produces a nonzero $\mathbf{B}' = -\frac{\gamma}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$, so $\mathbf{B}$ did not "stay zero" as a separate vector would. The operation becomes legal only when you boost the *tensor* $F$ (or use the transformation law that mixes the fields), never the fields as if they were unlinked.

> [!warning] 2. Assuming a magnetic field can change a particle's energy
> Because a magnetic field exerts a force, it is tempting to think it can speed a particle up. It cannot: the Lorentz force $q\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{B}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$, so $d\mathfrak{E}/dt = q\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{V}$ has no magnetic term ([[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force]]). The counterexample is a charge in a pure magnetic field: it circles forever at *constant* speed and energy. The operation "the magnet accelerates the particle" is legal only in the sense of changing the *direction* of momentum; the magnetic field never changes the *magnitude* of the velocity or the kinetic energy — that requires an electric field, which is why every accelerator's energy comes from $\mathbf{E}$.

> [!warning] 3. Using the cyclotron frequency as the orbital frequency at high energy
> The cyclotron frequency $\omega_B = qB/m$ is constant, depending only on the field and the charge-to-mass ratio, and it is tempting to take it as the frequency at which the particle actually orbits. The true orbital frequency is $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma_0$, slowed by the relativistic mass increase ([[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field]]). The counterexample is a relativistic proton in a cyclotron: tuning the accelerating field to $\omega_B$ keeps it in resonance only while $\Gamma_0\approx1$; at higher energy the particle falls out of step and stops gaining energy — which is precisely why the synchrocyclotron and synchrotron were invented. The naive $\omega_B$ is legal only in the non-relativistic limit.

> [!warning] 4. Forgetting the null case when reducing a field
> Having learned that any field can be boosted to make $\mathbf{E}\parallel\mathbf{B}$ or to kill one field, it is tempting to assert this *always* works. It fails for the **null** field, $I_1 = I_2 = 0$ ([[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields]]). The counterexample is a plane electromagnetic wave: it has $E = cB$ and $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ in every frame, so no boost makes it purely electric, purely magnetic, or static — the required boost would need $U = c$. The reduction is legal only for non-null fields; the radiation fields are the exceptional orbit that cannot be simplified.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won at the moment you decide whether you are working with the *tensor* $F$ (covariant, frame-independent) or with its *shadows* $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ (frame-dependent), and set up the bookkeeping of which observer measures what. Almost every electromagnetic-relativity error is a frame error, so begin by naming the frames and the observer in each.

If the problem **asks to transform fields between frames**, the decisive move is to split each field into parts parallel and perpendicular to the boost velocity $\mathbf{U}$, leave the parallel parts alone, and mix the perpendicular parts by $\Gamma$ using the [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields|transformation law]]. The reason this works is that the boost only rotates the time axis into the boost direction, which leaves the boost-parallel field components fixed (like the radial component of a vector under a rotation about that radius) and shuffles the boost-transverse electric and magnetic components into each other. The one decision that traps people is the sign and direction of the cross-product term: settle once whether $\mathbf{U}$ is the velocity of the new frame relative to the old, and check your formula against the non-relativistic limit $\mathbf{E}' = \mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}$.

If the problem **asks for the field of a moving source**, do not attempt to solve Maxwell's equations with a moving charge density. Go to the frame where the source is at rest, where the field is elementary — Coulomb for a charge, a static field for a steady current — and then apply the transformation law (and the Lorentz transformation of the coordinates) to boost the field into the lab frame. This is how the [[Def - Field of a Charge in Uniform Translation|field of a moving charge]] is obtained, and it reveals magnetism as a relativistic effect: there is no magnetic field in the charge's rest frame, so the lab-frame magnetic field is purely an artefact of the boost. The strategic lesson is that *relativity contains magnetostatics*: you never need an independent law for moving charges.

If the problem **asks to classify a field or to find what it can be reduced to**, compute the two [[Thm - The Electromagnetic Field Invariants|invariants]] $I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ in whichever frame the fields are simplest. Their signs are frame-independent and tell you everything: $I_1$ decides mostly-electric versus mostly-magnetic, $I_2 = 0$ marks $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$, and $I_1 = I_2 = 0$ flags the null (radiation) field that cannot be simplified. The [[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields|reduction theorem]] then tells you the canonical form: a frame with $\mathbf{E}'\parallel\mathbf{B}'$ for any non-null field, or a purely-electric/purely-magnetic frame when $I_2 = 0$. This converts a hard problem about a general field into an easy one about a pure field.

If the problem **asks for a charged particle's trajectory**, the routine is: identify the field type from the invariants, reduce to a pure or parallel field if helpful, write the equation of motion $m\,dU^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$ in a frame adapted to the field, and decouple it into a hyperbolic (electric) pair and a circular (magnetic) pair. The magnetic pair gives circular motion at the cyclotron frequency $\omega_B = qB/m$; the electric pair gives hyperbolic (uniformly accelerated) motion; their combination, for parallel fields, is a helix of increasing pitch ([[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field]]). Throughout, exploit that *the magnetic field does no work*: in any purely magnetic region the speed and $\Gamma$ are constant, which often shortcuts the whole calculation.

If the problem **is about an accelerator**, the master facts are three: the energy gain is $q\Delta V$ (electric only, since the magnetic field does no work); the orbital frequency is the velocity-dependent $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma$ (which detunes a fixed-frequency cyclotron); and to hold a particle on a fixed-radius ring the field must ramp as $B = P/(qR)$ (the synchrotron principle). Almost every accelerator question is an application of one of these, with the relativistic kinematics ($P = \Gamma mV$, $\mathfrak{E} = \Gamma mc^2$) supplying the connection between energy, momentum, and speed.

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **the electromagnetic field is one tensor, and $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$ are how an observer sees it.** Every question in this chapter is, at bottom, the question "what is the same in all frames, and what depends on the observer?" — the invariants $F$, $I_1$, $I_2$ are objective; the split into electric and magnetic, the three-force, and the energy are not. Decide which side of that line your quantity lives on, and the problem organises itself.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor|The field tensor F]]**: the antisymmetric $(0,2)$ tensor with $\mathbf{E}$ in its time-space block and $c\mathbf{B}$ in its space-space block. This is the most-used single object in the chapter, because it is the *invariant* carrier of the electromagnetic field: every covariant statement — the transformation law, the invariants, the equation of motion — is a manipulation of $F$. **Typical use:** whenever a problem gives $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ and asks for anything frame-independent or any change of frame, assemble $F_{\mu\nu}$, do the tensor operation, and read the new fields off the blocks. Its antisymmetry instantly gives the purity of the Lorentz force and the orthogonality of force to four-velocity.

- **[[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields|The field transformation law]]**: $\mathbf{E}'_\parallel = \mathbf{E}_\parallel$, $\mathbf{B}'_\parallel = \mathbf{B}_\parallel$, $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})_\perp$, $\mathbf{B}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{B}-\tfrac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E})_\perp$. Its recognisable setup is fields known to one observer and wanted by another. **Typical use:** to find the field of a moving source (boost the rest-frame field), to see that $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are observer-dependent (a pure electric field gains a magnetic field), and to reduce a field to a simpler form. The reusable content is "longitudinal unchanged, transverse mixed by $\Gamma$" — the universal behaviour of any 2-form under a boost.

- **[[Thm - The Electromagnetic Field Invariants|The invariants I₁, I₂]]**: $I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$, the only two Lorentz scalars built from $F$. **Typical use:** to classify a field independently of any observer (mostly electric, mostly magnetic, null), to decide what canonical form it can be boosted to, and as a consistency check on any field transformation (the invariants must come out the same). Reach for them the instant a frame-independent statement about a field is wanted; evaluate them where the fields are simplest. They are the real and imaginary parts of the single complex invariant $(\mathbf{E}+ic\mathbf{B})^2$.

- **[[Def - The Lorentz Four-Force|The Lorentz four-force]] and its corollary "$\mathbf{B}$ does no work"**: $f = qF(\cdot,U)$, projecting to $q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{B})$ and power $q\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{V}$. **Typical use:** the energy-only-from-$\mathbf{E}$ fact is the single most labour-saving device in trajectory and accelerator problems — in any magnetic region the speed is constant, and the total energy gain over a path is $q\Delta V$ regardless of the magnetic field. Recognise its applicability whenever energy, speed, or $\Gamma$ is in question.

- **[[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field|The cyclotron frequency and Larmor radius]]**: $\omega_B = qB/m$, $R = P\sin\theta/(qB)$, with orbital frequency $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma_0$. **Typical use:** to find the trajectory in a magnetic field, to measure momentum from a track radius ($P_\perp = qBR$), and to design cyclotrons and spectrometers. The reusable subtlety is the $\Gamma$-detuning of the orbital frequency, which dictates the entire architecture of high-energy circular accelerators (synchrocyclotron, synchrotron).

# Bridges

1. **Gauge theory — the field is the curvature of a $U(1)$ connection.** A connection on a principal $U(1)$ bundle is, locally, a one-form $A$ (the gauge potential), and its **curvature** is the 2-form $F = dA$ obtained by the [[Special Relativity XIX/Def - The Exterior Derivative|exterior derivative]]. The electromagnetic field tensor of this chapter is exactly that curvature, read in an inertial frame: the relation $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is the coordinate expression of $F = dA$, and gauge invariance $A\to A+d\chi$ leaves $F$ unchanged because $d^2\chi = 0$. The homogeneous Maxwell equations $dF = 0$ are the **Bianchi identity** for this curvature, automatic from $F = dA$. The vault's [[Gauge Theory I — Connections on Vector Bundles and the Electromagnetic Connection]] develops precisely this picture, with the electromagnetic connection as the abelian prototype; the non-abelian generalisation replaces $U(1)$ by a compact Lie group $G$, makes $A$ Lie-algebra-valued, and gives the curvature a self-interaction term $F = dA + A\wedge A$ — the structure of Yang–Mills theory underlying the weak and strong forces. Electromagnetism is the simplest gauge theory, and this chapter is where its field strength is first met as a geometric object.

2. **Exterior calculus — the field, its dual, and the invariants as forms.** The field $F$ is a degree-two [[Def - Alternate Forms and the Exterior Product|alternating form]]; its [[Def - The Hodge Star|Hodge dual]] $\star F$ is another 2-form, obtained by contracting with the [[Def - The Levi-Civita Tensor|Levi-Civita tensor]] and exchanging $\mathbf{E}\leftrightarrow c\mathbf{B}$ (up to sign). The two invariants are top-form constructions: $I_1 = \star(F\wedge\star F)$ and $I_2 = \tfrac12\star(F\wedge F)$, the Hodge duals of the 4-forms $F\wedge\star F$ and $F\wedge F$. That $\star^2 = -1$ on 2-forms in four-dimensional Lorentzian signature is what makes the field split into self-dual and anti-self-dual parts (the $\pm i$ eigenspaces of $\star$), carrying $\mathbf{E}\pm ic\mathbf{B}$; this is why there are exactly two real invariants — they are the real and imaginary parts of one complex invariant. The whole apparatus of [[Special Relativity XVIII — Tensors, Alternate Forms and Hodge Duality]] is what makes electromagnetism's covariant formulation possible, and this chapter is its first physical application.

3. **Plasma physics — the $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$ drift and guiding-centre motion.** The [[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields|reduction]] of a crossed field to a purely magnetic one is achieved by a boost at velocity $\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}/B^2$, and this same velocity is the **drift velocity** of plasma physics: in a magnetised plasma, a charged particle gyrates rapidly at the cyclotron frequency $\omega_B$ about a *guiding centre*, which drifts at $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}/B^2$ — independent of the particle's charge and mass. The relativistic reduction theorem is the exact statement of which the non-relativistic drift is the slow-field limit: in the drifting frame, the field is purely magnetic and the motion is pure gyration. Adding field gradients and curvature produces the further gradient and curvature drifts, and the whole guiding-centre approximation, with the Larmor radius $R = P_\perp/(qB)$ as the small parameter, is the foundation of magnetic-confinement fusion (tokamaks, stellarators) and of the dynamics of the Van Allen radiation belts and the solar wind. The cyclotron frequency and Larmor radius of [[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field|this chapter]] are the two fundamental scales of all magnetised-plasma physics.

4. **Accelerator physics — the three motion cases as machine designs.** The classification of charged-particle motion in [[Thm - Motion of a Charge in a Uniform Field|§21.3]] is the physical content of every accelerator. The **linac** exploits the purely-electric case: successive radio-frequency cavities each add $q\Delta V$ to the energy, and the SLAC machine reaches $50\,$GeV over $3.2\,$km. The **cyclotron** exploits the purely-magnetic case to recirculate the particle through a fixed accelerating gap, but works only while $\Gamma\approx1$ because the orbital frequency $\omega = \omega_B/\Gamma$ detunes — limiting proton cyclotrons to $\sim20\,$MeV and excluding electrons entirely (they go relativistic too soon, so electrostatic accelerators suffice for them). The **synchrocyclotron** ramps the accelerating frequency to follow $\omega_B/\Gamma$; the **synchrotron** instead holds the particle on a fixed-radius ring by ramping the magnetic field as $B = P/(qR)$ in lockstep with the rising momentum, which is how the LHC bends $7\,$TeV protons on a $4.3\,$km radius with $8.3\,$T superconducting magnets at $1.9\,$K. **Storage rings** hold pre-accelerated beams for collisions (RHIC, the LHC as a collider) or to harvest the synchrotron radiation emitted by circulating relativistic electrons (SOLEIL, ESRF). Every one of these is an application of "the magnetic field steers, the electric field accelerates", which is the corollary that the magnetic field does no work.

5. **General relativity — the field as a source of curvature.** The electromagnetic field tensor is the input to the field's [[Def - The Energy-Momentum Tensor|energy-momentum tensor]] $T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta})$, quadratic in $F$, whose components are the energy density $\tfrac12(\varepsilon_0\mathbf{E}^2 + \mathbf{B}^2/\mu_0)$, the Poynting momentum density $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}/(\mu_0 c^2)$, and the Maxwell stress. In [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild|general relativity]], $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the source on the right-hand side of Einstein's equations $G^{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T^{\mu\nu}/c^4$, so the electromagnetic field *gravitates*: an electromagnetic field curves spacetime, the basis of charged black holes (Reissner–Nordström) and of the energy conditions that constrain gravitational collapse. The trace-freeness of the electromagnetic $T^{\mu\nu}$ (a consequence of the conformal invariance of source-free Maxwell theory) is why a gas of photons has equation of state $p = \rho c^2/3$ — the radiation-dominated era of cosmology. The field tensor of this chapter is thus not only the carrier of electromagnetism but, through its energy-momentum, a source of gravitation.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are one tensor seen by an observer.** The single idea that reorganises all of electromagnetism is that the electric and magnetic fields are not two physical objects but two faces of one — the antisymmetric tensor $F$. An observer with four-velocity $U_0$ slices $F$ along its time axis: the contraction $F(\cdot,U_0)$ is the electric field, the dual contraction $\star F(U_0,\cdot)$ the magnetic field. A different observer slices differently and reports different $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, mixing one into the other, but the tensor $F$ they are slicing is the same. Once this is internalised, the structure of the chapter is predictable: there must be a transformation law (how the slices change), invariants (what the slicing cannot change), a classification (the orbit types of the tensor under the Lorentz group), and a force law built directly from the tensor. The slogan "magnetism is electricity seen from a moving frame" is the literal truth of this frame: the magnetic field of a current is the electric field of the moving charges, viewed from the lab. The deepest version is that even the distinction "electric versus magnetic" is observer-dependent — only $I_1$ and $I_2$ are objective — so the right ontology is the tensor, with $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ as its shadows.

**The true name of the electromagnetic field is "the curvature whose contraction with four-velocity is the four-force".** The coordinate matrix of $F$, with its $\pm\mathbf{E}$ and $c\mathbf{B}$ entries, is the right thing to *compute* with but the wrong thing to *think*. The operational characterisation — the one that generalises to gauge theory, that explains why the field acts as an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation, that connects to the action principle — is: $F$ is the antisymmetric tensor that, contracted with a particle's four-velocity, returns the four-force per unit charge, $f = qF(\cdot,U)$; equivalently, it is the curvature $F = dA$ of the electromagnetic connection. From the first characterisation, antisymmetry and rest-mass conservation are immediate; from the second, gauge invariance and the homogeneous Maxwell equations are immediate. When you see "electromagnetic field", do not picture the matrix — picture the object that turns velocities into forces, and that is the curvature of a $U(1)$ connection.

**A trigger-reaction pattern: "moving source $\Rightarrow$ boost the rest-frame field."** The single most reusable move in the chapter is, when faced with the field of any moving source, to refuse to solve for it directly and instead go to the frame where the source is at rest, where the field is elementary, and boost. A moving charge: its rest-frame field is Coulomb, so boost the Coulomb field. A current loop seen by a moving observer: go to the loop's rest frame. A relativistic beam: the field is the boosted static field of the bunch. This pattern is why special relativity *contains* magnetostatics — you never need a separate law for moving charges, only the transformation law applied to the static field. The reaction is automatic: see "moving source", go to its rest frame, boost out.

**Relativity makes the field more absolute, not less.** Just as relativistic kinematics replaces the separate absolutes of time and space with the single absolute interval, relativistic electromagnetism replaces the separate fields $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ — each frame-dependent — with the single absolute tensor $F$ and its two invariant scalars. The popular reading, that "everything is relative", is again backwards: the electric and magnetic fields are demoted to observer-dependent shadows, but in their place stand firmer objects — the tensor $F$, the invariants $I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2$ and $I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$, the rest mass a charge keeps through any field — that every observer agrees on. The working physicist's instinct, drilled by every exercise, is to find what does not change: the field's invariant class, the four-force as a four-vector, the energy-momentum it carries. The frame-dependent fields are the appearances; the tensor and its invariants are the objects casting them.

**Indefiniteness and the Hodge star give exactly two invariants.** The reason there are precisely two field invariants, and that they pair as the real and imaginary parts of one complex scalar, traces to the same indefinite signature that produces all of relativity. In four-dimensional Lorentzian space the Hodge star squares to $-1$ on 2-forms, so $\star$ has eigenvalues $\pm i$ and the (complexified) field splits into self-dual and anti-self-dual parts carrying $\mathbf{E}\pm ic\mathbf{B}$. A complex three-vector under the complexified rotation group $SO(3,\mathbb{C})$ has exactly one invariant — its complex square — and $(\mathbf{E}+ic\mathbf{B})^2 = -I_1 + 2iI_2$ is that invariant, its real and imaginary parts being the two we use. In Euclidean signature $\star^2 = +1$, the eigenvalues would be real, the field would split into two *real* (not complex) pieces, and the invariant structure would be different. The two invariants of the electromagnetic field are thus a direct fingerprint of the minus signs in the metric — the same minus signs that make the light cone, the causal structure, and the whole of relativity.
