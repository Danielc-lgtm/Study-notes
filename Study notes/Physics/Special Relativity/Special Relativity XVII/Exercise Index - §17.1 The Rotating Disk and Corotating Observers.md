## §17.1 The Rotating Disk and Corotating Observers — Exercises

The exercises of §17.1 drill the kinematics of the rotating disk: the helical worldline of a corotating observer, its centripetal four-acceleration, and its four-rotation. The unifying technique is to write the corotating worldline as a circular helix in inertial coordinates and read off everything by differentiation, computing the rim Lorentz factor $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ first and treating every result as a multiple of a power of $\Gamma$. Two surprises emerge that have no Newtonian counterpart: the four-acceleration is enhanced by $\Gamma^2$ over the Newtonian centripetal value, and the corotating observer's own four-rotation is $\Gamma^2$ times the disk's — the excess being the Thomas precession of a gyroscope dragged around a circle. The light-cylinder bound $r < c/\omega$, forced by the timelikeness of the worldline, recurs throughout and is the flat-spacetime ancestor of the Kerr ergosphere.

- [[Ex - The four-velocity and four-acceleration of a corotating observer]] (⭐⭐) — write the helical worldline, compute the velocity $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$ and the rim bound $r < c/\omega$, boost to the four-velocity, and differentiate in proper time to get the centripetal four-acceleration $\Gamma^2 r\omega^2$, with the two factors of $\Gamma$ traced to the proper-time derivative ([[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer]], [[Thm - 4-Acceleration and 4-Rotation of the Corotating Observer]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity]]).

- [[Ex - The four-rotation excess and Thomas precession of a corotating gyroscope]] (⭐⭐) — explain why a rigidly corotating observer's four-rotation $\Gamma^2\vec\omega$ exceeds the disk rate, identify the excess $(\Gamma^2 - 1)\vec\omega$ as the Thomas precession of a gyroscope in circular orbit (carrying the famous factor $\tfrac12$), and compute the per-revolution advance $2\pi v^2/c^2$ of a rim gyroscope relative to a hub gyroscope ([[Thm - 4-Acceleration and 4-Rotation of the Corotating Observer]], [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]], [[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer]]).

- [[Ex - Measuring rotation with gyroscopes and the light cylinder]] (⭐⭐) — show that a single free gyroscope is blind to rotation about its own spin axis (the kernel of the cross product $\vec\omega\times\vec s$) so two non-parallel gyroscopes are needed to determine $\vec\omega$, and show that the divergence of $\Gamma^2\omega$ at the light cylinder $r = c/\omega$ signals the non-existence of the corotating observer (null worldline) rather than a physical infinity ([[Def - Measurement of Rotation (Non-rotating Observer)]], [[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer]], [[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]]).
