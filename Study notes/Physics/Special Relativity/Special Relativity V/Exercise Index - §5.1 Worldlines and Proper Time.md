## §5.1 Worldlines and Proper Time — Exercises

The exercises of §5.1 drill the two foundational objects of relativistic kinematics: the [[Def - Worldline of a Particle|worldline]] as a timelike curve in spacetime, and the [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]] as the metric arc length along it — the time a comoving clock reads. The central computational skill is integrating $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$ along a worldline, and the central conceptual skill is recognising that this integrand depends on *speed* but not *acceleration*, so the proper time of any clock is a functional of its speed history alone. The accelerated-worldline exercise establishes the hyperbolic-motion template (constant proper acceleration $\Leftrightarrow$ linear rapidity $\Leftrightarrow$ hyperbola) that every later twin-paradox and accelerated-observer problem reuses; the ideal-clock exercise isolates the empirical postulate — separate from the geometry — that real clocks actually display proper time; and the classification exercise sorts worldlines into massive / photon / tachyon and shows why the speed limit is enforced by causality, not by force.

- [[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]] (⭐⭐) — derive the hyperbolic worldline of constant proper acceleration, integrate the proper time via the $\sinh$ substitution to the $\operatorname{arsinh}$ formula $\tau = (4T/\alpha)\operatorname{arsinh}(\alpha/4) \le T$, and read off the Newtonian and ultra-relativistic limits ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Rapidity]], [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]]).

- [[Ex - The ideal clock hypothesis and a circular-motion clock]] (⭐⭐) — state the ideal clock hypothesis as a postulate independent of the proper-time geometry, compute the one-revolution proper time $\Delta\tau = T/\gamma$ of a clock circling at speed $v$, and show the result depends only on speed (not the centripetal acceleration $a = \gamma^2 v^2/r$), licensing the CERN muon-storage-ring test ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Worldline of a Particle]]).

- [[Ex - Classifying worldlines and why tachyons would signal into the past]] (⭐⭐) — classify straight worldlines by the sign of the tangent's norm into massive / photon / tachyon, prove the classification is Lorentz invariant, show a spacelike worldline reverses its time-ordering under a boost so a tachyon signal would reach the past, and conclude that physical worldlines are timelike (or null) everywhere ([[Def - Worldline of a Particle]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]]).
