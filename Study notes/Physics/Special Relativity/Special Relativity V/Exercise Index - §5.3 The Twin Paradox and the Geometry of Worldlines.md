## §5.3 The Twin Paradox and the Geometry of Worldlines — Exercises

The exercises of §5.3 drill the geometric payoff of the chapter: that the straight (inertial) worldline carries the *most* proper time between two events ([[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time|the special-relativistic geodesic principle]]), so a traveller on a bent worldline ages less. The twin paradox is the canonical case — resolved by the reversed triangle inequality and the relativity of simultaneity, with the asymmetry traced to acceleration (and, more deeply, to the dissymmetry of the two worldlines). The galactic-centre exercise pushes the same proper-time geometry to astronomical scale, showing relativity permits visiting the galactic centre within a lifetime via time travel to the future, with no positive lower bound on ship-time. The Hafele–Keating exercise grounds all of this experimentally: atomic clocks flown on different worldlines really do read different elapsed times, confirming that proper time is the physical, path-dependent time — and showing that on a gravitating, rotating planet the general-relativistic redshift is a comparable, unavoidable second effect.

- [[Ex - The twin paradox]] (⭐⭐) — compute that the travelling twin ages $2T/\gamma$ against the stay-at-home twin's $2T$, locate the false symmetry (only the traveller accelerates, and acceleration is absolute), recast the result as the reversed triangle inequality $\|U+V\| \ge \|U\|+\|V\|$, and track on a spacetime diagram how the traveller's line of simultaneity sweeps the "missing" segment of the other twin's worldline at the turnaround ([[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]], [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]], [[Def - Proper Time]]).

- [[Ex - A round trip to the galactic centre]] (⭐⭐⭐) — using the acceleration timescale $T_* = 4c/g \approx 3.87$ yr, show $T = T_*\sinh(T'/T_*)$ and $d = \tfrac12 cT_*[\cosh(T'/T_*) - 1]$, deduce the galactic centre is reachable in $\sim 20$ yr of ship-time while $\sim 52{,}000$ yr pass on Earth, and reconcile this with the speed limit via length contraction and the short arc length of a near-null worldline ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Rapidity]]).

- [[Ex - The Hafele-Keating experiment]] (⭐⭐) — explain why all clocks (including the rotating ground clock) follow helical worldlines so flight direction matters, separate the special-relativistic (velocity) and general-relativistic (altitude) contributions, interpret the eastward ($-59$ ns) and westward ($+273$ ns) results, and cite the cleaner Alley split ($-5.7$ SR $+52.8$ GR $= +47.1$ ns) ([[Def - Proper Time]], [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]], [[Def - Worldline of a Particle]]).
