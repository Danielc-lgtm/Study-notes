# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole chapter: **we set $c = 1$** and use the **mostly-minus** signature $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$. With $c = 1$, time and space are measured in the same unit, and a four-velocity's components are dimensionless; where a formula is more recognisable with $c$ restored it is given both ways (restore $c$ by $t \to ct$, $v \to v/c$, and dimensional analysis). In the mostly-minus signature a four-vector $X$ is **timelike** when $X \cdot X > 0$, **spacelike** when $X \cdot X < 0$, **null** when $X \cdot X = 0$, and the [[Def - The Spacetime Interval|interval]] along a timelike step is $ds^2 = dt^2 - d\mathbf{x}^2 > 0$.

> [!warning] Convention
> This chapter follows Gourgoulhon's Chapter 2, but Gourgoulhon uses the **opposite (mostly-plus) signature** $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, in which timelike means $g(v,v) < 0$, the proper time is $c\,d\tau = \sqrt{-g(d\vec{x}, d\vec{x})}$, the four-velocity has $\vec{u}\cdot\vec{u} = -1$, and the four-acceleration has $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{a} \ge 0$. We have flipped the overall sign of the metric throughout. The translation: his $\sqrt{-g}$ becomes our $\sqrt{+ds^2}$; his timelike $g(v,v)<0$ becomes our $X\cdot X>0$; his $U\cdot U = -1$ becomes our $U\cdot U = +1$; his spacelike four-acceleration $\vec a\cdot\vec a > 0$ becomes our $A\cdot A < 0$. Be vigilant about signs when reading the source.

- $\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{L}'$ — worldlines (curves in spacetime); $\mathcal{L}_0$ — a straight (inertial/geodesic) worldline
- $A, B, P, E, R$ — events (points of Minkowski space $\mathbb{M}$); $\mathbb{M}$ or $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$ — Minkowski space
- $x^\mu = (t, x, y, z)$ — coordinates of an event in an inertial frame, $\mu = 0,1,2,3$, $x^0 = t$
- $\varphi : \lambda \mapsto \varphi(\lambda)$ — a parametrisation of a worldline; $\lambda$ a generic parameter
- $V = dX/d\lambda$ — the tangent (derivative) vector field of a parametrised worldline; $V^\mu = dx^\mu/d\lambda$
- $\tau$ — **proper time**, the metric arc length $\int\sqrt{ds^2}$ along a timelike worldline; the privileged, frame-independent parameter
- $\mathbf{u} = d\mathbf{x}/dt$ — ordinary three-velocity in a frame (bold); $u = |\mathbf{u}|$ its speed
- $\gamma = (1 - u^2)^{-1/2}$ — the Lorentz factor of a particle of speed $u$; $dt/d\tau = \gamma$
- $U = dX/d\tau = \gamma(1, \mathbf{u})$ — the **four-velocity**, the unit future timelike tangent; $U \cdot U = 1$
- $A = dU/d\tau$ — the **four-acceleration**; $A \cdot U = 0$, $A \cdot A \le 0$ (spacelike or zero); $a = \|A\| = \sqrt{-A\cdot A}$ the proper acceleration
- $X \cdot Y = \eta_{\mu\nu}X^\mu Y^\nu = X^0 Y^0 - \mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{Y}$ — the Minkowski inner product; $X_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}X^\nu$ the index-lowered form
- $P^\mu = mU^\mu = (E, \mathbf{p})$ — the [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|four-momentum]]; for a photon $P\cdot P = 0$ and $P = dX/d\lambda$ with $\lambda$ affine
- $\mathcal{I}(A), \mathcal{I}^\pm(A)$ — the light cone of event $A$, and its future/past sheets
- $(e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3)$ — the **Serret–Frenet tetrad** along a worldline, $e_0 = U$; $a, T_1, T_2$ — curvature, first and second torsions
- $\alpha = aT$ — dimensionless acceleration parameter of the Langevin traveller; $T_* = 4c/\gamma_{\!a}$ — the acceleration timescale
- Summation convention: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0,1,2,3$

# Motivation

Here is the chapter in one sentence: a particle is a curve in spacetime, the metric measures the length of that curve, and that length is the time the particle's own clock reads. Everything else — four-velocity, four-acceleration, the twin paradox, the bridge to gravity — is consequence.

[[Special Relativity IV — The Invariant Interval, Rapidity, and the Lorentz Group|The previous chapters]] built the stage: Minkowski space, the indefinite metric, the [[Def - The Spacetime Interval|invariant interval]], the [[Def - The Lorentz Group|Lorentz group]]. That was the kinematics of spacetime *itself*. Now we put a particle on the stage and ask how to describe its motion in a way that respects the geometry. The Newtonian answer — "position as a function of time" — is quietly broken, because it presupposes a universal time, and relativity has abolished that. The repair is to stop describing the particle one instant at a time and describe its *entire history* at once: a one-dimensional curve in spacetime, the **worldline**. A massive particle's worldline is everywhere [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors|timelike]] (it stays inside the light cone), a photon's is [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|null]], and these are the only physical possibilities.

The single new idea that makes everything work is **proper time**. The metric, used as "the operator that gives elapsed time along a trajectory", assigns to a timelike worldline its arc length $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2}$ — and this is the time a clock carried along the worldline actually reads. It is a Lorentz invariant (everyone agrees on a clock's reading), and it depends on the *worldline*, not just its endpoints. That last clause is the whole drama of the chapter: two travellers who part and reunite generically read *different* elapsed times, because their worldlines have different lengths. The structural backbone of the chapter is the comparison
$$
\tau[\text{bent worldline}] \;<\; \tau[\text{straight worldline}],
$$
the statement that the straight (inertial) worldline carries the *most* proper time — the exact reverse of the Euclidean fact that a straight line is the *shortest* path, and the source of the twin paradox.

Proper time also fixes the second problem the Newtonian description had: how to differentiate. Velocity is a rate of change, and in relativity you must differentiate with respect to an *invariant* parameter, or the result is not a four-vector. Coordinate time $t$ is frame-dependent and will not do; proper time $\tau$ is invariant and will. So the **four-velocity** is $U = dX/d\tau$ — a genuine four-vector, unit-normalised $U \cdot U = 1$ because proper time is arc length — and the **four-acceleration** is $A = dU/d\tau$, automatically orthogonal to $U$ and spacelike. The recurring move of all relativistic mechanics is born here: *to relativise a Newtonian rate of change, differentiate with respect to proper time, not coordinate time.* Multiplying the four-velocity by the [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|rest mass]] gives the four-momentum, the object that is conserved, and the whole of [[Special Relativity XIII — Energy and Momentum|relativistic dynamics]] follows.

The deepest reason to set all this up frame-independently — worldline as a curve, proper time as arc length, four-velocity as the unit tangent, inertial motion as extremal proper time — is that it *is already general relativity*, minus the curvature. Promote the constant metric $\eta$ to a position-dependent field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ and every construction transfers verbatim: proper time is still the arc length $\int\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu}$, free fall is still the worldline of extremal proper time (now a **geodesic**), and the four-acceleration is still what an accelerometer reads (zero in free fall). This chapter is the flat, zero-curvature prototype of the kinematics of gravitation, which is why the geometry is worth doing carefully.

The reader is assumed to have just worked through [[Special Relativity IV — The Invariant Interval, Rapidity, and the Lorentz Group|the invariant interval and the Lorentz group]], to be fluent with the indefinite metric and the timelike/spacelike/null trichotomy, and to know elementary curve geometry (tangent vectors, arc length) and single-variable calculus. The genuinely new ingredient is small — it is the willingness to treat proper time as a *length* and to let the indefiniteness of the metric reverse "shortest" into "longest".

# Concept Map

## §5.1 Worldlines and Proper Time

- **[[Def - Worldline of a Particle]]**
	- A worldline is a piecewise twice-differentiable curve $\mathcal{L}$ in Minkowski space all of whose tangent vectors are timelike, $V \cdot V > 0$ — the history of a massive particle, staying inside the light cone of each of its points. It is the primary object (not the instantaneous position, which presupposes a frame-dependent "now"), and it admits infinitely many parametrisations, all rescalings of one another. The model is the displacement between two events; the type (timelike / null / spacelike) is a Lorentz-invariant, pointwise fact, and a physical worldline keeps one type throughout. This is the flat prototype of the curved-spacetime worldline of general relativity.

- **[[Def - Proper Time]]**
	- The proper time $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$ along a timelike worldline is its metric arc length — the time a clock carried along it reads. It is a Lorentz invariant, equal in the rest frame to coordinate time, and it is independent of the parametrisation but *depends on the worldline* joining two events. The indefinite metric makes spatial motion *subtract* from elapsed time ($d\tau = dt\sqrt{1 - u^2} \le dt$), which is time dilation read as arc length. This is the metric arc length that becomes the seed of the geodesic principle of gravitation.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Geodesic Principle of Gravitation *(from General Relativity)*
> Proper time is the metric arc length of a timelike worldline. Promote the constant metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ to a position-dependent field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ and the *same* integral $\int\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu}$ is the proper time of **general relativity**, with freely-falling worldlines the **timelike geodesics** that extremise it, obeying $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho = 0$. The whole kinematic apparatus of this chapter transfers verbatim to curved spacetime; gravitational time dilation is this integral with $g_{00}(x)$ in place of $1$.

- **[[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Derive the hyperbolic worldline of constant proper acceleration ($U = (\cosh a\tau, \sinh a\tau, 0,0)$, worldline $x^2 - t^2 = a^{-2}$), integrate the proper time by the $\sinh$ substitution to $\tau = (4T/\alpha)\operatorname{arsinh}(\alpha/4) \le T$, and examine the Newtonian ($\alpha \to 0$, no deficit) and ultra-relativistic ($\alpha \to \infty$, proper time $\to 0$) limits.

- **[[Ex - The ideal clock hypothesis and a circular-motion clock]]** (⭐⭐)
	- State the ideal clock hypothesis (a clock reads proper time independent of acceleration) as a postulate separate from the geometry, compute $\Delta\tau = T/\gamma$ for a clock circling at constant speed $v$, and show the result depends only on the *speed* (not the centripetal acceleration $a = \gamma^2 v^2/r$), which is what licenses the CERN muon-storage-ring measurement of time dilation at $\sim 10^{18}g$.

- **[[Ex - Classifying worldlines and why tachyons would signal into the past]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Classify straight worldlines by the sign of the tangent's norm into massive ($>0$), photon ($=0$), tachyon ($<0$); prove the classification is Lorentz invariant; show a spacelike (tachyon) worldline reverses its time-ordering under a suitable boost, so a tachyon signal is received before sent; conclude that physical worldlines are timelike (or null) everywhere and cannot change type — the speed limit enforced by causality.

## §5.2 Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration

- **[[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]**
	- The four-velocity $U^\mu = dX^\mu/d\tau = \gamma(1, \mathbf{u})$ is the proper-time derivative of the four-position — a genuine four-vector (because $\tau$ is a scalar), the unique future-directed unit timelike tangent, $U \cdot U = 1$. The four-acceleration $A^\mu = dU^\mu/d\tau$ is automatically orthogonal to it ($A \cdot U = 0$, from differentiating $U \cdot U = 1$) and spacelike or zero ($A \cdot A \le 0$), with magnitude $\|A\|$ the proper acceleration an accelerometer reads. Differentiating with respect to proper time rather than coordinate time is what makes these four-vectors; constant proper acceleration gives hyperbolic motion.

- **[[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics]]**
	- A photon is a straight worldline whose tangent is null, $V \cdot V = 0$ — a null geodesic, also called a light ray. Along it the proper time is identically zero, so a photon has no proper time and (since no unit null vector exists) no four-velocity; it is parametrised by an affine parameter and carries a null [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon|four-momentum]] $P \cdot P = 0$ instead. The light cone $\mathcal{I}(A)$ of an event is the union of all null rays through it, separating timelike-related (causally connectible) from spacelike-related events; all light cones are parallel translates.

- **[[Ex - Four-velocity has unit norm and four-acceleration is orthogonal to it]]** (⭐)
	- Verify $U \cdot U = 1$ both in a general frame (via $\gamma^2(1 - u^2) = 1$) and in the rest frame, differentiate it to obtain $A \cdot U = 0$ in one line, prove that any vector orthogonal to a timelike vector is spacelike or zero (so $A \cdot A \le 0$), and confirm all three identities on hyperbolic motion — the algebraic backbone every later dynamical calculation rests on.

- **[[Ex - Velocity addition and aberration from the four-velocity]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Boost the four-velocity $U' = \Lambda U$ and read off component ratios to recover the relativistic velocity-addition law and the aberration formula $\tan\alpha' = u\sin\alpha/[\gamma_v(u\cos\alpha - v)]$ (stellar aberration for $u = 1$), and explain why four-velocities — living on the unit hyperboloid, not a vector space — *transform* by the Lorentz group rather than *adding*.

- **[[Ex - Why a photon has no four-velocity but a null four-momentum]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show the proper time vanishes along a null worldline so $U = dX/d\tau$ is undefined, prove no unit null vector exists (rescaling preserves zero norm), exhibit the surviving null four-momentum $P = E(1,1,0,0)$ with its affine parameter, and distinguish a null *curve* (the bending helix) from a null *geodesic* (a free photon's straight line).

> [!tip] Unlocked: Four-Momentum, $E = mc^2$, and the Four-Force *(from Energy and Momentum)*
> Multiplying the four-velocity by the invariant [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|rest mass]] gives the **four-momentum** $P^\mu = mU^\mu = (E, \mathbf{p})$, the conserved object; its time component is the energy $E = \gamma m = m + \tfrac12 mu^2 + \cdots$ (exhibiting the rest energy $mc^2$), and its norm is the mass-shell relation $P \cdot P = m^2$. Newton's second law becomes the covariant $F^\mu = dP^\mu/d\tau = mA^\mu$ with the [[Def - Four-Force|four-force]] inheriting $F \cdot U = 0$. All of [[Special Relativity XIII — Energy and Momentum]] is built on the four-velocity defined here.

> [!tip] Fermi–Walker Transport and the Frame of an Accelerated Observer *(from Observers and Accelerated Observers)*
> The four-acceleration is the first invariant of the worldline's [[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline|Serret–Frenet apparatus]]: $\|A\|$ is its curvature, and the orthonormal tetrad it generates is carried along the worldline by **Fermi–Walker transport**, the relativistic notion of a non-rotating (gyroscope) frame. This is the kinematic skeleton of an [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space|observer]]'s carried [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation|local frame]], developed in [[Special Relativity VI — Observers, Local Rest Spaces and Local Frames]] and [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers]].

## §5.3 The Twin Paradox and the Geometry of Worldlines

- **[[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]]**
	- Among all timelike worldlines joining two timelike-separated events, the straight (inertial) one has the *greatest* proper time, with equality only for the straight line itself — the special-relativistic geodesic principle. The proof is one pointwise inequality, $d\tau = dt\sqrt{1 - |\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2} \le dt$, integrated; equivalently it is the [[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality|reversed triangle inequality]] $\|U+V\| \ge \|U\|+\|V\|$ for future-timelike vectors. This is the exact reverse of the Euclidean "straight line is shortest", and it resolves the twin paradox: the inertial twin ages most.

- **[[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline]]**
	- Along a timelike worldline with nonzero four-acceleration, the Serret–Frenet tetrad $(e_0 = U, e_1, e_2, e_3)$ and three scalar invariants — the curvature $a = \|A\|$ and two torsions $T_1, T_2$ — encode the full local shape (bending, twisting out of the osculating plane, out of the osculating hyperplane). The tetrad evolves by $d(e_\alpha)/d\tau = \Omega^\alpha{}_\beta\,e_\beta$ with $\Omega$ an antisymmetric (Lorentz-algebra) "four-rotation" matrix. The curvature is what an accelerometer reads; the apparatus is the kinematic skeleton of an accelerated observer's frame and of Fermi–Walker transport.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Time Travel to the Future, and the Variational Principle of Mechanics *(from this chapter and the Principle of Least Action)*
> Because a bent worldline is *shorter* in proper time, a round trip can compress arbitrary coordinate time into short ship-time — **time travel to the future** (reach the galactic centre, ageing years while millennia pass on Earth), but never to the past (the worldline stays inside the forward light cone). And because the inertial worldline *extremises* proper time, promoting "$\delta\int d\tau = 0$" to a law gives the **action** $S = -m\int d\tau$ of the free particle, whose conjugate momentum is the four-momentum and whose Euler–Lagrange equations are $dU/d\tau = 0$ — see [[Special Relativity XV — The Principle of Least Action]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: Fermi–Walker Transport and Thomas Precession *(from Accelerated Observers)*
> The antisymmetric generator $\Omega$ of the [[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline|Serret–Frenet system]] is the four-rotation of the carried frame; splitting it into its boost part (curvature) and spatial-rotation part (torsions) separates the unavoidable relativistic rotation of an accelerated frame — **Thomas precession** — from any added spin. A frame whose only evolution is the boost part is **Fermi–Walker transported**, the relativistic definition of a non-rotating gyroscope, developed in [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers]].

- **[[Ex - The twin paradox]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Compute that the travelling twin ages $2T/\gamma$ against the stay-at-home twin's $2T$, locate the false symmetry (only the traveller accelerates, and acceleration is absolute), recast the result as the reversed triangle inequality, and track on a spacetime diagram how the traveller's line of simultaneity sweeps the "missing" segment of the other twin's worldline at the turnaround — the deepest point being that the paradox is about worldline *dissymmetry*, not acceleration per se.

- **[[Ex - A round trip to the galactic centre]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Using the acceleration timescale $T_* = 4c/g \approx 3.87$ yr, derive $T = T_*\sinh(T'/T_*)$ and $d = \tfrac12 cT_*[\cosh(T'/T_*) - 1]$, show the galactic centre ($26{,}000$ ly) is reached in $\sim 20$ yr of ship-time while $\sim 52{,}000$ yr pass on Earth, and reconcile the apparent superluminal travel with the speed limit via length contraction and the short arc length of a near-null worldline.

- **[[Ex - The Hafele-Keating experiment]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Explain why all clocks (including the rotating ground clock) follow helical worldlines so flight direction matters, separate the special-relativistic (velocity) and general-relativistic (altitude) contributions, interpret the eastward ($T' = T - 59$ ns) and westward ($T' = T + 273$ ns) results, and cite the cleaner Alley split ($-5.7$ SR $+ 52.8$ GR $= +47.1$ ns) — the experimental confirmation that proper time depends on the worldline.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The exercises of this chapter chase a small, recognisable set of goals, and naming the goal is the first move of every solution. The most common is to **compute an elapsed proper time** — the ageing of a traveller, the lifetime of a moving particle, the reading of a flown clock — always by the integral $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$ along the relevant worldline. A second is to **compare proper times of two worldlines** between the same events: which traveller is older, by how much, and why; this is the twin paradox and every ageing problem, decided by the [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time|geodesic principle]] that the straightest worldline is the longest. A third is to **build a four-vector kinematic quantity** — four-velocity, four-acceleration — and verify its defining identities ($U \cdot U = 1$, $A \cdot U = 0$), or to use it to transform a velocity or angle to another frame. A fourth is to **classify a worldline or settle a causal question**: is this trajectory a massive particle, a photon, or an excluded tachyon; can this signal be sent; does this worldline respect causality — all read off the sign of a norm and the [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|light-cone]] structure. A fifth, more geometric, is to **characterise the local shape of a worldline** through its [[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline|curvature and torsions]], or to identify the proper acceleration an accelerometer reads. The unifying remark across all five: you understand a worldline when you know its proper time (the invariant clock-reading), its four-velocity (the unit tangent), and its causal type (the sign of the tangent's norm) — every target is read off these.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A worldline is specified** — by its coordinate trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$, or by a parametrisation — and then its tangent, type, and proper time follow by differentiation and integration. **The proper acceleration is constant** — the richest source, because it forces hyperbolic motion (a [[Def - Rapidity|rapidity]] linear in proper time, $U = (\cosh a\tau, \sinh a\tau, 0, 0)$), making every integral elementary and every relation a hyperbolic function of one parameter. **A clock or particle is given in its rest frame** — where the four-velocity is $(1, \mathbf{0})$, proper time equals coordinate time, and inner products collapse to one-line statements; one computes there and transports the invariant answer out. **A quantity is recognised as a Lorentz scalar** — proper time, a four-vector norm, an inner product $U \cdot U'$ — and is then evaluated in whichever frame is easiest. **The ideal clock hypothesis is invoked** — a real clock reads proper time independent of its acceleration — which is what connects the geometric $\tau$ to a physical measurement. The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a specified worldline routes through $\int dt/\gamma$ to an elapsed time; constant proper acceleration routes through the hyperbolic parametrisation to a closed-form proper time; a rest-frame description routes through invariance to a frame-independent value; the sign of a tangent's norm routes through the light cone to a causal verdict. The [[Special Relativity V — Worldlines, Proper Time and Four-Velocity#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves almost every problem in the chapter is assembled from. Everything is self-contained: a reader who knows only that a worldline is a timelike curve, that proper time is its arc length $\int\sqrt{ds^2}$, and that the Minkowski inner product $X \cdot Y = X^0 Y^0 - \mathbf{X}\cdot\mathbf{Y}$ is the one combination every observer agrees on, should be able to follow each operation from its description alone. When stuck, scan the list and try each one.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Compute proper time as $\int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$ along the worldline.** The elapsed time for any clock or particle is the metric arc length of its [[Def - Worldline of a Particle|worldline]]; in an inertial frame this is $\int dt\sqrt{1 - u^2} = \int dt/\gamma$. *Trigger:* any question of the form "how much time passes for the particle / traveller / clock / muon". *Pattern:* parametrise the worldline, write $d\tau = dt\sqrt{1 - u(t)^2}$, integrate. The integrand contains the *speed* but never the acceleration — proper time is acceleration-blind.

2. **Differentiate with respect to proper time, not coordinate time.** To build a four-vector kinematic quantity, differentiate a four-vector with respect to the scalar $\tau$: $U = dX/d\tau$, $A = dU/d\tau$; see [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]. *Trigger:* any attempt to relativise a Newtonian rate of change. *Pattern:* convert with $d/d\tau = \gamma\,d/dt$. Using coordinate time $t$ is the standard illegal shortcut (below), because $t$ is frame-dependent and the result is not a four-vector.

3. **Use the unit-norm and orthogonality identities $U \cdot U = 1$, $A \cdot U = 0$ for free.** Every [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity]] satisfies $U \cdot U = 1$ (because proper time is arc length), and differentiating this gives $A \cdot U = 0$ at no cost; the four-acceleration is moreover spacelike, $A \cdot A \le 0$. *Trigger:* any problem involving velocity, acceleration, or force. *Pattern:* these are free equations — write them down before any dynamics; $A \cdot U = 0$ constrains the four-acceleration to three dimensions and guarantees the rest mass is preserved.

4. **Switch to rapidity / the hyperbolic parametrisation for constant proper acceleration.** Constant proper acceleration $a$ makes the [[Def - Rapidity|rapidity]] grow linearly with proper time, $\varphi = a\tau$, so $U = (\cosh a\tau, \sinh a\tau, 0, 0)$ and the worldline is the hyperbola $x^2 - t^2 = a^{-2}$. *Trigger:* the phrase "constant proper acceleration" or "uniformly accelerated". *Pattern:* hyperbolic functions of proper time are the fingerprint; every relation becomes a $\sinh, \cosh, \operatorname{arsinh}$ of one parameter.

5. **Compute an invariant in the rest frame.** Proper time, four-vector norms, and inner products $X \cdot Y$ are Lorentz scalars; evaluate them in the frame where the four-velocity is $(1, \mathbf{0})$, where most components vanish, and transport the answer everywhere. *Trigger:* any frame-independent number, or a quantity awkward in the given frame. *Pattern:* "in the rest frame, $U = (1, \mathbf 0)$, so..." — the single most powerful labour-saving device.

6. **Lorentz-transform a four-vector to find what another observer measures.** If a four-vector's components are known in one frame and a component is wanted in another, apply the matrix $U' = \Lambda U$ directly; see [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]]. *Trigger:* "what velocity / energy / frequency / angle does the *other* observer see". *Pattern:* transform the four-vector, then take ratios of components — this is how velocity addition and aberration are done, and it avoids transforming coordinates.

7. **Classify a worldline or four-vector by the sign of its norm.** Compute $V \cdot V$ for the tangent (or any four-vector): positive is timelike (massive particle), zero is null (photon), negative is spacelike (tachyon); see [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]]. *Trigger:* any causal question — is this a particle or a photon, can $A$ influence $B$, is this trajectory physically allowed. *Pattern:* the sign is Lorentz invariant, so the classification is absolute; timelike means causally connectible with invariant order, spacelike means causally disconnected with frame-dependent order.

8. **Read causal structure off the light cone.** The [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|light cone]] $\mathcal{I}(A)$ of an event partitions spacetime into the causally connectible (inside/on, timelike/null) and the causally disconnected (outside, spacelike). *Trigger:* "can $A$ influence $B$", "is this signal allowed", "do observers agree on the order". *Pattern:* $B$ is influenceable from $A$ iff $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is future timelike or null; all light cones are parallel translates, which forbids closed timelike curves in flat spacetime.

9. **Use the null four-momentum for a photon, never a four-velocity.** A [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|photon]] has no proper time and no four-velocity, but carries a null [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon|four-momentum]] $P \cdot P = 0$. *Trigger:* any massless particle in a problem. *Pattern:* reach for $P_\gamma$ with $P_\gamma \cdot P_\gamma = 0$ (an even stronger simplifier than the massive mass-shell relation, since the term vanishes when squared); parametrise by an affine parameter, not proper time.

10. **Apply the reversed triangle inequality to compare worldlines.** For future-timelike vectors, $\|U + V\| \ge \|U\| + \|V\|$ ([[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]]), so the straight worldline (sum) has more proper time than the bent one (legs). *Trigger:* "which traveller ages more", any comparison of proper times between the same two events. *Pattern:* model the legs as future-timelike vectors; straight beats bent, and the deficit measures how much the worldline bends — the [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time|geodesic principle]].

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Differentiating a four-vector with respect to coordinate time $t$
> To get a velocity one differentiates position with respect to time — so why not set $V^\mu = dX^\mu/dt$? Because $t$ is the time coordinate of one particular observer, not a Lorentz scalar. Under a boost both $X^\mu$ and $t$ change, and $dX^\mu/dt$ transforms in a tangled, non-tensorial way: it is *not* a four-vector, and a law written with it is not the same in every frame. The fix is to differentiate with respect to **proper time** $\tau$, which *is* invariant ([[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]); the relation $dt/d\tau = \gamma$ converts between the two, and the factor of $\gamma$ that distinguishes $U = dX/d\tau$ from the naive $dX/dt$ is exactly what makes $U$ a genuine four-vector. The operation becomes legal only when you remember to differentiate by the invariant clock.

> [!warning] 2. Treating "constant acceleration" as constant coordinate acceleration $d^2x/dt^2$
> Modelling a uniformly accelerated rocket by $d^2x/dt^2 = a$ (constant *coordinate* acceleration) gives the Newtonian parabola $x = \tfrac12 a t^2$ — which drives the speed past $c$ in finite time, an impossibility. The physically meaningful, frame-independent quantity is the *proper* acceleration $\|A\| = \gamma^3|d^2x/dt^2|$, what the rocket's accelerometer reads; holding *it* constant gives the hyperbola $x^2 - t^2 = a^{-2}$, with coordinate acceleration $a/\gamma^3$ that *decreases* as the speed builds, keeping $u < 1$ forever. The operation becomes legal exactly when "constant acceleration" is read as constant *proper* acceleration ([[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]]).

> [!warning] 3. Adding four-velocities, or treating their space as a vector space
> Since the four-velocity replaces the three-velocity, it is tempting to "add" two of them to combine motions. But four-velocities all satisfy $U \cdot U = 1$, so they lie on the unit *hyperboloid*, not a linear subspace: $U_1 + U_2$ has norm $1 + 1 + 2U_1\cdot U_2 \ne 1$ and is *not* a four-velocity. Velocities do not add — boosts compose, and they form a *group*, not a vector space. The correct combination is the Lorentz transformation $U \mapsto \Lambda U$ ([[Ex - Velocity addition and aberration from the four-velocity]]), whose nonlinear law is [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition|velocity addition]]; in [[Def - Rapidity|rapidity]] variables collinear boosts *do* add. The operation becomes legal only as composition of group elements, never as vector addition.

> [!warning] 4. Giving a photon a four-velocity (or a limit of one)
> One naturally wants a four-velocity for a photon, perhaps as the $m \to 0$ limit of $U = \gamma(1, \mathbf{u})$. This fails twice: along a null worldline $d\tau = 0$ so $dX/d\tau$ is undefined, and more sharply *no unit null vector exists* (rescaling a null vector preserves its zero norm, never reaching $1$). The limit $\gamma(1, \mathbf u)$ diverges as $u \to 1$. The object that survives is the four-*momentum* $P = mU$, which has a finite null limit $P \cdot P = m^2 \to 0$, its magnitude supplied by $E = \hbar\omega$ ([[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics]]). The operation becomes legal only by replacing the four-velocity with the null four-momentum throughout.

> [!warning] 5. Blaming the proper-time deficit on acceleration, or expecting it in proper time
> Because the twin who accelerates ages less, it is tempting to compute the ageing deficit as an integral *over the acceleration*, or to think acceleration enters the proper-time formula. It does not: the proper-time integrand $\sqrt{1 - u^2}\,dt$ contains the *speed*, never $\dot u$, so two clocks with the same speed history age identically regardless of how they accelerate ([[Ex - The ideal clock hypothesis and a circular-motion clock]]). Acceleration's role is only to make the worldline *bent* (dissymmetric), which is *why* the deficit arises — but its *size* is set by the speeds and durations, computed cleanly by the reversed triangle inequality, not by an acceleration integral. The operation becomes legal when you compute the deficit from worldline geometry (speeds, bending), not from the acceleration itself.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won the moment you identify which of three things is being asked — an elapsed proper time, a four-vector quantity, or a causal verdict — and reach for the matching tool. Begin by drawing the worldline (time up, space across, light at $45^\circ$) and labelling the events.

If the problem **asks for an elapsed time** — the ageing of a traveller, the lifetime of a particle, the reading of a flown clock — the route is fixed: write the [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]] as $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$ along the worldline and integrate. The only skill is choosing a parametrisation that makes the integral doable, and the single most important recognition is that the integrand $\sqrt{1 - u^2}\,dt$ depends on the *speed* alone, never the acceleration — so the answer is a functional of the speed history. When the proper acceleration is constant (the richest special case), switch to the hyperbolic parametrisation: the [[Def - Rapidity|rapidity]] grows linearly, $U = (\cosh a\tau, \sinh a\tau, 0, 0)$, the worldline is a hyperbola, and the proper time integral becomes an inverse hyperbolic sine via the substitution $at = \sinh\psi$ ([[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]]). For a clock at rest in some frame, the proper time is just that frame's coordinate time — start there and transport out.

If the problem **asks to compare the ageing of two travellers** between the same two meetings — the twin paradox in any guise — do *not* reach for time-dilation bookkeeping leg by leg, which invites the simultaneity confusion that manufactures the "paradox". Instead invoke the geometry directly: the straight (inertial) worldline carries the *most* proper time ([[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]]), so the inertial traveller ages most, and the deficit of any bent worldline is governed by the [[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality|reversed triangle inequality]] $\|U + V\| \ge \|U\| + \|V\|$. The asymmetry between the twins is always that one is inertial (straight worldline) and the other is not (bent, accelerating); the relativity of simultaneity is the mechanism that closes any leg-by-leg accounting, because the accelerating twin's "now-slice" of the other's worldline jumps at each turnaround. The deepest framing is that the effect is about worldline *dissymmetry*, not acceleration per se — in a non-simply-connected spacetime two geodesic (unaccelerated) twins can still age differently.

If the problem **asks for a four-vector quantity or what another observer measures** — a four-velocity, a transformed velocity, an aberration angle — build the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity]] $U = \gamma(1, \mathbf{u})$ and either use its free identities ($U \cdot U = 1$, $A \cdot U = 0$) or transform it directly, $U' = \Lambda U$, taking ratios of components at the end. This is the labour-saving heart of the formalism: the nonlinear velocity-addition and aberration laws collapse to a single matrix multiplication because the $\gamma$ packaged inside $U$ carries the time-dilation bookkeeping ([[Ex - Velocity addition and aberration from the four-velocity]]). Remember that four-velocities transform, they do not add — they live on a hyperboloid, and combining motions means composing boosts (a group operation). Evaluate any invariant inner product in the most convenient frame, almost always a rest frame.

If the problem **is causal** — is this a particle, a photon, or a tachyon; can $A$ influence $B$; is this signal allowed — compute the sign of the relevant norm and read off the [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|light-cone]] structure. Timelike ($V \cdot V > 0$) is a massive particle, causally connectible with an invariant time-order; null is a photon; spacelike ($V \cdot V < 0$) is an excluded tachyon, causally disconnected with a frame-dependent order ([[Ex - Classifying worldlines and why tachyons would signal into the past]]). A trajectory is physically allowed exactly when its worldline is everywhere timelike or null; the prohibition on faster-than-light *signalling* (not mere appearances) is enforced by causality, since a spacelike signal could be received before sent in some frame. For a photon, use the null four-momentum, never a four-velocity.

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **proper time is a length, and the indefinite metric makes the straightest worldline the longest.** Almost every confusion in the chapter — whose clock is slow, which twin is younger, why a traveller can reach the galactic centre in years — dissolves once you picture the worldlines as curves whose metric length is the elapsed proper time, with spatial motion *subtracting* from that length. The single question every problem in this chapter is secretly asking is: *how long is this worldline, in the metric?*

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Def - Proper Time|Proper time]] as the metric arc length, $\tau = \int\sqrt{ds^2} = \int dt/\gamma$.** This is the most-used construction in the chapter, because it is the answer to every "how much time elapses" question and it is *free*: it costs nothing and applies to any worldline. Reach for it whenever a clock, traveller, particle lifetime, or ageing is mentioned. Its decisive reusable features are that the integrand depends on *speed* but not acceleration (so the answer is a speed functional, and the [[Ex - The ideal clock hypothesis and a circular-motion clock|circling-clock]] result is acceleration-independent), and that it depends on the *worldline* not just the endpoints (so different paths give different ageing — the [[Ex - The twin paradox|twin paradox]]). Recognise its applicability the instant a time interval must be computed along a non-trivial trajectory.

- **The four-velocity $U = \gamma(1, \mathbf{u})$, unit-normalised $U \cdot U = 1$** ([[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]). The basic kinematic four-vector, from which the [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|four-momentum]] $P = mU$ and four-acceleration $A = dU/d\tau$ are built. Its reusable feature is the normalisation: $U \cdot U = 1$ is a free equation true for every particle, and differentiating it hands you the orthogonality $A \cdot U = 0$ at no cost. The contraction of two four-velocities, $U \cdot U' = \gamma_{\text{rel}}$, is the relative Lorentz factor — the cleanest route to relative speeds and [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition|velocity addition]]. Reach for $U$ whenever a problem mentions a particle's velocity and you want a four-vector to compute with, and remember it *transforms* ($U' = \Lambda U$), it does not add.

- **The geodesic principle: the straight worldline maximises proper time** ([[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]]). The single geometric fact that organises §5.3, and the reason "ageing" behaves like a length. Its reusable content is that comparing the ageing of two travellers between the same events requires *no* time-dilation bookkeeping — just compare how bent the worldlines are, since the straightest ages most. It is the reversed triangle inequality integrated, and it converts every twin-paradox variant into a one-line geometric statement. Recognise its applicability whenever two worldlines share endpoints and you must say which carries more proper time; its disguised use is as the special-relativistic seed of the general-relativistic geodesic equation.

- **The proper-acceleration / hyperbolic-motion template** ([[Ex - Proper time along an accelerated worldline]]). Constant proper acceleration $a$ $\Leftrightarrow$ rapidity linear in proper time $\Leftrightarrow$ hyperbolic worldline $x^2 - t^2 = a^{-2}$, with $U = (\cosh a\tau, \sinh a\tau, 0, 0)$. Its reusable role is that it makes every accelerated-worldline integral elementary (hyperbolic functions of one parameter) and is the relativistic replacement for "uniformly accelerated motion". The trigger is the phrase "constant proper acceleration" or "uniformly accelerated observer"; the template recurs in [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|Rindler coordinates]] and in the [[Ex - A round trip to the galactic centre|relativistic rocket]]. The proper acceleration $\|A\|$ is also what an accelerometer reads, the [[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline|curvature]] of the worldline.

- **The classification by the sign of the norm, and the light cone** ([[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics]]). The Lorentz-invariant trichotomy timelike / null / spacelike is the workhorse of every causal question. Its reusable content is that the sign of a four-vector's (or tangent's, or separation's) norm is frame-independent, so particle type and causal connectibility are absolute, while the time-ordering of spacelike-separated events is not. Typical use: compute $V \cdot V$, read off whether a worldline is a massive particle, a photon, or an excluded tachyon, and whether a signal is causally allowed. It is the technique behind the [[Ex - Classifying worldlines and why tachyons would signal into the past|exclusion of tachyons]] and the parallelism of light cones that forbids time travel to the past.

# Bridges

1. **General relativity — proper time becomes the geodesic principle of gravitation.** The metric is used in this chapter as the operator that turns a worldline into an elapsed time, $\tau = \int\sqrt{\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu}$, and a free particle follows the worldline that extremises it ([[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time]]). General relativity makes one change with enormous consequences: it promotes the constant metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ to a position-dependent dynamical field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, and keeps the proper-time integral unchanged in form, $\tau = \int\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu}$. Two things transfer verbatim. First, the clocks-and-rods content: an ideal clock still reads the arc length of its worldline, now in the curved metric, so gravitational time dilation is computed by exactly this integral with $g_{00}(x) = 1 + 2\Phi/c^2$ in place of $1$ — and this is the very effect that shows up as the general-relativistic term in the [[Ex - The Hafele-Keating experiment|Hafele–Keating]] experiment. Second, the variational principle: a freely-falling particle extremises $\int d\tau$, and its extremal is a **timelike geodesic** obeying $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho = 0$, where the Christoffel symbols $\Gamma$ are built from first derivatives of $g_{\mu\nu}$. In flat space, in inertial coordinates, $\Gamma = 0$ and this collapses to the straight worldline $dU/d\tau = 0$ of this chapter. So the single sentence "proper time is the metric arc length of a timelike worldline, and free particles extremise it" is the kinematic core of *both* special and general relativity; the only difference is whether the metric in the integral is the constant $\eta$ or the field $g(x)$, and the curvature of $g$ is the obstruction to making $\eta$ hold everywhere at once — gravity. Setting the worldline, proper time, and four-velocity up frame-independently here is precisely what makes the passage to gravitation a single substitution rather than a fresh start.

2. **The principle of least action — the relativistic action is proper time.** A free relativistic particle extremises the functional $S = -m\int d\tau$, the [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]] along its worldline weighted by minus the [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|rest mass]]. This is the exact relativistic counterpart of the principle of least action: the integrand $-m\sqrt{\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu}$ plays the role of the Lagrangian, and the Euler–Lagrange equations are precisely $dU^\mu/d\tau = 0$, the straight worldline. The conjugate momentum $\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu$ is exactly the four-momentum $P_\mu$, and the mass-shell constraint $P \cdot P = m^2$ appears as a *primary constraint* in the Hamiltonian sense — the relativistic free particle is a constrained system whose constraint generates reparametrisation of the worldline. Because the metric is indefinite, the [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time|geodesic principle]] makes this extremum a *maximum* of proper time, not a minimum: the action principle is a maximum-ageing principle. This is the cleanest entry to the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulation of relativistic mechanics ([[Special Relativity XV — The Principle of Least Action]]) and, beyond it, of relativistic field theory.

3. **Differential geometry — the worldline is a timelike curve and its four-acceleration is the geodesic curvature.** Everything in this chapter is the curve theory of a Lorentzian manifold, specialised to the flat case. A [[Def - Worldline of a Particle|worldline]] is a timelike immersed curve; the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity]] is its unit tangent in the arc-length (proper-time) parametrisation; the four-acceleration is the covariant derivative $A = \nabla_U U$ of the unit tangent along the curve — the *curvature vector* of the curve, whose magnitude $\|A\|$ is the [[Def - Curvature and Torsions of a Worldline|curvature]] of the worldline (the first invariant of its Serret–Frenet apparatus). A geodesic is a curve with $A = 0$, "transporting its own tangent without turning", and in flat space these are the straight lines. The Serret–Frenet tetrad and its curvature-and-two-torsions are Cartan's moving-frame construction in $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$: the frame can only evolve by an infinitesimal isometry (a four-rotation), which is why the connection matrix is antisymmetric. This is the same machinery that, with the metric made position-dependent, becomes the geodesic equation, parallel transport, and the Riemann curvature of general relativity — the worldline curvature here being the *extrinsic* bending of a curve, the cousin of the *intrinsic* curvature of spacetime.

4. **Particle physics and astrophysics — proper time, lifetimes, and time travel to the future.** The path-dependence of proper time is not a thought-experiment curiosity but a daily fact of experimental physics. A moving particle's [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]] is its internal clock, so its measured lifetime is dilated by $\gamma$: muons created in the upper atmosphere reach the ground only because their proper time runs slow, and muons in the CERN storage ring ([[Ex - The ideal clock hypothesis and a circular-motion clock]]) have lifetimes dilated by exactly the speed-determined $\gamma$ despite $\sim 10^{18}g$ of acceleration — confirming both time dilation and the ideal clock hypothesis. At astronomical scale the same geometry permits genuine *time travel to the future*: a relativistic rocket at $1\,g$ reaches the galactic centre in $\sim 20$ years of ship-time while $\sim 26{,}000$ years pass on Earth ([[Ex - A round trip to the galactic centre]]), and reaches Andromeda in a human lifetime — all while moving strictly slower than light, because proper time is the *short* arc length of a near-null worldline. The traveller arrives in Earth's distant future but can never reach its past, since the worldline stays inside the forward light cone; the asymmetry of future (reachable) and past (forbidden) is the parallelism of light cones in flat spacetime, broken only when gravity tips them over.

5. **Hyperbolic geometry — the four-velocity space is a model of $\mathbb{H}^3$.** The set of all [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocities]], constrained by $U \cdot U = 1$, is one sheet of the unit hyperboloid $\{X : X \cdot X = 1\}$ — and this hyperboloid, with the metric induced from $\eta$, is exactly the hyperboloid model of three-dimensional hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^3$. The [[Def - The Lorentz Group|Lorentz group]] acts on it as the full isometry group, and the [[Def - Rapidity|rapidity]] between two four-velocities is the hyperbolic *distance* on $\mathbb{H}^3$. This is the deep reason velocities do not add: "adding velocities" would be adding points of a curved space, which is meaningless; what is well-defined is the group action (composing boosts), and the nonlinearity of [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition|velocity addition]] is the curvature of velocity space. The failure of velocities to commute (the Wigner rotation / Thomas precession) is the holonomy of this hyperbolic geometry — a triangle of boosts closes up to a rotation, exactly as a triangle on a curved surface has angle defect. Relativistic velocity space is non-Euclidean, and the chapter's kinematics is its first appearance.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: a particle is a curve, and proper time is its length.** The single shift in viewpoint that organises the whole chapter is to stop thinking of a particle as a point that moves through time and start thinking of it as a fixed one-dimensional curve — its [[Def - Worldline of a Particle|worldline]] — laid out once and for all in spacetime, on which the metric installs a ruler. That ruler is [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]], the arc length of the curve, and it is the time the particle's own clock reads. Once this is internalised, every result becomes a statement about curves and their lengths: time dilation is "spatial motion adds length-in-space, which subtracts from length-in-time"; the [[Ex - The twin paradox|twin paradox]] is "two curves between the same endpoints have different lengths"; the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity]] is "the unit tangent to the curve"; the four-acceleration is "the curvature of the curve". The kinematics of a particle is the differential geometry of its worldline, and the indefinite metric is what makes that geometry behave oppositely to Euclidean intuition.

**The true name of proper time is "the metric arc length of a timelike worldline", and the indefiniteness flips the extremum.** The textbook gloss — "the time measured by a moving clock" — is correct but operationally incomplete; the useful characterisation is geometric: proper time is $\int\sqrt{ds^2}$, the length of the worldline in the metric $\eta$. This reframing explains at once every counterintuitive fact. Proper time is path-dependent because the length of a curve depends on the curve. It is frame-independent because length is basis-independent. And — the deepest consequence — the straightest worldline carries the *most* proper time, not the least, because the metric is *indefinite*: spatial displacement enters the line element $ds^2 = dt^2 - d\mathbf{x}^2$ with a minus sign, so moving through space *subtracts* from the elapsed time. In a Euclidean world (all plus signs) the straight line would be shortest, as intuition demands; the one minus sign reverses "shortest" to "longest" and is the entire source of the [[Thm - Inertial Worldlines Maximise Proper Time|geodesic principle]] and the twin paradox. Stop thinking of proper time as a clock reading and start thinking of it as the Minkowski length of a curve, and the chapter's surprises evaporate.

**Differentiate by the invariant clock, and Newtonian quantities become four-vectors.** A trigger-reaction pattern worth carrying everywhere: whenever you want to relativise a Newtonian rate of change, differentiate with respect to *proper time* $\tau$, never coordinate time $t$. The reason is structural — $\tau$ is a Lorentz scalar while $t$ is a frame-dependent coordinate, and differentiating a four-vector by a scalar yields a four-vector, while differentiating by a coordinate yields a tangled non-tensor. This single substitution, $d/dt \to d/d\tau = \gamma^{-1}\,d/dt$, generates the entire kinematic and dynamical apparatus: $dX/d\tau$ is the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity]], $dU/d\tau$ the four-acceleration, $d(mU)/d\tau$ the four-force, each automatically a four-vector and each obeying a law that holds in every frame. The factor $\gamma = dt/d\tau$ that appears everywhere is just the exchange rate between the observer's clock and the particle's clock. Relativistic mechanics is Newtonian mechanics with proper time as the clock — the disciplined transfer of $d/dt$ into $d/d\tau$.

**The photon is the degeneration, and the lesson is "find what survives".** It is tempting to treat the [[Def - Photons and Null Geodesics|photon]] as a problem case — no proper time, no four-velocity, the whole apparatus inapplicable. The better view is that the photon is the *boundary* of the theory, the limit where the timelike construction degenerates, and the discipline is to find the object that survives the degeneration and re-express everything through it. Proper time vanishes and the four-velocity ceases to exist (there is no unit null vector), but the four-*momentum* survives, finite and null, $P \cdot P = 0$, its magnitude supplied by quantum mechanics rather than by arc length. This is a general moral that recurs throughout physics: when a construction degenerates (a coordinate singularity, a massless limit, a critical point), do not patch the broken object — locate the combination that remains well-defined and rebuild on it. For the photon that object is the null four-momentum, and once you have it the photon participates in every conservation law on equal terms with massive matter, which is what makes the [[Special Relativity XIII — Energy and Momentum|Compton effect, pair production, and the Doppler shift]] computable.
