# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole topic: **we set $c = 1$**, and we use the **mostly-minus signature** $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike vector has $X\cdot X > 0$. This is the convention of every previous chapter and of the existing vault. Gourgoulhon's *Special Relativity in General Frames*, the source for this chapter, uses the opposite **mostly-plus** signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ with timelike $g(v,v) < 0$; every formula transcribed here has been translated by flipping the overall sign of the metric and scalar product. Where a sign of the wave operator or of a trace would otherwise mislead, a `> [!warning] Convention:` callout flags it. Where a formula is more recognisable with $c$ restored, it is given both ways; to restore $c$ replace $t$ by $ct$, every velocity $v$ by $v/c$, and insert factors by dimensional analysis. This is the final chapter of the special-relativity sequence, and its whole purpose is to show why special relativity is *not* the last word — so the gravitational potential, the Newtonian limit, and the curved metric of general relativity all appear, and the registry below carries them.

- $\Phi$ — the **Newtonian gravitational potential**, a scalar field on spacetime; $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$ in the weak-field (nonrelativistic) limit
- $G$ — Newton's gravitational constant, $G = 6.674\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$
- $\rho$ — Newtonian mass density; $\varepsilon$ — total energy density (including rest mass); $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}$ — internal energy density; $p$ — pressure
- $\Delta$, $\nabla^2$ — the spatial **Laplacian** $\partial^2/\partial x^2 + \partial^2/\partial y^2 + \partial^2/\partial z^2$ of an inertial observer
- $\Box$ — the **d'Alembertian** (wave operator) $\Box = \partial_\mu \partial^\mu = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$; in mostly-minus, $\Box = \partial_t^2 - \nabla^2$ (with $c$: $\Box = c^{-2}\partial_t^2 - \nabla^2$)
- $T$, $T^{\mu\nu}$ — the **energy-momentum tensor**; $T := T^\mu{}_\mu = \eta^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$ its **trace** (the "Laue scalar", the scalar source of scalar gravity)
- $\mathcal{S}$ — the relativistic source generalising the mass density $\rho$ in the field equation
- $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ — the Minkowski metric; $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ — a general, **position-dependent** spacetime metric (the field of general relativity)
- $\tilde{g}$, $g^\ast$ — "physical" metrics built from $\eta$ and a gravitational field; $\tilde g = (1+\Phi/c^2)^2\,\eta$ (conformal, Einstein-Fokker); $g^\ast = \eta + h$ (tensor theory)
- $h_{\mu\nu}$ — a symmetric type-$(0,2)$ tensor field on Minkowski space, the **gravitational tensor potential** (massless spin-2)
- $a^\mu$, $\vec{a}$ — the **four-acceleration** of an observer; $\gamma$ here also denotes a uniform gravitational/acceleration magnitude $|\vec g| = \gamma$ (not the Lorentz factor, which is written $\Gamma$ in this chapter to avoid the clash)
- $\Gamma$ — the **Lorentz factor** $(1-v^2)^{-1/2}$ in this chapter (renamed from the usual $\gamma$ because $\gamma$ is used for the gravitational field magnitude)
- $\Delta t$, $\Delta t'$ — proper-time periods of a received and an emitted periodic signal; $x_{\mathrm{em}}$ — altitude (abscissa) of the emitter in the receiver's frame
- $\Delta\nu/\nu$ — fractional frequency shift; redshift when negative, blueshift when positive
- $R_{\mu\nu}$, $R$ — the **Ricci tensor** and the **scalar curvature** $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ of a curved metric
- $\mathscr{E}$ — spacetime; an affine space in special relativity, a differentiable manifold in general relativity; $E_A$ — the tangent space at an event $A$
- $\delta\theta$ — the deflection angle of a light ray passing a massive body
- $U$, $\vec u$ — four-velocity; $\bot_u$ — the orthogonal projector onto the local rest space of an observer with four-velocity $\vec u$
- Einstein summation: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0,1,2,3$; Greek indices run $0$–$3$, Latin $i,j$ run $1$–$3$

# Motivation

Here is the entire chapter in one sentence: special relativity cannot accommodate gravity, and the precise reason it fails is the equivalence principle, which forces the flat Minkowski metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ to become a position-dependent field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ — and that is general relativity. Everything in the previous twenty-four chapters was built on one fixed, global, indefinite metric. This chapter is where that edifice is shown to have a ceiling, and where the door to the next theory is opened.

The setting is natural. Of the four fundamental interactions, only electromagnetism and gravitation are long-range and admit a non-quantum description, and electromagnetism fits beautifully into Minkowski spacetime — the chapters [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field|XXI]] and [[Special Relativity XXII — Maxwell's Equations|XXII]] are the proof. So one asks the obvious question: can gravity be written as a field on Minkowski spacetime too? The chapter answers by trying, in order of increasing sophistication, the three kinds of field a relativistic theory makes available — a scalar, a vector, a rank-2 tensor — and finding each one fatally flawed:

$$\underbrace{\text{scalar }\Phi}_{\text{no light bending, wrong perihelion}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{vector }A^\mu}_{\text{negative energy, unstable}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{tensor }h_{\mu\nu}}_{\text{matter ignores it, or it becomes GR}}.$$

The scalar theory — Nordström's, the most complete pre-Einstein theory of gravity — is mathematically well-posed and reduces correctly to Newton, but it disagrees with observation: it predicts the *wrong sign* for Mercury's perihelion motion and it predicts *no bending of light at all*, because light has a traceless energy-momentum tensor and the scalar field couples only to that trace. The vector theory copies electromagnetism, but the single sign flip that makes like masses attract (rather than like charges repel) makes the field energy negative and the theory radiatively unstable — Maxwell himself spotted this in 1865. The tensor theory, a symmetric field $h_{\mu\nu}$ contracted against the energy-momentum tensor, is the massless spin-2 field of Fierz and Pauli; but at lowest order matter does not feel it, and repairing that defect by adding the necessary higher-order terms turns the theory into general relativity in disguise, with the Minkowski metric demoted to an unobservable background and the physical metric becoming $g^\ast = \eta + h$.

What unifies all three failures is a single physical fact that singles gravity out from every other interaction: the **equality of inertial and gravitational mass**. Because every body responds to gravity in proportion to its inertia, *all bodies fall the same way*, and the motion of a test particle in a gravitational field is independent of what the particle is made of. Electromagnetism is not like this — a charged particle's motion depends on its charge-to-mass ratio. This universality is what Einstein elevated to the **equivalence principle**: a uniform gravitational field is locally indistinguishable from a uniformly accelerated frame. Section §25.2 states it; §25.3 draws its sharpest consequence. Using only the accelerated-observer kinematics of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|Special Relativity XVI]], the equivalence principle predicts the **gravitational redshift** — clocks deeper in a gravitational potential run slow — and then a beautiful argument due to Alfred Schild shows that *this redshift is incompatible with the Minkowski metric*. If two clocks at different altitudes disagree about elapsed proper time, and the field is static, then the Minkowski distance between successive light signals would have to be both equal (by time-translation symmetry) and unequal (by the redshift) — a contradiction. The only escape is to abandon $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ as the meter of proper time and replace it by a field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$.

That replacement is the whole of general relativity, and §25.3 closes the chapter — and the sequence — by stating where it leads: spacetime becomes a curved manifold, the metric becomes a dynamical field sourced by energy and momentum through the Einstein equation $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$, freely-falling bodies follow geodesics, and Minkowski space survives only as the tangent-space approximation valid at each event. The redshift, by then, is no longer a paradox but a theorem.

The reader is assumed to have worked through the whole special-relativity sequence, and three chapters in particular are load-bearing here. From [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|XVI]] comes the uniformly accelerated observer, the Rindler frame, and the spectral shift between accelerated clocks — the engine of the redshift derivation. From [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy|XXIII]] comes the energy-momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$ and its trace, the scalar source of scalar gravity. And from [[Special Relativity III — Minkowski Spacetime and the Metric|III]] comes the metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ itself, the object this chapter dethrones. A reader who has met the affine-space-plus-bilinear-form picture of Minkowski space, and who is comfortable that a Lorentz transformation is a global linear isometry, has exactly the background needed to appreciate why making the metric local and position-dependent is such a violent and necessary step.

# Concept Map

## §25.1 Gravity in Minkowski Spacetime and Its Failure

- **[[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]**
	- The simplest relativistic theory of gravity: a scalar field $\Phi$ on Minkowski spacetime obeying the d'Alembert equation $\Box\Phi = -4\pi G\,T/c^2$, where $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$ is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor (the source $\mathcal{S} = -T/c^2$ replacing the Newtonian mass density $\rho$). It is derived from a least-action principle in which the gravitational charge equals the inertial mass, $q_a = m_a$, hardwiring the equality of gravitational and inertial mass. In its nonlinear Nordström form the equation reads $(1+\Phi/c^2)\Box\Phi = -4\pi G\,T_{\mathrm{mat}}/c^2$, and Einstein and Fokker recast it as a *metric* theory in which particles follow geodesics of the conformal metric $\tilde g = (1+\Phi/c^2)^2\,\eta$ — the first purely metric theory of gravity, immediately before general relativity. It is well-posed and reduces to Poisson's equation $\Delta\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ in the weak-field limit.

- **[[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]**
	- Two further attempts and why each fails. The **vector theory** copies electromagnetism, replacing $\varepsilon_0 \leftrightarrow -1/(4\pi G)$ so that like masses attract; but this single sign flip makes the field energy density negative, $\rho_{\mathrm{grav}} = -(1/8\pi G)(\vec E\cdot\vec E + c^2\vec B\cdot\vec B)$, and the "gravitational Poynting vector" of an oscillating mass points *toward* the source, so the system gains energy and is unstable — not viable even on theoretical grounds (Maxwell, 1865). The **tensor theory** uses a symmetric type-$(0,2)$ field $h_{\mu\nu}$ with interaction Lagrangian $\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{inter}} = \tfrac{1}{2c}h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}$, the massless spin-2 field of Fierz and Pauli; but at lowest order matter does not feel gravity ($\vec\nabla\cdot T = 0$ by itself), and the higher-order terms needed to fix this turn the theory into general relativity, with the physical metric $g^\ast = \eta + h$ and the background $\eta$ losing all physical meaning.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Einstein Field Equation *(from General Relativity)*
> Once the tensor field $h_{\mu\nu}$ is in play, summing the infinite series of self-coupling corrections that the consistency of the theory demands produces the full **Einstein equation** $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$, with the linearised Fierz-Pauli equation as its weak-field limit $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$. The flat-spacetime tensor theory is general relativity *seen from a background*, and it is "simpler to use the framework of general relativity" directly — see [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Trace of the Stress Tensor as a Source *(from Field Theory)*
> The scalar theory exposes a structural fact reused throughout field theory: a scalar field can only couple to the Lorentz scalar built from matter, namely the **trace** $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$ of the energy-momentum tensor. Because the electromagnetic field is conformally invariant its stress tensor is traceless, $T^{\mathrm{em}} = 0$, so light is blind to any scalar field — the same mechanism that makes the **dilaton** couple to the trace anomaly and that governs scale invariance in [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy|relativistic field theory]].

## §25.2 The Equivalence Principle

- **[[Thm - The Equivalence Principle and the Failure of a Flat-Spacetime Gravity]]**
	- The capstone of the entire sequence. The **equivalence principle** states that, as far as physical measurements are concerned, an inertial observer in a uniform gravitational field is equivalent to a uniformly accelerated observer in the absence of gravity. It rests on the experimentally verified equality of inertial and gravitational mass (Eötvös, $10^{-8}$; today $3\times 10^{-13}$). Its decisive consequence, derived in §25.3, is that the equivalence principle implies a gravitational redshift between clocks at different altitudes, and that this redshift *cannot be accounted for by the Minkowski metric*: in a static field the metric distance between two successive light signals must be translation-invariant, forcing $\Delta t = \Delta t'$, which contradicts the redshift. Therefore proper time can no longer be measured by $\eta_{\mu\nu}$; the metric must become a position-dependent field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, and that is general relativity. This theorem closes the special-relativity arc by showing exactly where and why it ends.

- **[[Thm - Light Deflection]]**
	- A light ray passing a massive body is deflected, because — by the equivalence principle — photons follow curved paths in an accelerated frame just as they would in a gravitational field. For a ray grazing the Sun the deflection is $\delta\theta = 1.75''$, first measured in the 1919 eclipse. The equivalence-principle argument captures the *existence* of the effect and its order of magnitude in a locally homogeneous field, but it is not sufficient to compute the full deflection in the Sun's inhomogeneous field — the correct value requires the curved metric of general relativity, which famously doubles the naive Newtonian estimate. Crucially, Nordström's scalar theory predicts *no* deflection at all, because light's traceless stress tensor decouples it from a scalar field — the cleanest observational discriminator between scalar gravity and general relativity.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Weak Equivalence Principle and Universality of Free Fall *(from General Relativity)*
> The equality $m_{\mathrm{grav}} = m_{\mathrm{inert}}$ promotes to the **weak equivalence principle**: the worldline of a freely-falling test body is independent of its mass and composition. This is what allows gravity to be *geometrised* — if all bodies fall the same way, the trajectory is a property of spacetime, not of the body, so it can be the **geodesic** of a metric. The strong equivalence principle extends this to all local physics and is the founding postulate of [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild|general relativity]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: Gravitational Lensing *(from Astrophysics and Cosmology)*
> Light deflection by mass is the basis of **gravitational lensing** — the bending, magnification, and multiple-imaging of background sources by foreground masses, now a primary tool for mapping dark matter and measuring cosmological distances. The deflection angle this section introduces, doubled by general relativity, is the kernel of the lens equation.

## §25.3 Gravitational Redshift and the Road to General Relativity

- **[[Thm - Gravitational Redshift]]**
	- A clock deeper in a gravitational potential runs slow relative to a clock higher up: a periodic signal of proper period $\Delta t'$ emitted at altitude $x_{\mathrm{em}}$ is received at higher altitude with period $\Delta t = \Delta t'/(1 + \gamma x_{\mathrm{em}}/c^2)$, derived from the equivalence principle via the accelerated-frame kinematics of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|XVI]]. With $c$ restored the fractional shift is $\Delta\nu/\nu = -\Delta\Phi/c^2 = -gh/c^2$ for a height difference $h$ in surface gravity $g$ — a redshift on the way up. It has been verified by Pound and Rebka (1960, ${}^{57}\mathrm{Fe}$ gamma rays up a $22\,\mathrm{m}$ tower, Mössbauer effect, $1\%$ after Pound-Snider), by atomic clocks aboard aircraft (Hafele-Keating, Alley), by Vessot and Levine (1976, hydrogen maser on a rocket, discrepancy $\le 7\times 10^{-5}$), by ACES on the ISS, and — crucially for daily life — it is the dominant relativistic correction in GPS and the other satellite-navigation systems, which would accumulate $46\,\mu\mathrm{s}/\mathrm{day}$ and a $14\,\mathrm{km}$ position error if it were ignored.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Schwarzschild Redshift and Black-Hole Horizons *(from General Relativity)*
> The flat-space redshift $\Delta\nu/\nu = -\Delta\Phi/c^2$ is the linearisation of the exact general-relativistic formula $\nu_\infty/\nu_{\mathrm{em}} = \sqrt{1 - 2GM/(rc^2)}$ in the **Schwarzschild metric**. As the emitter approaches the Schwarzschild radius $r = 2GM/c^2$ the redshift diverges: light from the horizon is infinitely redshifted, which is *why* a black hole is black. See [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Metric as a Dynamical Field and the Curved Spacetime of General Relativity *(from General Relativity)*
> The redshift forces the single most consequential idea in physics: the metric is not a fixed background but a **field** $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, a different symmetric bilinear form at each event, obeying its own equation of motion sourced by energy and momentum. Spacetime becomes a **differentiable manifold** with a tangent space $E_A$ at each event; the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ has signature $(+,-,-,-)$ everywhere but is no longer globally $\eta_{\mu\nu}$; freely-falling bodies follow **geodesics**; and the obstruction to making $g$ equal $\eta$ everywhere at once is the **curvature**, which *is* the gravitational field. Minkowski space survives as the tangent-space model at each point. This is general relativity — see [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]] and, for the differential geometry of position-dependent metrics, [[Def - Semi-Riemannian Metric and Signature]].

- **[[Ex - Why the gravitational redshift forces a position-dependent metric]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Run Schild's argument in full: two successive light signals in a static field differ by a time translation, so their Minkowski lengths must be equal, giving $\Delta t = \Delta t'$; but the equivalence principle gives $\Delta t \neq \Delta t'$; conclude the metric cannot be $\eta$ and must be a field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The problems of this chapter pursue a recognisable handful of goals, and they are unusual for special relativity because most of them are *negative* — the chapter's job is to show that things fail. The most characteristic target is to **demonstrate that a candidate theory is inadequate**: that a scalar theory gives the wrong perihelion motion or no light deflection, that a vector theory has negative field energy, that a tensor theory leaves matter unmoved. Recognising a target of this kind means knowing what a successful theory must do — reduce to Newton in the weak field, bend light, give a positive-definite energy, couple to matter — and then exhibiting the one requirement the candidate violates. A second target is to **derive a Newtonian or post-Newtonian prediction from a relativistic field equation**: take the field equation, impose the slow-and-weak limit $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$, and recover Poisson's equation or a leading correction. A third, and the deepest, is to **prove an incompatibility** — Schild's argument is the prototype: assume the Minkowski metric, assume the equivalence principle, and derive a contradiction. A fourth is **purely computational**: evaluate a redshift, a clock rate, a deflection angle, a GPS timing error, to the precision an experiment demands. A fifth, the conceptual payoff, is to **identify what must change** to escape a no-go result — to see that abandoning the global metric, or geometrising the trajectory, is the way out. Across all five the unifying remark is that this chapter teaches you to recognise the *boundary* of a theory: you understand special relativity completely only when you can say precisely where it breaks and what replaces it.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A field equation is given** — Nordström's $\Box\Phi \propto T$, the vector or tensor analogue — and one extracts its predictions by taking limits or computing specific solutions. **The equivalence principle is given**, which is the single richest source: it converts any statement about a gravitational field into a statement about an accelerated frame, where the kinematics of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|XVI]] apply directly. **The energy-momentum tensor of some matter is given** — dust, a perfect fluid, the electromagnetic field — and its trace $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$ is the source that a scalar theory sees; recognising that the electromagnetic trace vanishes is the whole of the no-light-bending result. **A static or stationary field is assumed**, which supplies a time-translation symmetry strong enough to constrain the metric (this is the hinge of Schild's argument). **The weak-field condition $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$ is given**, licensing linearisation and the recovery of Newtonian physics. **The equality of inertial and gravitational mass is given**, which is what makes the gravitational coupling universal and lets one set the gravitational charge equal to the mass in the action. The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a field equation routes through a limit to a Newtonian prediction; the equivalence principle routes through accelerated-frame kinematics to the redshift; a static field plus the redshift routes through Schild's argument to the incompatibility with $\eta$; a traceless stress tensor routes through the scalar coupling to the absence of light bending. The [[Special Relativity XXV — Toward Relativistic Gravitation#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] section makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves the chapter's problems are assembled from. Because the chapter is about the *limits* of special relativity and the *transition* to general relativity, several operations are about taking limits, testing consistency, and translating between a gravitational field and an accelerated frame. When stuck, scan the list.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Take the Newtonian (weak, slow) limit of a relativistic field equation.** Impose $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$ and $|\partial_t^2 \Phi| \ll c^2|\nabla^2\Phi|$, so the d'Alembertian $\Box\Phi = c^{-2}\partial_t^2\Phi - \nabla^2\Phi$ collapses to $-\nabla^2\Phi$, and check that the equation reduces to Poisson's $\Delta\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* you have a covariant field equation and must verify it is a legitimate generalisation of Newton, or extract its leading prediction. *Pattern:* "drop the time derivative, identify the source's nonrelativistic limit ($-T/c^2 \to \rho$), read off Poisson."

2. **Invoke the equivalence principle to swap a gravitational field for an accelerated frame.** Replace "inertial observer $\mathcal{O}$ in a uniform field $\vec g = -\gamma\,\vec e_x$" by "uniformly accelerated observer with $\vec a = (\gamma/c^2)\vec e_x$ in flat spacetime", then apply the accelerated-observer results of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|XVI]]; see [[Thm - The Equivalence Principle and the Failure of a Flat-Spacetime Gravity]]. *Trigger:* any local question about a uniform gravitational field. *Pattern:* "by the equivalence principle, things must be the same as for a uniformly accelerated observer, so use the Rindler-frame kinematics."

3. **Compute the trace of the energy-momentum tensor.** Form $T = T^\mu{}_\mu = \eta^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$; for a perfect fluid $T = \varepsilon - 3p$, for dust $T = \rho$, for the electromagnetic field $T^{\mathrm{em}} = 0$; see [[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* you must find the scalar source a scalar gravity theory responds to, or test whether a given matter couples to it. *Pattern:* "the scalar field sees only the trace; if the trace vanishes, the matter is invisible to it."

4. **Set the gravitational charge equal to the inertial mass in the action.** In the interaction term $S_{\mathrm{inter}} = -\tfrac1c\sum_a q_a\int\Phi\,\sqrt{\eta_{\alpha\beta}\dot x_a^\alpha \dot x_a^\beta}\,d\lambda$, impose $q_a = m_a$ to hardwire the universality of free fall; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* you are building a gravitational action and want the equivalence principle built in from the start. *Pattern:* "gravitational mass = inertial mass means the coupling is the mass itself, not an independent charge."

5. **Use a static field to impose time-translation symmetry on successive signals.** In a stationary field the worldline of one periodic light signal is the time-translate of the next, so any time-translation-invariant quantity (in particular a Minkowski metric distance) must take equal values on the two; see [[Thm - The Equivalence Principle and the Failure of a Flat-Spacetime Gravity]]. *Trigger:* a static field plus periodic signals, especially when you want to constrain or contradict the metric. *Pattern:* "the field is static, so signal 2 is signal 1 shifted in time; whatever the metric says is unchanged by that shift."

6. **Derive the redshift from the accelerated-frame spectral shift.** Apply the accelerated-clock period relation $\Delta t = \Delta t'/(1+a\,x_{\mathrm{em}})$ (with $a = \gamma/c^2$) to two clocks at different altitudes; see [[Thm - Gravitational Redshift]]. *Trigger:* a frequency or clock-rate comparison between two heights in a gravitational field. *Pattern:* "higher receiver, lower emitter; the lower clock runs slow by $1+\Delta\Phi/c^2$."

7. **Linearise about Minkowski: write $g = \eta + h$.** Treat the metric as a small perturbation of $\eta$ and keep first order in $h$; this is the bridge between the flat-space tensor theory and weak-field general relativity; see [[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* a weak gravitational field, gravitational waves, or the connection between Fierz-Pauli and Einstein. *Pattern:* "$g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$, expand the field equation, keep $O(h)$."

8. **Read off the sign of a field energy density.** Compute the energy density a candidate theory assigns to its own field and check it is bounded below; a theory with a region of negative field energy is unstable; see [[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* testing a vector or tensor theory for theoretical viability. *Pattern:* "if the kinetic energy of the field can be made arbitrarily negative, the vacuum decays — reject."

9. **Identify a conformal rescaling of the metric.** Recognise $\tilde g = \Omega^2(x)\,\eta$ as a position-dependent rescaling that preserves light cones ($\tilde g(\vec v,\vec v) = 0 \Leftrightarrow \eta(\vec v,\vec v) = 0$) but changes proper times; this is the Einstein-Fokker form of Nordström's theory; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. *Trigger:* a metric of the form $\Omega^2\eta$, or a theory in which the causal structure is unchanged but clocks are not. *Pattern:* "conformal factor multiplies the metric; null cones unchanged, proper time scaled by $\Omega$."

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Assuming the energy density of matter is the source of relativistic gravity
> The naive relativistic generalisation of "mass density $\rho$ is the source" is "energy density $\varepsilon$ is the source", since $E = mc^2$. This is **wrong**, and seeing why is the whole subtlety of Nordström's theory. Energy density is not a Lorentz scalar — it is the $T^{00}$ component of a tensor, and it changes under a boost both because energy transforms and because volume Lorentz-contracts. A scalar field equation must have a *scalar* source, and the only scalar built from the matter is the **trace** $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. The repair is to set $\mathcal{S} = -T/c^2$, which does reduce to $\rho$ in the nonrelativistic limit (where $-T/c^2 = \rho + (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}-3p)/c^2 \to \rho$) but differs from $\varepsilon/c^2$ for relativistic matter — and that difference is exactly why light, with $T^{\mathrm{em}} = 0$ but $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{em}} \neq 0$, does not bend in scalar gravity.

> [!warning] 2. Treating the equivalence principle as exact over a finite region
> It is tempting to say "a gravitational field is *the same as* an accelerated frame", full stop, and conclude that gravity can always be transformed away by choosing the right frame. This is true only *locally*, at a single event and to first order. A real gravitational field is **inhomogeneous** — it points toward a centre and weakens with distance — so two test particles released apart fall along *converging* lines (tidal effect), which no single acceleration can mimic. The counterexample is a pair of particles dropped on opposite sides of the Earth: they accelerate toward each other, an effect absent in any uniformly accelerated frame. The condition that makes the equivalence-principle substitution legal is that the region be small enough that the field is uniform; the residual, irreducible part is the **tidal field**, encoded in general relativity by the curvature. The equivalence principle removes the field at a point; curvature is what is left over.

> [!warning] 3. Computing light deflection from the equivalence principle alone and trusting the number
> The equivalence-principle argument — photons curve in an accelerated frame, so they curve in a gravitational field — correctly predicts that light bends and gives the right order of magnitude, and it is tempting to trust the resulting deflection angle. But the field of the Sun is *not* uniform along the ray's path, and the equivalence-principle calculation, valid only in a locally homogeneous field, captures only half the effect; see [[Thm - Light Deflection]]. The counterexample is the grazing-Sun deflection: the equivalence-principle / Newtonian estimate gives $0.87''$, while the measured value, and the general-relativistic prediction, is $1.75''$ — a factor of two larger, the missing half coming from the spatial curvature of the metric that the flat-space argument cannot see. The repair is the full geodesic computation in the Schwarzschild metric.

> [!warning] 4. Building gravity on the electromagnetic template without flipping more than the sign
> Electromagnetism is the most successful relativistic field theory, so it is natural to model gravity on it: a vector potential, a field tensor, Maxwell-like equations, with $\varepsilon_0 \leftrightarrow -1/(4\pi G)$ to make like sources attract. The single sign flip looks harmless but is **disastrous**; see [[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]. The counterexample is the field energy density, which becomes $\rho_{\mathrm{grav}} = -(1/8\pi G)(\vec E\cdot\vec E + c^2\vec B\cdot\vec B) < 0$: the field can carry arbitrarily negative energy, and an oscillating mass radiates energy *toward* itself, so the system is unstable. There is no repair within a pure vector theory — gravity is *not* spin-1. The deeper reason is that the source of gravity is a rank-2 tensor (energy-momentum), not a vector (a current), so the mediating field must be rank-2 (spin-2), and only then does the energy come out positive.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won by recognising which of three modes you are in: *testing a theory*, *taking a limit*, or *forcing a transition*. Because the chapter's whole content is the failure of flat-spacetime gravity and the necessity of curved spacetime, almost every problem is one of these, and the first move is always to ask which.

If the problem **asks whether a candidate theory works**, the strategy is to confront it with the fixed list of requirements a relativistic gravity theory must meet and find the one it fails. The requirements are: reduce to Newton in the weak field, give the observed perihelion advance, bend light, possess positive field energy, and couple to matter so that matter actually moves. For a scalar theory the failures are observational — wrong-sign perihelion and no light bending — and the route is to compute the trace $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$ that the field couples to, noticing that for the electromagnetic field it vanishes ([[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]). For a vector theory the failure is theoretical — negative energy — and the route is to write down the field energy density and read off its sign ([[Def - Vector and Tensor Theories of Gravity]]). For a tensor theory the failure is that lowest-order matter is unmoved, and the resolution is that the repair *is* general relativity. The unifying recognition is that each spin fails differently: spin-0 disagrees with experiment, spin-1 is unstable, spin-2 works but is no longer a flat-space theory.

If the problem **asks for a Newtonian or weak-field prediction**, the strategy is to linearise. Impose $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$, drop the second time derivative in the d'Alembertian (slow fields), and identify the nonrelativistic limit of the source. The recurring trap is the source itself: it is *not* the energy density but $-T/c^2$, and only in the nonrelativistic limit does this become $\rho$ ([[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]). For metric problems the analogous move is $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $h$ small, after which a single component of the Einstein equation reproduces Poisson and the rest are higher order.

If the problem **asks you to force the transition to curved spacetime** — to show that flat spacetime is untenable — the instrument is almost always the gravitational redshift and Schild's argument. The route is: invoke the equivalence principle to get the redshift between two altitudes from accelerated-frame kinematics ([[Thm - Gravitational Redshift]]); then assume a static field, so successive light signals are time-translates of one another; then observe that the Minkowski metric, being time-translation invariant, would force the two signals' proper-time lengths to be equal, contradicting the redshift ([[Thm - The Equivalence Principle and the Failure of a Flat-Spacetime Gravity]]). The conclusion is that proper time is not measured by $\eta$, so the metric must be a field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$. The crucial subtlety is that the argument does not depend on the shape of the photons' worldlines — only on the static symmetry — which is what makes it airtight.

Threading through all of this is the meta-question of the chapter: **what is the boundary of special relativity, and what lies just past it?** Every problem here is, at bottom, the question "where does the assumption of a single flat global metric break, and what is forced in its place?" The answer is always the same — the equivalence principle breaks it, and a position-dependent metric replaces it — but the routes by which different problems reach that answer (a traceless stress tensor, a negative energy, a static redshift) are the substance of the chapter. Special relativity is the theory of the flat metric $\eta$; this chapter is the proof that gravity will not fit inside it, and the door to the theory where the metric is allowed to bend.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **The equivalence principle** (equality of inertial and gravitational mass): the most-used single fact in the chapter, because it is the bridge that converts every local gravitational question into an accelerated-frame question, where the machinery of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|XVI]] applies. Reach for it whenever a problem concerns a uniform gravitational field and a *local* measurement — a clock rate, a frequency, a light path. Its most powerful disguised use is in Schild's argument, where it supplies the redshift that contradicts the flat metric. **Typical use:** "by the equivalence principle, replace the field $\vec g = -\gamma\vec e_x$ by an acceleration $\vec a = (\gamma/c^2)\vec e_x$, then use the Rindler-frame result." Recognise its applicability the instant the words "uniform gravitational field" and "local" appear together.

- **The trace of the energy-momentum tensor** $T = T^\mu{}_\mu$: the scalar that a scalar gravity theory couples to, and the diagnostic that decides whether matter bends light in such a theory. For a perfect fluid $T = \varepsilon - 3p$; for dust $T = \rho$; for the electromagnetic field $T^{\mathrm{em}} = 0$. **Typical use:** "the scalar field sees only the trace, and the electromagnetic trace vanishes, so light does not couple — no deflection." The reusable skill is to read the trace as the *Lorentz-scalar content* of a stress tensor, and to remember that conformally invariant matter (radiation) is traceless. Recognise its applicability whenever a scalar field must couple to matter, or whenever scale invariance is in play.

- **The Newtonian limit** $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$ with slow fields: the universal test that a covariant field equation is a legitimate generalisation of Newton, and the route to every leading-order prediction. **Typical use:** "drop $c^{-2}\partial_t^2\Phi$, so $\Box\Phi \to -\nabla^2\Phi$, identify $-T/c^2 \to \rho$, recover $\Delta\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$." It is also the calibration that fixes the coupling constant ($4\pi G/c^2$ in Nordström, $8\pi G/c^4$ in Einstein). Reach for it whenever a relativistic gravity equation must be checked against, or reduced to, classical gravity.

- **The gravitational redshift** $\Delta\nu/\nu = -\Delta\Phi/c^2$: the experimental signature of the equivalence principle and the lever that pries spacetime loose from flatness. **Typical use:** "a clock lower in the potential by $\Delta\Phi$ runs slow by $\Delta\Phi/c^2$; over height $h$ in surface gravity $g$ that is $gh/c^2$." It is the quantity Pound-Rebka, Vessot-Levine, and GPS all measure, and it is the linearisation of the exact Schwarzschild redshift. Recognise its applicability whenever two clocks sit at different gravitational potentials, and remember it is the seed of the entire metric-as-field idea.

- **The linearisation $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$**: the formal bridge from the flat-space tensor theory to weak-field general relativity, and the setting in which gravitational waves and the Fierz-Pauli equation live. **Typical use:** "write the metric as Minkowski plus a small perturbation, expand the Einstein equation, keep first order in $h$, and recover the massless spin-2 wave equation." The reusable content is that *general relativity in the weak field is special relativity plus a tensor field on it* — which is exactly the tensor theory of §25.1, and the reason the two descriptions agree to leading order. Reach for it whenever the field is weak, or whenever the connection between the flat-space and curved-space pictures must be made explicit.

# Bridges

1. **General relativity — the theory this chapter opens the door to.** Special relativity is built on one fixed metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$; general relativity is built on a metric *field* $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, a different symmetric bilinear form of signature $(+,-,-,-)$ at each event. The bridge is forced, not chosen: §25.3 proves that the gravitational redshift cannot be carried by a flat metric, so the metric must vary from point to point. In general relativity spacetime is no longer the affine space of [[Special Relativity III — Minkowski Spacetime and the Metric|Special Relativity III]] but a **differentiable manifold** $\mathscr{E}$, with a separate tangent space $E_A$ at each event $A$ (the manifold version of the vector space of displacements), and the metric is a tensor field assigning a bilinear form $g(A)$ to each $E_A$. Freely-falling bodies follow **geodesics** of $g$ — timelike geodesics for massive particles, null geodesics for photons — which generalises the inertial straight-line worldlines of special relativity. The metric is no longer a passive backdrop: it is dynamical, sourced by energy and momentum through the **Einstein equation** $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$, where $R_{\mu\nu}$ is the Ricci tensor and $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ the scalar curvature. At the Newtonian limit one component of this equation reduces to Poisson's $\Delta\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ and the other nine become $0=0$; in the weak field $g = \eta + h$ the linearised Einstein equation is precisely the Fierz-Pauli equation of the tensor theory of §25.1. The vault's [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]] develops the Einstein equation and its Schwarzschild solution in full; this chapter is the special-relativistic prologue that explains *why* that theory is necessary.

2. **Semi-Riemannian geometry — the mathematics of a position-dependent indefinite metric.** The step from $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ to $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ is, mathematically, the step from a constant indefinite bilinear form on $\mathbb{R}^4$ to a **semi-Riemannian metric** on a manifold: a smoothly-varying, non-degenerate, symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor field whose signature is $(+,-,-,-)$ — a **Lorentzian** metric. The vault's [[Def - Semi-Riemannian Metric and Signature]] gives the precise definition: where a Riemannian metric is positive-definite (signature $(n,0)$), a semi-Riemannian metric allows the indefinite signatures, and the Lorentzian case is the one general relativity needs. Everything special relativity does with $\eta$ — raising and lowering indices ([[Def - Metric Duality and Index Manipulation]]), classifying vectors as timelike, spacelike, or null, building the light cone — carries over to $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ pointwise, in each tangent space $E_A$. What is genuinely new is that the metric now has *derivatives*, and those derivatives encode the gravitational field: the first derivatives give the connection (the Christoffel symbols of [[Special Relativity XIX — Fields on Spacetime and the Covariant Derivative|XIX]], now position-dependent and non-removable), and the second derivatives give the curvature. The flat metric $\eta$ is the special case in which all these derivatives vanish, which is exactly the statement that special relativity is the zero-curvature, constant-metric chapter of Lorentzian geometry.

3. **Curvature and geodesic deviation — the invariant signature of a gravitational field.** The equivalence principle says a uniform gravitational field can be transformed away by going to a freely-falling frame, which raises the question: what is *left* of gravity that cannot be transformed away? The answer is **tidal force**, and its geometric name is **curvature**. Two freely-falling test particles released side by side do not stay side by side in a real (inhomogeneous) gravitational field: they accelerate relative to each other, because the field points toward a common centre. This relative acceleration of initially-parallel geodesics is **geodesic deviation**, and it is governed by the Riemann curvature tensor — in Minkowski space, where geodesics are straight lines of the affine space, initially-parallel geodesics stay parallel forever, so the curvature vanishes and there is no gravitational field. The vault's [[Riemannian Geometry III — Riemann Curvature and Topology]] develops the curvature tensor and geodesic deviation in the Riemannian setting; the Lorentzian version is what makes "gravity is the curvature of spacetime" a precise statement rather than a slogan. The chain is: equivalence principle removes the field at a point $\Rightarrow$ the residual is the variation of the field $\Rightarrow$ that variation is the relative acceleration of nearby geodesics $\Rightarrow$ that is curvature.

4. **Conformal geometry — Nordström's theory as a conformal rescaling of Minkowski space.** Einstein and Fokker discovered that Nordström's scalar theory, which looks like a field $\Phi$ on a fixed Minkowski background, is secretly a *metric* theory: particles move on geodesics not of $\eta$ but of the **conformally rescaled** metric $\tilde g = (1+\Phi/c^2)^2\,\eta$; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. A conformal rescaling multiplies the metric by a positive position-dependent factor $\Omega^2(x)$, and its defining property is that it preserves the light cones exactly — $\tilde g(\vec v,\vec v) = 0$ if and only if $\eta(\vec v,\vec v) = 0$ — so the causal structure is identical to Minkowski's, but proper times along timelike worldlines are rescaled by $\Omega$. This is why Nordström's theory predicts a gravitational redshift (clocks are rescaled) but *no* light deflection (light cones are unchanged, so null rays are unbent): the conformal factor cannot bend light. General relativity's metric is *not* merely conformal to $\eta$ — it deforms the light cones too, which is exactly why it bends light where Nordström's does not. The conformal-versus-general distinction is the precise geometric statement of the difference between the first metric theory of gravity and the correct one.

5. **Electromagnetism — the template that gravity both imitates and breaks.** This chapter is haunted by the electromagnetic analogy. The scalar theory's action borrows the Klein-Gordon field; the vector theory copies Maxwell wholesale, substituting $\varepsilon_0 \leftrightarrow -1/(4\pi G)$; the tensor theory's interaction $\tfrac{1}{2c}h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}$ mirrors the electromagnetic $A_\mu J^\mu$ of [[Special Relativity XXII — Maxwell's Equations|XXII]]. Each analogy illuminates by *failing* in a specific way, and the failures are diagnostic of gravity's true character. The vector analogy fails because like charges repel while like masses attract, and the sign flip that encodes this makes the field energy negative — telling us gravity is not spin-1. The scalar analogy fails because the electromagnetic field, whose stress tensor is traceless ([[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field|the field invariants]] include $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} \propto B^2 - E^2$, but the trace $T^{\mathrm{em}} = 0$ identically), does not couple to a scalar — telling us gravity must couple to the *full* stress tensor, not just its trace. Only the tensor analogy survives, because the source of gravity is the rank-2 energy-momentum tensor, so the mediating field must be a rank-2 tensor; and even then it survives only by becoming general relativity. The lesson is that electromagnetism is the correct prototype for a *force* in Minkowski spacetime, and gravity's refusal to fit that prototype — its insistence on being universal, attractive, and coupled to all energy-momentum — is precisely what makes it geometry rather than a force.

6. **The principle of least action — gravity from a Lagrangian, and the universality of the variational method.** Every theory in this chapter is derived from an action, exactly as Maxwell's equations were in [[Special Relativity XXII — Maxwell's Equations|XXII]] and the free-particle worldline in [[Special Relativity XV — The Principle of Least Action|XV]]. Nordström's field equation comes from varying $S = S_{\mathrm{field}} + S_{\mathrm{inter}} + S_{\mathrm{free}}$ with respect to $\Phi$, where $S_{\mathrm{field}}$ is the massless Klein-Gordon (scalar kinetic) action $-\tfrac{1}{8\pi G c}\int g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\Phi\,\partial_\nu\Phi\,dU$ and $S_{\mathrm{inter}} = -\tfrac1c\sum_a m_a\int\Phi\sqrt{\eta_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta}\,d\lambda$ with the gravitational charge set equal to the mass; varying instead with respect to the worldline $x_a^\alpha$ gives the equation of motion $(c^2+\Phi)\vec a = -\nabla\Phi\circ\bot_u$; see [[Def - Nordström's Scalar Theory of Gravity]]. The same variational principle, applied to the Einstein-Hilbert action $S = \tfrac{c^4}{16\pi G}\int R\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, yields the Einstein equation — so the road from this chapter to general relativity is a road within the action formalism, changing only the Lagrangian. The bridge is that the least-action principle is *theory-agnostic*: it manufactures a well-posed dynamics from any Lorentz-scalar Lagrangian, which is why it is the natural tool both for testing candidate gravity theories and for writing down the correct one.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: gravity is the one interaction that cannot live in Minkowski space, and the reason is universality.** The whole chapter is organised around a single contrast. Electromagnetism, the weak force, the strong force — all fit comfortably as fields on the fixed stage of Minkowski spacetime, because they couple to *charges* that some particles carry and others do not, and a particle's response depends on its charge-to-mass ratio. Gravity is different in exactly one respect: its "charge" is mass-energy itself, which every particle carries, and it couples in proportion to inertia, so *every body responds identically*. This universality — the equality of gravitational and inertial mass — is what makes gravity geometrisable and ungeometrisable-otherwise. Because all bodies fall the same way, the falling is a property of the spacetime, not of the body, and can be encoded in the geometry; and because gravity affects everything including the rods and clocks used to measure spacetime, it cannot be a field *on* a fixed geometry but must be the geometry. The three failed theories of §25.1 are three attempts to treat gravity as just another field on Minkowski space, and they fail in three different ways, but the deep reason behind all three failures is the same: gravity's universality means it must deform the metric that everything else is measured against, and a field on a fixed metric simply cannot do that. The right frame for the entire chapter is: *every other force is a tenant of spacetime; gravity is the landlord.*

**The true name of the equivalence principle is "gravity can be locally transformed away, but not globally — and the obstruction is curvature".** The textbook statement — a uniform field equals an accelerated frame — is the right thing to *compute with* but hides the structure. The operational content is a statement about what can and cannot be removed by a change of frame. At any single event, a freely-falling frame removes the gravitational field entirely: the falling observer is weightless, and locally the laws of physics are those of special relativity. This is the *local* equivalence principle, and it is exact. What it cannot do is remove the field everywhere at once, because a real field is inhomogeneous, and the irreducible residue — the part of gravity that survives in every frame — is the **tidal field**, the relative acceleration of nearby freely-falling bodies. That residue is the curvature. So the true name packages three things at once: gravity is locally an illusion (removable by free fall), gravity is globally real (the tides cannot be removed), and the measure of the global reality is the curvature tensor. Every confusion about the equivalence principle — "if gravity can be transformed away, is it real?" — dissolves once one holds onto the word *locally*: the field at a point is frame-dependent and removable, the curvature is frame-independent and is the true gravitational field.

**Indefiniteness was the engine of special relativity; position-dependence is the engine of general relativity.** Looking back across the whole sequence, two sign-related facts have done all the work. [[Special Relativity III — Minkowski Spacetime and the Metric|Special Relativity III]] taught that the single change from a positive-definite metric to the indefinite $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$ produces the light cone, causal structure, the speed limit, and the reversed triangle inequality — every relativistic surprise traces to the minus signs. This chapter teaches the next change: from a *constant* indefinite metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ to a *position-dependent* one $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, which produces gravity, tides, geodesic focusing, black holes, and the expansion of the universe — every gravitational phenomenon traces to the position-dependence. The two changes are independent and composable: special relativity is constant-and-indefinite, Newtonian gravity is variable-and-degenerate (a degenerate "metric" measuring only time), and general relativity is variable-and-indefinite, the full Lorentzian manifold. The arc of the whole subject is the loosening of two assumptions about the metric — first its definiteness, then its constancy — and each loosening is a new physics. Special relativity is the chapter in which the metric is allowed to be indefinite but must stay constant; the moment it is also allowed to vary, one has left special relativity for good.

**Schild's argument is the cleanest "impossibility proof" in physics, and its power is that it needs almost nothing.** It is worth isolating what makes the redshift argument so decisive. It does not require solving any field equation, computing any trajectory, or even knowing the theory of gravity — it requires only two inputs: the equivalence principle (which gives a redshift) and the assumption that the field is static (which gives a time-translation symmetry). From these alone it derives a contradiction with the Minkowski metric, by the purely geometric observation that two successive light signals in a static field are time-translates of each other, so a translation-invariant metric must assign them equal lengths, contradicting the redshift. No detail of the photons' worldlines enters; the conclusion is forced by symmetry. This is the signature of a great no-go theorem: a contradiction extracted from a minimal set of robust assumptions, immune to the details of any specific model. The reusable lesson is a problem-solving pattern of the highest leverage — when you suspect a structure is impossible, look for a symmetry the structure would have to respect and a measurement that would have to violate it; the clash of the two is the proof. Schild used a static field's time-translation symmetry against the redshift, and in two paragraphs ended special relativity's claim to describe gravity.
