# Problem Statement

A point charge $q$ moves at constant velocity $\mathbf{U} = U\,e_x$ relative to an inertial observer $\mathcal{O}$, passing the origin at $t = 0$. In the charge's rest frame $\mathcal{O}'$ the field is the static Coulomb field $\mathbf{E}' = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\,\mathbf{r}'$, $\mathbf{B}' = 0$, with $r' = \sqrt{x'^2+y'^2+z'^2}$.

1. Using the [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields|field transformation law]] and the Lorentz transformation of coordinates, derive the electric field $\mathbf{E}$ measured by $\mathcal{O}$ in terms of $\mathcal{O}$'s coordinates, and show it points radially from the charge's *present* position $(Ut, 0, 0)$.
2. Show the field magnitude is $E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,\Gamma^2 R^2[1 - (U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{3/2}}$, where $R$ is the distance from the present position and $\theta$ the angle to $\mathbf{U}$. Deduce that the field is weaker by $\Gamma^2$ along the motion and stronger by $\Gamma$ transverse to it.
3. Find the magnetic field, show $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$, and recover the Biot–Savart law in the non-relativistic limit.
4. Verify that the field is mostly electric ($I_1 < 0$) with $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ ($I_2 = 0$) in every frame, consistent with its being a boosted Coulomb field.

**Recall:**

![[Def - Field of a Charge in Uniform Translation#The Definition]]

The transformation law for the fields under a boost of velocity $\mathbf{U}$ is $\mathbf{E}'_\parallel = \mathbf{E}_\parallel$, $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})_\perp$, $\mathbf{B}'_\parallel = \mathbf{B}_\parallel$, $\mathbf{B}'_\perp = \Gamma(\mathbf{B} - \tfrac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E})_\perp$ (see [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields]]); the inverse (from $\mathcal{O}'$ to $\mathcal{O}$) flips the sign of $\mathbf{U}$. The [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation|coordinate transformation]] is $x' = \Gamma(x - Ut)$, $y' = y$, $z' = z$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** A *field-of-a-moving-source* problem, the headline application of [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field#Problem-Solving Strategy|§21.2]]. The routine is to start in the source's rest frame, where the field is elementary (Coulomb), and boost both the field and the coordinates into the lab frame.

**Assumption pattern.** A charge in uniform motion, whose rest-frame field is *known* to be Coulomb with no magnetic part. The assumption "uniform velocity" is what guarantees a rest frame exists and that there is no radiation; the assumption "$\mathbf{B}' = 0$ in the rest frame" is the seed from which the lab-frame magnetic field is generated by the boost. The signpost is that the magnetic field is entirely an artefact of the change of frame.

**Theorem routing.** Part 1 routes the rest-frame Coulomb field through the inverse [[Thm - Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields|transformation law]] (with $\mathbf{B}' = 0$) and the [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation|coordinate transformation]] to the lab field. Part 2 is algebraic simplification of the denominator. Part 3 reads the magnetic field off the same transformation. Part 4 routes through the [[Thm - The Electromagnetic Field Invariants|invariants]].

**Key decision point.** The crux is recognising that the transverse electric field and the longitudinal electric field both acquire the *same* factor of $\Gamma$ — the transverse part via the field transformation law (the $\Gamma$ in $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma\mathbf{E}_\perp$), the longitudinal part via the coordinate transformation $x' = \Gamma(x - Ut)$ in the denominator — and this common $\Gamma$ is what makes the field point at the *present* position rather than some distorted direction. Missing this makes the radial-from-present-position result look miraculous instead of forced.

# Legal Operations Used

1. **Operation 3 (work in the source's rest frame, then boost out)** from the topic page: start with the Coulomb field in $\mathcal{O}'$ and transform to $\mathcal{O}$. This is the overall strategy.

2. **Operation 2 (apply the field transformation law)** from the topic page: with $\mathbf{B}' = 0$, transform $\mathbf{E}'$ and generate $\mathbf{B}$. This is parts 1 and 3.

3. **Operation 4 (compute the invariants)** from the topic page: verify $I_1 < 0$, $I_2 = 0$. This is part 4, confirming the field is mostly electric in every frame.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> Decompose the Coulomb field into parts parallel ($x'$) and perpendicular ($y', z'$) to $\mathbf{U}$. The inverse transformation (from $\mathcal{O}'$ to $\mathcal{O}$, so use $-\mathbf{U}$) with $\mathbf{B}' = 0$ gives $E_\parallel = E'_\parallel$ and $E_\perp = \Gamma E'_\perp$. Then substitute $x' = \Gamma(x - Ut)$, $y'=y$, $z'=z$.

> [!note]- Hint 2
> The longitudinal field $E_x = E'_x = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}x' = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\Gamma(x-Ut)$, and the transverse $E_y = \Gamma E'_y = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\Gamma y$. Both carry one factor of $\Gamma$ relative to $r'^3$, so $\mathbf{E} = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}[(x-Ut)e_x + ye_y + ze_z]$ — radial from the present position $(Ut,0,0)$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> Re-express $r'^3 = [\Gamma^2(x-Ut)^2 + y^2 + z^2]^{3/2}$. With $x_0 = x - Ut$, $R = \sqrt{x_0^2+y^2+z^2}$, and $y^2+z^2 = R^2\sin^2\theta$, factor out: $\Gamma^2 x_0^2 + y^2 + z^2 = \Gamma^2 R^2[\cos^2\theta + \Gamma^{-2}\sin^2\theta] = \Gamma^2 R^2[1 - (U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]$. Hence the magnitude formula.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For $\mathbf{B}$: the field transformation gives $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$ directly (from $\mathbf{B}_\perp = \Gamma\cdot\frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}'_\perp$ and re-expressing $\mathbf{E}'_\perp$ via $\mathbf{E}_\perp$). In the slow limit $\Gamma\to1$, the bracket $\to1$, and $\mathbf{B}\to\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{n}}{R^2}$ — Biot–Savart.

# Solution

The plan: boost the rest-frame Coulomb field to the lab. Step 1 transforms the field and shows both longitudinal and transverse parts carry a factor $\Gamma$, so the field is radial from the present position. Step 2 simplifies the denominator to the angular form, revealing the pancaking. Step 3 reads off the magnetic field and the Biot–Savart limit. Step 4 checks the invariants. The non-obvious move is that the *common* factor of $\Gamma$ (transverse from the field law, longitudinal from the coordinate boost) is what keeps the field radial.

**Step 1: Boost the Coulomb field; it points from the present position.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> In $\mathcal{O}'$ the field is $\mathbf{E}' = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}(x'e_x' + y'e_y' + z'e_z')$, $\mathbf{B}' = 0$. Transforming to $\mathcal{O}$ (the boost from $\mathcal{O}'$ to $\mathcal{O}$ has velocity $-\mathbf{U}$, but with $\mathbf{B}'=0$ the field law reads $\mathbf{E}_\parallel = \mathbf{E}'_\parallel$, $\mathbf{E}_\perp = \Gamma\mathbf{E}'_\perp$ either way):
> $$E_x = E'_x = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}x', \qquad E_y = \Gamma E'_y = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}y', \qquad E_z = \Gamma E'_z = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}z'.$$
> Now substitute the [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation|Lorentz transformation]] $x' = \Gamma(x - Ut)$, $y' = y$, $z' = z$. Crucially, $E_x = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\Gamma(x-Ut)$ also acquires a factor $\Gamma$ — from the coordinate, not the field law. So *every* component carries one $\Gamma$:
> $$\mathbf{E} = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\big[(x-Ut)\,e_x + y\,e_y + z\,e_z\big].$$
> The vector in brackets is the displacement from the charge's *present* position $(Ut,0,0)$ to the field point $(x,y,z)$. So $\mathbf{E}$ is radial from where the charge *is now*, not where it was — a special feature of uniform motion. This is exactly Eq. (17.51).

**Step 2: The magnitude pancakes.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Express $r'^3$ in lab coordinates: $r'^2 = x'^2 + y'^2 + z'^2 = \Gamma^2(x-Ut)^2 + y^2 + z^2$. Set $x_0 = x - Ut$, $R = \sqrt{x_0^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ (distance from present position), and let $\theta$ be the angle between $\mathbf{U}$ ($=e_x$) and the radius vector, so $x_0 = R\cos\theta$, $y^2 + z^2 = R^2\sin^2\theta$. Then
> $$r'^2 = \Gamma^2 R^2\cos^2\theta + R^2\sin^2\theta = \Gamma^2 R^2\Big[\cos^2\theta + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\Gamma^2}\Big] = \Gamma^2 R^2\Big[1 - \frac{U^2}{c^2}\sin^2\theta\Big],$$
> using $\Gamma^{-2} = 1 - U^2/c^2$, so $\cos^2\theta + (1 - U^2/c^2)\sin^2\theta = 1 - (U^2/c^2)\sin^2\theta$. Therefore $r'^3 = \Gamma^3 R^3[1-(U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{3/2}$, and the magnitude is
> $$E = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{R}{r'^3} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,\Gamma^2 R^2[1 - (U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{3/2}}.$$
> **Along the motion** ($\theta = 0$): the bracket is $1$, so $E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\Gamma^2 R^2}$ — *weaker* by $\Gamma^2$ than Coulomb. **Transverse** ($\theta = \pi/2$): the bracket is $(1-U^2/c^2)^{3/2} = \Gamma^{-3}$, so $E = \frac{q\Gamma}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^2}$ — *stronger* by $\Gamma$. The field is compressed into a transverse disk: the "pancake".

**Step 3: The magnetic field and Biot–Savart.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Transforming the (vanishing) rest-frame magnetic field: $\mathbf{B}'=0$ gives $\mathbf{B}_\parallel = 0$ and $\mathbf{B}_\perp = \Gamma\cdot\frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}'_\perp$. Since $\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma^{-1}\mathbf{E}_\perp$ (inverting the electric transverse law) and $\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}_\parallel = 0$,
> $$\mathbf{B} = \frac{\Gamma}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \frac{\Gamma}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times(\Gamma^{-1}\mathbf{E}_\perp) = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}.$$
> So $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$, circulating around the line of motion. Substituting the magnitude from Step 2,
> $$\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{qU}{\Gamma^2 R^2[1-(U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{3/2}}\,e_x\times\mathbf{n}, \qquad \mu_0 = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0 c^2}.$$
> In the **non-relativistic limit** $U\ll c$: $\Gamma\to1$ and the bracket $\to1$, leaving
> $$\mathbf{B} \simeq \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{n}}{R^2},$$
> the **Biot–Savart law** for the field of a current element $q\mathbf{U}$. So the magnetic field of a slowly moving charge is exactly the textbook Biot–Savart field, recovered here from a boost of the Coulomb field.

**Step 4: The invariants confirm a boosted Coulomb field.**

> [!note]- Derivation
> Since $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{E}$:
> $$I_2 = c\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c}\mathbf{E}\cdot(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}) = 0.$$
> And $|\mathbf{B}| = \frac{U}{c^2}|\mathbf{E}|\sin\alpha \le \frac{U}{c^2}|\mathbf{E}| < \frac{1}{c}|\mathbf{E}|$ (since $U < c$), so $c^2\mathbf{B}^2 < \mathbf{E}^2$ and
> $$I_1 = c^2\mathbf{B}^2 - \mathbf{E}^2 < 0.$$
> The field is **mostly electric** ($I_1 < 0$) with $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$ ($I_2 = 0$) in every frame — exactly as expected for a boosted Coulomb field, which in the rest frame has $I_1 = -E'^2 < 0$, $I_2 = 0$. By the [[Thm - Reduction to Parallel Electric and Magnetic Fields|reduction theorem]], the boost $U = c^2B/E$ that makes it purely electric recovers the charge's own rest frame.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> Start with the rest-frame Coulomb field $\mathbf{E}' = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}\mathbf{r}'$, $\mathbf{B}'=0$. The field transformation (with $\mathbf{B}'=0$) gives $E_x = E'_x$, $E_{y,z} = \Gamma E'_{y,z}$; substituting $x' = \Gamma(x-Ut)$, $y'=y$, $z'=z$ makes every component carry a factor $\Gamma$: $\mathbf{E} = \frac{\Gamma q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^3}[(x-Ut)e_x + ye_y + ze_z]$, radial from the present position. Writing $r'^2 = \Gamma^2 R^2[1-(U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]$ gives $E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\Gamma^2 R^2[1-(U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{3/2}}$ — weaker by $\Gamma^2$ at $\theta=0$, stronger by $\Gamma$ at $\theta=\pi/2$. The magnetic field is $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$, reducing to Biot–Savart $\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{n}}{R^2}$ as $U\ll c$. The invariants $I_2 = 0$ (orthogonal) and $I_1 < 0$ (mostly electric) hold in every frame, confirming the field is the boosted Coulomb field. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**Magnetism is electrostatics seen from a moving frame.** The single most important conclusion is that a charge with *no magnetic field at all* in its own frame produces a magnetic field $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{E}$ in any frame where it moves — and in the slow limit this is exactly the Biot–Savart law. The magnetic field is not a separate physical agent; it is the same [[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor|field tensor]] viewed from a boosted frame. This is the relativistic *origin* of magnetism: the force between currents, the field of a wire, the operation of a motor are all electrostatics seen from the frame of moving charges. The reusable strategy, applicable to any moving source, is to go to the rest frame where the field is elementary and boost — special relativity *contains* magnetostatics, requiring no independent law for moving charges. This is the lesson Einstein opened his 1905 paper with.

**The common factor of $\Gamma$ keeps the field radial from the present position.** The technically subtle point — and the reason the radial-from-present-position result is not a coincidence — is that the longitudinal and transverse electric components acquire the *same* factor of $\Gamma$, but by *different* mechanisms: the transverse part via the field transformation law ($\mathbf{E}'_\perp = \Gamma\mathbf{E}_\perp$), the longitudinal part via the coordinate contraction $x' = \Gamma(x - Ut)$ in the Coulomb denominator. Because both carry one $\Gamma$, the field vector is a uniform scaling of the displacement from the present position, hence radial. This delicate conspiracy is special to *uniform* motion; for an accelerated charge it fails, the field acquires a $1/R$ radiative part, and it tracks the retarded position instead. Recognising which factors of $\Gamma$ come from the field law and which from the coordinate boost is the key bookkeeping skill, and it generalises to any boosted-field computation.

**The pancake field and relativistic beaming.** The angular factor $[1 - (U/c)^2\sin^2\theta]^{-3/2}$ encodes the physical signature of a fast charge: the field is suppressed by $\Gamma^2$ ahead and behind, enhanced by $\Gamma$ abreast, collapsing into a thin transverse disk at high $\Gamma$. This is the field-strength analogue of the relativistic beaming of radiation into a forward cone, and it has direct consequences: the transversely enhanced field of a relativistic beam governs the beam–beam interaction in colliders, and the same compression underlies the Weizsäcker–Williams equivalent-photon method, in which the pancaked field of a fast nucleus is treated as a flux of real photons. The trigger — "fast charge, field compressed transverse" — recurs throughout accelerator physics and ultrarelativistic collision phenomenology. The invariants $I_1 < 0$, $I_2 = 0$ confirm throughout that, however distorted, this remains a *boosted Coulomb field*: mostly electric, with $\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{B}$, in every frame.
