## §13.2 Conservation and Collisions — Exercises

These are the workhorse exercises of the chapter, and they are all built from one law and one technique. The law is [[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum|conservation of four-momentum]]: in any process without external forces, $\sum_{\text{in}}P^\mu = \sum_{\text{out}}P^\mu$ — a single four-vector equation that bundles conservation of energy and three-momentum, replacing the separate Newtonian laws. The technique is the **invariant-mass technique**: whenever a particle's energy and direction are not wanted, isolate its four-momentum on one side of the conservation equation and take the Minkowski square, converting the unwanted four-vector into the known scalar $m^2$ (or $0$ for a photon) and collapsing a vector equation into a scalar one. This "rearrange to $P = \ldots$, then square" reflex solves Compton scattering (eliminate the recoil electron), two-body decay (eliminate one product), and every reaction threshold (square the total four-momentum). Two structural simplifications recur: photons contribute null four-momenta whose self-squares *vanish*, making them the easiest participants; and the centre-of-momentum frame, where the total spatial momentum vanishes, trivialises threshold problems because at threshold all products sit at rest there. The unifying observation is that you understand a relativistic process the moment you know its conserved total four-momentum and its invariant masses — everything asked is read off from those two.

- [[Ex - Compton scattering]] — derive the Compton wavelength shift $\lambda' - \lambda = \tfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta)$ for $\gamma + e^- \to \gamma + e^-$ by isolating and squaring the recoil electron to eliminate it via $P_e'\cdot P_e' = m_e^2$, show the photon always loses energy with maximal shift backward, and find the Compton edge and the energy scale $m_e c^2$ below which the effect is negligible (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Elastic Collisions and the Compton Effect]], [[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon]]).

- [[Ex - Inverse Compton scattering and the GZK cutoff]] — show that a *moving* electron can give energy to a photon, derive the maximum gain $\max E_\gamma'/E_\gamma \simeq 4\Gamma_e^2$ for an ultra-relativistic electron from the frame-independent Compton relation and the Taylor expansion $V_e \simeq 1 - 1/2\Gamma_e^2$, apply it to up-scattering the cosmic microwave background, and find the GZK threshold $E_p \approx 10^{20}$ eV for pion photoproduction on the CMB by the invariant-mass technique (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Elastic Collisions and the Compton Effect]], [[Thm - Inelastic Collisions and Particle Production]], [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon]], [[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]]).

- [[Ex - Two-body decay kinematics]] — a particle of mass $m_1$ at rest decays into two of masses $m_2, m_3$; isolate and square one product to show conservation fixes the product energies and Lorentz factors entirely, $\gamma_2 = (m_1^2 + m_2^2 - m_3^2)/(2m_1 m_2)$, and forbids the decay unless $m_1 \ge m_2 + m_3$; apply to Higgs $\to\gamma\gamma$ and the photon angle in a boosted frame (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Thm - Inelastic Collisions and Particle Production]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).

- [[Ex - Threshold energy for particle production]] — find the minimum energy to create new particles via the invariant $(\sum P)^2$, evaluated in the lab frame and again in the centre-of-momentum frame where at threshold all products are at rest; derive the antiproton-production threshold $E_1 \ge 7m_p \simeq 6.57$ GeV for a fixed target, and contrast the quadratic lab-energy growth with the linear collider growth (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Inelastic Collisions and Particle Production]], [[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).

- [[Ex - Whether a particle reaction is kinematically allowed]] — use conservation of four-momentum as a filter: show a free photon cannot decay to $e^+ e^-$ (a null four-vector can never equal a timelike one), find the two-photon pair-production threshold $E_1 E_2(1-\cos\theta) \ge 2m_e^2$, and explain why $\gamma + p \to e^+ + e^- + p$ needs a third body to absorb recoil (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon]], [[Def - Massless Particles]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]]).

- [[Ex - Mandelstam variables for two-body scattering]] — for $1 + 2 \to 3 + 4$ define $s, t, u$ as Minkowski squares of four-momentum sums and differences, prove they are Lorentz invariant and obey the constraint $s + t + u = \sum_i m_i^2$, identify $\sqrt{s}$ as the centre-of-mass energy, and compute $s$ for a symmetric collider versus a fixed target (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]], [[Thm - Inelastic Collisions and Particle Production]]).

- [[Ex - The relativistic rocket]] — a photon rocket emits radiation backward; applying conservation of four-momentum to each infinitesimal emission in the instantaneous rest frame gives $d\phi = -dM/M$, which integrates (because collinear boosts add rapidities) to the relativistic Tsiolkovsky equation $\gamma(1 + v) = M_0/M$; deduce the punishing mass ratios near $c$ and the radiated-energy budget (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]], [[Def - The Four-Momentum of a Photon]], [[Def - Rapidity]], [[Def - Four-Force]]).
