## §13.3 Four-Force and the Equation of Motion — Exercises

The exercises of §13.3 return to non-isolated particles and the covariant equation of motion $F^\mu = dP^\mu/d\tau$ ([[Thm - Relativistic Newton's Second Law]]). The master move is to *project* this single four-vector law onto an inertial observer: its spatial part is Newton's second law for the relativistic momentum, $d\mathbf{p}/dt = \mathbf{f}$ with $\mathbf{p} = \gamma m\mathbf{u}$, and its time part is the work–energy theorem $dE/dt = \mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}$. Two subtleties recur and are drilled here. First, the four-force splits against the four-velocity into a mass-preserving part $mA$ (orthogonal, $A\cdot U = 0$) and a mass-changing part $(dm/d\tau)U$ (parallel), with $F\cdot U = dm/d\tau$ deciding which forces alter a particle's rest mass; a **pure** force has $F\cdot U = 0$ and keeps the mass constant. Second, because the momentum $\mathbf{p} = \gamma m\mathbf{u}$ carries the speed-dependent factor $\gamma$, the acceleration is *not* parallel to the force — differentiating honestly produces a longitudinal $\gamma^3$ response and a transverse $\gamma$ response, the anisotropic inertia that dynamically enforces the speed limit. The unifying observation: the diverging $\gamma$ near $c$ (in the longitudinal mass, in the kinetic energy, and in the rocket's mass ratio) is the single mechanism by which a force can push a massive particle ever closer to the speed of light without ever reaching it. The one fundamental pure force, the electromagnetic Lorentz force, and its concrete charged-particle motion (the hyperbolic worldline of a linear accelerator) are developed in [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field|Special Relativity XXI]], which owns the field.

- [[Ex - The relativistic work-energy theorem]] — derive $dE/dt = \mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}$ from the constancy of the rest mass $\tfrac{d}{d\tau}(P\cdot P) = 0$ (energy and momentum are locked by the mass shell), confirm it is the time component of $F^\mu = dP^\mu/d\tau$ via the purity $F\cdot U = 0$, integrate it to the relativistic kinetic energy $W = (\gamma - 1)m$, and recover the Newtonian $\tfrac12 mu^2$ with its leading $+\tfrac38 mu^4$ correction (⭐) ([[Thm - Relativistic Newton's Second Law]], [[Def - Four-Force]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).

- [[Ex - The acceleration is not parallel to the force]] — differentiate $\mathbf{p} = \gamma m\mathbf{u}$ (keeping $d\gamma/dt = \gamma^3\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{a}$) to obtain $\mathbf{f} = \gamma m\mathbf{a} + \gamma^3 m(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}$, read off the longitudinal mass $\gamma^3 m$ and transverse mass $\gamma m$, show the acceleration leans away from the velocity with $\tan\alpha_{\mathbf{a}} = \gamma^2\tan\alpha_{\mathbf{f}}$, and explain via $a = f/(\gamma^3 m) \to 0$ why a constant force never reaches $c$ (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Relativistic Newton's Second Law]], [[Def - Four-Force]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).

- [[Ex - The four-force along the four-velocity and rest-mass change]] — decompose the four-force as $F = mA + (dm/d\tau)U$ and prove $F\cdot U = dm/d\tau$ (using $A\cdot U = 0$), so a pure force preserves rest mass and the orthogonal part $mA$ never touches it; apply to an excited atom emitting a photon (the recoil deficit $\omega < m_i - m_f$, with $\Delta m = \int F\cdot U\,d\tau$) and show the electromagnetic Lorentz force $qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$ is automatically pure because the field tensor is antisymmetric (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Four-Force]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Thm - Conservation of Four-Momentum]]).
