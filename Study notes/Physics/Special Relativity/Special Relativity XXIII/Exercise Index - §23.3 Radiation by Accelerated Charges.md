## §23.3 Radiation by Accelerated Charges — Exercises

The exercises of §23.3 cash out the chapter's most spectacular application of the energy-momentum tensor: the radiation of an accelerated charge. The unifying technique is to identify the *radiative* ($1/r$) part of the electromagnetic field, compute its Poynting flux, and integrate over a sphere at infinity — the universal recipe by which the [[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)|Larmor formula]] emerges, together with its [[Thm - Angular Distribution of Radiation|angular distribution]] and the corresponding [[Def - Synchrotron Radiation|synchrotron]] specialisation to circular motion. The single Larmor exercise drills the canonical derivation: rest-frame radiative field, dipole-donut $\sin^2\theta$ angular pattern, the universal $\int\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\Omega = 8\pi/3$ angular integral, and the lift to the Lorentz-invariant form $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/(6\pi\varepsilon_0 c)$ that exposes the Liénard $\Gamma^6$ as kinematic bookkeeping rather than new physics. The reusable thread of the section is that *only acceleration radiates, and the power is an invariant*: the $\Gamma$-factors that intimidate are the cost of expressing the frame-independent $|A|^2$ in laboratory three-vectors, and disciplined problem-solving computes the invariant first and only then specialises to a frame.

- [[Ex - The Larmor formula from the radiative field flux]] — identify the radiative ($1/r$) part of the Liénard-Wiechert field in the instantaneous rest frame as $\mathbf E_{\text{rad}} = q\hat{\mathbf n}\times(\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf a)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 c^2 r)$ with magnitude $q a\sin\theta/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 c^2 r)$ (transverse to $\hat{\mathbf n}$, dipole-donut pattern), compute the Poynting flux $\mathrm d\mathcal P/\mathrm d\Omega = q^2 a^2\sin^2\theta/(16\pi^2\varepsilon_0 c^3)$, evaluate the universal angular integral $\int_0^\pi\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\theta = 4/3$ (so $\int\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\Omega = 8\pi/3$) to recover the Larmor formula $\mathcal P = q^2 a^2/(6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3)$, and lift to the Lorentz-invariant form $\mathcal P = q^2|A|^2/(6\pi\varepsilon_0 c)$ with $|A|^2 = -A\cdot A$, showing the Liénard $\Gamma^6$ is just the invariant expressed in laboratory three-velocity and three-acceleration (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Radiation by an Accelerated Charge (Larmor Formula)]], [[Def - Energy-Momentum Tensor of the Electromagnetic Field]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]).
