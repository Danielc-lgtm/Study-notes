## §8.2 Doppler Effect and Aberration — Exercises

The exercises of §8.2 drill the two optical transformation laws — the Doppler shift of frequency and the aberration of direction — which are the two components of the boost acting on a photon's null tangent. The recurring technique is to recognise that a photon's relative velocity is a *unit* vector, so its frequency transforms by the Doppler factor and its direction by aberration, both from the same projection. The Ives–Stilwell exercise isolates the purely relativistic transverse Doppler factor (time dilation) by the symmetric-pairing trick that cancels the first-order effect; the stellar-aberration exercise computes the $20''$ yearly ellipse and distinguishes it from parallax by its velocity-dependence; the headlight exercise combines aberration and Doppler to beam an isotropic source into a forward cone of half-angle $\sim 1/\Gamma$. The unifying observation: Doppler and aberration are one map, the Lorentz action on the sky, and the half-angle aberration form already hints at its conformal (Möbius) nature.

- [[Ex - The Ives-Stilwell experiment and transverse Doppler]] (⭐⭐) — average the forward and backward radial Doppler shifts of atoms moving at $\pm V$ to obtain $\Gamma f_0$ (isolating the time-dilation factor that the first-order theory misses), and show the modern product test $f_1 f_2 = f_0^2$ is velocity-independent and exact ([[Thm - The Doppler Effect]], [[Def - Relativistic Doppler Factor]], [[Thm - Time Dilation (General Observer)]]).

- [[Ex - Stellar aberration and the celestial-sphere distortion]] (⭐⭐) — derive the stellar aberration $\sin\alpha = U/c$ giving the $20''$ yearly circle of a polar star, distinguish it from parallax by its distance-independence and $90^\circ$ phase offset, and show how a uniform sky-grid bunches toward the forward pole as $U \to c$ ([[Thm - Aberration of Light]], [[Def - Apparent Rotation and Images of Moving Objects]]).

- [[Ex - The relativistic headlight effect]] (⭐⭐⭐) — track the median rest-frame photon to show half an isotropic source's light is beamed into a forward cone of half-angle $\arccos V \approx 1/\Gamma$, and combine with the Doppler factor to find the forward-intensity boost $\sim\mathcal{D}^{3+\alpha}$ and the resulting one-sided appearance of relativistic jets ([[Thm - Aberration of Light]], [[Thm - The Doppler Effect]], [[Def - Apparent Rotation and Images of Moving Objects]]).
