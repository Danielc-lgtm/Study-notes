## §8.3 Images of Moving Objects — Exercises

The exercises of §8.3 drill the single most overturned expectation in relativistic optics: that a fast object *looks* FitzGerald-contracted. It does not. The recurring technique is to build the image as the slice of the object's worldtube by the observer's *past light cone* — the photons that arrive together, having been emitted at different retarded times — rather than the rest-space slice that defines the contracted position. The Penrose–Terrell exercise proves via stereographic projection that aberration is conformal, so a moving sphere photographs as a perfect circle; the superluminal-jet exercise shows a blob chasing its own light produces an apparent transverse speed $V_{\mathrm{app}} = V\sin\theta/(1 - V\cos\theta)$ exceeding $c$; the moving-ruler exercise reconstructs the image of a cube to reveal its hidden back face and identify the apparent rotation. The unifying observation: appearance is governed by the conformal action on the celestial sphere and the light-travel-time retardation, never by length contraction directly — image and position are different slices of the same worldtube.

- [[Ex - The Penrose-Terrell rotation of a moving sphere]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove via stereographic projection that the aberration formula acts as a constant dilation on the projected plane, hence conformally, so the sphere's circular outline maps to a circle for every observer and a moving sphere photographs as an undistorted disk ([[Def - Apparent Rotation and Images of Moving Objects]], [[Thm - Aberration of Light]], [[Thm - Length Contraction (General)]]).

- [[Ex - The apparent superluminal motion of an astrophysical jet]] (⭐⭐) — derive $V_{\mathrm{app}} = V\sin\theta/(1 - V\cos\theta)$ from two light-arrival times, show it exceeds $c$ with maximum $\Gamma V$ at $\cos\theta = V$, resolve the paradox via the image-versus-position distinction, and bound the M87 jet's $\Gamma \ge 6$ and $\theta \lesssim 19^\circ$ ([[Def - Apparent Rotation and Images of Moving Objects]], [[Thm - Reciprocity of Relative Velocity]]).

- [[Ex - Why a moving ruler is photographed elongated or rotated]] (⭐⭐) — reconstruct images from simultaneous photon arrival to show an approaching rod looks elongated by $\sqrt{(1+V)/(1-V)}$ and a transverse cube shows its hidden back face (width $Va$) plus its contracted front face (width $a/\Gamma$), forming a rotation by $\arcsin V$, with contraction essential to the clean rotation ([[Def - Apparent Rotation and Images of Moving Objects]], [[Thm - Length Contraction (General)]]).
