## §8.1 Reciprocity and Velocity Composition — Exercises

The exercises of §8.1 drill the three foundational change-of-observer results: reciprocity (two observers agree on their relative *speed* but not their relative *velocity vectors*), and the velocity-composition law in both its collinear and non-collinear forms. The recurring discipline is rest-space bookkeeping — knowing which velocity vector lives in which observer's rest space — and the recurring reward is an invariant that survives the change (the relative speed, the speed of light as a fixed point). The Fizeau exercise shows the collinear law's first-order expansion reproducing the pre-relativistic Fresnel drag; the reciprocity exercise establishes the magnitude-equality directly from the four-velocities; the Wigner-rotation exercise exposes the hidden rotation in non-collinear composition, the seed of the Lorentz group's structure theory. The unifying observation: a relative velocity is a projection of the relative motion onto a chosen rest space, and changing whose rest space is the entire game.

- [[Ex - The Fizeau experiment and the Fresnel drag]] (⭐⭐) — expand the collinear velocity-composition law to first order in $V/c$ to find the speed of light in moving water, recovering the Fresnel drag coefficient $\alpha = 1 - 1/n^2$ and the Fizeau phase shift, and showing relativity reproduces the pre-relativistic "partial aether drag" as a composition correction ([[Thm - Law of Velocity Composition]], [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition]]).

- [[Ex - Reciprocity of relative velocity from the four-velocities]] (⭐⭐) — prove directly from the four-velocity decompositions that $\lVert U'\rVert_g = \lVert U\rVert_g$ while $U' \ne -U$, show the vector identity is type-incorrect because $U \notin E_{u'}$, derive $U' = -\Gamma_0^{-1}\perp_{u'}U$, and recover the Galilean limit ([[Thm - Reciprocity of Relative Velocity]], [[Def - Velocity Relative to an Observer]], [[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space]]).

- [[Ex - Composing two perpendicular boosts and the Wigner rotation]] (⭐⭐⭐) — multiply two perpendicular boost matrices, detect the rotation through the asymmetry of the product (pure boosts being symmetric), extract the leading Wigner angle $\omega \approx \tfrac12\varphi\psi$, and explain why this shows the boosts do not form a subgroup of the Lorentz group ([[Thm - Law of Velocity Composition]], [[Def - The Lorentz Group]], [[Def - Rapidity]]).
