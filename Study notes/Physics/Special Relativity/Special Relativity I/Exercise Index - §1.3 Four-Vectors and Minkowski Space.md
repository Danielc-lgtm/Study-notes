## §1.3 Four-Vectors and Minkowski Space — Exercises

- [[Ex - The twin paradox]] — compute that the travelling twin ages $2T/\gamma$ against the stay-at-home twin's $2T$, locate the false symmetry (only the traveller accelerates, and acceleration is absolute), recast the result as the reversed triangle inequality $\|U+V\| \ge \|U\|+\|V\|$, and track on a spacetime diagram how the traveller's line of simultaneity sweeps the "missing" segment of the other twin's worldline at the turnaround (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]], [[Def - The Spacetime Interval]], [[Ex - The relativity of simultaneity]]).

- [[Ex - Causal structure and the light cone]] — describe the light cone and show its timelike, spacelike, and null regions are Lorentz invariant, prove timelike-separated events have a frame-independent order while spacelike-separated events do not, and conclude that faster-than-light influence is equivalent to backward-in-time signalling, so every causal worldline is timelike or null (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - The Spacetime Interval]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]], [[Def - The Lorentz Transformation]]).

- [[Ex - The invariant mass of a system of particles]] — show the invariant mass $M^2 = P\cdot P$ of a system's total four-momentum is Lorentz invariant, equals the total energy in the centre-of-momentum frame, and satisfies $M \ge \sum m_i$ by the reversed triangle inequality; apply this to find the invariant mass of two colliding photons and the energy threshold for electron–positron pair production (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Four-Vector]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]]).
