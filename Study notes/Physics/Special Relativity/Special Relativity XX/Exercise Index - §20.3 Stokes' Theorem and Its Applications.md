## §20.3 Stokes' Theorem and Its Applications — Exercises

The exercises of §20.3 drill the master identity $\int_{\mathscr{V}}\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial\mathscr{V}} A$ and the two divergence theorems it implies. The recurring technique is to recognise an integrand as an exterior derivative (or a flux as the integral of a Hodge dual), apply Stokes to trade the region for its boundary, and — for the Gauss–Ostrogradsky corollaries — insert the identity $\mathrm{d}\star\underline{v} = (\nabla\cdot\vec{v})\epsilon$. The conceptual payoff is twofold: first, that the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, the curl theorem, and the divergence theorem are all *one theorem* in different dimensions, with div/grad/curl being the exterior derivative on forms of degree $0,1,2$; second, and most importantly, that the four-dimensional Gauss theorem makes "local conservation $\nabla\cdot J=0$" and "global conservation: total charge slice-independent" the *same statement*, joined by a single equality. This last fact — charge conservation as a vanishing boundary flux — is the rigorous foundation of the conservation laws of the chapters to come, and the orientation bookkeeping of the closed boundary (future cap positive, past cap negative, side tube vanishing at infinity) is the load-bearing detail.

- [[Ex - Stokes' theorem for a three-form gives the four-dimensional Gauss theorem]] (⭐⭐⭐) — derive the 4D Gauss theorem $\Phi_{\partial\mathscr{V}}(\vec{v})=\int_{\mathscr{V}}\nabla\cdot\vec{v}\,\mathrm{d}U$ by applying Stokes to the flux 3-form $\star\underline{v}$ and proving $\mathrm{d}\star\underline{v}=(\nabla\cdot\vec{v})\epsilon$, then mirror it for a 3D region, identifying the metric-dependent step ([[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime]], [[Thm - Gauss-Ostrogradsky Theorem (3D and 4D)]], [[Def - Volume, Area, Length Elements and Flux Integrals]], [[Def - The Hodge Star]]).
- [[Ex - Charge conservation as a flux statement]] (⭐⭐⭐) — show $\nabla\cdot J=0$ is equivalent to vanishing flux through every closed hypersurface, apply the 4D Gauss theorem to a slab between two spacelike slices to prove $Q_{\Sigma_2}=Q_{\Sigma_1}$, and conclude total charge is conserved and slice-independent (Lorentz-invariant), with the local and global laws identical ([[Thm - Gauss-Ostrogradsky Theorem (3D and 4D)]], [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime]], [[Def - Volume, Area, Length Elements and Flux Integrals]]).
- [[Ex - Green and Kelvin-Stokes as cases of the master theorem]] (⭐⭐) — specialise $\int_{\mathscr{V}}\mathrm{d}A=\int_{\partial\mathscr{V}} A$ to recover the fundamental theorem of calculus ($p=1$), the Green–Riemann formula ($p=2$ plane), and the Kelvin–Stokes curl theorem ($p=2$ surface), identifying which component of $\mathrm{d}A$ each reads off and how div/grad/curl are the exterior derivative in disguise ([[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime]], [[Special Relativity XIX/Def - The Exterior Derivative|Def - The Exterior Derivative]], [[Def - Alternate Forms and the Exterior Product]]).
