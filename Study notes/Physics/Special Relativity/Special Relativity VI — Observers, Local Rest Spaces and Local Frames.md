# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole topic: **we set $c = 1$** and use the **mostly-minus** signature $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike vector has $X \cdot X > 0$, a spacelike vector $X \cdot X < 0$, and a null vector $X \cdot X = 0$. The speed of light is restored by $t \to ct$, $v \to v/c$, and dimensional analysis. This chapter draws on Gourgoulhon's Chapter 3, which uses the **opposite** signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ with timelike $\vec u \cdot \vec u = -1$; every scalar product and projector sign has been translated, and the one place this bites hardest — the sign in the orthogonal projector — carries a warning callout on the relevant pages.

- $\mathcal{O}$, $\mathcal{O}'$ — observers; each is a future-directed timelike unit worldline carrying a local frame
- $\mathcal{L}_0$ — the worldline of observer $\mathcal{O}$; $A$, $B$, $M$, $O(t)$ — events
- $U_0$ — the **four-velocity** of observer $\mathcal{O}$, a future-directed unit timelike vector, $U_0 \cdot U_0 = +1$ (Gourgoulhon's $\vec u$); the carried frame's time vector is $e_0 = U_0$
- $A_0$ — the **four-acceleration** of $\mathcal{O}$, $A_0 = dU_0/d\tau$, spacelike-or-zero with $A_0 \cdot U_0 = 0$ (Gourgoulhon's $c\vec a$; with $c=1$, $A_0$ equals his $\vec a$)
- $\tau$ or $t$ — the **proper time** of observer $\mathcal{O}$, read on the clock carried along $\mathcal{L}_0$
- $E_{U_0}$ or $E_{U_0}(A)$ — the **local rest space** of $\mathcal{O}$ at event $A$: the vector subspace $U_0^{\perp}$ of all vectors $g$-orthogonal to $U_0$, a three-dimensional spacelike subspace
- $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(A)$ — the **affine** local rest space (the hyperplane through $A$ orthogonal to $U_0$); $\Sigma_{U_0}(A)$ — the (exact) simultaneity hypersurface, which coincides with $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(A)$ only in the inertial/torsion-free cases
- $\Pi$, $\Pi(X)$ — the **orthogonal projector** onto the local rest space, $\Pi(X) = X - (X \cdot U_0)\,U_0$
- $h$ — the **spatial metric** on the rest space, $h(X,Y) = -\,g(X,Y) = -X\cdot Y$ for $X,Y \in E_{U_0}$; positive definite (Euclidean)
- $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|$ — the spatial length of a spacelike vector, $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = \sqrt{-\,\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}} = \sqrt{h(\overrightarrow{AB},\overrightarrow{AB})}$
- $t_1, t_2$ — proper times of photon emission and reception in the radar (Einstein–Poincaré) construction; date of an event $= \tfrac12(t_1+t_2)$
- $(e_\alpha) = (e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3)$ — the **local frame** (orthonormal tetrad) carried along $\mathcal{L}_0$, with $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$, $e_0 = U_0$, right-handed, differentiable; $(x^1,x^2,x^3)$ — spatial coordinates of an event relative to $\mathcal{O}$
- $\Omega$, $\underline{\Omega}$ — the **four-rotation**: $\Omega$ the endomorphism with $de_\alpha/d\tau = \Omega^\beta{}_\alpha\, e_\beta$, and $\underline{\Omega}(X,Y) = X\cdot\Omega(Y)$ the associated antisymmetric bilinear form
- $\vec{\omega}$ — the **spatial rotation rate** (3-rotation) of the local frame, a spacelike vector with $U_0 \cdot \vec\omega = 0$
- $\times_{U_0}$ — the **rest-space cross product**, $\vec v \times_{U_0} \vec w := \epsilon(U_0, \vec v, \vec w, \cdot)^\sharp$, built from the Levi-Civita tensor $\epsilon$
- $\underline{X}$ — the metric dual (lowered) of a vector $X$, $\underline{X} = g(X,\cdot)$; $X^\flat$ in musical notation
- $D_{\mathcal{O}}$ — the **derivative with respect to observer** $\mathcal{O}$; $D_{U_0}^{\mathrm{FW}}$ — the **Fermi–Walker derivative** along $\mathcal{L}_0$
- $q \otimes p$ — the tensor product of two linear forms, $(q\otimes p)(X,Y) = \langle q, X\rangle\langle p, Y\rangle$
- Summation convention: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed; Greek indices $\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta = 0,1,2,3$, Latin $i,j = 1,2,3$

# Motivation

A four-velocity is attached to a worldline; an *observer* is attached to a worldline together with everything needed to make measurements off it. That is the entire content of this chapter in one sentence. Up to now the only physical quantity we could speak of was proper time along a single worldline, read on a clock the particle carries. But a real observer wants to date events that do **not** lie on their worldline — to say "that supernova happened at noon my time" — and to measure the spatial distance to things, and to set up a coordinate grid. None of this is possible with proper time alone. The new ingredients are a definition of *simultaneity*, a *rest space* of directions the observer calls "purely spatial", and a *frame* of orthonormal axes carried along the worldline. The whole chapter is the careful construction of these three things, and the discovery of what is local about them.

The trouble starts immediately, and it is the same trouble that killed Newtonian absolute time. To date an event $M$ off your worldline you must decide which event on your worldline is *simultaneous* with $M$, and Minkowski spacetime supplies no canonical answer — the only structures it carries are the light cones. Einstein and Poincaré gave the operational fix: bounce a photon off $M$, and call $M$ simultaneous with the midpoint of the emission and reception times,
$$
t \;=\; \tfrac12\,(t_1 + t_2).
$$
This single formula is the backbone of the chapter. Worked through, it says that $M$ is simultaneous with the event $A$ on your worldline precisely when the displacement $\overrightarrow{AM}$ is **orthogonal** to your four-velocity — so simultaneity *is* metric orthogonality to $U_0$, and the set of simultaneous directions is the orthogonal complement $U_0^\perp$, the **local rest space**. Two observers with different four-velocities have different orthogonal complements, hence different notions of "now", hence no absolute time: that is the first theorem.

Once you have a rest space, you can measure. Because all rest-space vectors are spacelike, the metric restricted to the rest space is (after a sign flip) positive definite, and the rest space is an ordinary Euclidean three-space — Pythagoras, angles, and distances all behave normally. Better still, the radar construction lets you read off the *spatial distance* to a nearby object purely from photon round-trip times, with no ruler at all: Synge's formula $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$ makes length a derived notion and time the primary one. The same idea, applied to a pair of worldlines, gives Born's rigidity criterion (constant round-trip time = rigid ruler) and the sobering fact that there is no satisfactory notion of a rigid solid in relativity.

The deepest part of the chapter is what happens when you ask how the observer's frame *changes* along the worldline. The three spatial axes $e_1, e_2, e_3$ and the time axis $e_0 = U_0$ form an orthonormal tetrad, and their proper-time derivative is governed by a single antisymmetric object, the **four-rotation** $\underline\Omega$. The decisive structural result — and a beautiful piece of linear algebra in its own right — is that any antisymmetric bilinear form on Minkowski space splits canonically, relative to a chosen timelike direction, into an "electric" part and a "magnetic" part, both orthogonal to that direction:
$$
\underline\Omega \;=\; \underbrace{\text{(four-acceleration piece)}}_{\text{Fermi–Walker}} \;+\; \underbrace{\text{(spatial rotation piece)}}_{\vec\omega\,\times_{U_0}}.
$$
This is exactly the decomposition that, applied later to the electromagnetic field tensor, splits $F$ into $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. Applied here it says the frame's evolution is the sum of an unavoidable tilt forced by the four-acceleration (present even for a non-spinning observer) and a genuine spatial rotation measured by the vector $\vec\omega$. Stripping out the rotation gives the **Fermi–Walker derivative**, the relativistic notion of "non-rotating transport" — the law a gyroscope obeys. An observer is *inertial* exactly when both pieces vanish.

The reader is assumed to be fluent with the material of [[Special Relativity V — Worldlines, Proper Time and Four-Velocity|Special Relativity V]] — worldlines, [[Def - Proper Time|proper time]], the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity and four-acceleration]] — and with the metric structure of [[Special Relativity III — Minkowski Spacetime and the Metric|Special Relativity III]]: the indefinite [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric|Minkowski metric]], the [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors|timelike/spacelike/null classification]], [[Def - Metric Duality and Index Manipulation|raising and lowering indices]], and the [[Def - Spacetime Orientation|Levi-Civita tensor]]. The genuinely new mathematics is the orthogonal decomposition of antisymmetric bilinear forms; everything else is the disciplined application of "orthogonal to $U_0$" as the meaning of "spatial for this observer".

# Concept Map

## §6.1 Simultaneity and the Local Rest Space

- **[[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity]]**
	- An observer $\mathcal{O}$ dates an off-worldline event $M$ by bouncing a photon off it: if the photon is emitted at proper time $t_1$ and the reflection returns at $t_2$, then $M$ is declared **simultaneous** with the event of proper time $t = \tfrac12(t_1+t_2)$, and $t$ is the **date** of $M$ for $\mathcal{O}$. This is the radar (or "$\varepsilon = \tfrac12$") convention; Reichenbach's $t = (1-\varepsilon)t_1 + \varepsilon t_2$ with $\varepsilon \ne \tfrac12$ is logically consistent but uglier and breaks the metric connection. Worked through the null conditions on the two photon legs, the criterion becomes the clean statement $U_0 \cdot \overrightarrow{AM} = 0$: simultaneity is orthogonality to the four-velocity.

- **[[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space]]**
	- An **observer** is a future-directed timelike unit worldline $\mathcal{L}_0$ carrying a clock (later, a whole orthonormal frame); its four-velocity is $U_0$ with $U_0\cdot U_0 = +1$. The **local rest space** at an event $A$ is the set of displacements simultaneous with $A$ — by the simultaneity criterion, the orthogonal complement $E_{U_0} = U_0^\perp = \{X : X\cdot U_0 = 0\}$, a three-dimensional spacelike vector subspace; the affine version $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(A)$ is the hyperplane through $A$ orthogonal to $U_0$. It is the observer's instantaneous "space", the directions they call purely spatial; the qualifier *local* warns that it captures simultaneity only for nearby events unless the observer is inertial.

- **[[Thm - Nonexistence of Absolute Time]]**
	- Two observers $\mathcal{O}$ and $\mathcal{O}'$ with different four-velocities $U_0 \ne U_0'$ have different local rest spaces ($U_0^\perp \ne U_0'^\perp$), hence assign different dates to the same events: there is no observer-independent time function on Minkowski spacetime, only times defined relative to observers. Two events simultaneous for one observer are generically not simultaneous for another. This is the relativity of simultaneity restated frame-independently as a statement about orthogonal complements, and it is the precise sense in which Newtonian absolute time fails.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The 3+1 Split and the ADM Formalism *(from General Relativity)*
> Slicing spacetime into an observer's successive local rest spaces is the flat, infinitesimal seed of the **3+1 decomposition** of general relativity: a foliation of a curved spacetime by spacelike hypersurfaces, each carrying an induced Riemannian metric, with the normal congruence playing the role of $U_0$. The projector $\Pi$ becomes the projector onto the slice, and the machinery of lapse, shift, and extrinsic curvature is built on exactly this orthogonal split. It is the foundation of the **Hamiltonian (ADM) formulation** of general relativity and of numerical relativity.

- **[[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space]]**
	- Every vector splits uniquely into a spatial part in the rest space and a part along $U_0$: $X = \Pi(X) + (X\cdot U_0)\,U_0$, where $\Pi(X) = X - (X\cdot U_0)\,U_0$ is the **orthogonal projector** onto $E_{U_0}$. In mostly-minus signature the sign is $-$ (because $U_0\cdot U_0 = +1$), matching the familiar Euclidean projector $\mathrm{Id} - \langle u,\cdot\rangle u$ — and opposite to Gourgoulhon's $+$, which comes from his mostly-plus $\vec u\cdot\vec u = -1$. It satisfies $\Pi(U_0) = 0$, $\Pi|_{E_{U_0}} = \mathrm{Id}$, and idempotence $\Pi\circ\Pi = \Pi$; in components $\Pi^\mu{}_\nu = \delta^\mu{}_\nu - U_0^\mu\,(U_0)_\nu$.

- **[[Thm - Euclidean Character of the Local Rest Space]]**
	- All vectors of the local rest space are spacelike, so the metric restricted to $E_{U_0}$ is *negative* definite in mostly-minus signature; flipping the sign gives a positive-definite **spatial metric** $h = -\,g|_{E_{U_0}}$, under which $(E_{U_0}, h)$ is a genuine three-dimensional Euclidean space. Distances $d(M,N) = \|\overrightarrow{MN}\| = \sqrt{h(\overrightarrow{MN},\overrightarrow{MN})}$, angles $\cos\theta = h(\vec v,\vec w)/(\|\vec v\|\|\vec w\|)$, and Pythagoras all hold exactly as in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The slogan: in the local rest space, all of vector calculus is identical to ordinary three-dimensional Euclidean calculus.

- **[[Ex - Einstein synchronisation and the radar date]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Set up the two null conditions for the radar photon, solve the resulting linear system for $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$, and recover both the simultaneity criterion $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=0 \Leftrightarrow t = \tfrac12(t_1+t_2)$ and the scalar-square formula that feeds Synge.

- **[[Ex - The orthogonal projector is idempotent and symmetric]]** (⭐)
	- Verify directly from $\Pi(X) = X - (X\cdot U_0)U_0$ that $\Pi(U_0)=0$, that $\Pi$ fixes every rest-space vector, that $\Pi^2 = \Pi$, and that $\Pi$ is self-adjoint with respect to the metric, $g(\Pi X, Y) = g(X, \Pi Y)$ — the algebraic fingerprints of an orthogonal projector.

- **[[Ex - Two observers disagree on simultaneity]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Take a boosted observer $\mathcal{O}'$ and show that the hyperplane $U_0'^\perp$ is tilted relative to $U_0^\perp$, exhibit two events simultaneous for $\mathcal{O}$ but not for $\mathcal{O}'$, and connect the tilt angle to the relative rapidity — the frame-independent face of the relativity of simultaneity.

## §6.2 Measuring Distances and the Local Frame

- **[[Def - Synge World Function and Spatial Distance]]**
	- The radar construction measures spatial distance without a ruler: for a spacelike displacement $\overrightarrow{AB}$ from a worldline event $A$ to a nearby event $B$, with photon emission at $t_1$ and reception at $t_2$ ($A$ the midpoint event, proper time $t$), $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$ — **Synge's formula**. Length is thereby a *derived* notion and time the primary one, in exact agreement with the 1983 metre definition. Synge's formula is the Minkowskian analogue of the Euclidean power of a point with respect to a circle, with the circle's radius set to zero because the chords $BA_1, BA_2$ are null. (The world function $\Omega$ generalises $\tfrac12\,\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$ to curved spacetime.)

- **[[Def - Born Rigidity Criterion]]**
	- An infinitesimal ruler is a pair of neighbouring worldlines $(\mathcal{L}_0, \mathcal{L}_1)$; its length is the spatial distance $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|$ between simultaneous events on them, with $\overrightarrow{AB}$ orthogonal to $U_0$. The ruler is **rigid** (Born's criterion) when this length is constant — equivalently, when the photon round-trip time between the two ends, $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = \tfrac12 c\,(t_2 - t_1)$, stays constant. This is a chronometric, ruler-free rigidity test. The three-dimensional generalisation is so restrictive (Noether–Herglotz) that *there is no satisfactory notion of a rigid solid in relativity*.

- **[[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]]**
	- A **local frame** along $\mathcal{L}_0$ is an orthonormal tetrad $(e_\alpha(t)) = (e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3)$ defined at each event, with $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$, right-handed, differentiable, and time vector $e_0 = U_0$; the three spatial vectors $e_i$ span the local rest space. An **observer** is formally a worldline together with such a frame, and it furnishes coordinates $(t, x^i)$ for nearby events via $\overrightarrow{O(t)M} = x^i e_i(t)$, together with a fixed Euclidean **reference space** $R_{\mathcal{O}}$. The **four-rotation** of the frame is the antisymmetric object $\underline\Omega$ controlling $de_\alpha/d\tau = \Omega^\beta{}_\alpha e_\beta$; this page also carries the §6.3 decomposition of $\underline\Omega$ into four-acceleration and spatial rotation.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Fermi Normal Coordinates and Tidal Forces *(from General Relativity)*
> The observer's frame coordinates $(t, x^i)$ built from a Fermi–Walker-transported tetrad are the flat-spacetime case of **Fermi normal coordinates** in general relativity — the natural coordinates of a freely-falling, non-rotating laboratory. In curved spacetime the metric in these coordinates departs from $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ at second order, and the departure is precisely the **Riemann tidal tensor**: the coordinates expose gravity as the relative acceleration of neighbouring geodesics. The locality bound $r \ll a^{-1}$ of this chapter is the flat-space precursor of the tidal radius over which a local laboratory frame is trustworthy.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Optical Metric and Gravitational Lensing *(from General Relativity)*
> Synge's "distance from light travel time" is the germ of the **optical (Fermat) metric**: in a static gravitational field, the spatial paths of light rays are geodesics of a conformally rescaled spatial metric, and gravitational lensing is computed by doing Euclidean-style geometry in that optical metric. The radar definition of distance is the operational core, and Synge's world function $\Omega$ is the tool that makes "the squared interval between two events" a usable scalar field on a curved spacetime.

- **[[Ex - Synge's formula and the power of a point]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Derive $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$ from the scalar-square output of the radar system, then prove the Euclidean power-of-a-point identity $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|^2 = \overrightarrow{AA_1}\cdot\overrightarrow{AA_2}$ and show the Minkowskian version is the zero-radius limit because $B$ is null-separated from both photon events.

- **[[Ex - A Born-rigid congruence and the constant round-trip time]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- For a uniformly accelerated family of observers (a Rindler congruence), show that the proper distance between neighbours is constant in time and that the photon round-trip time between two ends is constant, so the congruence is Born-rigid; contrast with a uniformly rotating disk, where the Born criterion cannot be met globally (Ehrenfest).

- **[[Ex - Frame coordinates and the slicing by rest spaces]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Build the observer coordinates $(t, x^i)$ from a local frame, show the level sets of $t$ are the local rest spaces, and verify that for an accelerated observer neighbouring rest spaces intersect at proper distance $a^{-1}$, so the coordinates are single-valued only for $r \ll a^{-1}$.

## §6.3 Four-Rotation and Fermi–Walker Transport

- **[[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms]]**
	- Given a unit timelike vector $U_0$, every antisymmetric bilinear form $A$ on Minkowski space splits **uniquely** as $A = q\otimes\underline{U_0} - \underline{U_0}\otimes q + \epsilon(U_0, \vec b, \cdot, \cdot)$ with a linear form $q$ and a vector $\vec b$ both orthogonal to $U_0$ ($\langle q, U_0\rangle = 0$, $U_0\cdot\vec b = 0$). The form $q = A(\cdot, U_0)$ is the **electric part**, the vector $\vec b$ the **magnetic part**; the degree-of-freedom count is $3+3 = 6$, the entries of an antisymmetric $4\times4$ matrix. Restricted to the rest space, $A$ acts as the mixed (scalar triple) product with $\vec b$. This is the same decomposition that splits the electromagnetic field tensor into $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.

- **[[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation#The four-rotation and its decomposition|The four-rotation decomposed]]**
	- Applying the decomposition to the four-rotation $\underline\Omega$ (electric part $q = c\,\underline{A_0}$ fixed by $\Omega(U_0) = c A_0$, magnetic part $\vec b = -\vec\omega$) splits the frame's evolution into two physically distinct pieces: $de_\alpha/d\tau = \underbrace{c[(U_0\cdot e_\alpha)A_0 - (A_0\cdot e_\alpha)U_0]}_{\text{Fermi–Walker (four-acceleration)}} + \underbrace{\vec\omega\times_{U_0} e_\alpha}_{\text{spatial rotation}}$. The Fermi–Walker part tilts the time axis $e_0 = U_0$ by the four-acceleration even with no spinning; the spatial rotation part rotates the three axes at angular velocity $\vec\omega$ (the **four-rotation** vector, $U_0\cdot\vec\omega = 0$). An observer is **inertial** iff $A_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega = 0$.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Four-Rotation as a Lie-Algebra Element *(from the Lie Theory of the Lorentz Group)*
> The four-rotation $\underline\Omega$ is an antisymmetric bilinear form on Minkowski space, which is exactly an element of the **Lie algebra** $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$ of the [[Def - The Lorentz Group|Lorentz group]] — see [[Special Relativity X — The Lorentz Group as a Lie Group]]. The orthogonal decomposition into "electric" and "magnetic" parts is, in that language, the split of $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$ into boost generators (the four-acceleration piece) and rotation generators (the spatial rotation piece) relative to a chosen time direction. The evolution $de_\alpha/d\tau = \Omega^\beta{}_\alpha e_\beta$ is the statement that the frame is dragged along the worldline by a one-parameter family of infinitesimal Lorentz transformations, and Thomas precession is what survives when the boost part integrates up.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Spin Transport and the BMT Equation *(from Relativistic Spin Dynamics)*
> Fermi–Walker transport is the law a **gyroscope** obeys: a spin vector $S$ orthogonal to $U_0$, subject only to the worldline's acceleration and no torque, satisfies $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0} S = 0$. This is the kinematic skeleton of the **Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi (BMT) equation** for the precession of a particle's spin in an electromagnetic field, and the residual rotation it predicts for an accelerated, non-rotating frame is **Thomas precession** — the $\tfrac12$ that fixed the spin-orbit coupling in atomic fine structure. See [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers]].

- **[[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]]**
	- The **Fermi–Walker derivative** of a vector field $V$ along $\mathcal{L}_0$ is $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = dV/d\tau - c[(U_0\cdot V)A_0 - (A_0\cdot V)U_0]$ — the absolute derivative with the unavoidable four-acceleration tilt subtracted off. It depends only on the worldline, not on any spinning of the frame; $V$ is **Fermi–Walker transported** when $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = 0$, the relativistic notion of *non-rotating transport*. For a rest-space vector it equals the orthogonal projection of the absolute derivative, $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = \Pi(dV/d\tau)$, and the full **derivative with respect to the observer** adds back the rotation: $D_{\mathcal{O}}V = D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V - \vec\omega\times_{U_0}V$.

- **[[Ex - Fermi-Walker versus the absolute derivative]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show that the absolute derivative $dV/d\tau$ of a rest-space vector generally leaves the rest space, that the Fermi–Walker derivative does not (it is the orthogonal projection of $dV/d\tau$), and that $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}U_0 = 0$ identically — the precise senses in which Fermi–Walker transport is the "non-rotating" derivative.

- **[[Ex - The four-rotation is antisymmetric and inertial means non-rotating]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Differentiate $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$ to prove the four-rotation bilinear form is antisymmetric, read off the component relations $\Omega_{(0)(0)}=0$, $\Omega^i{}_0 = \Omega^0{}_i$, $\Omega^i{}_j = -\Omega^j{}_i$, and deduce that the frame is constant ($de_\alpha/d\tau = 0$) iff $A_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega = 0$ — the characterisation of an inertial observer.

- **[[Ex - Fermi-Walker transport of a gyroscope and Thomas precession]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- For an observer in uniform circular motion, integrate the Fermi–Walker transport law for a spin vector around one orbit and show it returns rotated relative to the lab frame by the Thomas precession angle, exhibiting the four-acceleration's "magnetic"-free part as a genuine, accumulating rotation.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The problems of this chapter chase a recognisable handful of goals. The most basic is to **decide simultaneity or compute a date**: given an observer and an off-worldline event, find which worldline event is simultaneous with it, or the radar date $\tfrac12(t_1+t_2)$. This is always settled by the orthogonality criterion $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AM} = 0$, and the skill is in setting up the null conditions on the photon legs. A second target is to **measure a spatial length or check rigidity**: produce $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|$ from round-trip times via Synge's formula, or verify the Born criterion by showing a round-trip time is constant. A third, more structural, is to **establish a projection or decomposition identity** — that $\Pi$ is idempotent and symmetric, that the rest space is Euclidean, that an antisymmetric form splits into electric and magnetic parts — where the work is pure linear algebra against the indefinite metric. A fourth is to **characterise the motion of a frame**: compute the four-acceleration and four-rotation of a given congruence (Rindler, rotating disk), and classify the observer as inertial, accelerated, or rotating. A fifth is to **transport a vector correctly**: distinguish the absolute derivative, the Fermi–Walker derivative, and the derivative with respect to the observer, and compute what a gyroscope does. Across all five the unifying remark is that you understand an observer's measurement when you know how to project onto the rest space and how the rest space tilts and turns along the worldline.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A four-velocity $U_0$ is given** (a unit future timelike vector), which immediately supplies the rest space $U_0^\perp$, the projector $\Pi$, and the spatial metric $h = -g|_{U_0^\perp}$; this is the richest source, because every "spatial for this observer" quantity is got by projecting. **A radar measurement is specified** — emission and reception proper times $t_1, t_2$ — which, through the null conditions on the photon legs, hands you the simultaneity criterion, the date, and (via Synge) the distance. **An orthonormal tetrad is carried along the worldline**, which is what turns abstract rest spaces into actual coordinates and makes $de_\alpha/d\tau$ a meaningful, computable object. **An antisymmetric bilinear form is presented together with a timelike direction** — the four-rotation, or later the electromagnetic field — which triggers the orthogonal decomposition into electric and magnetic parts, each living in the rest space. **A congruence of worldlines is given** (Rindler, rotating), from which one extracts the four-acceleration $A_0 = dU_0/d\tau$ and the four-rotation $\vec\omega$, and reads off rigidity and the inertial/accelerated/rotating classification. The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a given four-velocity routes through the projector to a spatial quantity; a radar measurement routes through Synge to a distance; a tetrad routes through $de_\alpha/d\tau$ and its decomposition to a four-acceleration and a four-rotation; an antisymmetric form plus a timelike direction routes through the decomposition theorem to an electric and a magnetic part. The [[Special Relativity VI — Observers, Local Rest Spaces and Local Frames#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

These are the moves almost every problem in the chapter is assembled from. The reader's stated difficulty is the physics, not the algebra, so each operation states explicitly which object lives in the rest space and which along $U_0$ — that bookkeeping is where the errors live. When stuck, scan the list and try each one.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Project onto the local rest space.** Given the observer's four-velocity $U_0$ and any vector $X$, split it as $X = \Pi(X) + (X\cdot U_0)U_0$ with the [[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space|orthogonal projector]] $\Pi(X) = X - (X\cdot U_0)U_0$. *Trigger:* you want the "spatial part of $X$ for this observer", or you must enforce that something lies in the rest space. *Pattern:* "project with $\Pi$; the part along $U_0$ is $(X\cdot U_0)U_0$, the spatial part is the rest." The component along $U_0$ is the "time component for $\mathcal{O}$"; the projected part is the "space component".

2. **Translate simultaneity into orthogonality.** Replace "$M$ is simultaneous with $A$ for $\mathcal{O}$" by the metric equation $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AM} = 0$; see [[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity]]. *Trigger:* any sentence containing "at the same time", "now", or "simultaneous". *Pattern:* "simultaneous $\Rightarrow$ orthogonal to $U_0$ $\Rightarrow$ lies in the local rest space." This is the chapter's master substitution and the engine of every dating and length computation.

3. **Set up the null conditions on radar photons.** For a photon emitted at $A_1$ (proper time $t_1$), reflected at $B$, received at $A_2$ (proper time $t_2$), write $\overrightarrow{A_1B}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_1B} = 0$ and $\overrightarrow{A_2B}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_2B} = 0$, expand via Chasles ($\overrightarrow{A_1B} = \overrightarrow{A_1A} + \overrightarrow{AB}$), and solve the resulting linear system for $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$. *Trigger:* a problem mentions light signals, radar, or photon round trips. *Pattern:* "two null legs $\Rightarrow$ two quadratic equations $\Rightarrow$ a $2\times2$ linear system for the two scalar products."

4. **Compute a spatial distance from round-trip times (Synge).** For a spacelike $\overrightarrow{AB}$ in the rest space, use $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$, reducing to $\tfrac12 c(t_2-t_1)$ when $A$ and $B$ are simultaneous; see [[Def - Synge World Function and Spatial Distance]]. *Trigger:* you must measure a length and the data are clock readings, not rulers. *Pattern:* "length $=$ geometric-mean of the two one-way times, $\times c$."

5. **Use the spatial metric $h = -g|_{E_{U_0}}$ for lengths and angles.** Inside the rest space, compute with the positive-definite $h(X,Y) = -X\cdot Y$: norms $\|X\| = \sqrt{-X\cdot X}$, angles $\cos\theta = -X\cdot Y/(\|X\|\|Y\|)$, and Pythagoras; see [[Thm - Euclidean Character of the Local Rest Space]]. *Trigger:* any Euclidean-geometry computation among rest-space vectors. *Pattern:* "drop the minus signs by flipping to $h$; then it is ordinary $\mathbb{R}^3$."

6. **Decompose an antisymmetric form into electric and magnetic parts.** Given an antisymmetric bilinear form $A$ and the timelike $U_0$, write $A = q\otimes\underline{U_0} - \underline{U_0}\otimes q + \epsilon(U_0,\vec b,\cdot,\cdot)$ with $q = A(\cdot, U_0)$ and $\vec b$ read off from the rest-space block; see [[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms]]. *Trigger:* a four-rotation, an electromagnetic field, or any antisymmetric $2$-tensor presented with a preferred observer. *Pattern:* "contract once with $U_0$ to get the electric form $q$; the leftover rest-space block is the magnetic vector $\vec b$ via the cross product."

7. **Differentiate an orthonormality identity to get an antisymmetry.** From $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$ (constant), $\tfrac{d}{d\tau}$ gives $\dot e_\alpha\cdot e_\beta + e_\alpha\cdot\dot e_\beta = 0$, i.e. the four-rotation form $\underline\Omega$ is antisymmetric; similarly from $U_0\cdot U_0 = 1$ get $A_0\cdot U_0 = 0$. *Trigger:* you need a constraint on the derivative of a frame or a unit vector. *Pattern:* "the derivative of a constant inner product is zero $\Rightarrow$ an orthogonality/antisymmetry relation for free."

8. **Project the absolute derivative to get the Fermi–Walker derivative.** For a vector $V$ in the rest space, $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = \Pi(dV/d\tau)$; in general $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = dV/d\tau - c[(U_0\cdot V)A_0 - (A_0\cdot V)U_0]$ and $D_{\mathcal{O}}V = D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V - \vec\omega\times_{U_0}V$; see [[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]]. *Trigger:* you want a derivative that keeps rest-space vectors in the rest space, or you want to transport a gyroscope. *Pattern:* "take $dV/d\tau$, then project / subtract the acceleration tilt / subtract the rotation."

9. **Read the four-acceleration and four-rotation off the frame evolution.** From $de_\alpha/d\tau$, identify the Fermi–Walker part (proportional to $A_0$, via $\Omega(U_0) = cA_0$) and the spatial-rotation part ($\vec\omega\times_{U_0}$); classify $\mathcal{O}$ as inertial ($A_0 = 0, \vec\omega = 0$), accelerated ($A_0\ne 0$), or rotating ($\vec\omega\ne 0$). *Trigger:* a congruence or a moving frame is given and you must characterise its motion. *Pattern:* "split $\dot e_\alpha$ into a boost piece and a rotation piece; the two coefficients are $A_0$ and $\vec\omega$."

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Carrying over the Euclidean projector sign $\mathrm{Id} - \langle u,\cdot\rangle u$ blindly — or the Gourgoulhon sign $\mathrm{Id} + \langle u,\cdot\rangle u$
> The projector onto a hyperplane orthogonal to a unit vector $u$ is $\Pi = \mathrm{Id} - \langle u,\cdot\rangle u$ **only when $u\cdot u = +1$**. In Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^3$ that always holds, so the sign is always $-$. In Minkowski space the sign depends on the signature and on the type of $u$: for the *timelike* $U_0$ with $U_0\cdot U_0 = +1$ in mostly-minus signature, the correct projector onto the rest space is $\Pi(X) = X - (X\cdot U_0)U_0$ (sign $-$, matching Euclidean) — but Gourgoulhon, working in mostly-plus where $\vec u\cdot\vec u = -1$, writes $\perp_u = \mathrm{Id} + \langle\vec u,\cdot\rangle\vec u$ (sign $+$). Copying his $+$ into our convention gives a map with $\Pi(U_0) = 2U_0 \ne 0$, which is not a projector at all. The repair is to track $U_0\cdot U_0$: the coefficient is always $-1/(U_0\cdot U_0)$ times $(X\cdot U_0)U_0$, which is $-$ here and $+$ in Gourgoulhon. ([[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space]].)

> [!warning] 2. Treating the local rest space as if it were a global "space" with an absolute time
> It is tempting to think the slicing of spacetime by an observer's rest spaces defines a universal time, so that "the rest space at time $t$" is the same surface for everyone. It is not: a different observer $\mathcal{O}'$ has a *different* orthogonal complement, and the two foliations are genuinely different ([[Thm - Nonexistence of Absolute Time]]). Concretely, two events on one observer's rest space are generically *not* simultaneous for a boosted observer — the relativity of simultaneity. Worse, even for a *single* accelerated observer the rest spaces are only *local*: neighbouring rest spaces $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(t)$ and $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(t+dt)$ intersect at proper distance $a^{-1} = \|A_0\|^{-1}$ from the worldline, so the frame coordinates are single-valued only for $r \ll a^{-1}$. The repair: never speak of "the time of an event" without naming the observer, and for an accelerated observer never use the frame beyond the locality radius.

> [!warning] 3. Using the absolute derivative $dV/d\tau$ as the "rate of change for the observer"
> The naive rate of change of a vector field along the worldline is the absolute derivative $dV/d\tau$, and it is tempting to call this "how $V$ changes for $\mathcal{O}$". But $dV/d\tau$ does **not** preserve the rest space: if $V$ lies in $E_{U_0}$, then $U_0\cdot(dV/d\tau) = -A_0\cdot V$, which is generically nonzero, so the absolute derivative of a purely spatial vector acquires a spurious time component. A concrete counterexample: a gyroscope spin $S$ orthogonal to $U_0$ on an accelerated worldline has $dS/d\tau$ tilting out of the rest space even when nothing physically rotates it. The repair is to use the [[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative|Fermi–Walker derivative]] (which projects the tilt away, $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = \Pi(dV/d\tau)$ on the rest space) or the derivative with respect to the observer (which additionally removes the frame's spin). Only these keep "spatial for $\mathcal{O}$" stable along the worldline.

> [!warning] 4. Assuming an extended body can be rigid
> Newtonian intuition says a solid body has a fixed shape, and it is tempting to carry a rigid lattice of observers around. The one-dimensional Born criterion (constant photon round-trip time between two ends) is consistent, but its three-dimensional generalisation — every pair of neighbouring points a rigid ruler — is *overdetermined*: the Noether–Herglotz theorem shows the only Born-rigid motions of an extended body are a six-parameter family (essentially the isometric / uniformly rotating ones), far too few to describe a real accelerating, deforming solid. A rotating disk cannot even be set spinning rigidly (the Ehrenfest paradox). The repair: there is no Lorentz-invariant notion of a rigid solid; speak only of *infinitesimal* rigid rulers (pairs of worldlines) or of the restricted Born-rigid congruences, and treat extended bodies with an honest stress–energy description, not a rigidity constraint. ([[Def - Born Rigidity Criterion]].)

> [!warning] 5. Forgetting that the four-rotation $\vec\omega$ and four-acceleration $A_0$ are independent — and that the time axis tilts even without rotation
> Because the three spatial axes $e_1, e_2, e_3$ rotate at rate $\vec\omega$, it is tempting to think a "non-rotating" observer ($\vec\omega = 0$) has a *constant* frame. It does not: the time axis $e_0 = U_0$ still tilts, because $de_0/d\tau = cA_0$ is forced by any four-acceleration, independently of $\vec\omega$. The frame is constant ($de_\alpha/d\tau = 0$ for all $\alpha$) only when *both* $A_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega = 0$, i.e. for an inertial observer ([[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms]], applied to the four-rotation). The trap is to conflate "non-rotating" (Fermi–Walker, $\vec\omega = 0$) with "inertial" (also $A_0 = 0$): a Fermi–Walker-transported gyroscope on an accelerated worldline is non-rotating yet its frame still evolves, and the residual is exactly Thomas precession. The repair: keep the two pieces of $\underline\Omega$ separate, and remember the Fermi–Walker part is the *unavoidable* tilt that even a perfect gyroscope cannot escape.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won the instant you decide which vectors are "spatial for the observer" and which point along the worldline, and then keep that split clean through every derivative. The observer's four-velocity $U_0$ is the hinge: it defines the rest space $U_0^\perp$, the projector $\Pi$, and the spatial metric $h = -g|_{U_0^\perp}$, and almost every computation is "project, then do Euclidean geometry in the rest space".

If the problem **asks about simultaneity or a date**, the decisive move is to translate the words into the orthogonality equation $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AM} = 0$ ([[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity]]). If radar data are given, set up the two null conditions on the photon legs, $\overrightarrow{A_1B}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_1B} = 0$ and $\overrightarrow{A_2B}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_2B} = 0$, expand each with Chasles into a quadratic in $\overrightarrow{AB}$, and subtract to get the linear system whose solution is $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$ (which vanishes exactly when $t = \tfrac12(t_1+t_2)$) and $\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}$ (which feeds Synge). This single computation underlies dating, distance, and rigidity. The reason it works is that two null legs give two equations for exactly the two unknown scalar products, and the determinant is nonzero whenever $B$ is off the worldline.

If the problem **asks for a spatial length or a rigidity check**, route through Synge's formula $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$ ([[Def - Synge World Function and Spatial Distance]]), which expresses distance purely in clock readings. For a length between simultaneous events this collapses to $\tfrac12 c(t_2-t_1)$, and Born's criterion ([[Def - Born Rigidity Criterion]]) is then simply "this round-trip time is constant in proper time". The conceptual payoff to keep in mind is that time is primary and length is derived — there is no ruler in relativity, only clocks and light.

If the problem **is a linear-algebra identity** — that $\Pi$ is idempotent and symmetric, that the rest space is Euclidean, that an antisymmetric form decomposes — work entirely with the metric and the projector. The recurring trick is to differentiate a *constant* inner product: $U_0\cdot U_0 = 1$ gives $A_0\cdot U_0 = 0$, and $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$ gives the antisymmetry of the four-rotation ([[Ex - The four-rotation is antisymmetric and inertial means non-rotating]]). For the decomposition of an antisymmetric form, contract once with $U_0$ to extract the electric form $q = A(\cdot, U_0)$, subtract the corresponding $q\otimes\underline{U_0} - \underline{U_0}\otimes q$ piece, and the remainder lives on the rest space where it is the mixed product with the magnetic vector $\vec b$ ([[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms]]).

If the problem **concerns the motion of a frame or the transport of a vector**, first compute $de_\alpha/d\tau$ (or $dV/d\tau$), then split it into the Fermi–Walker (four-acceleration) part and the spatial-rotation part. The four-acceleration is read off from $de_0/d\tau = cA_0$; the four-rotation $\vec\omega$ is the magnetic part of $\underline\Omega$. To transport a gyroscope, use the [[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative|Fermi–Walker derivative]] and set it to zero; the residual rotation after integrating around a closed orbit is Thomas precession ([[Ex - Fermi-Walker transport of a gyroscope and Thomas precession]]). Always check the consistency conditions $A_0\cdot U_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega\cdot U_0 = 0$ — a four-acceleration or four-rotation with a nonzero time component is an arithmetic error.

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **everything is "project onto $U_0^\perp$, then watch how $U_0^\perp$ moves".** The rest space is where the observer's physics happens; the four-acceleration tilts it and the four-rotation spins it, and the entire chapter is the bookkeeping of those two motions. The single question every problem secretly asks is: *for this observer, what is spatial, and how does "spatial" change along the worldline?*

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space|The orthogonal projector]]** $\Pi(X) = X - (X\cdot U_0)U_0$: the workhorse of the whole chapter. **Typical use:** whenever you need "the spatial part of $X$ for observer $\mathcal{O}$", apply $\Pi$; the leftover $(X\cdot U_0)U_0$ is the time part. It is idempotent ($\Pi^2 = \Pi$), self-adjoint, kills $U_0$, and fixes the rest space, so it cleanly splits every vector and every tensor index into a time-for-$\mathcal{O}$ piece and a space-for-$\mathcal{O}$ piece. Recognise its applicability the instant an observer is named and a vector must be resolved into temporal and spatial parts — relative velocity, relative acceleration, electric and magnetic fields are all built by projecting with $\Pi$ in later chapters.

- **The simultaneity-is-orthogonality criterion** $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AM} = 0$ ([[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity]]): the chapter's master translation. **Typical use:** convert any verbal statement about "now", "at the same time", or "simultaneous" into a metric equation, and conversely read off that the simultaneous events are exactly the rest space. Its reach is enormous because it turns a slippery temporal notion into a one-line algebraic condition; every dating computation, the relativity of simultaneity, and the very definition of the rest space all flow from it. Reach for it whenever simultaneity appears, in any disguise.

- **[[Def - Synge World Function and Spatial Distance|Synge's formula]]** $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = c\sqrt{(t-t_1)(t_2-t)}$: distance from clock readings alone. **Typical use:** measure a spatial length when you have photon round-trip times but no ruler, and check Born rigidity by demanding the time be constant. The reusable content is the principle that length is derived from time in relativity, and the geometric-mean structure (a power-of-a-point with zero radius) is a memorable, robust form. Recognise it whenever a "distance" must be extracted from light signals.

- **[[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms|The electric–magnetic decomposition]]** $A = q\otimes\underline{U_0} - \underline{U_0}\otimes q + \epsilon(U_0,\vec b,\cdot,\cdot)$: the most transferable single theorem here. **Typical use:** any antisymmetric $2$-tensor presented with a preferred timelike direction splits into a rest-space "electric" form and a rest-space "magnetic" vector — applied to the four-rotation it gives the four-acceleration and the spatial rotation; applied to the electromagnetic field tensor it gives $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. The reusable skill is "contract once with $U_0$ for the electric part, read the rest-space block for the magnetic part". It is the bridge from observer kinematics to electromagnetism and to the Lie algebra of the Lorentz group, and it should be reached for whenever antisymmetry meets a chosen observer.

- **[[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative|The Fermi–Walker derivative]]** $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_{U_0}V = dV/d\tau - c[(U_0\cdot V)A_0 - (A_0\cdot V)U_0]$: the relativistic "non-rotating" rate of change. **Typical use:** transport a gyroscope or any spin vector along a worldline without spurious rotation, and define what it means for an accelerated, non-rotating frame; on the rest space it is just the orthogonal projection of the absolute derivative, $\Pi(dV/d\tau)$. The reusable content is the clean separation of three derivatives — absolute, Fermi–Walker, and observer — differing by the four-acceleration tilt and the frame's spin. Recognise its applicability whenever a vector must be carried along an accelerated worldline and you need to know whether it "really" rotated, with Thomas precession as the headline application.

# Bridges

1. **The Lie algebra of the Lorentz group — the four-rotation is an $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$ element, and its decomposition is the boost/rotation split.** An antisymmetric bilinear form on Minkowski space is, after raising one index with the metric, an endomorphism $\Omega$ satisfying $g(\Omega X, Y) = -g(X, \Omega Y)$ — and that is exactly the defining condition of the [[Def - The Lorentz Group|Lorentz]] Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$, the tangent space at the identity of the group of metric isometries (developed in [[Special Relativity X — The Lorentz Group as a Lie Group]]). The frame-evolution equation $de_\alpha/d\tau = \Omega^\beta{}_\alpha e_\beta$ therefore says the local frame is dragged along the worldline by a one-parameter family of *infinitesimal Lorentz transformations* generated by $\underline\Omega$. The orthogonal decomposition of this chapter — electric part $\leftrightarrow$ four-acceleration, magnetic part $\leftrightarrow$ spatial rotation — is, in Lie-algebra language, the decomposition of $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$ into the three boost generators $K_i$ and the three rotation generators $J_i$ relative to a chosen timelike direction. The boost generators are what the four-acceleration excites; the rotation generators are what $\vec\omega$ excites. Exponentiating the boost part around a closed loop is what produces the Thomas rotation, so this chapter is the kinematic shadow of the group theory to come.

2. **Electromagnetism — the same decomposition splits the field into $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.** The orthogonal decomposition of an antisymmetric bilinear form is introduced here for the four-rotation, but Gourgoulhon names the two pieces "electric part" and "magnetic part" for a reason: the [[Def - The Electromagnetic Field Tensor|electromagnetic field]] is itself an antisymmetric $2$-tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$, and decomposing it relative to an observer's four-velocity $U_0$ yields precisely the electric field $E_\mu = F_{\mu\nu}U_0^\nu$ (the electric *form* $q$) and the magnetic field $B^\mu$ (the magnetic *vector* $\vec b$) that *that* observer measures. The construction is literally the same theorem; the electric field an observer sees is the contraction of $F$ with their four-velocity, and a boosted observer, contracting with a different $U_0'$, reads off a different split of $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ — which is why electric and magnetic fields mix under a change of observer. This chapter therefore builds, for free, the algebraic skeleton of the observer-dependence of the electromagnetic field, finished in [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field]].

3. **General relativity — the $3+1$ split, the projector, and Fermi normal coordinates.** The entire apparatus of this chapter — slice spacetime by an observer's rest spaces, project tensors onto each slice with $\Pi$, watch the slice tilt and turn — is the flat, infinitesimal model of the **$3+1$ decomposition** that underlies the Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity. There the congruence of observers becomes the normal to a foliation by spacelike hypersurfaces, the projector $\Pi$ becomes the projector onto each surface, the induced [[Def - Semi-Riemannian Metric and Signature|spatial metric]] $h = -g|_{U_0^\perp}$ becomes the dynamical three-metric, and the way the rest space changes along the congruence becomes the extrinsic curvature. A non-rotating, Fermi–Walker-transported tetrad gives **Fermi normal coordinates** — the natural coordinates of a freely-falling, non-spinning laboratory — in which the curved metric reduces to $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ on the worldline and departs from it at second order through the Riemann tidal tensor (see [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]]). The locality bound $r \ll \|A_0\|^{-1}$ derived here is the flat-space ancestor of the tidal radius over which such a frame is trustworthy.

4. **Riemannian geometry — Fermi–Walker transport is the parallel transport of the worldline's Frenet frame.** Along a [[Def - Worldline of a Particle|worldline]], the absolute derivative $d/d\tau$ is the flat-spacetime restriction of the covariant derivative, and Fermi–Walker transport is the modification that keeps a frame non-rotating relative to the worldline's own bending. In the language of [[Riemannian Geometry I — Connections and Covariant Differentiation|connections]], it is the transport that parallel-propagates vectors *orthogonal to the velocity* while letting the velocity itself follow its acceleration — the Frenet apparatus of the worldline. For an inertial (geodesic) worldline the four-acceleration vanishes and Fermi–Walker transport collapses to ordinary parallel transport, which is why a freely-falling gyroscope simply keeps its direction. The Serret–Frenet expression $\vec\omega = cT_2 e_1 + cT_1 e_3$ ties the four-rotation directly to the curvature and torsions of the worldline studied in [[Special Relativity V — Worldlines, Proper Time and Four-Velocity|Special Relativity V]].

5. **Rotating and accelerated observers — this chapter is the engine room.** The abstract machinery built here is put to work on concrete congruences in the next chapters of the subject. The uniformly accelerated (Rindler) observer of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers]] has constant four-acceleration and vanishing four-rotation, so its frame evolves purely by Fermi–Walker transport; its rest spaces sweep out the Rindler wedge and intersect at the horizon distance $a^{-1}$ computed here. The uniformly rotating observer of [[Special Relativity XVII — Rotating Observers]] has nonzero four-rotation $\vec\omega$ and cannot synchronise clocks globally — the chronometric expression of the Sagnac effect and the Ehrenfest paradox, both of which are statements about the failure of the rest-space slicing to close up, exactly the locality phenomenon this chapter isolates. Thomas precession, the residual rotation of a Fermi–Walker-transported gyroscope on a circular orbit, is computed from the four-acceleration's "magnetic"-free Fermi–Walker part, and is the bridge from observer kinematics to the fine structure of atomic spectra.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: an observer is a worldline plus a way to call directions "spatial", and the whole chapter is the consequences of one choice — orthogonality to $U_0$ means spatial.** Everything in this chapter unfolds from a single decision: declare a direction "purely spatial for $\mathcal{O}$" exactly when it is metric-orthogonal to the four-velocity $U_0$. That choice is *forced* by the radar definition of simultaneity, not imposed by fiat — the Einstein–Poincaré construction, worked through, hands you $U_0\cdot\overrightarrow{AM} = 0$ as the meaning of "simultaneous", and the simultaneous directions are precisely $U_0^\perp$. From this one identification, the rest space, the projector, the Euclidean spatial metric, the radar distance, and the entire frame apparatus follow mechanically. The deep payoff is that "space" and "time" are not features of spacetime but features of an *observer*: a different four-velocity gives a different orthogonal complement, hence a different space and a different time, with no privileged choice. The chapter is the disciplined working-out of "spatial = orthogonal to $U_0$", and once that frame is internalised, every result is unsurprising.

**The true name of the four-rotation is "the infinitesimal Lorentz transformation that drags the frame".** The four-rotation is presented as an array of functions $\Omega^\beta{}_\alpha$ in $de_\alpha/d\tau = \Omega^\beta{}_\alpha e_\beta$, but the right way to think of it is as a single antisymmetric object — an element of the Lorentz Lie algebra — that generates how the observer's orthonormal frame is carried along the worldline. Its antisymmetry is not a computational accident; it is the statement that the frame stays orthonormal, that the dragging is by genuine (infinitesimal) Lorentz isometries and not arbitrary linear maps. The orthogonal decomposition then says any such generator splits, relative to the time direction, into a *boost* part (the four-acceleration, which tilts the time axis) and a *rotation* part (the spatial rotation $\vec\omega$, which spins the three space axes). This is exactly the boost-versus-rotation split of the Lorentz algebra, seen kinematically a full four chapters before the group theory is developed — and recognising it now is what makes Thomas precession, the BMT equation, and the electromagnetic $\mathbf{E}$–$\mathbf{B}$ split all instances of one idea.

**A trigger-reaction pattern: see "constant inner product", differentiate it, harvest an orthogonality.** The most productive single move in the chapter is to take the proper-time derivative of an inner product that is constant by construction. The unit-norm condition $U_0\cdot U_0 = 1$ differentiates to $A_0\cdot U_0 = 0$ — the four-acceleration is automatically spatial. The orthonormality $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$ differentiates to the antisymmetry of the four-rotation. The constancy of $\vec\omega\cdot U_0$ keeps the four-rotation in the rest space. Each time, a constant inner product yields, for free, a constraint that would otherwise have to be imposed. The general lesson, transferable far beyond relativity: whenever a quantity is pinned to a fixed value (a norm, a normalisation, an orthogonality), its derivative is a free linear relation among the rates of change — and in a field built on the indefinite metric, those free relations are exactly the structural facts (acceleration is spacelike, the four-rotation is antisymmetric) you most want.

**Inheritance: the Euclidean geometry of the rest space is inherited from the indefiniteness of the spacetime metric, not in spite of it.** It looks paradoxical that an *indefinite* metric on spacetime gives a perfectly ordinary *positive-definite* Euclidean geometry on the rest space. The resolution is inheritance through orthogonality: the one direction with positive norm is used up by $U_0$, leaving the orthogonal complement with three negative-norm directions, and flipping the overall sign turns those into three positive-norm Euclidean directions. The light cone, the reversed triangle inequality, all the strangeness of Minkowski geometry lives in the time direction; project it out and the residue is the familiar $\mathbb{R}^3$. This is the precise sense in which an observer "sees" ordinary space: they are looking at the negative-definite orthogonal complement of their own four-velocity, sign-flipped. The same inheritance is why the spatial metric of the $3+1$ split in general relativity is Riemannian even though spacetime is Lorentzian — the signature $(1,3)$ is exactly "one timelike direction, whose orthogonal complement is Riemannian", and an observer's rest space is where you go to cash that out.

**Locality is the price of acceleration, and the radius is breathtakingly large.** The qualifier "local" on the local rest space and local frame is not pedantry: for an accelerated observer, the construction genuinely fails beyond a finite distance. Neighbouring rest spaces $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(t)$ and $\mathscr{E}_{U_0}(t+dt)$ are tilted relative to each other (because the four-velocity is rotating), and they intersect in a plane at proper distance $a^{-1} = \|A_0\|^{-1}$ from the worldline; past that distance the slicing folds over itself and a single event gets two different sets of frame coordinates. So the frame can be trusted only for $r \ll a^{-1}$. The arithmetic is worth carrying around as a sanity anchor: for a human-scale acceleration of $g = 10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, the radius $a^{-1} = c^2/g \approx 9\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{m} \approx 1$ light-year. The locality restriction is therefore essentially never a practical constraint in a laboratory, but it is conceptually decisive: it is the flat-spacetime seed of the fact that, in general relativity, a freely-falling frame is only *locally* inertial, valid out to the tidal radius set by spacetime curvature. An inertial observer has $A_0 = 0$, the radius is infinite, and the rest spaces foliate all of spacetime — which is exactly why only inertial observers carry a *global* notion of simultaneity.
