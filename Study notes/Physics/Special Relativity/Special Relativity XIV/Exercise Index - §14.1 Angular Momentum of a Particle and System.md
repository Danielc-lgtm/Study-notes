## §14.1 Angular Momentum of a Particle and System — Exercises

The exercises of §14.1 build the angular momentum four-tensor $J^{\alpha\beta} = x^\alpha p^\beta - x^\beta p^\alpha$ and drill the two skills it demands: treating it as an honest rank-two tensor and reading off the two three-vectors an observer measures. The central discipline is to stop thinking of angular momentum as a vector — in four dimensions it is an antisymmetric tensor with $\binom42 = 6$ components, not the $\binom32 = 3$ of the Newtonian cross product — and to let the tensor transformation law $J'^{\alpha\beta} = \Lambda^\alpha{}_\mu\Lambda^\beta{}_\nu J^{\mu\nu}$ carry every frame-change for free. Relative to an observer the six components split, exactly as the electromagnetic tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$ splits into $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, into the angular momentum vector $\vec\sigma = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ (space-space block) and the mass-energy dipole moment (time-space block). The change-of-origin rule $J_{C'} = J_C + \overrightarrow{C'C}^\flat\wedge P$ and the conservation law for an isolated particle are the two structural facts that organise everything downstream — the change-of-origin inhomogeneity is what later makes the spin point-independent, and conservation is the second great relativistic conservation law after four-momentum.

- [[Ex - The angular momentum tensor transforms as a rank-two tensor]] — assemble $J^{\alpha\beta} = x^\alpha p^\beta - x^\beta p^\alpha$ from $\overrightarrow{CM}$ and $p$, verify antisymmetry, and prove it transforms as $J'^{\alpha\beta} = \Lambda^\alpha{}_\mu\Lambda^\beta{}_\nu J^{\mu\nu}$ under a Lorentz transformation by transforming the factors separately — establishing that every component-level claim about angular momentum follows from the tensor transformation law (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Angular Momentum Four-Tensor]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]], [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space]]).

- [[Ex - The angular momentum vector as a cross product]] — split the antisymmetric tensor $J^{\alpha\beta}$ relative to an observer's four-velocity, identify the space-space block as the angular momentum vector $\vec\sigma_C = \overrightarrow{CM}\times\mathbf{p}$ and the time-space block as the mass-energy dipole moment, and confirm the decomposition is the same one that produces $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{E}$ from $F_{\mu\nu}$ — so a boost mixes $\vec\sigma$ and the dipole just as it mixes the magnetic and electric fields (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Angular Momentum Four-Tensor]], [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space]], [[Def - The Levi-Civita Tensor]]).

- [[Ex - Change of origin for angular momentum]] — derive the inhomogeneous change-of-reference-point rule $J_{C'} = J_C + \overrightarrow{C'C}^\flat\wedge P$ with $P$ the total four-momentum, show the shift vanishes precisely when $P = 0$ (the barycentric frame) or when the displacement lies along $P$, and conclude that the angular momentum about a point is genuinely point-dependent except in those cases — the algebraic seed of the later point-independence of spin (⭐) ([[Def - Angular Momentum Four-Tensor]], [[Thm - Conservation of Angular Momentum]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).

- [[Ex - Conservation of angular momentum for an isolated particle]] — differentiate $J_C = \overrightarrow{CM}^\flat\wedge p$ along the worldline, show the $d\overrightarrow{CM}/d\tau\wedge p$ term dies because $d\overrightarrow{CM}/d\tau = U$ is parallel to $p = mU$ and the $\overrightarrow{CM}\wedge dp/d\tau$ term dies by the law of inertia, concluding $J_C = \text{const}$; then explain why for a *system* conservation is an independent first principle, on the same footing as four-momentum conservation, because relativity has no strong action–reaction law (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Conservation of Angular Momentum]], [[Def - Angular Momentum Four-Tensor]], [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass]]).
