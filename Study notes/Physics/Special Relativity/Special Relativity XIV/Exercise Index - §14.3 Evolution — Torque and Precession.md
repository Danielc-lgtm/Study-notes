## §14.3 Evolution — Torque and Precession — Exercises

The exercises of §14.3 turn from *what* angular momentum is to *how* it changes. The master object is the four-torque $N_C = dJ_C/d\tau = \overrightarrow{CM}^\flat\wedge f$, the relativistic moment of the four-force, which reduces for an inertial observer and a fixed point to the verbatim Newtonian torque law $d\vec\sigma_C/dt = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$. The deeper material is the evolution of *spin*, where the constraint $\vec s\cdot\vec u = 0$ does the decisive work: because the spin is locked to a rest space that tilts as the particle accelerates, "no torque" cannot mean $d\vec s/d\tau = 0$ — it must mean the Fermi–Walker derivative vanishes, $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_u\vec s = 0$, and the difference between the two derivatives is a real, observable rotation, the Thomas precession. A free gyroscope precesses from geometry alone, with no force acting; place it, charged, in an electromagnetic field and the BMT equation governs the precession, cleanly separating the dynamical magnetic-moment term ($\frac g2$) from the kinematic Thomas term ($\frac g2 - 1$). That separation is exactly what makes the anomalous moment $g-2$ measurable in a storage ring. The unifying skill is to find an evolution law by differentiating a defining relation and imposing a constraint — differentiate the wedge for the torque, differentiate $\vec s\cdot\vec u = 0$ to pin the Fermi–Walker correction — and always to distinguish the precession forced by the worldline's shape from the precession forced by a genuine torque.

- [[Ex - The relativistic torque law]] — derive the four-torque $N_C = \overrightarrow{CM}^\flat\wedge f$ by differentiating $J_C = \overrightarrow{CM}^\flat\wedge p$ (the $d\overrightarrow{CM}/d\tau\wedge p$ term vanishing by $U\parallel p$), specialise to an inertial observer to recover the Newtonian law $d\vec\sigma_C/dt = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$, apply it to a charge in a magnetic field, and show a central force conserves angular momentum about its centre — the first integral of the relativistic central-force problem (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Four-Torque]], [[Thm - Evolution of the Angular Momentum Vector]], [[Def - Four-Force]]).

- [[Ex - The free gyroscope and Fermi-Walker transport]] — show "no torque" on a gyroscope's spin must be stated as $D^{\mathrm{FW}}_u\vec s = 0$ rather than $d\vec s/d\tau = 0$ (the naive form violates $\vec s\cdot\vec u = 0$ on an accelerated worldline), prove Fermi–Walker transport preserves the spin norm so the spin precesses, compute the Thomas precession $\Delta\phi = 2\pi(\gamma - 1)$ per orbit for circular motion, and explain why the precession is purely kinematic — the same $(\frac g2 - 1)$ effect that survives in the BMT equation even at $g = 1$ (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - The BMT Equation]], [[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]], [[Def - Spin Four-Vector]]).

- [[Ex - The muon g minus 2 precession in a storage ring]] — for a muon circulating in a uniform vertical magnetic field, derive the cyclotron frequency $\omega_c = qB/(\gamma m)$ from the Lorentz force and the spin precession frequency $\omega_s$ from the BMT equation, subtract to obtain the energy-independent anomalous frequency $\omega_a = \omega_s - \omega_c = \frac{q}{m}aB$ with $a = (g-2)/2$, and explain why the cancellation of $\gamma$ is the feature that makes the $g-2$ measurement possible — a direct readout of the Thomas term of the BMT equation (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Thm - The BMT Equation]], [[Def - Spin Four-Vector]], [[Def - Four-Force]]).
