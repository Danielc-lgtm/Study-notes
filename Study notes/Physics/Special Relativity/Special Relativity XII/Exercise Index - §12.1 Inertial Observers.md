## §12.1 Inertial Observers — Exercises

The exercises of §12.1 drill the characterisation of inertial observers and the globality of their rest space. The defining test is the pair of conditions $a = 0$ and $\omega = 0$: vanishing four-acceleration integrates the worldline to a straight line, while vanishing four-rotation prevents the carried frame from spinning. The central subtlety, returned to repeatedly, is that *straightness is not inertiality* — a straight worldline guarantees only the first condition, and an observer can move uniformly in a straight line while its spatial triad precesses. The geometric payoff is that the constancy of the four-velocity makes the rest-space hyperplanes parallel translates of a single $U^\perp$, never intersecting, which is exactly what licenses a global inertial coordinate system; the accelerated observer, whose four-velocity rotates, is the cautionary contrast where the rest spaces pivot and cross. The unifying observation: everything special about an inertial observer flows from the single fact that its four-velocity is one fixed vector rather than a varying field.

- [[Ex - Inertial worldline is a straight line]] (⭐) — integrate $\mathrm{d}U/\mathrm{d}\tau = 0$ twice to the affine worldline $x^\alpha(\tau) = U^\alpha\tau + x_0^\alpha$, identify the eight constants as initial four-velocity and event, and recognise that this establishes only $a = 0$, not full inertiality ([[Def - Inertial Observer]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Worldline of a Particle]], [[Def - Proper Time]]).

- [[Ex - A straight worldline need not be inertial]] (⭐⭐) — construct an observer with constant four-velocity but a spatial triad rotating at rate $\Omega$ in proper time, verify $a = 0$ but $\omega = \Omega\,e_3 \neq 0$, and conclude that "straight worldline" captures strictly less than "inertial observer" (Gourgoulhon's Remark 8.1) ([[Def - Inertial Observer]], [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]).

- [[Ex - Parallel rest spaces of an inertial observer]] (⭐⭐) — write the rest spaces as level sets of the affine function $\phi(M) = U\cdot\overrightarrow{O(0)M}$, prove they are parallel and disjoint, exhibit the tilted simultaneity lines $t - vx = \text{const}$ of slope $v$ for a moving observer, and contrast with the accelerated observer whose rest spaces pivot about the Rindler apex and intersect ([[Def - Inertial Observer]], [[Thm - Globality of the Local Rest Space for Inertial Observers]], [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space]]).
