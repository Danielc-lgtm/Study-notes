## §4.3 The Structure of the Lorentz Group O(1,3) — Exercises

The exercises of §4.3 dissect the [[Def - The Lorentz Group|Lorentz group]] as a manifold and as a group. The two locally-constant invariants $\det\Lambda = \pm 1$ and $\mathrm{sgn}\,\Lambda^0{}_0$ cut $O(1,3)$ into four connected components, of which only the restricted group $SO^+(1,3)$ — the identity component — is a subgroup, with the discrete symmetries parity, time reversal, and spacetime inversion forming the Klein four-group as coset representatives. The first exercise carries out this decomposition. The second proves the chapter's geometric signature result, the [[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality|reversed triangle inequality]], and its corollary that the inertial worldline maximises proper time — the geometric resolution of the twin paradox. The third counts the six parameters four independent ways (constraint count, explicit generators, polar decomposition, coordinate planes) and reconciles them. The unifying observation: $O(1,3)$ is the rotation group of $\mathbb{R}^4$ under an indefinite metric, six-dimensional ($= \binom{4}{2}$) with three rotations and three boosts, and split by two discrete signs into four pieces.

- [[Ex - The four connected components of the Lorentz group]] (⭐⭐) — extract the invariants $\det\Lambda = \pm 1$ and $\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1$ or $\le -1$ from the defining equation, place the identity, parity, time reversal, and spacetime inversion in the four pieces, show only $SO^+(1,3)$ is a subgroup with the others its cosets, and identify $\{\mathrm{Id}, P, T, PT\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ ([[Def - The Lorentz Group]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]]).

- [[Ex - The reversed triangle inequality and the longest worldline]] (⭐⭐) — prove the reversed Cauchy–Schwarz $U\cdot V \ge \|U\|\|V\|$ and hence $\|U+V\| \ge \|U\|+\|V\|$ for future-timelike vectors, compute the twin-paradox deficit $2T(1 - 1/\gamma)$, prove by polygonal induction that the inertial worldline maximises proper time, and resolve the twin paradox geometrically ([[Thm - The Reversed Triangle Inequality]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - Proper Time]], [[Thm - Invariance of the Spacetime Interval]]).

- [[Ex - Counting the six parameters and the rotation-boost split]] (⭐⭐) — count $\dim O(1,3) = 16 - 10 = 6$ from the symmetric defining equation, exhibit three rotation and three boost one-parameter subgroups, reconcile the $7$-parameter polar decomposition $\Lambda = R\,B_x\,R'$ with the group being six-dimensional (the rotation about the boost axis is redundant), and interpret $6 = \binom{4}{2}$ as the coordinate planes ([[Def - The Lorentz Group]], [[Def - Boosts as Hyperbolic Rotations]]).
