## §9.2 Classification of Restricted Lorentz Transformations — Exercises

The exercises of §9.2 drill the fine anatomy of the connected core $SO^+(1,3)$: the dichotomy that *every* restricted transformation is either a **four-screw** (two invariant null directions, a commuting boost and rotation in orthogonal planes) or a **null rotation** (one invariant null direction, a parabolic slide along the light cone), with spatial rotations and boosts as the degenerate four-screws. The organising principle — and the single most important habit the section teaches — is to *build frames from null vectors, not from the timelike axis*: take an invariant null eigenvector $\ell$, manufacture a partner $k$ with $\ell\cdot k = 2$, let $e_0 = \tfrac12(\ell+k)$ emerge, and read the three-parameter normal form ($\psi, \theta, \alpha$) off the adapted matrix. The classification then *is* a count of invariant null directions. The spectral fingerprints separate the types cleanly: a boost has real eigenvalues $e^{\pm\psi}$ (the Doppler factors) on real null eigenvectors; a rotation has complex unimodular eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$; a null rotation has the single eigenvalue $1$ with a defective Jordan block of size three. The trace shortcuts $\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda - 1 = \cosh\psi$ (boost) or $\cos\theta$ (rotation) extract the parameters without diagonalising.

- [[Ex - Classifying a given Lorentz transformation by its invariant null directions]] — given an explicit restricted $\Lambda$, solve $\Lambda(\ell) = \lambda\ell$ subject to $\ell\cdot\ell = 0$ to find the invariant null directions, count them to decide whether $\Lambda$ is a boost, rotation, four-screw, or null rotation, and cross-check against the trace formulas $\cosh\psi = \tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda - 1$ and $\cos\theta = \tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda - 1$ (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Invariant Null Direction of a Restricted Lorentz Transformation]], [[Def - Classification of Restricted Lorentz Transformations]], [[Thm - Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of a Lorentz Boost]], [[Def - Null Rotations and Four-Screws]]).

- [[Ex - A null rotation fixes a unique null direction and is parabolic]] — write the null-rotation matrix in an adapted frame, verify it fixes the null direction $\mathrm{Span}(\ell)$ and the degenerate null plane $\mathrm{Span}(\ell, e_3)$, show that $N := \Lambda - \mathrm{Id}$ satisfies $N^3 = 0$ but $N^2 \ne 0$ (a single Jordan block of size three), and confirm the eigenvalue $1$ has algebraic multiplicity four but geometric multiplicity two — the genuinely non-Euclidean, non-diagonalisable element of the classification (⭐⭐) ([[Def - Null Rotations and Four-Screws]], [[Def - Classification of Restricted Lorentz Transformations]], [[Thm - Invariant Null Direction of a Restricted Lorentz Transformation]]).

- [[Ex - The eigenvalues of a boost are the Doppler factors]] — diagonalise a boost in the null basis $(\ell_+, \ell_-, e_2, e_3)$ with $\ell_\pm = e_0 \pm e_1$, obtain the eigenvalues $e^{\pm\psi}$ on the null eigenvectors, identify them with the longitudinal Doppler factors $\sqrt{(1\pm V)/(1\mp V)}$, contrast with a rotation's complex eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$ on complex eigenvectors, and apply the redshift eigenvalue to a photon's observed frequency in a receding frame (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of a Lorentz Boost]], [[Def - Boosts as Hyperbolic Rotations]], [[Def - Rapidity]]).

- [[Ex - A four-screw is characterised by two invariant null directions]] — prove the equivalence "two distinct invariant null directions $\iff$ four-screw" both ways: a four-screw fixes both null lines of its boost plane, and conversely two invariant null directions $\ell, k$ force the partner construction to terminate with null-rotation parameter $\alpha = 0$, yielding the commuting boost-times-rotation normal form, with diagonalisability restored exactly when $\alpha = 0$ (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Def - Null Rotations and Four-Screws]], [[Def - Classification of Restricted Lorentz Transformations]], [[Thm - Invariant Null Direction of a Restricted Lorentz Transformation]], [[Thm - Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of a Lorentz Boost]]).
