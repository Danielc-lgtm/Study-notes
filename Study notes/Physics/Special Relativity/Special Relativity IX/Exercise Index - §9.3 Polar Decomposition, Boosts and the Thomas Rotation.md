## §9.3 Polar Decomposition, Boosts and the Thomas Rotation — Exercises

The exercises of §9.3 drill the chapter's centrepiece — the polar decomposition $\Lambda = S\circ R$ (relative to a chosen observer $e_0$, every restricted transformation factors uniquely as a boost times a rotation) — and its sting in the tail, the **Thomas rotation**. The master technique is uniform: *the boost is read off the image $\Lambda(e_0)$, and the rotation is whatever is left*, $R = S^{-1}\Lambda$. This single move produces the polar decomposition, refines into the $KAK$ (Cartan) rotation–boost–rotation form, and — applied to a product of two non-coplanar boosts — extracts the Thomas (Wigner) rotation. The structural lesson the section drives home is that the decomposition is of each *element* relative to an observer, not of the *group*: the boosts do not form a subgroup, because $SO^+(1,3)$ is *simple* and admits no proper normal subgroup to host them. The Thomas rotation is the visible obstruction — it is the exact failure of velocity addition to commute (gyrocommutativity $\mathbf{V}_1\oplus\mathbf{V}_2 = R(\mathbf{V}_2\oplus\mathbf{V}_1)$, equal magnitudes, directions rotated), the holonomy of hyperbolic velocity space, and the kinematic seed of Thomas precession with its famous factor of one-half. The diagnostic throughout: a boost has a symmetric matrix, so an asymmetric product flags a Thomas rotation.

- [[Ex - Proving the polar decomposition of a restricted Lorentz transformation]] — carry out the polar decomposition for a concrete boost-times-rotation matrix (compute $\Lambda(e_0)$, build the boost $S$ from its time- and spatial-components, form $R = S^{-1}\Lambda$, verify $R$ fixes $e_0$ and is orthogonal while $S$ is symmetric positive-definite), then prove uniqueness in general by showing the boost factor is forced by $\Lambda(e_0)$ alone (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Polar Decomposition of the Lorentz Group]], [[Def - Boosts as Hyperbolic Rotations]], [[Thm - Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of a Lorentz Boost]]).

- [[Ex - The Thomas rotation angle for two perpendicular boosts]] — compose two perpendicular boosts, obtain the composite Lorentz factor $\Gamma = \Gamma_1\Gamma_2$, show the product matrix is asymmetric and extract the Thomas rotation in the plane of the velocities, derive $\cos\varphi_T = (\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2)/(1+\Gamma_1\Gamma_2)$, and check the non-relativistic limit $\varphi_T \approx -\tfrac12 V_1 V_2$ — the seed of the spin–orbit one-half (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - The Thomas Rotation Angle]], [[Thm - Composition of Coplanar Boosts gives a Boost times Thomas Rotation]], [[Thm - Polar Decomposition of the Lorentz Group]], [[Def - Thomas Rotation]]).

- [[Ex - The KAK decomposition and the six parameters]] — establish the rotation–boost–rotation factorisation $\Lambda = R(H)\,B[V]\,R(H')$ (the Cartan $KAK$ decomposition) by standardising the polar boost's axis with a rotation, show the boost speed $\Lambda^0{}_0 = \Gamma$ is a double-coset invariant, and reconcile the apparent seven parameters with the six-dimensional group by locating the one-parameter redundancy (rotations about the boost axis, the stabiliser $SO(2)$) (⭐⭐) ([[Thm - Polar Decomposition of the Lorentz Group]], [[Def - Boosts as Hyperbolic Rotations]], [[Def - Subgroups and Components of the Lorentz Group]]).

- [[Ex - Boosts do not form a subgroup and the Wigner rotation]] — prove the product of two non-coplanar boosts is asymmetric (hence not a boost, so boosts are not a subgroup), identify the residual Wigner rotation as the polar rotation factor, derive the gyrocommutativity identity $\mathbf{V}_1\oplus\mathbf{V}_2 = R[\mathbf{V}_1,\mathbf{V}_2](\mathbf{V}_2\oplus\mathbf{V}_1)$ showing velocity addition fails to commute by exactly the Thomas angle, and explain via the simplicity of $SO^+(1,3)$ why no boost-subgroup can exist (⭐⭐⭐) ([[Def - Thomas Rotation]], [[Thm - Composition of Coplanar Boosts gives a Boost times Thomas Rotation]], [[Thm - Polar Decomposition of the Lorentz Group]], [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition]]).
