## §16.3 Thomas Precession — Exercises

The exercises of §16.3 isolate a phenomenon with no Newtonian analogue: the spatial frame of a *non-rotating* accelerated observer rotates relative to the inertial laboratory, whenever the acceleration is not collinear with the velocity. The first computes the precession for a free gyroscope on a circular orbit, obtaining $\vec\omega_T = -(\Gamma-1)\Omega\,\mathbf{e}_3^*$ — opposite to the orbital sense, a lag of $2\pi(\Gamma-1)$ per revolution that is a holonomy of velocity-space transport. The second derives the precession rate from pure group theory, showing it is the continuous accumulation of the infinitesimal [[Def - Thomas Rotation|Thomas rotations]] left over when non-collinear boosts are composed ($\Lambda\circ S = S'\circ R$), and confirms this kinematic route agrees with the Fermi–Walker computation of the Thomas equation. The third carries the low-velocity rate $\tfrac12\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{v}/c^2$ into atomic physics and shows it supplies the **Thomas half** that halves the naive spin–orbit coupling, matching the observed fine-structure splitting and the Dirac equation. The unifying observation is that "rotation" is not a single concept: a frame can be intrinsically non-rotating ($\vec\omega = 0$, Fermi–Walker transported) yet rotate relative to the distant stars, the mismatch being the accumulated Thomas rotation — the flat-spacetime, classical-spin member of the family that includes geodetic precession and the Berry phase.

- [[Ex - Thomas precession of a gyroscope in circular orbit]] (⭐⭐⭐) — for a free gyroscope on a uniform circular orbit (radius $R$, angular velocity $\Omega$), write $\mathbf{V} = R\Omega\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ and $\boldsymbol\gamma = -R\Omega^2\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ (centripetal, $\perp\mathbf{V}$), compute $\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mathbf{V} = -R^2\Omega^3\mathbf{e}_3^*$, substitute into $\vec\omega_T = \frac{\Gamma-1}{V^2}\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mathbf{V}$ to get $\vec\omega_T = -(\Gamma-1)\Omega\,\mathbf{e}_3^*$ (opposite to the orbit, a holonomy of $2\pi(\Gamma-1)$ lag per revolution), and take the low-velocity limit $\vec\omega_T\simeq -\tfrac12(R\Omega/c)^2\Omega\,\mathbf{e}_3^*$ ([[Thm - The Thomas Equation]], [[Def - Thomas Precession]], [[Def - Spin Four-Vector]]).

- [[Ex - Thomas precession from the composition of two boosts]] (⭐⭐) — derive the precession rate from boost kinematics: write the non-rotating frame's evolution as $S_{t+\mathrm{d}t} = \Lambda\circ S_t$ with $\Lambda$ the infinitesimal boost of velocity $\mathbf{W} = c^2\mathrm{d}t\,\boldsymbol\gamma$, apply the boost-composition theorem $\Lambda\circ S = S'\circ R$ to extract the residual [[Def - Thomas Rotation|Thomas rotation]] $R$, read off its rate to recover $\vec\omega_T = \frac{\Gamma^2}{c^2(1+\Gamma)}\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mathbf{V}$, and confirm it equals the Fermi–Walker result — showing the precession is pure group theory, the differential of the gyrogroup gyration ([[Def - Thomas Precession]], [[Def - Thomas Rotation]], [[Thm - The Thomas Equation]]).

- [[Ex - The Thomas half and atomic fine structure]] (⭐⭐⭐) — carry out the semiclassical spin–orbit calculation for a hydrogen-like atom: boost the nuclear Coulomb field to the electron rest frame to get $\mathbf{B}' = -\tfrac{1}{ec}\mathbf{v}\times\nabla V$, write the naive energy $H^{\mathrm{naive}}_{\mathrm{SO}} = \tfrac{1}{m^2c^2}\tfrac1r\tfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}r}\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}$ (twice too large), compute the Thomas-precession energy $H_T = \mathbf{S}\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega_T = -\tfrac{1}{2m^2c^2}\tfrac1r\tfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}r}\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}$ (half, opposite sign), and add them to get the correct $H_{\mathrm{SO}} = \tfrac{1}{2m^2c^2}\tfrac1r\tfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}r}\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}$ — the Thomas half, matching experiment and the Dirac equation ([[Def - Thomas Precession]], [[Thm - The Thomas Equation]], [[Def - Spin Four-Vector]]).
