## §16.1 The Uniformly Accelerated Observer and the Rindler Horizon — Exercises

The exercises of §16.1 drill the single object on which the whole chapter is built: the worldline of constant proper acceleration. The first integrates the defining condition to obtain the hyperbola, the velocity tending to $c$, and the linearly-growing rapidity, and fixes the crucial distinction between the constant *proper* acceleration $a = \|A\|$ (a Lorentz scalar) and the decaying *coordinate* acceleration. The second turns the hyperbola's asymptote into a causal horizon, showing that an eternally accelerating observer permanently outruns light from a region of flat spacetime and that the boundary $\mathcal{H}: ct_* = x_* + a^{-1}$ is a null hyperplane — the flat-spacetime model of a black-hole event horizon. The third applies the worldline to a realistic constant-$g$ interstellar voyage, extracting the logarithmic-in-distance crew time, the rest-to-rest trip via rapidity additivity, and the relativistic rocket equation. The unifying observation is that "constant acceleration" means "constant rapidity-rate", so the hyperbolic functions $\cosh(a\tau), \sinh(a\tau)$ replace the Newtonian parabola, and the length scale $a^{-1} = c^2/g$ (about a light-year for Earth gravity) is the ruler against which the horizon, the redshift, and the rigidity of §16.2 are all measured.

- [[Ex - Hyperbolic motion under constant proper acceleration]] (⭐⭐) — integrate $a = (1-u^2)^{3/2}\dot u$ to get $u = at_*/\sqrt{1+(at_*)^2}$, the worldline $x_* = a^{-1}[\sqrt{1+(at_*)^2}-1]$, the hyperbola $(ax_*+1)^2-(at_*)^2=1$, and the proper time $\tau = a^{-1}\sinh^{-1}(at_*)$, then read off that the rapidity $\varphi = a\tau$ grows linearly while the coordinate acceleration $a/\gamma^3$ decays — the distinction between constant proper and decaying coordinate acceleration ([[Def - Uniformly Accelerated Observer (Hyperbolic Motion)]], [[Thm - Worldline of a Uniformly Accelerated Observer]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]).

- [[Ex - The Rindler horizon and the light that never catches up]] (⭐⭐) — show a light ray from $(0,b)$ with $b<-a^{-1}$ never reaches the accelerating observer, derive the general accessibility condition $x_*^{\mathrm{em}}-ct_*^{\mathrm{em}}>-a^{-1}$ from the null-separation condition $2(ax_*^{\mathrm{em}}+1)\cosh(act)=1+a^2(\cdots)$ using $\cosh\ge 1$, locate the boundary hyperplane $\mathcal{H}: ct_*=x_*+a^{-1}$, prove its normal $e_0^*+e_1^*$ is null (hence $\mathcal{H}$ is a null hyperplane), and interpret the result as the flat-spacetime model of a causal horizon that dissolves if the observer coasts ([[Def - Rindler Horizon]], [[Thm - Worldline of a Uniformly Accelerated Observer]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]]).

- [[Ex - The relativistic rocket and a constant-g voyage]] (⭐⭐⭐) — treat a rocket at constant proper acceleration $g$: write $x_* = (c^2/g)[\cosh(g\tau/c)-1]$ and $\tau = (c/g)\sinh^{-1}(gt_*/c)$, show crew time grows logarithmically with distance (galactic centre in $\sim 10$ years of crew time against $\sim 30{,}000$ Earth years), handle the rest-to-rest accelerate-decelerate trip by rapidity additivity, connect the bounded perceived distance to the Rindler horizon (Tong's "doesn't think he's gone far"), and derive the relativistic rocket equation $\Delta v = c\tanh(g\tau_{\mathrm{burn}}/c)$ and mass ratio $M_i/M_f = \exp(c\,\Delta\varphi/v_{\mathrm{ex}})$ from four-momentum conservation ([[Thm - Worldline of a Uniformly Accelerated Observer]], [[Def - Uniformly Accelerated Observer (Hyperbolic Motion)]], [[Def - Rindler Horizon]]).
