# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole topic: **we set $c = 1$** and use the **mostly-minus** signature $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike four-vector $X$ has $X\cdot X > 0$, a spacelike one has $X\cdot X < 0$, and a null one has $X\cdot X = 0$. Rotation is the chapter where $c$ refuses to disappear: the combination $r\omega$ — the rim speed of a disk of radius $r$ spinning at angular velocity $\omega$ — must be measured against $c$, and the dimensionless ratio $r\omega/c$ controls every formula. So most results are given both ways. To restore $c$, replace $t$ by $ct$, every velocity by $v/c$, and insert factors of $c$ by dimensional analysis; the rim Lorentz factor is $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, which with $c = 1$ reads $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2)^{-1/2} = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2}$ with $v = r\omega$.

> [!warning] Convention: Gourgoulhon's opposite signature
> The source for this chapter, Gourgoulhon's *Special Relativity in General Frames* (Chapter 13), uses the **mostly-plus** signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, in which his four-velocity satisfies $\vec u\cdot\vec u = -1$. Every scalar product, norm, and orthogonality relation transcribed here has had its overall sign flipped: his timelike condition $\vec X\cdot\vec X < 0$ becomes our $X\cdot X > 0$, and his $\overrightarrow{A_1A_2}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_1A_2} = -4c^2T^2(1 - \tilde V^2/c^2) < 0$ becomes our $\overrightarrow{A_1A_2}\cdot\overrightarrow{A_1A_2} = +4c^2T^2(1 - \tilde V^2/c^2) > 0$. The *magnitudes* $\omega = \|\vec\omega\|$ and $V = \|\vec V\|$ are positive in either convention and are carried over unchanged. Be vigilant about signs in every transcribed formula.

- $\mathcal{O}$ — the **central uniformly rotating observer**, on the rotation axis; four-velocity $U$, proper time $t$, four-acceleration zero
- $\mathcal{O}_*$ — the **inertial observer** sharing $\mathcal{O}$'s worldline; carries the non-rotating inertial frame $(e^*_\alpha)$ and the inertial coordinates $(t, x_*, y_*, z_*)$
- $\mathcal{O}'$ — a **corotating observer**, fixed on the disk at constant $(r,\varphi)$ relative to $\mathcal{O}$; four-velocity $U'$, proper time $t'$
- $\vec\omega$ — the **four-rotation** vector of $\mathcal{O}$, spacelike, with $U\cdot\vec\omega = 0$; magnitude $\omega = \|\vec\omega\|$; $\vec\omega = \omega\, e^*_3$ along the axis
- $(r, \varphi)$ — cylindrical coordinates labelling a corotating observer; constant along $\mathcal{O}'$'s worldline; **mere labels**, not direct physical measures
- $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$ — the velocity of $\mathcal{O}'$ relative to the inertial observer $\mathcal{O}_*$; $\vec n = -\sin\varphi\, e_1 + \cos\varphi\, e_2$ is the azimuthal unit vector (spacelike)
- $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ — the **Lorentz factor** of $\mathcal{O}'$ relative to $\mathcal{O}$ (equivalently $\mathcal{O}_*$)
- $(e'_\alpha) = (U', e'_1, e'_2, e'_3)$ — the local frame of $\mathcal{O}'$; $e'_2$ points radially **inward** (towards the axis), $e'_3 = e_3 = e^*_3$ along $\vec\omega$
- $\vec a'$, $\vec\omega'$ — the four-acceleration and four-rotation of the corotating observer $\mathcal{O}'$: $\vec a' = (\Gamma^2/c^2) r\omega^2\, e'_2$ and $\vec\omega' = \Gamma^2\vec\omega$
- $\times_U$ — the **cross product in the local rest space** of an observer with four-velocity $U$, defined via the spacetime Levi-Civita tensor $\epsilon$ by $\vec a\times_U\vec b = \epsilon(U,\vec a,\vec b,\cdot)$ raised
- $d\vec\ell$ — the **proper separation vector** between two neighbouring corotating observers in $\mathcal{O}$'s rest space; $\mathscr{C}$ a closed curve of corotating observers in the reference space $R_{\mathcal{O}}$
- $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}}$ — the **clock-desynchronization gap** accumulated around a closed loop $\mathscr{C}$; for small velocities $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} \simeq (2/c^2)\,\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$
- $\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ — the **area vector** of the surface enclosed by $\mathscr{C}$, oriented by the sense of traversal
- $L'$, $R'$ — the circumference and radius of the rotating disk as measured by corotating observers: $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R'$, with $R' = R$ unchanged
- $\Delta t'$ — the **Sagnac delay**, the difference in arrival times (per the emitting corotating observer) of a prograde and a retrograde signal sent once around the disk: $\Delta t' = 4\omega A/c^2$ for a circular loop of enclosed area $A$
- $\Delta\phi$ — the Sagnac **phase shift** in an interferometer, $\Delta\phi = (8\pi/c\lambda)\,\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ for light of wavelength $\lambda$
- Greek indices $\alpha,\beta,\dots$ run $0$–$3$; Latin $i,j,\dots$ run $1$–$3$; repeated index once up once down is summed

# Motivation

A disk is spinning, and you are standing on its rim. You feel pressed outward, your wristwatch runs slow compared to the friend standing at the hub, and — this is the strange part — if you and your fellow rim-dwellers lay tape measures end to end around the edge, you find the circumference is *more* than $2\pi$ times the radius. Nothing in your immediate experience is moving faster than light; the rim speed $r\omega$ is a perfectly ordinary fraction of $c$. Yet your rotating world is not Euclidean, your clocks cannot be made to agree, and a beam of light sent clockwise around the rim comes back at a measurably different instant from one sent counter-clockwise. These are not illusions or instrument faults. They are the genuine geometry of a rotating reference frame, and working them out is the business of this chapter.

The previous chapter studied observers whose four-rotation vanishes but whose four-acceleration does not — the linearly accelerated, the hyperbolic-motion observers. Here we study the complementary case: observers whose four-rotation $\vec\omega$ is nonzero. The cleanest realization is the **rotating disk**, a congruence of **corotating observers** all turning rigidly about a common axis at angular velocity $\omega$. The central observer $\mathcal{O}$ on the axis is inertial — it goes nowhere — but carries a *spinning frame*, like an astronaut tumbling in place; the off-axis observers $\mathcal{O}'$ are genuinely in circular motion, accelerating centripetally and dragging their own spinning frames along. The kinematics splits cleanly: a corotating observer has a four-acceleration (pointing inward, the relativistic centripetal acceleration) *and* a four-rotation (the rate at which its gyroscopes precess), and computing both is the first task.

The single structural fact from which the rest of the chapter flows is the failure of clock synchronization. Two neighbouring corotating observers *can* synchronize their clocks by the Einstein–Poincaré radar procedure, and they get a definite answer: to keep them simultaneous, their coordinate-time readings must differ by

$$
dt \;=\; \Gamma^2\,\frac{\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell}{c^2},
$$

where $d\vec\ell$ is the proper vector joining them. This is harmless locally. But carry the synchronization step by step around a closed loop on the disk and the little gaps accumulate; when you return to your starting observer, your clock disagrees with the one that stayed behind by $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \frac{1}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell \ne 0$. **There is no way to assign a single consistent time to the whole rotating disk.** Everything dramatic in this chapter — the Ehrenfest paradox, the Sagnac effect, the corrections that keep GPS and International Atomic Time honest — is this one nonzero loop integral wearing different clothes.

Two of those costumes deserve top billing. The **Ehrenfest paradox** is the observation that the disk's rest geometry is non-Euclidean: corotating observers measure a circumference $L' = 2\pi R'/\sqrt{1 - (R'\omega/c)^2} > 2\pi R'$ while the radius $R'$ is unchanged, so the ratio of circumference to radius exceeds $2\pi$. A flat disk, measured from within its own rotating rest frame, behaves like a curved surface — and this is the first place in physics where acceleration manifestly produces non-Euclidean geometry, a direct seed of the curved spacetime of [[Special Relativity XXV — Toward Relativistic Gravitation|general relativity]]. The **Sagnac effect** is the time difference $\Delta t' = 4\omega A/c^2$ between counter-propagating signals, independent of the nature of the signal and even of its speed, with the $c^2$ coming not from any propagation velocity but from the Minkowski metric itself. It is the working principle of every ring-laser and fibre-optic gyroscope flying in an aircraft or guiding a missile today.

The reader is assumed to have worked through [[Special Relativity VI — Observers, Local Rest Spaces and Local Frames|observers and local frames]] — in particular the [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation|four-rotation]] $\vec\omega$, the [[Def - Observer and Local Rest Space|local rest space]], and [[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity|Einstein–Poincaré simultaneity]] — and through [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|accelerated observers]], whose machinery for the non-inertial frame and the desynchronization integral carries over almost verbatim. The four-velocity, [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity|Lorentz factor]], and law of velocity composition from [[Special Relativity VII — Kinematics I, Motion Relative to an Observer|Kinematics I]] and [[Special Relativity VIII — Kinematics II, Change of Observer|Kinematics II]] are used freely. A passing familiarity with Stokes' theorem (the desynchronization gap is a circulation, equal by Stokes to a flux of vorticity) makes the small-velocity formulas transparent.

# Concept Map

## §17.1 The Rotating Disk and Corotating Observers

- **[[Def - Measurement of Rotation (Non-rotating Observer)]]**
	- An observer is **non-rotating** if and only if the spatial vectors of its local frame are Fermi–Walker transported along its worldline, equivalently if and only if its four-rotation $\vec\omega$ vanishes. Such an observer is physically realized by carrying three free gyroscopes in orthogonal directions and aligning each spatial axis with one gyroscope's spin (the spin of a free gyroscope is Fermi–Walker transported). The four-rotation $\vec\omega$ of *any* observer $\mathcal{O}$ is then measurable as the rate at which $\mathcal{O}$'s own spatial frame rotates relative to a non-rotating frame $\mathcal{O}'$ on the same worldline: $D_{\mathcal{O}'}e_i = \vec\omega\times_U e_i$. A single gyroscope is not enough — it constrains only the component of $\vec\omega$ perpendicular to its spin.
- **[[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer]]**
	- A uniformly rotating observer $\mathcal{O}$ is one with **vanishing four-acceleration** ($\vec a = 0$, hence an inertial straight worldline) and **constant four-rotation** ($\vec\omega = \text{const}$). It sits on the rotation axis; an inertial observer $\mathcal{O}_*$ shares its worldline with $\vec\omega = \omega e^*_3$. The corotating observers $\mathcal{O}'$ are those fixed on the disk at constant cylindrical $(r,\varphi)$ relative to $\mathcal{O}$; their inertial worldlines are helices $x_*(t) = r\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, $y_*(t) = r\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$, with velocity $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$ relative to $\mathcal{O}_*$. The requirement $\|\vec V\| < c$ forces $r < c/\omega$: a corotating observer at $r = c/\omega$ would move at the speed of light, and beyond it the would-be worldline is spacelike.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Frame-Dragging / Rotating Spacetimes of General Relativity *(from General Relativity)*
> A uniformly rotating *congruence* — a family of observers each with the same constant $\vec\omega$, none inertial except the central one — is the flat-spacetime template for the rotating spacetimes of general relativity. In the **Kerr metric** of a spinning black hole, and in the **Lense–Thirring / frame-dragging** effect around any rotating mass, the local inertial frames are themselves dragged into rotation, exactly as the corotating frames here precess relative to the global inertial frame $\mathcal{O}_*$. The four-rotation $\vec\omega'= \Gamma^2\vec\omega$ of an off-axis corotating observer is the special-relativistic ancestor of the angular velocity of the dragged frames.

- **[[Thm - 4-Acceleration and 4-Rotation of the Corotating Observer]]**
	- A corotating observer $\mathcal{O}'$ at radius $r$ has four-acceleration $\vec a' = \dfrac{\Gamma^2}{c^2}r\omega^2\,e'_2$, directed radially **inward** along $e'_2$ — the relativistic centripetal acceleration, with magnitude $\Gamma^2 r\omega^2$ (in $c=1$ units), exceeding the Newtonian $r\omega^2$ by the factor $\Gamma^2$. Its own four-rotation is $\vec\omega' = \Gamma^2\vec\omega$, *larger* than the central $\vec\omega$ by the same $\Gamma^2$: a gyroscope carried by the rim observer precesses faster than one at the hub. Both reduce to their Newtonian values only in the non-relativistic limit $\Gamma\to 1$, and both are constant along $\mathcal{O}'$'s worldline, so $\mathcal{O}'$ is a stationary observer.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Thomas Precession as a Limiting Case *(from Spin Dynamics)*
> The excess $\vec\omega' - \vec\omega = (\Gamma^2 - 1)\vec\omega$ of the corotating observer's four-rotation over the central one is, for a particle in circular orbit, exactly the **Thomas precession** rate of [[Special Relativity XVI — Accelerated Observers|Chapter XVI]]. A gyroscope dragged around a circle, even with no torque, precesses relative to the inertial frame, and the precession is a pure kinematic consequence of the non-commutativity of Lorentz boosts — the same effect that splits atomic spectral lines through the spin–orbit coupling's Thomas factor.

## §17.2 Clock Desynchronization and the Ehrenfest Paradox

- **[[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]**
	- Two *neighbouring* corotating observers can synchronize their clocks by Einstein–Poincaré radar, the condition being $dt = \Gamma^2(\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell)/c^2$ between the coordinate-time readings. But synchronization cannot be made *global*: carried around a closed loop $\mathscr{C}$ of corotating observers, the gaps accumulate to $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \frac{1}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell \ne 0$, the circulation of $\Gamma^2\vec V$. For a loop at constant radius this is $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \pm 2\pi\Gamma r^2\omega/c^2$; for small velocities, by Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$, it is $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} \simeq \frac{2}{c^2}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$. The Hafele–Keating around-the-world clock experiment is a direct measurement of this gap, and correcting for it is what defines International Atomic Time.
- **[[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]**
	- Corotating observers measure the rim circumference of the disk as $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R = 2\pi R/\sqrt{1 - (R\omega/c)^2}$ — the velocity is tangent to the rim, so each little arc is length-contracted relative to the inertial measurement, and from *within* the rotating frame the contraction makes the circumference *larger* than $2\pi R$. The radius, being perpendicular to the motion, is uncontracted: $R' = R$. Hence $L' = 2\pi R'/\sqrt{1 - (R'\omega/c)^2} > 2\pi R'$, a **non-Euclidean** relation. Ehrenfest's "paradox" is that a rigid disk cannot be spun up while keeping $L' = L_0 = 2\pi R_0$; the resolution is that no material disk can stay Born-rigid during spin-up (Herglotz–Noether), so the circumference genuinely stretches.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Equivalence Principle and Curved Space *(from General Relativity)*
> The non-Euclidean rest geometry of the rotating disk is, historically and conceptually, the doorway from special to general relativity. Einstein reasoned: by the **equivalence principle**, the centrifugal field on the disk is locally indistinguishable from a gravitational field; and on the disk the spatial geometry measured by corotating rulers is non-Euclidean. Therefore *gravity bends the geometry of space*, and a flat Minkowski metric cannot describe a gravitational field. The rotating disk is the simplest, fully special-relativistic example in which acceleration forces $C/R \ne 2\pi$, and it is the reason the metric of a general spacetime must be a position-dependent $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ rather than the constant $\eta_{\mu\nu}$.

- **[[Ex - The line element on the rotating disk]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Transform the Minkowski line element into the corotating coordinates $(t, r, \varphi)$ and read off the spatial metric measured by corotating observers, recovering $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R$ and the non-Euclidean circumference-to-radius ratio.

## §17.3 The Sagnac Effect

- **[[Thm - The Sagnac Effect]]**
	- A corotating observer who emits two signals at the same event, one prograde (with the rotation) and one retrograde, and lets each travel once around a closed loop, receives them back at *different* times. The **Sagnac delay** is $\Delta t' = t'_+ - t'_- = \frac{2}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$, twice the desynchronization gap, and is always positive (the prograde signal arrives later). For a circular loop $\Delta t' = 4\pi\Gamma r^2\omega/c^2$; for small velocities $\Delta t' \simeq \frac{4}{c^2}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}} = 4\omega A/c^2$. The delay is **independent of the signal speed** — it is the same for light, electrons, neutrons, or atoms — because the $c^2$ comes from the spacetime metric, not from propagation. The proper *travel* times of the two signals are equal; only their arrival times, as dated by the emitting observer, differ.
- **[[Thm - Sagnac Delay and the Optical Sagnac Interferometer]]**
	- Splitting a monochromatic light beam of frequency $f$ at a corotating semi-transparent mirror, sending the halves prograde and retrograde around a closed path, and recombining them produces interference fringes shifted by the phase $\Delta\phi = 2\pi f\,\Delta t' = \frac{8\pi f}{c^2}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}} = \frac{8\pi}{c\lambda}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$. Sagnac measured this in 1913; Michelson, Gale, and Pearson measured the phase shift due to the *Earth's* rotation in 1925 with a $613\times339$ m interferometer. For matter waves the phase is $\Delta\phi = 4\Gamma_p\frac{m}{\hbar}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$, larger than the optical phase by $\sim\Gamma_p mc^2/hf \sim 10^8$, which is why atom and neutron interferometers are exquisitely sensitive rotation sensors.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Aharonov–Bohm Effect and Geometric Phase *(from Quantum Mechanics)*
> The matter-wave Sagnac phase $\Delta\phi = 4\Gamma_p(m/\hbar)\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ is a **geometric phase** — it depends only on the enclosed area and the rotation, not on the detailed path or the speed of the particle, and it survives in the limit of slow transport. This is structurally the rotational analogue of the **Aharonov–Bohm effect**, in which a charged particle's phase is shifted by the magnetic flux it encircles; the four-rotation $\vec\omega$ plays the role of the magnetic field and the metric coupling $\Gamma^2\vec V$ the role of the vector potential. Both are line integrals of a connection around a loop, equal by Stokes to a flux through the enclosed surface.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The problems of this chapter chase a recognizable handful of goals. The first is to **compute the kinematic invariants of a corotating observer** — its four-velocity, its centripetal four-acceleration $\vec a'$, its four-rotation $\vec\omega'$ — given only the radius $r$ and the angular velocity $\omega$. These are pure substitutions once the helical worldline is written down, and the entire art is keeping track of which frame ($\mathcal{O}$, $\mathcal{O}_*$, or $\mathcal{O}'$) measures which quantity, and remembering that the relativistic answers carry factors of $\Gamma^2$ that the Newtonian ones lack. A second target is to **establish the impossibility of some global construction** — a single time coordinate on the disk, a Born-rigid spin-up, a consistent simultaneity slicing — by showing that a certain loop integral does not vanish. This is the chapter's signature move: a quantity that can be made to vanish locally (a synchronization gap, a length mismatch) fails to vanish globally because its line integral around a closed loop is a nonzero circulation. A third target is to **compute a measurable physical effect** — the Ehrenfest circumference excess, the Hafele–Keating clock difference, the Sagnac delay, the interferometer phase shift — and to express it in the form most useful to an experimenter, typically as $\propto\omega A/c^2$ with $A$ the enclosed area. A fourth, more conceptual, is to **resolve an apparent paradox** by locating the false premise: Ehrenfest's assumes rigidity, and the resolution is that rigidity fails. A fifth is to **trace a relativistic effect to its source in the metric versus its source in dynamics** — the Sagnac $c^2$ is metric, not propagation; the desynchronization is geometric, not a clock malfunction — a distinction the chapter sharpens at every turn.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **A uniform rotation is given** — a constant $\omega$ and a constant $\vec\omega = \omega e^*_3$ — which immediately supplies the helical worldlines, the velocity $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$, and the rim Lorentz factor $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. This is the richest source: from it everything else is computed. **A corotating observer is specified by its radius $r$** (and azimuth $\varphi$, a mere label), which fixes its speed $r\omega$ and hence its $\Gamma$, $\vec a'$, $\vec\omega'$, and proper-time rate. **A closed loop of corotating observers is given** — and then the key quantity is a circulation $\oint\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$, which Stokes' theorem converts to a flux of $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$ through the enclosed area, yielding the universal $\propto\omega A$ form. **The equality of the two signal speeds relative to corotating observers is given** ($v_+ = v_-$, automatically $c$ for light by the local invariance of the speed of light) — this is exactly what makes the Sagnac delay nonzero and independent of the signal. **A measurement is specified as made by corotating rulers or clocks**, as opposed to the inertial $\mathcal{O}_*$ — and the difference between the two is the whole content of the Ehrenfest and synchronization results. The recurring move is to route a source to a target: a given $\omega$ and $r$ route through the helix to the kinematic invariants; a given loop routes through the circulation and Stokes to a $\propto\omega A$ effect; a given pair of counter-propagating signals routes through the desynchronization gap to the Sagnac delay. The [[Special Relativity XVII — Rotating Observers#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves nearly every problem in the chapter is assembled from. The recurring difficulty is not the algebra but the bookkeeping of *which observer measures what*, so each operation names its frame explicitly. When stuck, scan the list and try each.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Write the corotating worldline as a helix in inertial coordinates.** A corotating observer at radius $r$, azimuth $\varphi$ has inertial coordinates $x_*(t) = r\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, $y_*(t) = r\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$, $z_* = 0$; see [[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer]]. *Trigger:* any quantity of a corotating observer is wanted (velocity, acceleration, proper time). *Pattern:* differentiate the helix with respect to inertial time $t$ to get $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$, then again, with the relativistic chain rule $dt = \Gamma\,d\tau$, to get the four-acceleration. The helix is the single object from which all corotating kinematics is read.

2. **Compute the rim Lorentz factor first.** Every formula in the chapter carries $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, the [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity|Lorentz factor]] of the corotating observer at radius $r$ relative to the central one. *Trigger:* a radius $r$ and angular velocity $\omega$ are given. *Pattern:* compute $\Gamma$ once; it is the currency in which the four-acceleration ($\Gamma^2 r\omega^2$), the four-rotation ($\Gamma^2\omega$), the proper-time rate ($t' = \Gamma^{-1}t$), and the contracted circumference ($\Gamma\,2\pi R$) are all paid.

3. **Use the local Einstein–Poincaré synchronization condition.** Two neighbouring corotating observers are simultaneous when their coordinate times differ by $dt = \Gamma^2(\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell)/c^2$; see [[Def - Einstein-Poincaré Simultaneity]] and [[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]. *Trigger:* a synchronization or simultaneity question between nearby disk points. *Pattern:* the condition is the orthogonality $U'\cdot\overrightarrow{A_{(\lambda)}A_{(\lambda+d\lambda)}} = 0$ of the corotating four-velocity to the separation; expand it and the $dt$ formula falls out.

4. **Integrate the local gap around a closed loop to test for a global obstruction.** Whenever a quantity vanishes locally but is wanted globally, compute $\oint_{\mathscr{C}}$ of the local increment; see [[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]. *Trigger:* "can the disk be globally synchronized / spun up rigidly / sliced by simultaneity surfaces?" *Pattern:* the answer is no exactly when the loop integral is nonzero, and the nonzero value is the measurable defect. This is the chapter's master move.

5. **Convert a circulation to a flux by Stokes' theorem.** For small velocities the circulation $\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$ equals the flux $\int_S\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V\cdot d\vec{\mathcal{A}}$, and for rigid rotation $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$. *Trigger:* a loop integral is wanted in closed form and $r\omega\ll c$. *Pattern:* the circulation becomes $2\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$, giving every effect the universal area-proportional form $\propto\omega A/c^2$.

6. **Measure a length with corotating rulers, accounting for contraction.** A tangential proper length on the disk is $d\ell' = \Gamma\,d\ell = \Gamma r\,d\varphi$ (contracted along the motion), while a radial proper length is unchanged, $d\ell' = dr$; see [[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]. *Trigger:* a circumference or radius measured *on the disk* (not by the inertial observer). *Pattern:* integrate $d\ell'$ to get $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R$ tangentially but $R' = R$ radially — the non-Euclidean mismatch.

7. **Apply the relativistic velocity-composition law to a signal on the disk.** The speed $v$ of a signal relative to a corotating observer composes with the rim speed $r\omega$ to give its angular velocity $\Omega_\pm$ in the inertial frame: $r\Omega_+ = (v + r\omega)/(1 + r\omega v/c^2)$; see [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition]]. *Trigger:* the time for a prograde or retrograde signal to circle the disk is wanted. *Pattern:* solve $\Omega_\pm t_{B_\pm} = \omega t_{B_\pm} \pm 2\pi$ for the return time, then difference the two.

8. **Equate the two signal speeds relative to corotating observers.** For the Sagnac effect, assume $v_+ = v_- =: v$ (automatically $c$ for light, by the local invariance of $c$ for *any* observer, accelerated or rotating); see [[Thm - The Sagnac Effect]]. *Trigger:* counter-propagating signals of the same type. *Pattern:* this single equality makes the proper travel times equal and the arrival-time difference equal to twice the desynchronization gap — and it is why the $c^2$ in the answer is metric, not kinematic.

9. **Translate the frequency of a wave through the Planck–Einstein relation for matter waves.** For a matter-wave interferometer, the relevant frequency in $\Delta\phi = 2\pi f\,\Delta t'$ is the de Broglie frequency $f = E/h = \Gamma_p mc^2/h$; see [[Thm - Sagnac Delay and the Optical Sagnac Interferometer]]. *Trigger:* a Sagnac phase shift for massive particles. *Pattern:* substitute $f = \Gamma_p mc^2/h$ to get $\Delta\phi = 4\Gamma_p(m/\hbar)\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$, vastly larger than the optical phase.

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Assuming a global time coordinate exists on the rotating disk
> It is overwhelmingly tempting to suppose that because each corotating observer carries a clock, and any two neighbours can synchronize, the whole disk has a consistent time. It does not. Synchronizing neighbour-to-neighbour around a closed loop returns you to your starting observer with a clock offset $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \pm 2\pi\Gamma r^2\omega/c^2 \ne 0$ ([[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]). The local synchronization is a closed one-form only in the inertial case; on the disk it has nonzero "curl" $2\vec\omega$, so its loop integral is path-dependent. This becomes legal exactly when $\omega = 0$ — for a non-rotating array, the local gaps integrate to zero and a global time exists. The same obstruction is why GPS clocks and International Atomic Time must add an explicit Sagnac correction term, never simply average local readings.

> [!warning] 2. Assuming the rotating disk stays Born-rigid during spin-up
> Ehrenfest's paradox is generated entirely by the unstated assumption that a disk, set spinning from rest, keeps all proper distances between its parts fixed — Born rigidity — so that its circumference stays $L_0 = 2\pi R_0$. But the corotating-frame circumference is $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R > 2\pi R$ ([[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]), contradicting $L' = L_0$. The resolution is that **no material disk can be spun up Born-rigidly**: the Herglotz–Noether theorem forbids it, and physically the rim must stretch tangentially (acquiring elastic stress) while the radius stays fixed. Assuming rigidity is the smuggled-in falsehood. The rigid-rod intuition becomes legal only for motions that *do* admit a Born-rigid realization — uniform translation, or the already-rotating stationary state, but never the transition between rest and rotation.

> [!warning] 3. Reading the coordinate label $r\,d\varphi$ as a physical arc length
> It is natural to treat the cylindrical coordinates $(r,\varphi)$ of a corotating observer as ordinary polar coordinates, so that an arc subtends physical length $r\,d\varphi$. This is wrong: $(r,\varphi)$ are *labels*, and the proper arc length measured by corotating rulers is $d\ell' = \Gamma r\,d\varphi \ne r\,d\varphi$ ([[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]). The discrepancy is the $\Gamma$ factor, and it is exactly the non-Euclidean content: the coordinate circumference is $2\pi r$ but the measured one is $\Gamma\,2\pi r$. This becomes legal only at $\omega = 0$ or on the axis $r = 0$, where $\Gamma = 1$ and labels coincide with measures. Confusing the two is the error that makes the Ehrenfest geometry look paradoxical instead of merely non-Euclidean.

> [!warning] 4. Attributing the Sagnac $c^2$ to the propagation speed of the signal
> Because the Sagnac delay $\Delta t' = 4\omega A/c^2$ carries a $c^2$, and because the canonical Sagnac experiment uses light, it is tempting to read $c$ as the speed of the light and conclude the effect is about how light propagates. It is not. The delay is **independent of the signal speed** — identical for light, electrons, neutrons, and atoms ([[Thm - The Sagnac Effect]], Remark) — and the $c^2$ comes from the Minkowski metric (the conversion between time and space), not from any velocity. The give-away is that for a matter wave one must *not* replace the $c$ by the particle's phase velocity $v$; the correct matter-wave formula $\Delta\phi = (8\pi v/c^2\lambda)\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ keeps the metric $c^2$. Treating the $c$ as propagation speed gives the wrong matter-wave phase by a factor $(c/v)^2$.

> [!warning] 5. Expecting a Newtonian limit of the desynchronization or Sagnac effect
> The small-velocity formulas $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} \simeq (2/c^2)\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ and $\Delta t' \simeq (4/c^2)\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ look like they might be Newtonian limits, recovered as $c\to\infty$. They are not Newtonian at all: in genuine Newtonian physics all observers share one absolute time, so $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = 0$ identically ([[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]], Remark). The tell is the surviving $c^2$ in the denominator — a true Newtonian formula contains no $c$. These are *small-velocity approximations to a genuinely relativistic effect*, not the $c\to\infty$ classical limit, and the distinction matters: setting $c\to\infty$ kills the effect, whereas $r\omega\ll c$ merely simplifies it.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won by first deciding which of three things is being asked — a kinematic quantity of a single corotating observer, a global obstruction tested by a loop integral, or a measurable counter-propagation effect — and then routing the given data through the standard machinery for that type. As always in relativity, the bookkeeping of frames is where errors live, so begin by naming the three observers in play: the central inertial $\mathcal{O}$, its inertial twin $\mathcal{O}_*$ carrying the non-rotating frame, and the off-axis corotating $\mathcal{O}'$ whose measurements differ from the inertial ones.

If the problem **asks for a kinematic invariant of a corotating observer** — its four-acceleration, four-rotation, or proper-time rate — write down the helical worldline $x_*(t) = r\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, $y_*(t) = r\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$ and differentiate. The velocity relative to $\mathcal{O}_*$ is $\vec V = r\omega\,\vec n$ with $\|\vec V\| = r\omega$, so the [[Def - Lorentz Factor and Relative Velocity|Lorentz factor]] is $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$; compute it first, because every subsequent answer is a multiple of a power of $\Gamma$. The four-acceleration is centripetal, $\vec a' = (\Gamma^2/c^2)r\omega^2 e'_2$ pointing inward, larger than Newton's $r\omega^2$ by $\Gamma^2$; the four-rotation is $\vec\omega' = \Gamma^2\vec\omega$, again $\Gamma^2$ times the central value ([[Thm - 4-Acceleration and 4-Rotation of the Corotating Observer]]). The single conceptual point is that an off-axis corotating observer is *not* inertial — it accelerates and its gyroscopes precess — even though the disk turns rigidly, and the precession excess over the central observer is the Thomas precession.

If the problem **asks whether some global structure can exist on the disk** — a single time, a rigid spin-up, a coherent simultaneity slicing — do not try to construct it; instead test the obstruction by a loop integral. The local synchronization condition $dt = \Gamma^2(\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell)/c^2$ can always be satisfied between neighbours, but its integral around a closed loop $\mathscr{C}$ is the nonzero circulation $\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$ ([[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]). When the velocities are small, convert this circulation to a flux by Stokes' theorem and use $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$ to get the universal answer $\propto\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$. The reason this strategy is forced is structural: the obstruction is the failure of a certain one-form to be exact, and the cleanest detector of non-exactness is a closed-loop integral. The Ehrenfest paradox is the same strategy applied to *length* rather than *time*: spin-up cannot preserve all proper lengths because the tangential ones must grow by $\Gamma$ while the radial ones do not, and no Born-rigid motion reconciles this ([[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]).

If the problem **asks for a counter-propagation effect** — the Sagnac delay or interferometer phase — the route is through the difference of return times of a prograde and a retrograde signal. The clean derivation writes the two signal worldlines as helices with inertial angular velocities $\Omega_+ > 0$ and $\Omega_- < 0$, solves $\Omega_\pm t_{B_\pm} = \omega t_{B_\pm} \pm 2\pi$ for the return times, and differences them; the relativistic [[Thm - Relativistic Velocity Addition|velocity-composition law]] supplies $\Omega_\pm$ from the common signal speed $v$ relative to the disk ([[Thm - The Sagnac Effect]]). The decisive assumption is $v_+ = v_-$ — true for light by the local invariance of $c$, and assumed for matter — which makes the proper travel times equal and the *arrival*-time difference equal to twice the desynchronization gap. For the interferometer, multiply by $2\pi f$ to get the phase; for matter waves, the frequency is the de Broglie frequency $f = \Gamma_p mc^2/h$, which makes the matter-wave phase enormous ([[Thm - Sagnac Delay and the Optical Sagnac Interferometer]]).

Threading through all of this is one meta-principle: **on a rotating disk, the geometry of the corotating frame is not flat, and almost every effect is a loop integral of that non-flatness.** The desynchronization gap, the Ehrenfest circumference excess, and the Sagnac delay are three readings of the same underlying fact — that the corotating congruence has nonzero vorticity $\vec\omega$, and the circulation of the velocity around any loop is twice the enclosed vorticity flux. Every question in this chapter is, at bottom, the question "what does the vorticity of the rotating congruence do when you integrate it around a loop?"

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Def - Uniformly Rotating Observer|The helical worldline and the rim Lorentz factor]]**: a corotating observer at radius $r$ traces $x_*(t) = r\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, $y_*(t) = r\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$, with speed $r\omega$ relative to the inertial frame and Lorentz factor $\Gamma = (1 - r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. **Typical use:** this is the single object from which all corotating kinematics is computed — differentiate once for the velocity, twice for the centripetal four-acceleration, and divide proper-time increments by $\Gamma$ to relate disk clocks to the central clock. Reach for it the instant a problem mentions a point fixed on a rotating disk; the bound $r < c/\omega$ that it forces (no corotating observer can sit beyond the light cylinder) is itself a frequently-used fact.

- **[[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk|The desynchronization circulation]]**: the gap accumulated by synchronizing clocks around a closed loop is $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \frac{1}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$, which for small velocities is $\frac{2}{c^2}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$. **Typical use:** this is the chapter's universal obstruction. Whenever you must decide if a global construction (a time coordinate, a rigid motion, a simultaneity slicing) is possible, compute this loop integral; nonzero means impossible, and the value is the measurable defect. It underlies the Hafele–Keating clock asymmetry, the GPS Sagnac correction, and — doubled — the Sagnac delay itself, so recognizing a problem as "a loop integral of the rotating velocity" routes it immediately to this formula.

- **[[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox|The non-Euclidean disk geometry]]**: corotating observers measure $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R'$ with $R' = R$, so the circumference-to-radius ratio is $\Gamma\,2\pi > 2\pi$. **Typical use:** this is the reusable statement that *acceleration produces non-Euclidean spatial geometry*, the special-relativistic seed of curved spacetime. Reach for it whenever a problem contrasts coordinate labels with proper measurements on the disk (the labels give $2\pi r$, the rulers give $\Gamma\,2\pi r$), and whenever the conceptual bridge to general relativity is wanted — the rotating disk is the historically decisive example that forced Einstein to a position-dependent metric.

- **[[Thm - The Sagnac Effect|The Sagnac delay]]**: counter-propagating signals around a loop return separated by $\Delta t' = \frac{2}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell = 4\omega A/c^2$ (small velocity), independent of the signal speed. **Typical use:** this is the rotation-sensing workhorse. Its recognizable setup is any pair of signals sent in opposite senses around a closed path on a rotating platform; the reusable content is that the delay is purely geometric ($\propto\omega A$, metric $c^2$, no propagation speed) and hence the same for any signal. Combine it with $\Delta\phi = 2\pi f\,\Delta t'$ for an interferometer phase, and with the Planck–Einstein relation for the matter-wave enhancement; it is the principle of every fibre-optic and ring-laser gyroscope.

- **The vorticity identity $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$**: for a rigidly rotating congruence, the curl of the velocity field equals twice the four-rotation vector. **Typical use:** this is what turns every circulation in the chapter into an area-proportional flux via Stokes' theorem, producing the universal $\propto\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ form of the desynchronization gap and the Sagnac delay. Recognize its applicability whenever a loop integral of the rotating velocity appears and the velocities are small; it is the bridge from the path-dependent line integral to the clean enclosed-area answer, and it makes manifest that the rotation $\vec\omega$ is the local source of all the global effects.

# Bridges

1. **General relativity — the rotating disk as the gateway to curved space.** The non-Euclidean rest geometry of the rotating disk, $L' = \Gamma\,2\pi R' > 2\pi R'$ ([[Def - The Ehrenfest Paradox]]), is the historically decisive example that led Einstein from special to general relativity. The reasoning is a two-step bridge. First, by the **equivalence principle**, the centrifugal field experienced by corotating observers is locally indistinguishable from a uniform gravitational field — an observer in a sealed box cannot tell whether they are on a spinning disk or in a gravitational field with the appropriate gradient. Second, the spatial geometry on the disk, as measured by corotating rulers, is genuinely non-Euclidean: the ratio of a circle's circumference to its radius exceeds $2\pi$. Combining the two, Einstein concluded that a gravitational field must bend the geometry of space itself, and therefore that gravity cannot be described by the flat constant metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ but requires a position-dependent metric $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ on a curved manifold — the central object of [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild|general relativity]]. The rotating disk is thus the simplest fully special-relativistic system in which acceleration forces curvature, and the precise carrier of the analogy is the spatial metric induced on the corotating congruence's rest space, which has nonzero Gaussian curvature even though the spacetime is flat. The chapter [[Special Relativity XXV — Toward Relativistic Gravitation|Toward Relativistic Gravitation]] develops this bridge in full.

2. **Fluid dynamics and exterior calculus — the desynchronization gap is a circulation, and the rotation is vorticity.** The corotating congruence is a velocity field on $\mathcal{O}$'s rest space, and its four-rotation $\vec\omega$ is precisely the **vorticity** of that field: $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$, the familiar relation that the curl of a rigid-rotation velocity field is twice the angular velocity. The desynchronization gap $\Delta t'_{\text{desync}} = \frac{1}{c^2\Gamma_{(0)}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}}\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$ ([[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]) is the **circulation** of $\Gamma^2\vec V$ around the loop $\mathscr{C}$, and Stokes' theorem converts it to the flux of the vorticity through the enclosed area — exactly as Kelvin's circulation theorem in fluid mechanics relates the circulation around a loop to the vorticity flux it encloses. This is not a loose analogy: the synchronization one-form $\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$ is a genuine differential one-form on $R_{\mathcal{O}}$, its failure to be closed (its nonzero exterior derivative) is the obstruction to global synchronization, and the exterior derivative is the vorticity two-form. The impossibility of global synchronization is therefore the statement that this one-form is closed but not exact on the multiply-connected disk-minus-axis — a de Rham cohomology fact, the rotational twin of the Aharonov–Bohm flux.

3. **Quantum mechanics — the matter-wave Sagnac phase is a geometric (Aharonov–Bohm-type) phase.** The matter-wave Sagnac phase $\Delta\phi = 4\Gamma_p\frac{m}{\hbar}\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ ([[Thm - Sagnac Delay and the Optical Sagnac Interferometer]]) is a **geometric phase**: it depends only on the enclosed area and the rotation, not on the particle's speed or the detailed path, and it persists in the slow-transport limit. This is structurally identical to the **Aharonov–Bohm effect**, in which the phase of a charged particle's wavefunction is shifted by $\frac{q}{\hbar}\oint\vec A\cdot d\vec\ell = \frac{q}{\hbar}\Phi_B$, the magnetic flux it encircles, even when the particle travels through field-free regions. The dictionary is exact: the four-rotation $\vec\omega$ plays the role of the magnetic field $\vec B$, the metric coupling $\Gamma^2\vec V$ plays the role of the vector potential $\vec A$, and the enclosed-area flux $\vec\omega\cdot\vec{\mathcal{A}}$ plays the role of the magnetic flux $\Phi_B$. Both phases are line integrals of a connection around a closed loop, both equal a flux through the enclosed surface by Stokes' theorem, and both are observable only through interference, not through any local measurement — the defining signature of a geometric phase. The Sagnac effect is, in this light, the gravomagnetic / rotational Aharonov–Bohm effect.

4. **Inertial navigation and metrology — the Sagnac effect runs gyroscopes, GPS, and atomic time.** The abstract loop integral of this chapter is, in practice, one of the most consequential pieces of applied physics. The Sagnac delay $\Delta t' = 4\omega A/c^2$ is the operating principle of the **ring-laser gyroscope** (a helium–neon laser in an annular cavity, where the rotation splits the frequencies of the clockwise and counter-clockwise cavity modes and the beat frequency measures $\omega$) and the **fibre-optic gyroscope** (where the light traverses a coil of fibre wound $N$ times, multiplying the enclosed area and hence the phase by $N$) — together the heart of every modern inertial navigation system, since they have no moving parts and never drift mechanically. The same desynchronization gap is why the **Global Positioning System** must apply an explicit Sagnac correction when comparing signals from satellites, and why **International Atomic Time** (TAI) is defined by correcting each ground atomic clock — which reads the proper time of a corotating observer on the spinning Earth — to the central inertial Geocentric Coordinate Time, adding the integral of the synchronization gap ([[Thm - Impossibility of Global Clock Synchronization on a Rotating Disk]]). The Hafele–Keating experiment, flying atomic clocks east and west around the Earth and finding the predicted asymmetric desynchronization, is the direct laboratory confirmation. What looks like a recondite cohomological obstruction is, in the end, what keeps satellite navigation and global timekeeping consistent.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: every effect in this chapter is the vorticity of the rotating congruence, integrated around a loop.** It is tempting to see the desynchronization gap, the Ehrenfest circumference excess, and the Sagnac delay as three separate phenomena that happen to all involve rotation. They are not separate; they are one fact read three ways. The corotating congruence is a velocity field with nonzero vorticity $\mathrm{curl}\,\vec V = 2\vec\omega$, and the synchronization one-form $\Gamma^2\vec V\cdot d\vec\ell$ is closed locally but not globally — its loop integral does not vanish, precisely because its exterior derivative is the vorticity. The desynchronization gap is this loop integral; the Sagnac delay is twice it (prograde and retrograde signals pick up the gap with opposite signs); and the Ehrenfest excess is the spatial analogue, the failure of the tangential proper-length one-form to integrate consistently because the rim must stretch by $\Gamma$. Once you see that the chapter is *about* the vorticity of a rotating frame and what happens when you integrate it around a closed curve, every formula becomes predictable: there must be a circulation, it must equal an enclosed flux of $2\vec\omega$ by Stokes, and the answer must therefore be $\propto\omega A$. The rotation $\vec\omega$ is the local source; the loop integral is the global symptom.

**The true name of the Sagnac effect is "the synchronization gap, doubled".** The Sagnac effect is usually introduced through light beams and interferometers, which makes it look like a statement about how light propagates. The operational truth is cleaner and more general: the Sagnac delay is exactly twice the clock-desynchronization gap, $\Delta t' = 2\Delta t'_{\text{desync}}$, and it has nothing to do with the speed or nature of the signal. A prograde signal accumulates the gap $+\Delta t'_{\text{desync}}$, a retrograde signal accumulates $-\Delta t'_{\text{desync}}$, and their arrival-time difference is the sum of the magnitudes. This is why the effect is identical for light, electrons, neutrons, and atoms; why the $c^2$ in $4\omega A/c^2$ is the metric conversion factor and not a propagation speed; and why the proper *travel* times of the two signals are equal while only their *arrival* times (dated by the rotating emitter) differ. When you see a Sagnac problem, do not picture racing light beams — picture the impossibility of synchronizing the clocks around the loop, and the delay is just that impossibility made into an arrival-time difference.

**Relativity makes the rotating disk non-Euclidean, and this is the first crack in flat spacetime.** The deepest lesson of the chapter is conceptual. Special relativity is the physics of flat Minkowski spacetime, and one might expect that flatness to guarantee Euclidean spatial geometry for every observer. It does not. A corotating observer, measuring with their own rulers, finds the circumference of a circle to be $\Gamma\,2\pi$ times its radius — a non-Euclidean relation, in a perfectly flat spacetime. The resolution of the apparent contradiction is subtle and important: the spacetime is flat, but the *rest space* of the rotating congruence is a non-flat three-dimensional slice, because the congruence has vorticity and no family of corotating observers shares a common simultaneity surface. The geometry is non-Euclidean not because spacetime is curved but because the rotating observers cannot agree on what "space at one instant" means. This is the precise sense in which acceleration produces non-Euclidean geometry within special relativity, and it is why the rotating disk was Einstein's stepping stone: it showed that the geometry an accelerated observer measures need not be flat, which made it natural to suppose that a *gravitational* field — equivalent to acceleration — bends geometry too. The constant metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is adequate for inertial observers; the moment you rotate, the geometry you measure demands a position-dependent metric, and that demand is the beginning of general relativity.
