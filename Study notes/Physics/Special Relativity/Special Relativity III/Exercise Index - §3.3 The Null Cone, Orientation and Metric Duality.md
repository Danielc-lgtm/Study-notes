## §3.3 The Null Cone, Orientation and Metric Duality — Exercises

The exercises of §3.3 drill the three structures that complete the Minkowski four-tuple: the **null cone with its time arrow** (and the two lemmas that make the arrow coherent), the **orientation** given by the Levi-Civita volume form, and the **metric duality** that raises and lowers indices. The recurring technique for the causal-structure exercises is again operation 1 — the adapted basis that collapses a scalar product to a single sign — now used to prove the same-sheet criterion $U\cdot V > 0$ and to read $U\cdot V$ as the relative Lorentz factor of two four-velocities. The duality exercise installs the index calculus ("time component unchanged, space components flip sign", with the contraction $X_\mu X^\mu = X\cdot X$ as the error-check), and the orientation exercise warns that in four dimensions a cyclic permutation is odd — the dimension-dependent sign that governs every Hodge-star and four-volume computation.

- [[Ex - The same-sheet criterion for causal vectors via an adapted basis]] (⭐⭐) — re-derive Gourgoulhon's two lemmas in mostly-minus convention by the adapted basis, identify the role of non-collinearity in the null case, and read U·V as the frame-independent relative Lorentz factor γ_rel of two four-velocities ([[Thm - Two Lemmas on Causal Vectors]], [[Def - The Null Cone and the Time Arrow]], [[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]])
- [[Ex - Raising and lowering indices and the dual basis]] (⭐⭐) — lower and raise indices with η, verify the round trip and the contraction check X_μ X^μ = X·X, prove η^{μρ}η_{ρν} = δ^μ_ν, and distinguish the metric-free dual basis from the metric dual ([[Def - Metric Duality and Index Manipulation]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]], [[Def - Four-Vector]])
- [[Ex - Orientation and the parity of Levi-Civita permutations]] (⭐⭐) — compute that a cyclic permutation of the Levi-Civita tensor is odd in four dimensions, show the top antisymmetric forms are one-dimensional so orientation is binary, and separate orientation-reversing reflections from orientation-preserving boosts ([[Def - Spacetime Orientation]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]])
