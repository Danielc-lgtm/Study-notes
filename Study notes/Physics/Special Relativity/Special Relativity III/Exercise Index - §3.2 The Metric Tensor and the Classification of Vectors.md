## §3.2 The Metric Tensor and the Classification of Vectors — Exercises

The exercises of §3.2 drill the indefinite metric and the trichotomy it produces. The unifying technique is operation 1 — align an orthonormal basis with a distinguished timelike vector so the scalar product collapses to a single component — and the unifying conceptual theme is that *indefiniteness is the physics*: it is what allows nonzero null vectors, what makes a vector orthogonal to a timelike vector spacelike (hence what builds an observer's Euclidean rest space), and what *reverses* the Cauchy-Schwarz and triangle inequalities. The classification by the sign of $X\cdot X$ is the cheapest and most-used computation in the chapter, and the reversed inequalities are its signature results — each proved by mimicking the Euclidean argument and tracking where the one minus sign in the signature flips the conclusion.

- [[Ex - Why a nonzero null vector has zero norm]] (⭐) — exhibit a nonzero null vector, contrast with the positive-definite Euclidean case, show the null cone is closed under scaling but not addition, and connect zero interval to light rays joining distinct events ([[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]], [[Def - The Null Cone and the Time Arrow]])
- [[Ex - A vector orthogonal to a timelike vector is spacelike]] (⭐⭐) — prove that any nonzero vector orthogonal to a timelike vector is spacelike, deduce the local rest space is Euclidean (−g positive definite), and show the statement fails for a self-orthogonal null vector ([[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]], [[Def - Metric Duality and Index Manipulation]])
- [[Ex - The reversed Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for timelike vectors]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove the reversed Cauchy-Schwarz inequality |U·V| ≥ ‖U‖‖V‖ for timelike vectors and the reversed triangle inequality for future timelike vectors, and isolate the minus sign in the signature as the reversal mechanism ([[Def - Classification of Four-Vectors]], [[Def - Minkowski Space and the Metric]], [[Thm - Two Lemmas on Causal Vectors]])
