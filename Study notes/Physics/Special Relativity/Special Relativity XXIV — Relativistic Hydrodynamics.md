# Notation Registry

A standing convention for the whole topic: **we set $c = 1$** and use the **mostly-minus signature** $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike vector $X$ has $X \cdot X > 0$ and the fluid four-velocity is normalised to $u \cdot u = +1$. Where a formula is more transparent with $c$ restored, it is given both ways. Two warnings about names. First, **$\rho$ denotes the proper energy density** — the total energy per unit volume measured by a comoving observer, including rest-mass energy — *not* the rest-mass density; this matches the canonical energy–momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$. The rest-mass density, when it is needed (in the dust limit and the nonrelativistic limit), is written $\rho_{\mathrm m}$. Second, the source for this chapter (Gourgoulhon) uses the opposite signature and writes $\varepsilon$ for what we call $\rho$; every formula transcribed below has had its signs flipped accordingly.

- $u$, $u^\mu$ — the **fluid four-velocity** field, a future-directed timelike unit vector field, $u \cdot u = 1$; its field lines are the **fluid lines** (worldlines of fluid particles)
- $\rho$ — the **proper energy density**, the energy per unit volume in the fluid rest frame, $\rho = T(u,u)$
- $p$ — the **pressure**, a scalar field; the fluid stress in the rest frame is the isotropic $p\,\delta_{ij}$
- $\rho_{\mathrm m}$ — the **rest-mass density** $\rho_{\mathrm m} = \sum_a n_a m_a$; appears only in the dust and nonrelativistic limits, where $\rho = \rho_{\mathrm m} c^2 + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}$
- $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}$ — the **internal energy density**, the energy density over and above the rest-mass energy density
- $h$ — the **enthalpy per baryon**, $h = (\rho + p)/n = \mu + TS$; the combination $\rho + p$ is the **proper enthalpy density**
- $n$, $n_a$ — the **proper baryon density** and the proper number density of particle species $a$ (comoving)
- $s$ — the **proper entropy density**; $S = s/n$ is the **entropy per baryon**
- $T$ — the temperature; $\mu$, $\mu_a$ — the chemical potential (per baryon, or of species $a$)
- $j_{\mathrm b}$, $j_{\mathrm b}^\mu = n u^\mu$ — the **baryon four-current**
- $T^{\mu\nu}$ — the **energy–momentum tensor**; $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$ its divergence; $f^\nu$ the external four-force density
- $a$, $a^\mu = u^\nu \nabla_\nu u^\mu$ — the **four-acceleration** of the fluid (of a comoving observer)
- $u_0$, $u_0^\mu$ — the four-velocity of an **arbitrary inertial observer** $\mathcal{O}$; $\Gamma = u \cdot u_0$ the fluid Lorentz factor relative to $\mathcal{O}$
- $E = \Gamma^2(\rho+p) - p$ — the fluid energy density relative to $\mathcal{O}$; $\mathbf{V}$ — the fluid three-velocity relative to $\mathcal{O}$, with $u = \Gamma(u_0 + \mathbf{V})$
- $c_s$ — the **speed of sound**, $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial \rho)_S$
- $\pi$, $\pi_\mu = h u_\mu$ — the **fluid momentum one-form**; $\Omega = d\pi$ — the **vorticity two-form**, $\Omega_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu(h u_\nu) - \partial_\nu(h u_\mu)$
- $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ — the **kinematic vorticity vector**, the curl of $u$ in the fluid rest space
- $\Psi$ — the **velocity potential** of an irrotational flow, $h u = d\Psi$
- $\mathcal{C}$, $C(\mathcal{C}) = \oint_\mathcal{C} \pi$ — a closed curve and the **fluid circulation** around it
- $d$ — the [[Def - The Exterior Derivative|exterior derivative]]; $\nabla$ — the [[Def - The Covariant Derivative|covariant derivative]]; $\star$ — the [[Def - The Hodge Star|Hodge star]]; $\square$ — the d'Alembertian $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$
- Summation convention: a repeated index, once up once down, is summed over $0,1,2,3$ (Greek) or $1,2,3$ (Latin)

> [!warning] Convention
> Gourgoulhon's *Special Relativity in General Frames*, the source for this chapter, uses the **mostly-plus** signature $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, in which the four-velocity satisfies $u \cdot u = -1$, the perfect-fluid tensor reads $T = (\varepsilon + p)\,\underline{u}\otimes\underline{u} + p\,g$, and the Lorentz factor is $\Gamma = -u\cdot u_0$. We use **mostly-minus** throughout. The translation is uniform: flip the overall sign of every metric contraction. So $u\cdot u = -1$ becomes $u\cdot u = +1$, the tensor becomes $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$, and $\Gamma = u\cdot u_0$. # Motivation

A fluid is what you get when you stop tracking individual particles and keep only their collective bookkeeping: at each event, a mean four-velocity, an energy density, and a pressure. Relativistic hydrodynamics is the dynamics of that bookkeeping when the bulk flow, or the internal motion of the constituents, is a non-negligible fraction of the speed of light. This is not an exotic regime. The jets fired from active galactic nuclei and micro-quasars move at Lorentz factors of three to ten; the quark–gluon plasma made in heavy-ion colliders is a relativistic fluid; the interiors of neutron stars and the early universe are governed by these equations. The remarkable thing — and the organising fact of the whole chapter — is that the entire theory follows from one tensor and one conservation law, exactly as the nonrelativistic theory follows from the continuity and Euler equations.

The one tensor is the **energy–momentum tensor of a perfect fluid**, and the structural backbone of the chapter is its form:
$$T^{\mu\nu} \;=\; (\rho + p)\,u^\mu u^\nu \;-\; p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}.$$
Everything is read off this. A "perfect" fluid is one with no viscosity and no heat conduction, which is the precise statement that, in the rest frame of any fluid element, the stress is isotropic — a single pressure $p$ pushing equally in all directions, with no shear. The whole dynamical content is then the single equation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, the local conservation of energy and momentum, which you have already met for general media in [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy]]. The genius of the subject is that this one four-component equation, when you project it cleverly, splits into the two equations a fluid dynamicist expects: an **energy equation** and the **Euler equation**.

The projection is the technique to internalise. The conservation law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is a four-vector equation. Project it *along* the fluid four-velocity $u$ and you get one scalar equation — the evolution of energy, which for a thermodynamically closed fluid is the statement that entropy is conserved along the flow (the flow is adiabatic). Project it *orthogonal* to $u$, onto the three-dimensional rest space, and you get three equations — the **relativistic Euler equation**, the statement "$\rho_{\text{eff}}\,\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{F}$" for a fluid element. The combination $\rho + p$ appears throughout where the nonrelativistic theory would have the mass density alone: it is the **inertia of a fluid element**, and that pressure contributes to inertia is one of the genuinely relativistic facts of the subject. In the nonrelativistic limit $p \ll \rho_{\mathrm m} c^2$, the combination collapses to $\rho_{\mathrm m}$ and the relativistic Euler equation becomes the classical one, $\partial_t \mathbf{V} + (\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{V} = -\rho_{\mathrm m}^{-1}\nabla p$.

There is a second, more beautiful, formulation, and it is the one a reader fluent in exterior calculus should keep at the centre. Package the flow into a single **fluid momentum one-form** $\pi = h\,u$, where $h = (\rho+p)/n$ is the enthalpy per baryon, and define the **vorticity two-form** $\Omega = d\pi$ by applying [[Def - The Exterior Derivative|the exterior derivative]]. Then the entire equation of motion compresses to one line — the *canonical equation* $\Omega(u, \cdot) = T\,dS$ — which involves no covariant derivative and no Christoffel symbol, only $d$. From this single form-equation the classical conservation laws fall out almost for free: **Bernoulli's theorem** (a conserved scalar along streamlines in a stationary flow), **irrotational flow** (the vanishing of $\Omega$, equivalently $hu = d\Psi$ for a velocity potential $\Psi$), and **Kelvin's circulation theorem** (the conservation of $\oint \pi$ around a loop carried by the flow) are each one or two steps from $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$. This is the relativistic completion of the vorticity-and-circulation picture of classical fluid mechanics, and it is cleaner in relativity than in the nonrelativistic theory, because the four-dimensional exterior derivative does the work that the awkward material derivative does classically.

The reader is assumed to be fluent in nonrelativistic fluid dynamics — the continuity equation, the Euler equation, vorticity, Bernoulli, and Kelvin's theorem — and to have those reflexes available for comparison; the chapter is largely the pleasure of watching each classical statement re-emerge from a four-dimensional parent. From the relativity side, the prerequisites are the [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy|energy–momentum tensor and its conservation]], the [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration|four-velocity and four-acceleration]] of [[Special Relativity V — Worldlines, Proper Time and Four-Velocity]], and the machinery of forms on spacetime: the [[Def - The Exterior Derivative|exterior derivative]] from [[Special Relativity XIX — Fields on Spacetime and the Covariant Derivative]], the [[Def - The Hodge Star|Hodge star]] from [[Special Relativity XVIII — Tensors, Alternate Forms and Hodge Duality]], and [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]] from [[Special Relativity XX — Integration in Spacetime and Stokes' Theorem]]. A little equilibrium thermodynamics — the first law, the Gibbs relation, enthalpy — is recalled where it is used.

# Concept Map

## §24.1 The Perfect Fluid and Conservation Laws

- **[[Def - Perfect Fluid]]**
	- A perfect fluid is a continuous medium whose energy–momentum tensor takes the form $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$, where $u$ is a future-directed timelike unit four-velocity field ($u\cdot u = 1$) and $\rho$, $p$ are scalar fields, the proper energy density and the pressure. The defining physical content is that, in the rest frame of any fluid element, the stress is **isotropic**: $S_{ij} = p\,\delta_{ij}$, no shear and no heat flux. The field lines of $u$ are the worldlines of "fluid particles" (a congruence), and $\rho = T(u,u)$ is the energy density a comoving observer measures. The model excludes viscosity and conduction; including them adds terms to $T^{\mu\nu}$ that spoil the isotropy.

- **[[Def - Baryon Four-Current and Its Conservation]]**
	- The baryon four-current is the vector field $j_{\mathrm b}^\mu = n\,u^\mu$, with $n$ the proper baryon density; its flux through a spatial domain counts the baryons inside. Baryon number conservation is the postulate that this flux through any closed hypersurface vanishes, equivalent by [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]] and Hodge duality to the local law $\nabla_\mu(n u^\mu) = 0$. Relative to an inertial observer it becomes the ordinary continuity equation $\partial_t N + \nabla\cdot(N\mathbf{V}) = 0$, with no relativistic correction factor. A companion identity, $\nabla_\mu u^\mu = (1/V)\,dV/d\tau$, identifies the four-divergence of $u$ with the fractional expansion rate of a comoving volume element.

- **[[Def - Equation of State and Speed of Sound]]**
	- The equation of state is the thermodynamic relation $\rho = \rho(s, n_1, \dots, n_N)$ fixing the proper energy density from the entropy and particle densities; temperature and chemical potentials are its partial derivatives, $T = (\partial\rho/\partial s)$ and $\mu_a = (\partial\rho/\partial n_a)$, and the fundamental relation $d\rho = T\,ds + \sum_a \mu_a\,dn_a$ holds. A **simple fluid** has an equation of state in two variables, $\rho = \rho(s,n)$; a **barotropic** fluid has $\rho = \rho(n)$ alone (then $T=0$). The speed of sound is $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial \rho)_S$ at fixed entropy per baryon; causality demands $c_s \le 1$, and the "hardest" causal equation of state $p = \rho$ saturates $c_s = c$.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff Equation *(from General Relativity and Astrophysics)*
> A perfect fluid in a static, spherically symmetric **general-relativistic** spacetime obeys the **Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation**, the relativistic equation of hydrostatic equilibrium $dp/dr = -(\rho + p)(m + 4\pi r^3 p)/[r(r - 2m)]$. It is the static, gravitating version of the relativistic Euler equation: the inertia $\rho + p$ of this chapter reappears, and so does the fact that pressure gravitates. Closed with an equation of state $p = p(\rho)$, it determines the structure of **neutron stars** and the maximum mass beyond which collapse to a black hole is unavoidable — see [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: Relativistic Shocks and the Rankine–Hugoniot Conditions *(from Computational Astrophysics)*
> Writing the conservation laws in flux-conservative form $\partial_t U_A + \partial_j F_A^j = 0$ for the state vector $U = (N, E, \boldsymbol{\varpi})$ exposes the system as a **hyperbolic system of conservation laws** whenever $c_s < c$. This is exactly the structure that admits **shock waves** and is solved by **high-resolution shock-capturing schemes**; the jump conditions across a relativistic shock are the **Rankine–Hugoniot conditions** for $T^{\mu\nu}$ and $j_{\mathrm b}^\mu$, and they are the workhorse of every numerical simulation of relativistic jets and accretion.

## §24.2 The Relativistic Euler Equation and Sound

- **[[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation projected (Euler + energy equation)]]**
	- The single conservation law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = f^\nu$, applied to a perfect fluid, splits into two pieces by projection along and orthogonal to $u$. The projection **onto** $u$ gives the energy equation $u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu = -f\cdot u$; for an isolated simple fluid it reduces to $\nabla_\mu(s u^\mu) = 0$, conservation of entropy, and hence $\nabla_u(s/n) = 0$ — the flow is **adiabatic**. The projection **orthogonal** to $u$ gives the four-dimensional Euler equation $(\rho+p)a^\mu = -\nabla^\mu p - (u^\nu\nabla_\nu p)u^\mu + (\text{force terms})$, with $a^\mu = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu$ the four-acceleration. This is the master statement from which both the energy balance and the equation of motion descend.

- **[[Thm - Relativistic Euler Equation]]**
	- Projecting $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = f^\nu$ onto the rest space of an inertial observer yields the relativistic Euler equation for the fluid three-velocity $\mathbf{V}$: $\partial_t\mathbf{V} + (\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{V} = -\frac{1}{E+p}\big[\nabla_\perp p + (\partial_t p + P_{\mathrm{ext}})\mathbf{V}\big] + \frac{1}{E+p}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ (here with $c$ restored where the structure is clearest). It is "$\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{F}/(\rho_{\text{eff}})$" with the effective inertia $E + p$ in place of the mass density — pressure contributes to inertia. In the nonrelativistic limit $E + p \to \rho_{\mathrm m} c^2$ and it collapses to the classical Euler equation $\partial_t\mathbf{V} + (\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{V} = -\rho_{\mathrm m}^{-1}\nabla p + \rho_{\mathrm m}^{-1}\mathbf{F}$.

- **[[Ex - Dust as the pressureless limit of a perfect fluid]]** (⭐)
	- Set $p = 0$ in the perfect-fluid tensor to obtain dust, $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,u^\mu u^\nu$, and show its conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ splits into mass conservation $\nabla_\mu(\rho_{\mathrm m} u^\mu)=0$ and force-free geodesic motion $a^\mu = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu = 0$ — pressureless matter free-falls. (⭐)

- **[[Ex - Projecting the conservation law onto the four-velocity]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Contract $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ with $u_\nu$ and use $u\cdot u = 1$, $u\cdot a = 0$ to derive the energy equation $u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu = 0$; interpret it via $\nabla_\mu u^\mu = \dot V/V$ as the first law $d(\rho V) = -p\,dV$ for a comoving volume, and deduce entropy conservation for a simple fluid. (⭐⭐)

- **[[Ex - The relativistic Euler equation from the orthogonal projection]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Apply the orthogonal projector to $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ to obtain $(\rho+p)a^\mu = -(\eta^{\mu\nu} - u^\mu u^\nu)\nabla_\nu p$, the four-dimensional Euler equation, and check that contracting with $u_\mu$ gives $0 = 0$ so the four equations are genuinely the three rest-space components. (⭐⭐)

- **[[Ex - The speed of sound from linearised perturbations]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Linearise the energy and Euler equations about a homogeneous fluid with an adiabatic perturbation, derive the wave equation $-c_s^{-2}\partial_t^2\delta\rho + \nabla^2\delta\rho = 0$, and read off $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$; evaluate it for a photon gas ($p = \rho/3$, $c_s = 1/\sqrt3$) and for the stiff equation of state $p = \rho$ ($c_s = c$). (⭐⭐)

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Energy Equation as a Conservation Law in Cosmology *(from Cosmology)*
> In a homogeneous expanding universe the fluid is at rest in comoving coordinates and the energy equation becomes the **cosmological continuity equation** $\dot\rho + 3\frac{\dot a}{a}(\rho + p) = 0$, where $a(t)$ is the scale factor and $\nabla_\mu u^\mu = 3\dot a/a$ is the Hubble expansion. This single equation, with an equation of state $p = w\rho$, gives the scaling of every cosmic component: matter ($w=0$) dilutes as $a^{-3}$, radiation ($w=1/3$) as $a^{-4}$, and a cosmological constant ($w=-1$) stays constant — the backbone of the **Friedmann** cosmology.

## §24.3 Vorticity, Bernoulli and Kelvin's Theorem

- **[[Def - Vorticity 2-Form]]**
	- For a simple fluid one packages the flow into the fluid momentum one-form $\pi = h\,u$ (components $\pi_\mu = h u_\mu$), with $h = (\rho+p)/n$ the enthalpy per baryon, and defines the vorticity two-form $\Omega = d\pi$ by applying [[Def - The Exterior Derivative|the exterior derivative]], so $\Omega_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu(h u_\nu) - \partial_\nu(h u_\mu)$. The entire equation of motion then collapses to the **canonical equation** $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$, which involves only $d$ and not the covariant derivative. The two-form $\Omega$ is the relativistic enthalpy-weighted generalisation of the classical vorticity $\nabla\times\mathbf{v}$, and its vanishing defines irrotational flow.

- **[[Thm - Relativistic Bernoulli Theorem]]**
	- In a flow that is stationary with respect to an inertial observer $\mathcal{O}$ (four-velocity $u_0$), the scalar $\langle\pi, u_0\rangle = h\,\Gamma$ is constant along each fluid line, where $\Gamma = u\cdot u_0$ is the fluid Lorentz factor. This follows in two lines from the canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$ plus stationarity. It is a special case of a Noether-type result: any symmetry generator $G$ of the flow yields a conserved scalar $\langle\pi, G\rangle$ along fluid lines. In the nonrelativistic limit it becomes the classical Bernoulli statement that $H + \tfrac12 V^2$ (specific enthalpy plus kinetic energy) is constant along streamlines.

- **[[Thm - Kelvin's Circulation Theorem (exterior-calculus formulation)]]**
	- The fluid circulation $C(\mathcal{C}) = \oint_\mathcal{C}\pi$ around a closed curve $\mathcal{C}$ equals $\int_\mathcal{S}\Omega$ over any surface bounded by $\mathcal{C}$, by [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]]. When $\mathcal{C}$ is carried along the fluid lines, the circulation is **conserved** — $C(\mathcal{C}') = C(\mathcal{C})$ — provided the fluid is barotropic ($T=0$) or the entropy per baryon $S$ is constant on the initial loop. The obstruction to conservation is exactly the baroclinic term $\int_\mathcal{T} T\,\nabla_{e_3}S$; an associated local statement is the conservation of the **potential vorticity** $e = (h/n)\nabla_{\boldsymbol\omega}S$ along fluid lines.

- **[[Ex - Bernoulli's theorem along a streamline]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Use the canonical equation in a stationary flow to prove $\nabla_u\langle\pi, u_0\rangle = 0$, identify the conserved scalar as $h\Gamma$, and recover the nonrelativistic Bernoulli constant $H + \tfrac12 V^2$; apply it to steady flow through a nozzle to relate enthalpy drop to acceleration. (⭐⭐)

- **[[Ex - Irrotational flow and the velocity potential]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show that $\Omega = 0$ is equivalent to $d\pi = 0$, hence (Poincaré) $hu = d\Psi$ for a potential $\Psi$; note that the relativistic condition is $\pi = hu$ exact, not $u$ exact, and derive the potential equation $\square\Psi + \nabla\ln(n/h)\cdot\nabla\Psi = 0$, which reduces to a wave equation for $h \propto n$. (⭐⭐)

- **[[Ex - Conservation of circulation by transport along fluid lines]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Carry a loop $\mathcal{C}$ along the fluid lines to $\mathcal{C}'$, apply Stokes' theorem on the fluid tube together with the canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$, and prove $C(\mathcal{C}') = C(\mathcal{C}) - \int_\mathcal{T} T\,\nabla_{e_3}S$; conclude that circulation is conserved for barotropic or isentropic flow, and connect to the conserved potential vorticity. (⭐⭐⭐)

> [!tip] Unlocked: Magnetohydrodynamics and the Frozen-In Theorem *(from Relativistic MHD)*
> Coupling the perfect fluid to the electromagnetic field of [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field]] gives **relativistic magnetohydrodynamics**. For a perfectly conducting fluid the analogue of Kelvin's theorem is **Alfvén's frozen-in theorem**: the magnetic flux through any loop carried by the fluid is conserved, so field lines move with the matter. The vorticity two-form $\Omega$ and the electromagnetic two-form $F$ play structurally identical roles — both are closed two-forms whose flux is a transported invariant — and this is the engine of jet collimation and magnetic-field amplification in astrophysics.

> [!tip] Unlocked: The Carter Hamiltonian and Variational Fluid Dynamics *(from Geometric Mechanics)*
> The canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$ is, as Brandon Carter showed, a Hamilton equation $\dot\pi = -\nabla H$ for the fluid momentum one-form $\pi$, with a quadratic Hamiltonian on the cotangent bundle. This places relativistic fluid dynamics inside the [[Geometric Mechanics I — Symplectic Manifolds and Hamiltonian Dynamics|symplectic / Hamiltonian framework]]: the vorticity two-form is the pullback of the canonical symplectic form, Bernoulli's theorem is a momentum map for the time-translation symmetry, and circulation is a relative integral invariant of Poincaré–Cartan type.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The problems of relativistic hydrodynamics chase a recognisable handful of goals. The most common is to **derive an evolution equation by projection**: starting from the single tensor law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, project along or orthogonal to the four-velocity and produce the energy equation or the Euler equation. The entire skill is in choosing the projection and tracking which terms survive — the identity $u\cdot a = 0$, which follows from differentiating $u\cdot u = 1$, is the workhorse that kills half the terms. A second target is to **take a nonrelativistic limit and recognise the classical law**: send $p/\rho_{\mathrm m}c^2 \to 0$ and $\Gamma \to 1$ and confirm that the relativistic equation reduces to the continuity equation, the classical Euler equation, the classical Bernoulli constant, or classical Kelvin. This is both a sanity check and the way the relativistic factors earn their interpretation (the $\Gamma^2$ in the energy density is length contraction; the $\rho + p$ inertia is the relativistic enthalpy). A third target is to **establish a conservation law along the flow**: that entropy per baryon is conserved (adiabaticity), that a Bernoulli scalar is constant along streamlines, that circulation is invariant under transport. These are proved not by brute computation but by the exterior-calculus formulation, where each is a corollary of the canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$. A fourth target is **thermodynamic**: to derive the relations $\rho + p = Ts + \sum\mu_a n_a$, $dp = s\,dT + \sum n_a\,d\mu_a$, or to compute the speed of sound $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$ from an equation of state. A fifth, more physical, is to **decide a causality or stability question** — is the equation of state causal ($c_s \le 1$), is the system of conservation laws hyperbolic, does a perturbation propagate or grow — settled by examining the characteristic speeds.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **The perfect-fluid form of $T^{\mu\nu}$ is given**, which is the richest source: it supplies the energy density, the pressure, the inertia $\rho + p$, and the isotropy of the rest-frame stress, and it is what every projection acts on. **The four-velocity is a unit field**, $u\cdot u = 1$, whose differentiated form $u\cdot a = 0$ is invoked in nearly every projection to discard terms. **An equation of state is specified** — $\rho = \rho(s,n)$ for a simple fluid, $\rho = \rho(n)$ for a barotropic one, or a concrete law like $p = w\rho$ — which closes the system and supplies the thermodynamic derivatives $T$, $\mu$, and $c_s$. **Baryon number is conserved**, $\nabla_\mu(nu^\mu) = 0$, which combines with entropy conservation to give adiabaticity and is the relativistic continuity equation. **A symmetry of the flow is given** — stationarity (time translation) for Bernoulli, axisymmetry for angular-momentum conservation — and the symmetry alone, via the Noether-type identity $\nabla_u\langle\pi, G\rangle = 0$, produces a conserved scalar. **The flow is irrotational or isentropic** — the vorticity two-form vanishes, or $dS = 0$ — which collapses the canonical equation to $\Omega(u,\cdot) = 0$ and introduces a velocity potential. The recurring move is to route a source to a target through a projection or through the canonical equation: the tensor plus the unit-norm identity route through projection to the Euler and energy equations; an equation of state routes through the thermodynamic relations to the speed of sound; a symmetry routes through the canonical equation to a Bernoulli scalar; a transported loop routes through Stokes' theorem and the canonical equation to Kelvin's theorem.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves nearly every problem in the chapter is assembled from. The reader's fluid-dynamics background means the physical targets are familiar; the new content is the relativistic and exterior-calculus machinery, so each operation states precisely what gets projected, differentiated, or transported. When stuck, scan the list.

**Legal operations:**

1. **Project the conservation law along the four-velocity.** Contract $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = f^\nu$ with $u_\nu$. Using $u\cdot u = 1$ and $u\cdot a = 0$ (from differentiating the norm), the acceleration term drops and you are left with a single scalar equation — the energy equation $u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu = -f\cdot u$; see [[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation projected (Euler + energy equation)]]. *Trigger:* you want the evolution of energy or entropy, or you want to prove adiabaticity. *Pattern:* "contract with $u_\nu$, drop the $u\cdot a$ term, identify $\nabla_\mu u^\mu = \dot V/V$ as the volume expansion."

2. **Project the conservation law orthogonal to the four-velocity.** Apply the orthogonal projector $\perp^\mu{}_\nu = \delta^\mu_\nu - u^\mu u_\nu$ to $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = f^\nu$. The result is the equation of motion $(\rho+p)a^\mu = -\nabla^\mu p - (u^\nu\nabla_\nu p)u^\mu + (\text{projected force})$; see [[Thm - Relativistic Euler Equation]]. *Trigger:* you want the equation of motion for the fluid, the analogue of "$\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{F}/m$". *Pattern:* "project orthogonal to $u$; the pressure gradient acquires its $u$-component subtracted off so the equation lives in the rest space."

3. **Use the unit-norm identity $u\cdot a = 0$.** Differentiating $u_\mu u^\mu = 1$ along any direction gives $u_\mu \nabla_\nu u^\mu = 0$; in particular the four-acceleration is orthogonal to the four-velocity. This single identity discards the acceleration term in the parallel projection and is what makes the four equations of the orthogonal projection reduce to three independent ones; see [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]]. *Trigger:* any projection or contraction involving $u$ and its derivatives. *Pattern:* "the four-acceleration is spacelike and orthogonal to $u$, so its contraction with $u$ vanishes."

4. **Take the nonrelativistic limit.** Send $\Gamma \to 1$, $\mathbf{V}/c \to 0$, and impose $p/c^2 \ll \rho_{\mathrm m}$ and $|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}|/c^2 \ll \rho_{\mathrm m}$, so that the inertia $E + p \to \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$. Every relativistic factor collapses and the equation becomes its classical counterpart. *Trigger:* you want to identify a relativistic equation with a known classical law, or to interpret a relativistic factor physically. *Pattern:* "replace $E + p$ by $\rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$, drop $\Gamma$, recover the textbook equation."

5. **Form the fluid momentum one-form and take its exterior derivative.** Build $\pi = h\,u$ with $h = (\rho+p)/n$, and set $\Omega = d\pi$; see [[Def - Vorticity 2-Form]]. The equation of motion then reads $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$, a statement in $d$ alone. *Trigger:* you want a conservation law (Bernoulli, Kelvin, irrotational flow), or you want to avoid Christoffel symbols. *Pattern:* "lower the velocity, multiply by enthalpy per baryon, apply $d$; the dynamics is the canonical equation."

6. **Invoke baryon-number and entropy conservation together.** $\nabla_\mu(nu^\mu) = 0$ and, for an isolated simple fluid, $\nabla_\mu(su^\mu) = 0$ combine to give $\nabla_u(s/n) = 0$: the entropy per baryon $S = s/n$ is constant along each fluid line, so the flow is adiabatic; see [[Def - Baryon Four-Current and Its Conservation]]. *Trigger:* you need $dS = 0$ along the flow to simplify the canonical equation, or to prove a flow is isentropic. *Pattern:* "two conserved currents with the same flow lines give a conserved ratio."

7. **Exploit a symmetry via the Noether-type scalar.** If $G$ generates a symmetry of the flow, then $\langle\pi, G\rangle$ is conserved along fluid lines, $\nabla_u\langle\pi, G\rangle = 0$; see [[Thm - Relativistic Bernoulli Theorem]]. Time-translation ($G = u_0$) gives Bernoulli; axisymmetry gives a conserved angular momentum per baryon. *Trigger:* the flow is stationary, axisymmetric, or otherwise symmetric. *Pattern:* "contract $\pi$ with the symmetry generator; the result is constant along streamlines."

8. **Convert a circulation into a flux by Stokes' theorem.** The circulation $\oint_\mathcal{C}\pi$ equals $\int_\mathcal{S}\Omega$ for any $\mathcal{S}$ with $\partial\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{C}$, by [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]]; see [[Thm - Kelvin's Circulation Theorem (exterior-calculus formulation)]]. *Trigger:* you want to evaluate or differentiate a circulation, or relate it to vorticity. *Pattern:* "the line integral of $\pi$ is the surface integral of $\Omega = d\pi$; if $\Omega = 0$ the circulation vanishes."

9. **Apply the Poincaré lemma to a closed one-form.** When $\Omega = d\pi = 0$, the one-form $\pi$ is closed, hence locally exact: $\pi = d\Psi$ for a scalar potential. For the fluid this means $hu = d\Psi$; see [[Ex - Irrotational flow and the velocity potential]]. *Trigger:* the flow is irrotational. *Pattern:* "vorticity vanishes $\Rightarrow$ momentum one-form is a gradient $\Rightarrow$ introduce a velocity potential."

10. **Compute thermodynamic derivatives from the equation of state.** From $\rho = \rho(s,n)$ read $T = (\partial\rho/\partial s)_n$ and $\mu = (\partial\rho/\partial n)_s$; the enthalpy per baryon is $h = (\rho+p)/n = \mu + TS$, and the speed of sound is $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$; see [[Def - Equation of State and Speed of Sound]]. *Trigger:* you have an explicit equation of state and need a temperature, chemical potential, enthalpy, or sound speed. *Pattern:* "differentiate the equation of state; enthalpy and sound speed are derived quantities."

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Using the rest-mass density where the inertia $\rho + p$ belongs
> The seductive error, carried over from the nonrelativistic Euler equation, is to write "$\rho_{\mathrm m}\,\mathbf{a} = -\nabla p$" with the rest-mass density as the inertia. The relativistic Euler equation has the **proper enthalpy density $\rho + p$** as the inertia, not $\rho_{\mathrm m}$ and not even $\rho$: a fluid element under pressure is harder to accelerate than its mass alone would suggest, because the pressure carries momentum flux. Concretely, for a photon gas with $p = \rho/3$ the inertia is $\tfrac43\rho$, a third again larger than the energy density. The operation becomes legal only in the nonrelativistic limit $p \ll \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}} \ll \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$, where $\rho + p \to \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$ and the distinction disappears. Whenever you write a fluid equation of motion, ask: have I used $\rho + p$, or have I smuggled in the mass density?

> [!warning] 2. Assuming an irrotational relativistic flow has $u = d\Psi$
> In nonrelativistic fluids irrotationality means $\nabla\times\mathbf{v} = 0$, hence $\mathbf{v} = \nabla\Psi$: the velocity itself is a gradient. The naive relativistic translation, $u = d\Psi$, is **wrong**. The vorticity two-form is $\Omega = d(hu)$, not $d(u)$, so $\Omega = 0$ gives $hu = d\Psi$ — it is the *enthalpy-weighted* velocity that is exact, not the velocity. The counterexample is any flow with non-uniform enthalpy: there $hu$ can be a gradient while $u$ is not, because the enthalpy factor varies along the flow. The operation becomes legal in the nonrelativistic limit, where $h \to m_{\mathrm b}c^2$ is constant and can be divided out, recovering $u \propto d\Psi$. The enthalpy weighting is the whole subtlety of relativistic irrotational flow.

> [!warning] 3. Expecting circulation to be conserved for any equation of state
> Kelvin's theorem feels universal, so it is tempting to assert "circulation is always conserved by the flow". It is not. The change in circulation under transport is $-\int_\mathcal{T} T\,\nabla_{e_3}S$, a **baroclinic** term that vanishes only when the fluid is barotropic ($T = 0$) or when the entropy per baryon $S$ is constant on the loop. The counterexample is a flow with crossed gradients of pressure and entropy — a baroclinic flow, as in a stratified atmosphere or a star with composition gradients — where circulation is *generated*, not conserved, and vorticity is continuously created. The operation becomes legal exactly when $T\,dS = 0$, i.e. for barotropic or isentropic flow; this is also precisely the condition under which the canonical equation reduces to $\Omega(u,\cdot) = 0$. Before invoking Kelvin, check whether pressure and entropy gradients are aligned.

> [!warning] 4. Treating the speed of sound as $\sqrt{p/\rho}$ or some fixed multiple of $c$
> A dimensional guess for the sound speed is "$c_s \sim \sqrt{p/\rho}$", and for a relativistic gas one might guess $c_s = c/\sqrt3$ universally. Neither is the definition. The speed of sound is the **derivative** $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$ at fixed entropy per baryon — a slope of the equation of state, not a ratio. For a photon gas $p = \rho/3$ is linear, so the derivative happens to equal the ratio and $c_s = 1/\sqrt3$; but for a general equation of state the derivative and the ratio differ, and using the ratio gives the wrong propagation speed. The operation $c_s^2 = p/\rho$ is legal only for the specific linear law $p \propto \rho$; in general one must differentiate. Causality additionally requires $c_s \le 1$, which constrains the *slope* of the equation of state, not its value.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The problems of this chapter are won by recognising that almost everything descends from a single tensor equation, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, through a small number of standard manipulations. The first decision in any problem is therefore: which projection, or which reformulation, turns this master equation into the statement I want?

If the problem **asks for an evolution equation** — how does the energy density change, how does the velocity field evolve — the move is to project the conservation law. Project *along* the four-velocity (contract with $u_\nu$) to get the scalar energy equation; the identity $u\cdot a = 0$ from differentiating $u\cdot u = 1$ is what makes this clean, killing the acceleration term and leaving $u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu = 0$. Project *orthogonal* to the four-velocity (apply $\delta^\mu_\nu - u^\mu u_\nu$) to get the three-component [[Thm - Relativistic Euler Equation|Euler equation]]. The reason the split works is that the four-velocity gives spacetime a preferred time direction at each event, and projecting along versus across that direction separates energy balance from momentum balance, exactly as the $\nu = 0$ and $\nu = i$ components do for an inertial observer. When you have the equation, interpret each relativistic factor by taking the nonrelativistic limit: $\rho + p \to \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$ tells you the inertia is the enthalpy, $E = \Gamma^2(\rho+p) - p$ exposes the $\Gamma^2$ as length contraction of the energy density.

If the problem **asks for a conservation law along the flow** — that entropy is conserved, that a Bernoulli scalar is constant, that circulation is invariant — do not grind through the covariant Euler equation. Switch to the exterior-calculus formulation: build the [[Def - Vorticity 2-Form|fluid momentum one-form]] $\pi = hu$, form $\Omega = d\pi$, and use the canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$. Adiabaticity is then the combination of baryon and entropy conservation, $\nabla_u(s/n) = 0$. [[Thm - Relativistic Bernoulli Theorem|Bernoulli]] is the canonical equation plus stationarity: $\langle\pi, u_0\rangle$ is conserved along streamlines because the time-translation symmetry kills the right-hand side. [[Thm - Kelvin's Circulation Theorem (exterior-calculus formulation)|Kelvin]] is the canonical equation plus [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]] on the fluid tube. The unifying reason the form-language is so much cleaner here is that the exterior derivative $d$ is metric-independent and satisfies $d^2 = 0$, so closedness and exactness do the bookkeeping that the material derivative does laboriously in the nonrelativistic theory.

If the problem **is thermodynamic** — find the speed of sound, the chemical potential, the enthalpy, or verify a thermodynamic identity — the route is through the [[Def - Equation of State and Speed of Sound|equation of state]] $\rho = \rho(s,n)$ and its derivatives. Temperature and chemical potential are partial derivatives; the enthalpy per baryon is $h = (\rho+p)/n = \mu + TS$; the speed of sound is the slope $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$. The key discipline is to hold the right variable fixed — sound is an *adiabatic* compression, so the entropy per baryon $S$ is constant, and the derivative is taken at fixed $S$. A frequent sub-problem is to check causality: $c_s \le 1$ is a constraint on the slope of the equation of state, and the stiffest causal law is $p = \rho$, for which sound travels at the speed of light.

If the problem **is a limit or a consistency check** — show that the relativistic equation reduces to a classical one, or that two derivations agree — set up the nonrelativistic expansion carefully. The essential approximations are $\Gamma \simeq 1 + \tfrac12 V^2$, $h \simeq m_{\mathrm b}c^2(1 + H/c^2)$ with $H$ the specific internal enthalpy, and $E + p \simeq \rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$. With these, the relativistic Euler equation becomes classical Euler, relativistic Bernoulli becomes "$H + \tfrac12 V^2$ constant", and relativistic Kelvin becomes classical Kelvin. The limit is not merely a check: it is how the relativistic quantities acquire their physical names.

The single unifying question of the chapter is this: **what does $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ become when I look at it along the flow, across the flow, or through the eyes of the exterior derivative?** Energy balance, momentum balance, and the circulation theorems are the three answers, and every problem is asking for one of them.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Thm - Energy-Momentum Conservation projected (Euler + energy equation)|The projection of the conservation law]]**: $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ splits, by projection along and orthogonal to $u$, into the energy equation and the Euler equation. This is the most-used structural fact of the chapter, because it is how *every* dynamical statement is obtained: there is one law, and the two pieces a fluid dynamicist needs are its parallel and orthogonal shadows. The reusable skill is the reflex — when handed a conserved symmetric tensor and a preferred four-velocity, contract with the velocity for the scalar (energy) equation and project with $\delta - u\otimes u$ for the vector (momentum) equation. The identity $u\cdot a = 0$ is the lubricant that makes both projections collapse to their irreducible content. Recognise the applicability the instant you see $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ together with a unit timelike field.

- **The inertia is the proper enthalpy density $\rho + p$**: wherever the nonrelativistic theory has the mass density, relativity has $\rho + p$. It is the coefficient of the four-acceleration in the [[Thm - Relativistic Euler Equation|Euler equation]], the coefficient of the velocity in the momentum density $\boldsymbol\varpi = (E+p)\mathbf{V}/c^2$, and the quantity divided by $n$ to make the enthalpy per baryon $h$. The reusable content is the slogan "pressure contributes to inertia": a hot or compressed fluid resists acceleration more than its rest mass alone. Reach for $\rho + p$ whenever you write a relativistic fluid equation of motion, and check any classical-looking formula for whether the mass density should be promoted to the enthalpy density.

- **[[Def - Vorticity 2-Form|The canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$]]**: the entire equation of motion of a simple fluid, written in the exterior derivative alone. Its typical use is to generate conservation laws without touching the covariant derivative: Bernoulli, irrotational flow, and Kelvin's theorem each follow in a step or two. The reusable move is to package the flow into $\pi = hu$, form $\Omega = d\pi$, and read dynamics off $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$ — the left side is purely geometric (a closed-form-derivative contracted with the velocity), the right side is the thermodynamic source. Recognise its applicability whenever a problem asks for a quantity conserved *along the flow* or *around a loop*, because those are exactly the statements the form-language makes transparent.

- **[[Def - Baryon Four-Current and Its Conservation|Baryon-number conservation $\nabla_\mu(nu^\mu) = 0$]]** and its partner, entropy conservation. Their typical combined use is to produce adiabaticity: two conserved currents sharing the same flow lines give the conserved ratio $S = s/n$, hence $\nabla_u S = 0$. The reusable principle is that a conserved current $\nabla_\mu(\rho_X u^\mu) = 0$ is the relativistic continuity equation for the scalar $\rho_X$, and that the four-divergence $\nabla_\mu u^\mu$ measures the fractional expansion rate $\dot V/V$ of a comoving volume. Reach for these whenever you need to relate the change of a density along the flow to the local expansion, or to establish that a flow is isentropic so that the canonical equation simplifies to $\Omega(u,\cdot) = 0$.

- **[[Def - Equation of State and Speed of Sound|The speed of sound $c_s^2 = (\partial p/\partial\rho)_S$]]**: the slope of the equation of state at fixed entropy per baryon, and the characteristic speed of the hydrodynamic system. Its typical uses are to test causality ($c_s \le 1$), to determine whether the conservation-law system is hyperbolic (hence whether shock-capturing methods apply), and to compute the dispersion of small perturbations. The reusable content is that sound is an adiabatic compression, so the relevant derivative is at constant $S$, and that for the linear law $p = w\rho$ the sound speed is simply $\sqrt{w}$. Recognise its role whenever a problem involves wave propagation, stability, shocks, or the maximum stiffness of matter.

# Bridges

1. **Nonrelativistic fluid dynamics — the classical Euler equation is the orthogonal projection in the slow-motion limit.** The classical Euler equation $\partial_t\mathbf{V} + (\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{V} = -\rho_{\mathrm m}^{-1}\nabla p + \rho_{\mathrm m}^{-1}\mathbf{F}$ is not an independent starting point but the nonrelativistic shadow of the relativistic Euler equation. The construction is explicit: project $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ orthogonal to the four-velocity to get $(\rho+p)a^\mu = -\perp^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu p$, write it in components relative to an inertial observer, and then impose $\Gamma \to 1$, $\mathbf{V}/c \to 0$, and $p/c^2 \ll \rho_{\mathrm m}$ so that the inertia $E + p$ collapses to the rest-mass-energy density $\rho_{\mathrm m}c^2$. What was the four-acceleration $a^\mu = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu$ becomes the material acceleration $\partial_t\mathbf{V} + (\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{V}$, and the relativistic pressure-gradient term becomes the classical one. The lesson that transfers is that the awkward material derivative of classical fluids is just the four-velocity-directed derivative $u^\mu\nabla_\mu$ seen in a slow frame, and the mysterious appearance of pressure in the inertia of a relativistic fluid is the genuinely new physics that the limit erases.

2. **Equilibrium thermodynamics — the energy–momentum tensor knows the first law.** The combination $\rho + p$ in $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the proper enthalpy density, and this is not a coincidence of notation. Projecting the conservation law along the flow gives $u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho + (\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu = 0$, which, using $\nabla_\mu u^\mu = \dot V/V$ for a comoving volume $V$, is exactly $d(\rho V) = -p\,dV$ — the **first law of thermodynamics** for an adiabatic process, $dU = -p\,dV$ with $U = \rho V$. The thermodynamic identities $d\rho = T\,ds + \sum_a\mu_a\,dn_a$ (the Gibbs relation), $\rho + p = Ts + \sum_a\mu_a n_a$ (the Euler relation, which is the source of the enthalpy combination), and $dp = s\,dT + \sum_a n_a\,d\mu_a$ (the Gibbs–Duhem relation) all enter the derivation of the equations of motion. So relativistic hydrodynamics is equilibrium thermodynamics made local and put into motion: each fluid element is a small thermodynamic system carried along its worldline, and the conservation law enforces its first law. This is why an equation of state — a thermodynamic input — is needed to close the dynamical system.

3. **Exterior calculus and the geometry of forms — the vorticity two-form and the canonical equation.** The cleanest formulation of the chapter lives entirely in the language of [[Special Relativity XIX — Fields on Spacetime and the Covariant Derivative|differential forms on spacetime]]. The flow is encoded in the one-form $\pi = hu$ (the enthalpy per baryon times the lowered four-velocity), the vorticity is the two-form $\Omega = d\pi$ obtained by [[Def - The Exterior Derivative|the exterior derivative]], and the equation of motion is $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$. Three properties of $d$ carry the entire theory. First, $d$ is metric-independent, so the canonical equation needs no Christoffel symbols — a striking simplification over the covariant Euler equation. Second, $d^2 = 0$, which is why an irrotational flow ($\Omega = d\pi = 0$) admits a velocity potential ($\pi = d\Psi$) by the Poincaré lemma, exactly as a closed form is locally exact. Third, $d$ commutes with pullback and integration, which is what makes [[Thm - Stokes Theorem on Spacetime|Stokes' theorem]] turn the circulation $\oint\pi$ into the flux $\int\Omega$ and deliver Kelvin's theorem. The vorticity two-form is structurally identical to the [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field|electromagnetic field two-form]] $F = dA$: both are exact two-forms whose flux through a transported loop is the conserved invariant, and Kelvin's theorem is to fluids what flux conservation is to a perfectly conducting plasma.

4. **Symplectic and Hamiltonian mechanics — Carter's canonical formulation.** Brandon Carter observed that the canonical equation $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$ is a Hamilton equation. Taking the fluid momentum one-form $\pi$ as the canonical momentum and a quadratic Hamiltonian $H(x,\pi) = \frac{1}{2T}(g^{\mu\nu}\pi_\mu\pi_\nu/h + h) - S$ on the cotangent bundle, the equation of motion is $\dot\pi = -\nabla H$, of the form studied in [[Geometric Mechanics I — Symplectic Manifolds and Hamiltonian Dynamics]]. The vorticity two-form $\Omega$ plays the role of the pullback of the canonical symplectic form to the flow, so its closedness $d\Omega = d^2\pi = 0$ is the relativistic-fluid analogue of the conservation of the symplectic structure. In this language the Noether-type conservation law $\nabla_u\langle\pi, G\rangle = 0$ for a symmetry generator $G$ is a momentum map, [[Thm - Relativistic Bernoulli Theorem|Bernoulli's theorem]] is the conserved quantity for the time-translation symmetry, and circulation $\oint\pi$ is a relative integral invariant of Poincaré–Cartan type. The fluid is, in effect, a continuum of Hamiltonian systems, one per fluid line, sharing a single symplectic geometry.

5. **General relativity and astrophysics — the perfect fluid as the source of gravity.** The perfect-fluid energy–momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$ is the matter source in the **Einstein field equations** $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ of [[General Relativity I — Einstein's Equations and Schwarzschild]], with the flat metric $\eta$ promoted to the curved $g_{\mu\nu}$. The conservation law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, which in special relativity is a postulate, becomes in general relativity an *identity* forced by the contracted Bianchi identity — gravity automatically enforces fluid conservation. Two of this chapter's results are the seeds of relativistic astrophysics. The static, spherically symmetric specialisation of the relativistic Euler equation, with gravity included, is the **Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation** of stellar structure, whose solutions are neutron stars and whose maximum-mass bound predicts collapse to black holes. And the cosmological specialisation of the energy equation, $\dot\rho + 3(\dot a/a)(\rho+p) = 0$ in a homogeneous expanding universe, governs how matter, radiation, and dark energy dilute with the scale factor — the thermodynamic backbone of the hot Big Bang. The fact that pressure contributes to the inertia $\rho + p$ here becomes the fact that pressure gravitates, with the sign that a sufficiently negative pressure (a cosmological constant) drives accelerated expansion.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: one conservation law, seen three ways.** The deepest organising idea of relativistic hydrodynamics is that there is essentially one equation, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, and the whole subject is the study of what it becomes under different decompositions. Project it along the four-velocity and it is the *energy equation* — for a closed fluid, the conservation of entropy along the flow, the first law of thermodynamics carried along each worldline. Project it orthogonal to the four-velocity and it is the *Euler equation* — Newton's second law for a fluid element, with the proper enthalpy density $\rho + p$ as the inertia. Rewrite it in the exterior derivative and it is the *canonical equation* $\Omega(u,\cdot) = T\,dS$ — the parent of Bernoulli, irrotational flow, and Kelvin's circulation theorem. These are not three theories but three viewings of one platonic object, and a problem is solved the moment you decide which viewing answers it. The nonrelativistic theory, with its separate continuity, Euler, and vorticity equations, looks like a collection of independent laws; the relativistic theory reveals them as facets of a single tensor identity, which is the usual gift of relativity — fewer, deeper equations with more structure.

**The true name of the inertia is enthalpy, not mass.** A reader trained on the nonrelativistic Euler equation will instinctively write the mass density as the coefficient of acceleration, and this instinct is wrong in relativity in a way that is physically illuminating rather than merely technical. The correct inertia of a fluid element is the proper enthalpy density $\rho + p$, and the extra $p$ is real: a fluid under pressure carries momentum flux, and accelerating it requires pushing that flux around, so a hot gas is harder to set in motion than its rest mass would suggest. This is the fluid version of the fact that energy has inertia — the same fact that gives $E = mc^2$ — now applied to the internal energy and the pressure of a continuum. The reusable diagnostic is to treat any classical fluid formula with suspicion at the point where the mass density appears, and to ask whether relativity promotes it to the enthalpy density. The combination $\rho + p$ recurs everywhere — in the inertia, in the momentum density, in the enthalpy per baryon $h = (\rho+p)/n$, in the speed-of-sound denominator — precisely because it is the single quantity that measures "how much there is to push".

**Vorticity is a two-form, and that is why circulation is conserved.** Classical fluid mechanics treats vorticity as a vector $\nabla\times\mathbf{v}$ and circulation as a line integral, and the conservation theorems (Kelvin, Helmholtz) require somewhat delicate arguments about material derivatives and transported loops. Relativity reveals the cleaner truth: vorticity is the two-form $\Omega = d(hu)$, an exact two-form, and circulation $\oint\pi = \int\Omega$ is its flux. The conservation of circulation is then almost a tautology of exterior calculus: $\Omega$ is closed ($d\Omega = d^2\pi = 0$), so its flux through a tube of fluid lines is conserved, because the side walls of the tube are tangent to the flow and contribute nothing. The baroclinic obstruction — the failure of conservation when pressure and entropy gradients cross — appears precisely as the one term, $T\,dS$, that prevents $\Omega(u,\cdot)$ from vanishing. The structural parallel with electromagnetism is exact and worth holding onto: the electromagnetic two-form $F = dA$ has conserved flux through transported loops in a perfect conductor (the frozen-in theorem), and the vorticity two-form $\Omega = d\pi$ has conserved flux through transported loops in a barotropic fluid (Kelvin's theorem). Two closed two-forms, two transport invariants, one geometric mechanism.

**The nonrelativistic limit is where the relativistic quantities are baptised.** It is tempting to regard the nonrelativistic limit as a mere consistency check — confirmation that the relativistic theory does not contradict the classical one. It is more than that: it is the procedure by which each relativistic quantity earns its physical name. The factor $\Gamma^2$ in the energy density $E = \Gamma^2(\rho+p) - p$ is opaque until the limit shows it is one power of $\Gamma$ from the energy-per-particle and one from the length contraction of the volume. The combination $\rho + p$ is just a symbol until the limit reveals it as the inertia. The fluid momentum one-form $\pi = hu$ is an abstraction until the limit identifies $\langle\pi, u_0\rangle$ with the Bernoulli constant $H + \tfrac12 V^2$. The general lesson for reading any relativistic theory is that the classical limit is not below the relativistic theory but inside it, as the dictionary that translates the four-dimensional symbols into the three-dimensional quantities the symbols were built to generalise. The relativistic equations are primary; the classical equations are their pronunciations in the slow-motion dialect.
