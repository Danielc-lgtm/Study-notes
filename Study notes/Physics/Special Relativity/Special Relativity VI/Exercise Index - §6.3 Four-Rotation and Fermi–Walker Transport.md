## §6.3 Four-Rotation and Fermi–Walker Transport — Exercises

The exercises of §6.3 drill the decomposition of the frame's evolution into a four-acceleration tilt and a spatial rotation, and the three derivatives along a worldline. The Fermi–Walker-versus-absolute exercise shows the absolute derivative tilts a spatial vector out of the rest space while the Fermi–Walker derivative — the orthogonal projection of the absolute derivative — keeps it in, and preserves the four-velocity, pinning down the three precise senses of "non-rotating"; the antisymmetry exercise differentiates the orthonormality to prove the four-rotation is antisymmetric (with the subtle mixed-index split into a symmetric boost block and an antisymmetric rotation block), and characterises an inertial observer as $A_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega = 0$; and the Thomas-precession exercise integrates the Fermi–Walker law around a circular orbit to find the net kinematic rotation $2\pi(\gamma - 1)$ per orbit, the holonomy that supplies the factor of $\tfrac12$ in atomic spin–orbit coupling. The unifying technique: differentiate the constant frame relations, split the four-rotation by the decomposition theorem, and integrate transport around loops to find holonomies.

- [[Ex - Fermi-Walker versus the absolute derivative]] (⭐⭐) — show the absolute derivative of a rest-space vector acquires a time component (leaves the rest space), that the Fermi–Walker derivative equals the orthogonal projection $\Pi(dV/d\tau)$ and preserves both the rest space and the four-velocity, and conclude why the absolute derivative is the wrong gyroscope law ([[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]], [[Def - The Orthogonal Projector onto the Local Rest Space]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]]).

- [[Ex - The four-rotation is antisymmetric and inertial means non-rotating]] (⭐⭐) — differentiate $g(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \eta_{\alpha\beta}$ to prove the four-rotation form is antisymmetric, derive the mixed-index relations $\Omega^i{}_0 = \Omega^0{}_i$ (symmetric boost) and $\Omega^i{}_j = -\Omega^j{}_i$ (antisymmetric rotation), and characterise the inertial observer as $A_0 = 0$ and $\vec\omega = 0$ ([[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]], [[Thm - Orthogonal Decomposition of Antisymmetric Bilinear Forms]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Metric Duality and Index Manipulation]]).

- [[Ex - Fermi-Walker transport of a gyroscope and Thomas precession]] (⭐⭐⭐) — set up the centripetal four-acceleration for circular motion, integrate the Fermi–Walker transport law for a torque-free spin around one orbit to find the net rotation $\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{Thomas}} = 2\pi(\gamma - 1)$, and recover the slow-limit rate $\tfrac12\mathbf a\times\mathbf v$ that fixes the atomic spin–orbit coupling ([[Def - Fermi-Walker Derivative]], [[Def - Local Frame and Four-Rotation]], [[Def - Four-Velocity and Four-Acceleration]], [[Def - Rapidity]]).
