# Notation

We set $c = 1$ and use the signature $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, so a timelike vector $X$ has $X\cdot X > 0$ and a unit four-velocity satisfies $U\cdot U = 1$. A charged particle $\mathcal P_a$ has charge $q_a$, worldline $\mathcal L_a$, proper time $\tau$, position $X_a(\tau)$, and four-velocity $U_a(\tau)$. The **electric four-current** is the vector field $J$ with components $J^\mu$; its metric-dual $1$-form is $\underline J$, $J_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}J^\nu$. An observer $O$ has four-velocity $U_0$; $\rho$ is the charge density and $\mathbf J$ the current density measured by $O$. The proper charge density is $\rho_0$. Here $\delta_A$ is the Dirac distribution on spacetime centred at the event $A$, satisfying $\int_{\mathbb M}\delta_A f\,\epsilon = f(A)$ for any scalar field $f$, where $\epsilon$ is the spacetime volume $4$-form. Full registry on [[Special Relativity XXII — Maxwell's Equations]].

This is a compound page: it defines two interlocking notions — the **microscopic four-current** (the sum of Dirac currents along worldlines) and the **four-current of a continuous medium** ($J = \rho_0 U$) — together with the observer-relative split into charge density $\rho$ and current density $\mathbf J$, because they are introduced together and none is fully usable without the others.

# Axiom Motivation

The problem the four-current solves is this: electromagnetism needs a *source*. The field $F$ from [[Special Relativity XXI — The Electromagnetic Field|the previous topic]] was taken as given, but physically it is generated by electric charge in motion, and to write the law $F = (\text{something})\times(\text{source})$ we must first say what the source is, as a single object that lives on spacetime and transforms correctly under Lorentz transformations. The desideratum is a four-dimensional, observer-independent description of "all the moving charge", from which any observer can extract the charge density and current density they measure.

Start from what an observer measures. Elementary electromagnetism gives an observer two things: a scalar charge density $\rho$ (charge per unit volume) and a vector current density $\mathbf J$ (charge crossing unit area per unit time). These are three-dimensional and observer-dependent: a charge at rest has $\rho \ne 0$, $\mathbf J = 0$, but to a moving observer the same charge is a current, $\mathbf J \ne 0$. So $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ cannot be the fundamental object; they are slices of something larger. The natural guess — and it is forced by how they transform — is that $(\rho, \mathbf J)$ are the time and space components of a single four-vector $J^\mu = (\rho, \mathbf J)$ (with $c$: $(\rho, \mathbf J/c)$). This is the relativistic unification: charge density and current density are not two things but one four-vector seen by one observer, exactly as energy and momentum are one four-vector.

Why must it be a four-*vector*, and not some other tensor? Because the total charge in a region must be a Lorentz scalar — charge is invariant, the same number for every observer, an experimental fact at the heart of electromagnetism. The total charge is obtained by integrating the source over a spatial hypersurface, and the only way that integral can be observer-independent is if the integrand is the flux of a four-vector through the hypersurface. So the four-vector structure is not an aesthetic choice; it is what makes charge invariant. If the source were a scalar, its integral over a hypersurface would not be invariant; if it were a higher tensor, it would carry indices that no observer's $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ could account for.

Now the microscopic construction. A single point charge is not a continuous field — its charge is concentrated on a worldline, a one-dimensional curve in spacetime. To represent this as a four-current density (a vector field on all of spacetime) we need an object "peaked" on the worldline, and the right tool is the Dirac distribution $\delta_A$ on spacetime, which is zero away from $A$ and integrates to $1$. Smearing a charge $q$ along its worldline $X(\tau)$ with four-velocity $U(\tau)$ gives the current $J(M) = q\int\delta_{X(\tau)}(M)U(\tau)\,d\tau$: a vector field supported on the worldline, pointing along the four-velocity, with weight $q$. The four-velocity factor is essential — it is what makes $J$ a vector and what encodes the *direction* of charge flow in spacetime. Summing over all particles gives the total current. This is the only construction that (i) is a vector field, (ii) is supported on the charges' worldlines, (iii) reduces to $\rho = q\delta^{(3)}(\text{position})$ in the charge's rest frame, and (iv) has invariant total charge.

The continuous limit is where the definition becomes most useful and most memorable. For a macroscopic medium with huge particle number, the sum of Dirac currents smooths into a continuous vector field, and it takes the form $J = \rho_0 U$ — the proper charge density $\rho_0$ (charge per unit rest-frame volume, a scalar measured in the matter's own rest frame) times the four-velocity field $U$ of the medium. This is the exact analogue of the dust four-momentum $P = mU$, with rest mass replaced by proper charge density. The reason the proper density appears, rather than the observer's density $\rho$, is that $\rho_0$ is the only scalar in sight: it is measured in the rest frame, where there is no current and the description is simplest, and the four-velocity factor then boosts it to any frame. An observer sees $\rho = \gamma\rho_0$ (length contraction of the rest-frame volume packs the charge denser) and $\mathbf J = \rho_0\gamma\mathbf v = \rho\mathbf v$, recovering the elementary relation between current and charge in motion.

# The Definition

Let $(\mathcal P_a)_{1\le a\le N}$ be a finite set of charged particles, $\mathcal P_a$ carrying charge $q_a$ and following the worldline $\mathcal L_a$ with four-velocity $U_a(\tau)$ and position $X_a(\tau)$ at proper time $\tau$. The **electric four-current** is the vector field
$$
J(M) \;:=\; \sum_{a=1}^{N} q_a \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \delta_{X_a(\tau)}(M)\,U_a(\tau)\,d\tau,
$$
(with $c$ restored, an extra factor $c$ appears in each integrand: $\delta_{X_a(\tau)}(M)\,U_a(\tau)\,c\,d\tau$). Here $\delta_A$ is the Dirac distribution on spacetime centred at $A$, satisfying $\int_{\mathbb M}\delta_A f\,\epsilon = f(A)$. The four-current is supported on the union of the worldlines and points, at each event of a worldline, along the four-velocity there.

For a **continuous medium** the discrete sum is replaced by its continuum limit, a smooth (indeed differentiable) vector field on spacetime, which takes the form
$$
J \;=\; \rho_0\, U,
$$
where $\rho_0$ is the **proper charge density** — the electric charge per unit volume measured in the local rest frame of the matter — and $U$ is the medium's four-velocity field.

Given an observer $O$ of four-velocity $U_0$, the orthogonal decomposition of $J$ with respect to $U_0$ defines the **charge density** $\rho$ and **current density** $\mathbf J$ measured by $O$:
$$
J \;=\; \rho\,U_0 + \mathbf J, \qquad U_0\cdot\mathbf J = 0,
$$
so that
$$
\rho \;=\; U_0\cdot J, \qquad \mathbf J \;=\; J - (U_0\cdot J)\,U_0 \;=\; \perp_{U_0} J.
$$
In the inertial coordinates adapted to $O$, the components are $J^\mu = (\rho, \mathbf J)$ (with $c$: $J^\mu = (\rho, \mathbf J/c)$). The **total charge** in a spatial domain $\mathcal V$ in $O$'s rest space is the flux of $J$ through $\mathcal V$,
$$
Q \;=\; \int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,dV \;=\; \int_{\mathcal V}\star J,
$$
the integral of the Hodge-dual $3$-form $\star J$ over $\mathcal V$.

> Sign-convention note: Gourgoulhon, in the mostly-plus signature, writes $\rho = -\vec u_0\cdot\vec j$ and decomposes $\vec j = \rho\vec u_0 + c^{-1}\vec J$. In our mostly-minus signature the timelike $U_0$ has $U_0\cdot U_0 = +1$, so the sign becomes $\rho = +U_0\cdot J$, with no minus.

# Categorical / Structural Definition

The four-current is a **conserved current** in the sense of de Rham cohomology and Noether's theorem, and this is its cleanest structural home. Its metric-dual $1$-form $\underline J$ has a Hodge-dual $3$-form $\star J$, and the physical content "charge is conserved" is the statement that $\star J$ is a **closed** $3$-form, $d{\star}J = 0$ (established in [[Thm - Electric Charge Conservation and the Gauss Theorem]]). A closed $3$-form on a $4$-manifold defines, by integration over $3$-cycles, an element of the third de Rham cohomology paired against $3$-dimensional homology; the integral $\int_{\mathcal V}\star J = Q$ over a spatial slice is the period of this class, and its independence of which slice is chosen (so long as the slices are homologous and no charge crosses the side wall) is the cohomological statement that $Q$ is a topological invariant of the slice's homology class.

In the language of [[Gauge Theory — Series Map|gauge theory]], $J$ is the **Noether current** of the global $\mathrm{U}(1)$ phase symmetry of the matter fields. By Noether's first theorem, every continuous global symmetry of a field theory's action yields a divergence-free current; the symmetry "multiply every charged field by a constant phase $e^{i\alpha}$" yields exactly the electric four-current, with $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$ as its conservation law. This is why $J$ couples to the [[Def - The Four-Potential|gauge potential]] $A$ through the interaction term $\int A_\mu J^\mu$: the current is precisely the object that the connection's gauge transformations act upon, and the coupling $A_\mu J^\mu$ is the unique gauge-invariant (up to a total derivative) pairing of a connection with a conserved current.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The four-current is the electromagnetic instance of a universal object: a **conserved flux four-vector** carrying a scalar charge. The same structure governs the **baryon-number current** $\rho_0 U$ in [[Special Relativity XXIV — Relativistic Hydrodynamics|relativistic hydrodynamics]] (with $\rho_0$ the proper baryon-number density), the **probability current** of relativistic quantum mechanics, and — with one extra index — the **energy–momentum tensor** $T^{\mu\nu}$ of [[Special Relativity XXIII — The Energy-Momentum Tensor and Field Energy|topic XXIII]], whose conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is the four-current pattern raised by one tensor rank.

**True name:** the four-current is "the proper charge density times the four-velocity", $J = \rho_0 U$. This is the form to reach for in every computation: it says the current is just the charge sitting in the matter, carried along by the matter's motion through spacetime. Everything an observer measures — the density $\rho = \gamma\rho_0$, the current $\mathbf J = \rho_0\gamma\mathbf v$ — follows by projecting this single object onto the observer's frame. The microscopic Dirac-sum form is the definition; $J = \rho_0 U$ is what you use.

The compression with [[Def - Four-Momentum and Rest Mass|four-momentum]] is exact and worth holding onto. Dust has $P = mU$ (rest mass times four-velocity) and the four-current has $J = \rho_0 U$ (proper charge density times four-velocity): mass and charge play identical structural roles, each a scalar invariant of the matter that the four-velocity transports. The conservation laws match too — $\nabla\cdot P = 0$ (force-free) and $\nabla\cdot J = 0$ (charge conserved) — making the four-current the charge analogue of the momentum of dust.

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — a single charge at rest.** A charge $q$ at rest at the spatial origin has four-velocity $U = (1, 0, 0, 0)$ and rest-frame charge density $\rho_0 = q\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x)$, so $J^\mu = \rho_0 U^\mu = (q\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x), 0, 0, 0)$: pure charge density, no current. Any observer moving relative to this charge sees, by boosting, both a (denser) charge density and a current — the same charge "in motion" is a current. This is the prototype: charge at rest is the time-component of the four-current.

**Is an instance — a uniformly moving line of charge.** A wire carrying steady current is, in its own rest frame, a line of charge with proper linear density $\rho_0$ at rest; in the lab frame, where the charges drift at velocity $\mathbf v$, the four-current is $J = \rho_0 U$ with $U = \gamma(1, \mathbf v)$, giving $\rho = \gamma\rho_0$ and $\mathbf J = \gamma\rho_0\mathbf v$. (For a neutral wire one superposes two such currents, of opposite charge and opposite drift, with densities arranged so $\rho = 0$ but $\mathbf J \ne 0$ — the everyday current-carrying wire, electrically neutral yet magnetically active.)

**Is NOT an instance — the coordinate pair $(\rho, \mathbf J)$ of a single observer treated as fundamental.** The list $(\rho, \mathbf J)$ measured by one observer is *not* the four-current; it is the four-current's components *in that observer's frame*. Treating $(\rho, \mathbf J)$ as the primary object fails because a change of observer mixes $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ (a static charge becomes a current), whereas the four-vector $J$ itself is observer-independent. The error is the same as confusing a four-vector with its component list; $J$ is the platonic object, $(\rho, \mathbf J)$ a particular viewing.

**Is NOT an instance — a generic vector field with nonzero divergence.** A vector field $K$ on spacetime with $\nabla\cdot K \ne 0$ cannot be a physical electric four-current, because [[Thm - Electric Charge Conservation and the Gauss Theorem|charge conservation]] forces $\nabla\cdot J = 0$. A field that "creates charge from nothing" — with charge appearing in a region without flowing in across its boundary — violates the continuity equation and is not a legal source for Maxwell's equations. Divergence-freedom is a hard constraint, not an option.

**Corollary — the charge density transforms with a single factor of $\gamma$.** From $\rho = U_0\cdot J = U_0\cdot(\rho_0 U) = \rho_0(U_0\cdot U) = \rho_0\gamma$, where $\gamma = U_0\cdot U$ is the Lorentz factor between observer and matter. So a moving charge distribution appears denser by $\gamma$: the rest-frame volume is length-contracted, packing the same charge into less space. This is the charge analogue of relativistic mass increase, and it is exactly what makes a current-carrying wire's relativistic length contraction produce the magnetic force.

**Corollary — total charge is the flux of $J$, hence invariant.** The charge in a spatial domain $\mathcal V$ is $Q = \int_{\mathcal V}\star J$, the integral of the Hodge-dual $3$-form. Because $\star J$ is closed ($d{\star}J = 0$), this integral depends only on the homology class of $\mathcal V$, and two observers' spatial slices through the same physical region give the same $Q$ — charge is a Lorentz invariant. This is the corollary that justifies demanding $J$ be a four-vector in the first place.

**Calibration check.** If you have understood the four-current you can: (i) write down $J^\mu$ for a charge $q$ moving at constant velocity $\mathbf v$ and read off both $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$; (ii) verify that $\rho = \gamma\rho_0$ by contracting $J = \rho_0 U$ with the observer's four-velocity; and (iii) explain why the total charge $\int\rho\,dV$ is the same for every inertial observer, by recognising it as the flux of a four-vector through a hypersurface.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Maxwell's Equations *(from §22.1)*
> With a source $J$ in hand, the inhomogeneous [[Thm - Maxwell Equations|Maxwell equation]] $d{\star}F = \mu_0{\star}J$ can finally be written: the four-current is what the electromagnetic field is sourced by, and applying $d$ to this equation forces $\nabla\cdot J = 0$, [[Thm - Electric Charge Conservation and the Gauss Theorem|charge conservation]], for free.

> [!tip] The Baryon Current and Relativistic Fluids *(from Relativistic Hydrodynamics)*
> The form $J = \rho_0 U$ is the template for the **baryon-number four-current** $n_0 U$ of a relativistic fluid, whose conservation $\nabla_\mu(n_0 U^\mu) = 0$ expresses the conservation of particle number along the flow — the relativistic continuity equation of [[Special Relativity XXIV — Relativistic Hydrodynamics|hydrodynamics]], from which the relativistic Euler equation is derived by projecting the conservation of $T^{\mu\nu}$.

> [!tip] The Noether Current and Charge Quantisation *(from QFT and Gauge Theory)*
> In quantum field theory the electric four-current is the **Noether current** of the global $\mathrm{U}(1)$ phase symmetry, and its conserved charge $Q$ generates that symmetry on the Hilbert space. Gauging the symmetry — promoting the constant phase to a spacetime-dependent one — forces the existence of the photon and couples it to exactly this current through $A_\mu J^\mu$, the deep reason that the gauge field couples to charge. The discreteness of the symmetry group (for compact $\mathrm{U}(1)$) is connected to the experimental **quantisation of electric charge**.
