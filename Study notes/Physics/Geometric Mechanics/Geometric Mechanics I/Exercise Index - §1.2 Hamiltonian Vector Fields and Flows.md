## §1.2 Hamiltonian Vector Fields and Flows — Exercises

The exercises in this section drill the **dynamics generated by a Hamiltonian function**: deriving the Hamiltonian vector field from $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$, integrating it to obtain the Hamiltonian flow, verifying flow properties (energy conservation, symplectic preservation, volume preservation), and recognizing important examples (geodesic flow, magnetic dynamics). The unifying theme is **the symplectic structure converts energy functions into flows, and the resulting flows preserve everything that matters geometrically**. The flagship example is the **geodesic flow as a Hamiltonian system** — the cleanest non-trivial example of "physical dynamics from geometric input alone" (just a Riemannian metric).

- [[Ex - Geodesic Flow on a Riemannian Manifold is Hamiltonian]] (⭐⭐) — construct $H = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{ij}p_ip_j$ on $T^*M$ and show its Hamiltonian flow projects to geodesics on $M$, with conserved energy $= \tfrac{1}{2}|\dot\gamma|^2_g$ (squared speed) ([[Def - Hamiltonian Vector Field]], [[Def - Riemannian Metric]], [[Def - The Canonical Symplectic Form on a Cotangent Bundle]]).

- [[Ex - The Lagrange Submanifold of a Magnetic Field]] (⭐⭐⭐) — minimal coupling $p \to p - eA$ as a gauge transformation in phase space; equivalence of "canonical symplectic + modified Hamiltonian" and "twisted symplectic + simple Hamiltonian" formulations; gauge invariance ([[Def - Hamiltonian Vector Field]], [[Def - Symplectomorphism (Canonical Transformation)]], [[Def - Lagrangian Submanifold]]).

- [[Ex - Harmonic Oscillator in Phase Space]] (⭐) — also drills Hamiltonian vector field computation, explicit integration of Hamilton's equations, and direct verification of energy conservation; the universal prototype of a Hamiltonian flow ([[Def - Hamiltonian Vector Field]], [[Def - Hamiltonian Function]]).
