# §6 — A probability measure on homotopy and homology classes

Back to the [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology|hub]]. This is the paper's payoff. Sections 4 and 5 established that the total loop mass on a hyperbolic surface is finite (a Selberg zeta value, or a spectral-determinant after renormalisation in the finite-area case); dividing each free-homotopy class's mass by that total upgrades the loop measure to an honest **probability measure on topological types**. Section 6.1 does this at the homotopy level: the geodesic length $L = m\ell_\gamma$ becomes the natural random variable, and its moment generating function is a ratio $\log Z_X(s + r)/\log Z_X(s)$ of Selberg zeta values, so every moment falls out of derivatives of the zeta. Section 6.2 then coarsens the partition from *homotopy* (a conjugacy class, remembering the order in which handles are traversed) to *homology* (an integer vector, remembering only net winding); the coarsening is many-to-one but tractable via **Fourier analysis on the abelian group $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$** — the paper twists the Selberg zeta by a unitary character (getting a Selberg $L$-function, the geodesic analogue of a Dirichlet $L$-function), regroups its log-expansion by homology class, and Fourier-inverts to extract each class-mass. The same character-orthogonality mechanism, applied to the characteristic function of the loop soup's total homology, yields the full distribution of $\beta(\lambda) := \sum_{\eta \in \mathcal L^*_\lambda}[\eta]$ in closed form.

**Symbols.** $\kappa > 0$ the killing rate, $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$ the spectral parameter (assumed $> \delta$); $Z_X(s)$ the Selberg zeta, $L_X(s, \chi)$ its character-twisted $L$-function; $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ the first homology (a lattice $\mathbb{Z}^r$), $\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})}$ the character torus $(S^1)^r$; $\chi : H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \to S^1$ a unitary character, $\beta \in H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ a homology class; $L = m\ell_\gamma$ the geodesic length of the class $C_X(\gamma^m)$; $\mathcal L_\lambda$ the loop soup of intensity $\lambda$.

### From earlier sections (paper items)

- **[[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2 — Mass of a free homotopy class]]** (§3) — for a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form, $\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, \tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z)$: the strip-integral identity that makes each topological-type mass finite and computable.
- **[[Thm - Mass of a Subordinate Brownian Loop Class|Theorem 3.5 — Killed loop mass in closed form]]** (§3.1.2) — specialised to Bernstein $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda + \kappa$: $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$, with $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$. Every mass §6 discusses is a sum of these positive numbers.
- **[[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Corollary 4.3 — Total mass as a Selberg zeta value]]** (§4.1.1) — $\sum_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\sum_{m \ge 1}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = -\log Z_X(s)$: the total killed loop mass equals the negative logarithm of the Selberg zeta at $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$. This is the normalising constant of §6.1's probability measure and the reference against which §6.2's character-twisted version is measured.
- **[[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1 — Renormalised total in the finite-area case]]** (§5.1) — for a closed hyperbolic surface where the untwisted total $-\log Z_X(1)$ is divergent, the renormalised version is $\log\det_\zeta \Delta_X - \operatorname{Area}(X)E$ up to $O(\kappa)$ corrections that vanish as $\kappa \to 0^+$. Referenced by the paper's remark that §6.1's construction extends to $\kappa = 0$ via §5's expressions.

### Above-floor prerequisites — §3.0-baseline recalls

> [!recall]- Free homotopy class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ on $X$
> **Formally:** two oriented closed loops on $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ are *freely homotopic* if one continuously deforms into the other on $X$ with the basepoint allowed to move. The set of free homotopy classes is in bijection with the set of conjugacy classes $[h]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \{qhq^{-1} : q \in \Gamma\}$ of the discrete group $\Gamma$; the class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ corresponds to the conjugacy class of $\tau^m \in \Gamma$, where $\tau$ is the primitive hyperbolic element of translation length $\ell_\gamma$ representing $\gamma$. Winding around $\gamma$ once gives $C_X(\gamma)$; winding $m$ times gives $C_X(\gamma^m)$.
> **In words:** each loop on the surface "goes around some collection of holes in some pattern"; two loops going around the same holes in the same pattern (with the starting point free to drift) belong to one class. The class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is "the taut closed geodesic $\gamma$, traced $m$ times". The countable index of §6.1's measure is exactly these classes: pairs $(\gamma, m)$ with $\gamma \in \mathcal P_X$ and $m \ge 1$.
> **Concretely:** on the torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian, so free homotopy classes are in bijection with $\mathbb{Z}^2$: the class of $(a, b)$ is "$a$ times horizontally, $b$ times vertically". On a hyperbolic closed genus-$2$ pretzel, $\Gamma$ is non-abelian and free-homotopy classes are indexed by pairs $(\gamma, m)$ with $\gamma$ picking a primitive closed geodesic and $m \ge 1$ counting iterations. Full detail: [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- First homology $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$, Hurewicz map $[\gamma]$, and unitary characters
> **Formally:** the *first homology group* $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ is the abelianisation of the fundamental group $\Gamma$: quotient $\Gamma$ by its *commutator subgroup* $[\Gamma, \Gamma]$ (the subgroup generated by all elements $q h q^{-1} h^{-1}$). The Hurewicz map $\gamma \mapsto [\gamma]$ is the quotient homomorphism; it satisfies $[\gamma^m] = m[\gamma]$. Rank: $r = 2g$ for a closed genus-$g$ surface, $r = 2g + b - 1$ for genus $g$ with $b \ge 1$ ends, so $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$. A *unitary character* is a group homomorphism $\chi : H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \to S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$; the set of all such is the *character torus* $\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})} \cong (S^1)^r$, a compact abelian group.
> **In words:** homotopy remembers the *whole* sequence of loops around each handle, in order — a non-commutative datum; *homology* is the "abelianised shadow" that remembers only the *net* winding around each independent cycle — a commutative integer vector. The Hurewicz map is the "forget the order" operation. A unitary character is a rule for assigning a complex phase $\chi(\beta) \in S^1$ to each homology class $\beta$, in a way that respects addition of windings ($\chi(\beta_1 + \beta_2) = \chi(\beta_1)\chi(\beta_2)$). The character torus is the space of *all* such rules — the natural "Fourier dual" of $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$.
> **Concretely:** on the torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ (genus $1$, closed), $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is already abelian, so $[\Gamma, \Gamma] = 0$ and $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$: a class is an integer pair $(a, b) = ($net horizontal, net vertical$)$. Characters are $\chi_{(u, v)}(a, b) = e^{2\pi i(au + bv)}$, parametrised by $(u, v) \in [0, 1)^2 = \widehat{H_1(T^2, \mathbb{Z})}$. On a closed genus-$2$ surface, $\Gamma$ has $4$ generators with one commutator relation, so $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^4$ and the character torus is $[0, 1)^4$. On a once-punctured torus ($g = 1$, $b = 1$), $r = 2$, still $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$. Full detail: [[Def - First Homology, Characters, and Finite Fourier Analysis]].

> [!recall]- Character orthogonality on $\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})}$
> **Formally:** the character torus $\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})} \cong (S^1)^r$ carries a *normalised Haar measure* $d\chi$ (the unique translation-invariant probability measure; concretely $d\chi = d\theta_1\cdots d\theta_r$ on $(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^r$). *Character orthogonality:* for every $\beta, \beta' \in H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$,
> $$\int_{\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})}}\chi(\beta')\overline{\chi(\beta)}\,d\chi = \begin{cases}1, & \beta' = \beta,\\ 0, & \beta' \ne \beta.\end{cases}$$
> **In words:** the character torus is the "space of all Fourier modes on $H_1$"; distinct characters are orthogonal when integrated against each other over the whole torus with the natural probability measure. The identity is Fourier inversion for the abelian group $H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$: if you write a function on $H_1$ as a sum of character values (a Fourier series), then multiplying by the character-value at one specific $\beta$ and integrating over the torus extracts the coefficient at $\beta$. Same recipe as ordinary Fourier series on the circle, generalised to a lattice.
> **Concretely:** on the circle $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ with $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}$, characters are $\chi_u(n) = e^{2\pi i n u}$; orthogonality reads $\int_0^1 e^{2\pi i(n' - n)u}\,du = \delta_{n', n}$ — the elementary Fourier identity. On $T^2$ with $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$: $\int_0^1\!\int_0^1 e^{2\pi i((a' - a)u + (b' - b)v)}\,du\,dv = \delta_{a', a}\delta_{b', b}$, a product of two circle identities. Full detail: [[Def - First Homology, Characters, and Finite Fourier Analysis]].

> [!recall]- Loop soup $\mathcal L_\lambda$ (Poisson point process on loop space)
> **Formally:** for the σ-finite intensity $\lambda\,\mu^\kappa_X$ on the loop space $C_X$, the *loop soup* $\mathcal L_\lambda$ is the Poisson point process with that intensity — a random countable subset $\mathcal L_\lambda \subset C_X$ satisfying (i) for every measurable $A \subset C_X$ with $\mu^\kappa_X(A) < \infty$, the count $N_A := \#(\mathcal L_\lambda \cap A)$ is Poisson-distributed with mean $\lambda\,\mu^\kappa_X(A)$; (ii) disjoint measurable sets give independent counts. Write $\mathcal L^*_\lambda \subset \mathcal L_\lambda$ for the loops of the soup that genuinely wrap at least one hole — i.e. throw away both the contractible loops and the "peripheral" ones that just circle a puncture / cusp (a puncture-shaped end of the surface — infinite length but finite area — whose encircling loops can be pushed all the way to the puncture and so shrink to zero length).
> **In words:** a random scatter of loops on $X$; the number falling into any given measurable class is Poisson with mean the class's mass times $\lambda$; loops in disjoint classes are independent. Feeding the killed loop measure in as intensity converts each numerical mass (the number in §3.1's closed form) into a distributional statement about a *random* collection of loops. On the surface, this is the same construction as a Poisson scatter of points in the plane, but with "points" replaced by "closed loops".
> **Concretely:** on a surface with total killed loop mass $-\log Z_X(s) = 0.68$ and intensity $\lambda = 1$: $\#\mathcal L^*_\lambda$ is Poisson with mean $0.68$ ($\approx 51\%$ chance of $0$ loops, $\approx 35\%$ chance of $1$, $\approx 12\%$ chance of $2$, $\approx 3\%$ chance of $\ge 3$). Each loop, given it exists, has homology class distributed proportionally to $\mu^\kappa_X(\beta)$. Full detail: [[Def - Poisson Point Process and the Loop Soup]].

> [!recall]- Poisson exponential (Campbell) formula
> **Formally:** for a Poisson point process $\Pi$ on $(S, \mathcal S)$ with σ-finite intensity $\Lambda$ and any measurable $F : S \to \mathbb{C}$ satisfying $\int|e^F - 1|\,d\Lambda < \infty$,
> $$\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{x \in \Pi}e^{F(x)}\Big] = \exp\!\Big(\int_S(e^{F(x)} - 1)\,\Lambda(dx)\Big).$$
> **In words:** the expected value of a multiplicative functional over a Poisson process equals the exponential of an *additive* integral of $e^F - 1$ against the intensity. It is *the* fundamental identity of Poisson processes — the reason so much about Poisson scatters is expressible as an integral of an ordinary function of a single point. Proof idea: condition on the total number of points in each finite-mass region (which is Poisson); given $n$ points they are i.i.d. draws from $\Lambda/\Lambda(\cdot)$; expectations of products of i.i.d. become $n$-th powers; summing over Poisson-distributed $n$ turns polynomial-in-$n$ into $\exp$.
> **Concretely:** for a rate-$\lambda$ Poisson process on $\mathbb{R}$ with $F(x) = \log(1 + f(x))$, $f \ge 0$: $\mathbb{E}\prod_i(1 + f(x_i)) = \exp(\lambda\int f\,dx)$. In our setting, $F(\eta) = \log\chi([\eta])$ (a phase) gives $\prod_\eta \chi([\eta]) = \chi(\sum_\eta [\eta]) = \chi(\beta(\lambda))$, so its expectation is $\exp(\lambda\int(\chi([\eta]) - 1)\,d\mu^\kappa_X)$ — the identity Proposition 6.7 evaluates. Reference: Kingman, *Poisson Processes*, §3.

## Concept map

### §6.1 — A probability measure on free homotopy classes

- **[[Def - Probability Measure on Homotopy Classes|Definition (probability measure on homotopy classes)]]** — Normalises the killed loop mass by its finite total: $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) := \mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))/(-\log Z_X(s))$, with $\kappa > 0$ and $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$. The denominator is the Selberg zeta value from [[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Corollary 4.3]], finite and positive for $s > \delta$. Under $\mathbb{P}_s$ each free-homotopy class carries probability proportional to its mass; the geodesic length $L = m\ell_\gamma$ becomes a random variable.
  - **Moments and the tilt identity.** The atomic subpage carries the full derivation, in two collapsible callouts. First: the tilt identity $\mathbb{E}_s[e^{-rL}] = \log Z_X(s + r)/\log Z_X(s)$ (shifting $s$ by $r$ is exactly tilting by length, because the mass depends on $s$ only through $e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}$); differentiating $F(s) = -\log Z_X(s)$ pulls down $-L$, so $\mathbb{E}_s[L^n] = (-1)^n F^{(n)}(s)/F(s)$ gives all moments at once. Mean is $-(d/ds)\log F$, variance is $(d^2/ds^2)\log F$; convexity of $\log F$ forces $s \mapsto \mathbb{E}_s[L]$ to be strictly decreasing — more killing shortens typical classes.
  - **Systole limit.** As $s \to \infty$, mass concentrates on primitive systolic classes (those realising the shortest length $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$). Because no hyperbolic element of a torsion-free Fuchsian group is conjugate to its inverse, the systole is realised by at least two oriented classes ($N_{\mathrm{sys}} \ge 2$); $\mathbb{P}_s$ concentrates uniformly on them with $\mathbb{E}_s[L] \to \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$. Analytic side: $-\log Z_X(s) \sim \frac{N_{\mathrm{sys}}}{1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}}e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}$, so both $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ are read off from large-$s$ asymptotics of the Selberg zeta.

### §6.2 — A probability measure on homology classes

- **[[Def - Mass in a Homology Class|Definition 6.1 — Mass in a homology class]]** — The killed loop mass regrouped by *homology* rather than *homotopy*: $\mu^\kappa_X(\beta) := \sum_{(\gamma, m):\,m[\gamma] = \beta}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ for $\beta \in H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})$. Infinitely many free-homotopy classes lie above each $\beta$ on a surface of genus $\ge 2$, but the sum converges (dominated by the Selberg zeta identity). Homology is the abelianised shadow of homotopy: forget the order in which handles are traversed, keep only net winding.

- **[[Remark - Provenance of the Homology Class Definition|Remark 6.2 — Provenance and motivation]]** — The homology-graded Brownian loop measure was first defined by Le Jan [LJ11] in a compact-only setup with a different apparatus; the authors developed Definition 6.1 independently, later found the two agree, and observe that the character-twist route here extends the construction to the geometrically finite non-compact case. Motivation for grading by homology rather than homotopy: *algebraic* intersection numbers of closed curves are homology invariants (unlike *geometric* intersection numbers, which are homotopy invariants), so the homology-graded measure is the natural object for intersection questions. The mechanism that makes the regrouping tractable is the identity $\chi([\gamma])^m = \chi(m[\gamma]) = \chi(\beta)$: unitary characters see only homology, so the character-weighted sum over $(\gamma, m)$ regroups by $\beta$.

- **Definition 6.3 — Selberg $L$-function ([[Def - Selberg L-Function]])** — The character-twisted Selberg zeta: $L_X(s, \chi) := \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\prod_{k \ge 0}(1 - \chi([\gamma])e^{-(s+k)\ell_\gamma})$ for $\operatorname{Re}s > \delta$ and $\chi : H_1(X, \mathbb{Z}) \to S^1$ unitary. Convergence uses $|\chi([\gamma])| = 1$ so the twisted terms have the same absolute size as the untwisted ones. Meromorphic continuation to $\mathbb{C}$; when $\chi \equiv 1$, $L_X(s, \chi) = Z_X(s)$. The geodesic analogue of a Dirichlet $L$-function.

- **Corollary 6.4 — Selberg $L$-function identity ([[Cor - Selberg L-Function Identity]])** — Log-expansion: $-\log L_X(s, \chi) = \sum_{\gamma, m}\chi([\gamma])^m\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$. **Mechanism (essentially the same as the untwisted Selberg identity).** Take the log of the Euler product; Taylor-expand each $-\log(1 - z)$ using $|z| = e^{-(\operatorname{Re}s + k)\ell_\gamma} < 1$; the character phase $\chi([\gamma])$ factors out of the $k$-sum unchanged; the geometric series $\sum_k e^{-(s+k)m\ell_\gamma}$ collapses to $\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$, recognised as the closed-form killed loop mass from §3.1.2 up to a character weight $\chi([\gamma])^m$.

- **[[Thm - Fourier Inversion by Homology Class|Theorem 6.5 — Fourier expansion and inversion by homology class]]** — Two identities: (i) *Fourier expansion*, $-\log L_X(s, \chi) = \sum_\beta \chi(\beta)\,\mu^\kappa_X(\beta)$ (obtained from Corollary 6.4 by using $\chi([\gamma])^m = \chi(\beta)$ when $m[\gamma] = \beta$ and regrouping the double sum by homology class); (ii) *inversion*, $\mu^\kappa_X(\beta) = \int_{\widehat{H_1}}(-\log L_X(s, \chi))\,\overline{\chi(\beta)}\,d\chi$ (character orthogonality extracts the coefficient at $\beta$). The paper's core §6.2 result: the mass in each homology class is a single integral over the character torus, bypassing any enumeration of the infinitely many free-homotopy classes lying above $\beta$.

- **[[Remark - Jacobian Form of the Fourier Inversion|Remark 6.6 — Jacobian form of the inversion]]** — On a *closed* hyperbolic surface, the character torus is naturally identified with the Jacobian variety, $\widehat{H_1(X, \mathbb{Z})} \cong \operatorname{Jac}(X)$, via the Hodge-theoretic map $[\omega] \mapsto \chi_{[\omega]}$, $\chi_{[\omega]}(\beta) = e^{2\pi i\int_\beta \omega}$ (a harmonic $1$-form determines a unitary character by exponentiating $2\pi i$ times the period). The inversion formula (Theorem 6.5) then reads $\mu^\kappa_X(\beta) = \int_{\operatorname{Jac}(X)}(-\log L_X(s, \chi_{[\omega]}))\,e^{-2\pi i\int_\beta \omega}\,d[\omega]$. Same statement, Hodge-theoretic coordinates; the identification with the Jacobian is available *only* in the closed case.

- **[[Prop - Total Homology of the Loop Soup|Proposition 6.7 — Distribution of the loop soup's total homology]]** — For the killed loop soup $\mathcal L_\lambda$ of intensity $\lambda$ and $\beta(\lambda) := \sum_{\eta \in \mathcal L^*_\lambda}[\eta]$ its total homology (a finite sum a.s., since $\#\mathcal L^*_\lambda$ is Poisson of finite mean $-\lambda\log Z_X(s)$), the characteristic function is $\mathbb{E}[\chi(\beta(\lambda))] = (Z_X(s)/L_X(s, \chi))^\lambda$; Fourier-inverting gives $\mathbb{P}(\beta(\lambda) = \beta) = Z_X(s)^\lambda\int_{\widehat{H_1}}L_X(s, \chi)^{-\lambda}\overline{\chi(\beta)}\,d\chi$. **Mechanism:** apply the Poisson exponential formula with $e^F = \chi([\cdot])$ to convert $\mathbb{E}[\chi(\beta(\lambda))]$ into $\exp(\lambda\int(\chi([\eta]) - 1)d\mu^\kappa_X)$; evaluate the intensity integral using Corollary 6.4 for the $\chi([\eta])$ term and Corollary 4.3 for the $-1$ term, yielding $\lambda\log(Z_X(s)/L_X(s, \chi))$; exponentiate; Fourier-invert as in Theorem 6.5.
