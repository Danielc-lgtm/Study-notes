# Paper — Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology

> [!abstract] What this paper does
> On a hyperbolic surface — a surface of constant negative curvature, like a many-holed doughnut given its natural geometry — there is a natural, if peculiar, way to assign a *size* to a bundle of random loops. You run a Brownian particle (the continuous random wandering that models a speck of pollen in water), let it return to where it started to form a loop, and you weight loops by a scale-invariant rule that counts loops of every possible duration on equal footing. The total weight of *all* loops is infinite, but the paper shows that once you sort the loops by their **topological type** — how many times, and around which holes, each loop winds — each individual type carries only a *finite* weight, and moreover that weight has a clean closed form built from the length of the corresponding closed geodesic (the shortest closed curve of that type). Summing these finite weights and renormalising the infinite total via a **zeta function** (a bookkeeping device that tames divergent sums), the authors turn the loop weights into an honest **probability distribution over topological types** — you can now ask "what is the probability that a random loop winds around this handle rather than that one?" and get a number. The distribution's normalising constant turns out to be a classical object of spectral geometry (the Selberg zeta function and the determinant of the Laplacian), tying random loops to the geometry of the surface, its length spectrum, and even Polyakov's formula from string theory.
>
> The two headline results are: (1) a **decomposition theorem** giving the exact finite mass of the Brownian loop measure in each free homotopy class in terms of the geodesic length (Theorem 3.2), and (2) the construction of a genuine **probability measure on homotopy and homology classes** whose normalisation is a Selberg zeta value (Sections 4–6), extended to hyperbolic 3-manifolds in Section 7.

**Citation.** Dmitry Belyaev and Farhad Huseynli, *A probability measure on homotopy & homology classes via Brownian loops*, 2025 (preprint; the source PDF is in `paper_source/`). These notes follow the paper's own section order.

**The floor.** These notes assume only undergraduate analysis, linear algebra, and elementary probability. Everything the paper uses above that — measure theory (σ-finite and signed measures, disintegration), Riemannian and hyperbolic geometry, the Laplace–Beltrami operator and its heat kernel, Brownian motion on a manifold, Dirichlet forms, Bernstein functions and subordinators, Fuchsian groups and closed geodesics, Poisson point processes, Selberg and Ruelle zeta functions, and zeta-regularised determinants — is recalled at its point of use or written out in a linked atomic note in this folder, and every proof is rewritten so each step is checkable without leaving the page. Because the paper's whole stage is **measure-theoretic probability on a curved space**, two prerequisites pervade everything: a *measure* is a rule assigning sizes to sets (not necessarily total size 1 — here the loop "measure" has infinite total mass), and the *heat kernel* $p(t,x,y)$ is the probability density for a Brownian particle to travel from $x$ to $y$ in time $t$. Both are set up in the §2 notes.

# Notation and Standing Conventions

**Standing conventions.** (1) **Sign of the Laplacian.** $\Delta_X = -\operatorname{div}_g \operatorname{grad}_g$ is the *positive* (geometer's) Laplace–Beltrami operator, so its spectrum lies in $[0,\infty)$ and the heat semigroup is $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ (note the minus sign). (2) **Brownian motion runs at speed 2** — it is generated by $-\Delta_X$ rather than $-\frac12\Delta_X$; this only rescales time and fixes the short-time heat-kernel constant to $p(t,x,x)\sim 1/(4\pi t)$. (3) **Surfaces are hyperbolic unless stated** — from §3 on, $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ is a quotient of the hyperbolic plane by a Fuchsian group $\Gamma$. (4) **Homotopy classes are non-trivial and non-peripheral** unless stated (they do not bound a disc, and do not wind into a cusp or around a boundary). (5) Two symbols the paper overloads are separated here: the **subordination/proper-time variable is $s$** and the **spectral / zeta variable is written $\mathfrak s$** where both occur near each other; the paper writes both as $s$ (⚠️ flagged where it matters).
