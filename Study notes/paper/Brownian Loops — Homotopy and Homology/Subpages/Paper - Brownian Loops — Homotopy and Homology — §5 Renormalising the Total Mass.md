# §5 — Renormalising the total mass of Brownian loops

Back to the [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology|hub]]. **Section opener.** [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §4 Zeta Functions and Total Mass|§4]] identified the total Brownian loop mass over non-trivial classes on a hyperbolic surface $X$ with $-\log Z_X(1)$ — a Selberg-zeta value that is finite for infinite-area surfaces ($\delta < 1$) but *divergent* on finite-area ones ($\delta = 1$, and $s = 1$ hits the critical exponent). To promote this into an honest probability measure on homotopy classes in §6, the divergent total must be renormalised. The right renormalised value turns out to be a classical spectral invariant, the **zeta-regularised determinant of the Laplacian** $\det_\zeta\Delta_X$ (compact case) or its cusped-surface analogue $\det_0\Delta_X$ (finite-area case). This section expresses $-\log\det$ as a (renormalised) total loop mass in the compact case (§5.1, [[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1]]), transports it to any conformally equivalent metric (§5.1.1, [[Cor - Polyakov Formula via Brownian Loop Measure|Corollary 5.4]] via [[Thm - Polyakov Conformal Anomaly Formula|Polyakov, Theorem 5.3]]), and handles the finite-area cusped case (§5.2, [[Thm - Determinant via Loop Measure, Finite-Area Case|Theorem 5.7]] via [[Thm - Borthwick-Judge-Perry Determinant Formula|Borthwick–Judge–Perry, Theorem 5.5]]). Much of the machinery — the Selberg trace formula, Naud's determinant expansion, D'Hoker–Phong, Borthwick–Judge–Perry, Polyakov — is external; the paper's own content is the *reorganisation into loop masses* and a clean $\kappa \to 0$ limit in which a $\log\kappa$ divergence cancels against the Selberg zeta's simple zero at $s = 1$.

## Prerequisites recap

A reader landing on this section without having read §2–§4 finds every above-floor concept the section uses either as a three-field `> [!recall]-` callout, or as a wikilinked one-line "From earlier sections" reminder for the paper's own results reused here.

### From earlier sections (paper items)

- **Selberg zeta $Z_X(s)$ (Definition 4.1) ([[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent]])** (§4) — $Z_X(s) = \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\prod_{k \ge 0}(1 - e^{-(s+k)\ell_\gamma})$, converging for $\operatorname{Re}s > \delta$, extending meromorphically to $\mathbb C$; each Laplace eigenvalue produces a zero of $Z_X$ on the interval $s \in [1/2, 1]$, and the zero mode $\lambda_0 = 0$ (present when $\operatorname{Area}(X) < \infty$) forces a **simple zero at $s = 1$**. This simple zero is what §5's $\kappa \to 0$ limit cancels the $\log\kappa$ divergence against.
- **[[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Selberg zeta identity (Corollary 4.3)]]** (§4) — the total non-trivial killed loop mass $\sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ equals $-\log Z_X(s)$ with $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$. This identity is substituted into Naud's formula (compact) and Borthwick–Judge–Perry (cusped) to turn Selberg-zeta content into loop-mass content.
- **[[Thm - Prime Geodesic Theorem|Prime geodesic theorem]] and its refinement** (§4) — $N_X(R) := \#\{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X : \ell_\gamma \le R\} \sim e^{\delta R}/(\delta R)$, and (refined, closed case) $N_X(R) - \widetilde{\mathrm{Li}}(e^R) = O_X(e^{(1-\epsilon)R})$. Bounds the growth of the geodesic counting function and makes the renormalising integral of case (i) converge.
- **[[Thm - Mass of a Subordinate Brownian Loop Class|Class mass closed forms (Theorem 3.5)]]** (§3) — the four closed-form class masses used to identify the non-primitive part of Naud's integral as a sum of loop masses.

### Above-floor prerequisites recalled here

> [!recall]- Zeta-regularised determinant of the Laplacian
> **Formally:** for a closed surface with Laplace eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots \to \infty$, the **spectral zeta function** is $\zeta_X(s) := \sum_{j \ge 1}\lambda_j^{-s}$ (convergent for $\operatorname{Re}s > 1$, since $\lambda_j \to \infty$ like $j$ by Weyl's law). It equals the Mellin transform $\Gamma(s)^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}(\operatorname{Tr}e^{-t\Delta_X} - 1)\,dt$, meromorphically continues to $\mathbb C$, and is regular at $s = 0$. Then $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X := -\zeta_X'(0)$.
> **In words:** you want the "product of all Laplace eigenvalues" $\prod_{j \ge 1}\lambda_j$, but this product is infinite (there are infinitely many $\lambda_j$ growing to infinity). The zeta-regularised determinant is a finite, canonical stand-in for that divergent product. The trick: use $\log\prod_j\lambda_j = \sum_j\log\lambda_j$; note that formally $\sum_j\log\lambda_j = -\frac{d}{ds}\big|_{s=0}\sum_j\lambda_j^{-s} = -\zeta_X'(0)$; and although the series $\sum_j \lambda_j^{-s}$ only converges when $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$, it extends to a well-defined complex-analytic function of $s$ on all of $\mathbb C$ except a few isolated poles — so its derivative at $s = 0$ is a definite finite number, and $-\zeta'(0)$ is finite. Define $\det_\zeta\Delta := e^{-\zeta_X'(0)}$ by fiat. It reduces to the ordinary determinant when there are only finitely many eigenvalues.
> **Concretely:** if $\Delta$ has only three eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = 2, \lambda_3 = 3$, then $\zeta(s) = 1 + 2^{-s} + 3^{-s}$; $\zeta'(0) = -\log 2 - \log 3 = -\log 6$; so $\log\det_\zeta = -\zeta'(0) = \log 6$; giving $\det_\zeta = 6 = 1\cdot 2\cdot 3$, the ordinary product. For infinitely many $\lambda_j$, the same recipe extends via analytic continuation. On the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb R^2/(2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$, $\det_\zeta\Delta$ is a finite Jacobi-theta-product number. See [[Def - Zeta-Regularised Determinant of the Laplacian]].

> [!recall]- Renormalised trace and 0-determinant (cusped case)
> **Formally:** on a finite-area but non-compact hyperbolic surface (one with cusps — puncture-shaped ends going off to infinity), Melrose's compactification $\bar X$ adds a boundary at infinity, with a smooth boundary defining function $x : \bar X \to [0, \infty)$ ($x = 0$ exactly on the boundary). The **renormalised integral** $^{0}\!\!\int_X f := \operatorname{FP}_{z=0}\int_X x^z f\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_g$ takes the divergent $\int_X f$, multiplies the integrand by $x^z$ (making it convergent for $\operatorname{Re}z > 0$), analytically continues in $z$, and takes the **finite part** (constant term in the Laurent expansion) at $z = 0$. The **0-trace** is $^{0}\!\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_X}) := \,^{0}\!\!\int_X p(t, z, z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_g$; then $\zeta^0_X(s) := \Gamma(s)^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}(^{0}\!\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_X}) - P)\,dt$ (with $P$ the projection onto the finite-dimensional $L^2$-null-space), and $\det_0\Delta_X := e^{-(\zeta^0_X)'(0)}$. On a closed surface (no boundary, so $x \equiv 1$) this reduces to $\det_\zeta\Delta_X$.
> **In words:** on a cusped surface, the ordinary heat trace $\int_X p(t, z, z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}$ diverges — but **not** because the cusp has large volume (a model cusp $\{y > c\}/\langle z\mapsto z+1\rangle$ has finite volume $1/c$). The real reason is spectral: unlike on a compact surface, $\Delta_X$ has continuous spectrum $[1/4, \infty)$ realised by non-$L^2$ Eisenstein-series eigenfunctions, and their contribution to $p(t,z,z)$ makes $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ fail to be trace-class. The renormalisation multiplies the integrand by $x^z$, where $x$ is a coordinate that vanishes at the cusp — this makes the integral converge for large $z$, and analytic continuation to $z = 0$ picks out the "regular part" (throwing away the spectrally divergent contribution). What remains is the honest, finite spectral content — that goes into the ordinary zeta-regularisation recipe.
> **Concretely:** on the modular surface $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb Z)\backslash\mathbb H^2$ (one cusp), the naive heat trace at time $t$ is $+\infty$, but the 0-trace equals a finite number that depends on $t$; taking $t \to 0$ gives an *asymptotic* expansion whose constant term is $-\log(4\pi t)/2 + \text{const}$. On the trivial closed sphere (no cusps), $x \equiv 1$ and the entire construction reduces to the usual $\zeta_X'(0)$ definition. **Source:** Melrose, *The Atiyah–Patodi–Singer Index Theorem*; Borthwick, *Spectral Theory of Infinite-Area Hyperbolic Surfaces*, Ch. 5. See [[Def - Zeta-Regularised Determinant of the Laplacian]].

> [!recall]- Naud's geodesic heat-trace term $S_X(t)$
> **Formally:** on a closed hyperbolic surface, the geodesic part of the Selberg heat trace is $S_X(t) := \sum_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\sum_{m \ge 1}\frac{e^{-t/4}}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\frac{\ell_\gamma\,e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/4t}}{2\sinh(m\ell_\gamma/2)}$; exponentially small as $t \to 0^+$ (each term carries $e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/4t}$ with $m\ell_\gamma > 0$), and $S_X(t) \to 1$ as $t \to \infty$ (dominated by the $\lambda_0 = 0$ eigenvalue contribution). $S^p_X(t)$ is its primitive part (only $m = 1$).
> **In words:** the length-spectrum part of the heat trace: summing over all closed geodesics (primitive and their multiples), each contributes a Gaussian-in-length weight $e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/4t}$ times a geometric $\ell_\gamma/(2\sinh(m\ell_\gamma/2))$ factor. It is what remains of the heat trace after the "area" (identity-class) term is subtracted, and it is precisely the loop-mass integrand of §3 in disguise.
> **Concretely:** for large $t$, only very short loops matter; the $\gamma^m$ term with the smallest length ($\gamma$ the systole) dominates. For small $t$, each term $e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/4t}$ is tiny because $m\ell_\gamma \gg 0$, so $S_X(t)$ is exponentially small at $t = 0$ — reflecting the fact that short-time Brownian bridges cannot close around a hole.

> [!recall]- Universal Naud constant $E = \frac{1}{4\pi}\!\left(4\zeta_R'(-1) - \frac12 + \log 2\pi\right)$
> **Formally:** $E$ is the specific numerical constant coming from the *identity* (geometric-area) part of Naud's expansion of $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta$ on a hyperbolic surface; $\zeta_R$ the Riemann zeta function, $\zeta_R'(-1) \approx -0.165$ a well-known universal constant, giving $E \approx 0.0538$. The area-density term of $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta$ is exactly $-\operatorname{Area}(X)\,E$.
> **In words:** the "coefficient per unit hyperbolic area" of $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta$ — an universal spectral density constant, the same on every hyperbolic surface.
> **Concretely:** on a genus-$2$ compact hyperbolic surface, $\operatorname{Area}(X) = 4\pi$; the area-density piece of $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta$ is $-4\pi\cdot E \approx -0.68$. This is one of the two "universal" pieces (the other being the Selberg-zeta contribution $\log Z_X'(1)$) that together give the D'Hoker–Phong determinant formula (see [[Remark - D'Hoker-Phong Determinant Formula|Remark 5.2]]).

> [!cite]- External input — Selberg trace formula and Naud's determinant expansion
> **Statement (typed):** on a closed hyperbolic surface, the heat trace splits into an *identity* (geometric-area) term and a *geodesic* term,
> $$\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-t\lambda_j}=\operatorname{Area}(X)\frac{e^{-t/4}}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}\!\int_0^\infty\frac{r\,e^{-r^2/4t}}{\sinh(r/2)}\,dr+S_X(t),$$
> and (Naud) $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X=-\operatorname{Area}(X)\,E-\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}+\int_0^1\frac{S_X(t)}{t}\,dt+\int_1^\infty\frac{S_X(t)-1}{t}\,dt$, where $\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}\approx0.5772$ is Euler–Mascheroni and $S_X$ is the geodesic term above.
> **Why it's true:** the trace formula equates a spectral sum with a sum over the identity and hyperbolic conjugacy classes of $\Gamma$ (the geodesics); feeding it through the Mellin/$\zeta'(0)$ machine turns $-\log\det_\zeta$ into an integral of the geodesic term. **Source:** Selberg; Naud (via [WX25]). Take on faith; the geodesic term $S_X(t)$ is exactly the $t \downarrow 0$ heat-kernel weight of Brownian loops of each length, which is why it re-expresses as a loop mass. Not re-derived here — used as a black box in [[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1]] and its cusped analogue [[Thm - Determinant via Loop Measure, Finite-Area Case|Theorem 5.7]].

> [!cite]- External input — refined prime geodesic theorem (closed case)
> **Statement (typed):** for a closed hyperbolic surface ($\delta = 1$), $N_X(R) = \operatorname{Li}(e^{R}) + \sum_{0 < \lambda_j \le 1/4}\operatorname{Li}(e^{s_j R}) + O_X(e^{3R/4}/R)$ as $R \to \infty$, where $\operatorname{Li}(x) = \int_2^x du/\log u \sim x/\log x$ and $s_j = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 - \lambda_j}$. Consequently $|N_X(R) - \widetilde{\mathrm{Li}}(e^R)| = O_X(e^{(1 - \epsilon)R})$ for some $\epsilon > 0$.
> **Why it's true:** small eigenvalues $\lambda_j < 1/4$ contribute extra $\operatorname{Li}(e^{s_j R})$ terms beyond the main $\operatorname{Li}(e^R)$; the error is controlled by the zero-free region of $Z_X$. **Source:** Selberg/Hejhal; via [WX25]. Gives the bound that makes the case-(i) renormalising integral $\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{e^R - 1}\,d(N_X - \widetilde{\mathrm{Li}}(e^R))$ converge.

## Concept map

### §5.1 — The compact case

- **Naud's geodesic term $S_X(t)/t$ *is* the total loop-mass integrand ([[Remark - Naud's Geodesic Term as a Total Loop Mass]])** — Wang–Xue Lemma 3.4 with subordination variable $s = t$ says the strip integral of the $\mathbb H^2$ heat kernel across $\tau^m$ equals the $(\gamma, m)$-term of $S_X(t)/t$; multiplying by $e^{-\kappa t}$ and integrating over $t \in (0, \infty)$ recovers $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ term by term (Theorem 3.5 with $\phi = \lambda + \kappa$). For $\kappa > 0$, Fubini gives $\int_0^\infty e^{-\kappa t}S_X(t)/t\,dt = M_\kappa$, the total killed non-trivial loop mass. This is the load-bearing identification underneath all of §5: it is what turns Naud's spectral-side determinant expansion into a probabilistic total-loop-mass statement.

- **[[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1 — Determinant as a renormalised loop mass (compact case)]]** — On a closed hyperbolic surface, $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X$ (zero mode excluded) equals a renormalised total Brownian loop mass. Cleanest form: for killing rate $\phi = \lambda + \kappa$ with $\kappa \to 0^+$, $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X = \operatorname{Area}(X)\,E + \log Z_X'(1)$ — the classical D'Hoker–Phong formula. **Mechanism:** Naud writes $-\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X$ as $-\operatorname{Area}(X)\,E - \gamma_{\mathrm{EM}} + \int_0^1 S_X/t + \int_1^\infty (S_X - 1)/t$; the geodesic integrand $S_X(t)/t$ is exactly the loop-mass integrand of §3 (by the identification bullet above); substituting the [[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Selberg zeta identity]] $\sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = -\log Z_X(s)$ and taking $\kappa \to 0$, the $\log\kappa$ (introduced by the exponential integral $E_1(\kappa) = -\gamma_{\mathrm{EM}} - \log\kappa + O(\kappa)$) cancels exactly against $-\log(s - 1)$ from the Selberg zeta's simple zero at $s = 1$.
  - Four sub-cases: (i) Brownian $\phi = \lambda$ (renormalised geometrically via Wang–Xue's primitive/non-primitive split and the refined PGT); (ii) killed $\phi = \lambda + \kappa$ (finite-$\kappa$ identity and the $\kappa \to 0$ limit — the cleanest analytical case); (iii) $\alpha$-stable $\phi = \lambda^{\alpha/2}$ (scales case (i) by $\alpha/2$ since $\zeta_{\Delta^{\alpha/2}}(s) = \zeta_X(\alpha s/2)$). All four proofs are on the atomic subpage.

- **[[Remark - D'Hoker-Phong Determinant Formula|Remark 5.2 — The D'Hoker–Phong determinant formula]]** — The $\kappa \to 0$ endpoint $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_X = \operatorname{Area}(X)\,E + \log Z_X'(1)$ is precisely the classical D'Hoker–Phong / Sarnak identity $\det_\zeta\Delta_X = Z_X'(1)\,e^{(2g - 2)(2\zeta_R'(-1) - \frac14 + \frac12\log 2\pi)}$: what Belyaev–Huseynli contribute is the *loop-measure route* to it. ⚠️ **Flag preserved:** the factor-of-2 bookkeeping between the paper's constant $E$ and the D'Hoker–Phong exponent is not fully reconciled — the identity is stated as the paper gives it.

### §5.1.1 — Polyakov's conformal anomaly formula

- **[[Thm - Polyakov Conformal Anomaly Formula|Theorem 5.3 — Polyakov's conformal anomaly formula]] (external input)** — For conformally-equivalent smooth metrics $g_0$ and $g = e^{2\sigma}g_0$ on a closed surface, $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_g$ differs from $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_{g_0}$ by an explicit local integral in $\sigma$ and $K_0$: $-\frac{1}{12\pi}\int|\nabla_{g_0}\sigma|^2 - \frac{1}{6\pi}\int K_0\,\sigma + \log(\operatorname{vol}_g/\operatorname{vol}_{g_0})$. The zeta-determinant of the Laplacian is not conformally invariant on a surface; the failure is this "conformal anomaly", a purely local functional. Not proved here — stated with source (Polyakov; Osgood–Phillips–Sarnak).

- **[[Cor - Polyakov Formula via Brownian Loop Measure|Corollary 5.4 — Polyakov via loop measure]]** — For $g = e^{2\sigma}g_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ any smooth metric in the conformal class of the hyperbolic metric on a closed $X$, $\log\det_\zeta\Delta_g = P_X(\sigma) + \operatorname{Area}(X)\,E + \log Z_X'(1)$, where $P_X(\sigma) := -\frac{1}{12\pi}\int|\nabla\sigma|^2\,dA_{\mathrm{hyp}} + \frac{1}{6\pi}\int\sigma\,dA_{\mathrm{hyp}} + \log(\operatorname{vol}_g/(4\pi(g - 1)))$ is the Polyakov correction. **Mechanism:** substitute Polyakov (Theorem 5.3) with $g_0 = g_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ (so $K_0 \equiv -1$, contributing the sign flip in the second term) into [[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1]]'s $\kappa \to 0$ formula for the hyperbolic representative. The determinant for *any* metric in the conformal class is the loop-measure value on the hyperbolic representative plus the explicit local Polyakov correction — a clean split of spectral/geodesic information from conformal information.

### §5.2 — The finite-area (cusped) case

- **[[Remark - Compact Determinant Construction Breaks in the Cusped Case|Why the compact construction breaks in the cusped case, and Melrose's fix]]** — On a finite-area non-compact $X$, cusps supply Eisenstein series $E_j(z, s)$ (one per cusp) that populate $\sigma(\Delta_X)$ with continuous spectrum $[1/4, \infty)$ of multiplicity $n_C$. As a consequence, $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ is not trace class and $\int_X p_X(t, z, z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}(z) = +\infty$: no discrete eigenvalue list, no compact-case spectral zeta, no compact-case determinant. Melrose's fix compactifies $X$ to $\bar X$ (adding a boundary circle at each cusp), takes a boundary defining function $x$, and replaces $\int_X$ by the renormalised integral ${}^0\!\!\int_X f := \operatorname{FP}_{z = 0}\int_X x^z f\,d\!\operatorname{vol}$; this yields the finite **0-trace** ${}^0\!\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_X})$, its Mellin transform $\zeta^0_X$, and the **0-determinant** $\det_0\Delta_X := e^{-(\zeta^0_X)'(0)}$ that on closed surfaces (where $x \equiv 1$) reduces to the ordinary $\det_\zeta\Delta_X$ of §5.1.

- **[[Thm - Borthwick-Judge-Perry Determinant Formula|Theorem 5.5 — Borthwick–Judge–Perry determinant formula]] (external input)** — For a geometrically finite hyperbolic surface with $n_C$ cusps and Euler characteristic $\chi$, $\det_0(\Delta_X - s(1 - s))$ factors *explicitly* as $Z_X(s)\,e^{M + F s(1 - s)}\,G_\infty(s)^\chi\,(\text{explicit cusp factor})^{-n_C}$ with $M = \chi(\frac12\log 2\pi - 2\zeta_R'(-1) + \frac14)$, $F = -\chi$, $G_\infty(s) = (2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)G(s)^2$ (Barnes $G$). **Consequence:** $\det_0\Delta_X = C_X\,Z_X'(1)$ (finite area, with $C_X = e^M(2\pi)^{-\chi}(\sqrt 2\,\pi)^{-n_C}$) or $C_X\,Z_X(1)$ (infinite area). Not proved here — stated with source (Borthwick–Judge–Perry 2005; Borthwick 2007).

- **Remark 5.6 — Why the Selberg zeta derivative $Z_X'(1)$ appears ([[Remark - Why the Selberg Zeta Derivative Appears in Finite Area]])** — The finite-area answer involves $Z_X'(1)$ rather than $Z_X(1)$ precisely because $\lambda_0 = 0$ is an $L^2$-eigenvalue when $\operatorname{Area}(X) < \infty$: constants are integrable, so $0$ is a genuine eigenvalue; this forces $Z_X$ a simple zero at $s = 1$, and that zero is divided out in the definition of $\det_0\Delta_X$, leaving the derivative. Contrast with the infinite-area case (Remark 5.8).

- **[[Thm - Determinant via Loop Measure, Finite-Area Case|Theorem 5.7 — Determinant via loop measure (finite-area case)]]** — For finite-area cusped $X$, $\kappa \ge 0$, $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa} > 1$: $-\log\det_0(\Delta_X + \kappa) = F\kappa - M + \sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) - D_X(s)$, and dividing out the simple zero at $s = 1$ gives $\log\det_0\Delta_X = \log C_X + \log Z_X'(1)$. **Mechanism:** substitute the [[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Selberg zeta identity]] $-\log Z_X(s) = \sum_{\gamma,m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ into the [[Thm - Borthwick-Judge-Perry Determinant Formula|Borthwick–Judge–Perry formula]], then take $\kappa \to 0^+$; the $\log\kappa$/$\log(s - 1)$ cancellation is the same as in [[Thm - Determinant as Renormalised Loop Mass|Theorem 5.1(ii)]].

- **[[Remark - The Infinite-Area Determinant Case|Remark 5.8 — The infinite-area determinant case]]** — When $\operatorname{Area}(X) = \infty$, $\delta < 1$ and (by §4) the total loop mass is *already* finite without killing; $0$ is not an $L^2$-eigenvalue so $Z_X(1) \ne 0$ and no derivative/renormalisation is needed — the [[Thm - Borthwick-Judge-Perry Determinant Formula|Borthwick–Judge–Perry]] identity $\det_0\Delta_X = C_X\,Z_X(1)$ holds directly at $s = 1$. Lemonde–Wang [LW26] treat this case. This is the redundancy of the renormalisation apparatus in the infinite-area regime; the paper's §5 concentrates on the finite-area case where the renormalisation is genuinely needed.

Continue to [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §6 A Probability Measure on Classes|§6]], where these finite normalisations become probability measures.
