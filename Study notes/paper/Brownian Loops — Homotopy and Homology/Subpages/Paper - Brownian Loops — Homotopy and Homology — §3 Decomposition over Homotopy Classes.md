# §3 — Decomposition over homotopy classes

Back to the [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology|hub]]. In §2 the total Brownian loop mass on a hyperbolic surface $X$ came out *infinite*, and the divergence traced to a small-$t$ (short-loop) effect concentrated on the contractible class. This section is the paper's turning point: it **sorts loops by topological type** and shows that each non-trivial non-peripheral type carries *finite* mass, with an explicit closed form. The mechanism is geometric — a loop's topological type is recorded by which element of $\Gamma$ carries the starting lift to the ending lift when the loop is lifted upstairs to $\mathbb{H}^2$ — the loop's monodromy element, defined precisely in the recall below; winding around a hole forces the loop to be at least as long as the corresponding closed geodesic, and that length lower bound is exactly what cuts off the small-$t$ divergence. The headline is [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]] (the mass of a free homotopy class is a single integral over a fundamental strip); §3.1 evaluates it in closed form for every process the paper uses; §3.2–3.4 draw out the physics (loop-soup + partition functions) and the geometry (length-spectrum recovery and metric rigidity).

## Prerequisites recap

A reader landing on this section cold — without having read §2 — will find below every above-floor concept the section uses, either as a three-field `> [!recall]-` callout or as a wikilinked one-line "From earlier sections" reminder. The invariants of §2 (transition kernels, Dirichlet forms, subordination, weighted potential measures, loop measures) that this section builds on are recalled first as compact three-field callouts; the eight geometric prerequisites (hyperbolic plane, Fuchsian group, universal cover, monodromy, free homotopy ↔ conjugacy, peripheral class, primitive hyperbolic, fundamental strip) are recalled at the level of §3.0 baseline.

### From earlier sections (paper items)

- **Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ ([[Def - Dirichlet Form and its Operator and Semigroup]])** (§2) — a bilinear energy functional on functions in $L^2(X, \rho_X)$ that generates a symmetric Markov semigroup $P^E_t$ with integral kernel $p^E$. Its $\Gamma$-invariance is the key hypothesis of the section: $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, hz, hw) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, w)$ for every $h\in\Gamma$.
- **Bernstein function $\phi$ and its subordinator ([[Def - Bernstein Function, Subordinator, and Subordination]])** (§2) — $\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ concave, non-decreasing, with completely-monotone derivative; its subordinator is a non-decreasing càdlàg process with $\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda S_t}] = e^{-t\phi(\lambda)}$. Concrete cases used in §3.1: $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda$ (Brownian), $\lambda + \kappa$ (killing), $\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ ($\alpha$-stable), $(\lambda + \kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ (shifted stable).
- **Subordinate Brownian loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$ ([[Def - Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure]])** (§2) — the loop measure of $Y = B\circ S$ where $B$ is Brownian motion on $\mathbb{H}^2$ and $S$ a $\phi$-subordinator; kernel $p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, w) = \int p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(s, z, w)\,\psi^\phi_t(ds)$. In §3, all class-masses are computed relative to this measure (or the Dirichlet-form generalisation).
- **Weighted potential measure $V_\phi$ ([[Def - Weighted Potential Measure]])** (Def 2.9) — the $dt/t$-average of the subordinator laws: $V_\phi(ds) = \int_0^\infty\psi^\phi_t(ds)\,dt/t$. Packages $\phi$'s dependence in a single measure on $(0, \infty)$.
- **[[Lemma - Collapsing the Time Integral of the Subordinate Kernel|Lemma 2.11 (time-integral collapse)]]** — $\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int h(s)\psi^\phi_t(ds) = \int h(s)V_\phi(ds)$: the two-parameter integral against $(t, s)$ with weight $dt/t$ reduces to a single integral against $V_\phi$.
- **Dirichlet-form loop measure $\mu^E_X$ ([[Def - Dirichlet Form Loop Measure]])** (§2) — the general loop measure of a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $X$; specialised to $\mu^\phi_X$ in the subordinate case.
- **Brownian loop measure $\mu_X$ ([[Def - Brownian Loop Measure]])** (Le Jan) — the loop measure of ordinary Brownian motion, the case $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda$ of the subordinate framework; the archetype whose class-masses give the Wang–Xue $1/(m(e^L - 1))$ formula.
- **Bridge measure $\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to w}$ ([[Def - Disintegration and the Bridge Measure]])** (§2) — the conditional law of the process from $z$ to $w$ over $[0, t]$, with total mass $p^E(t, z, w)$; the loop measure at a base point is the total-mass of $\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to z}$ integrated against $dt/t$.

### Above-floor prerequisites — the §3.0 lifting picture

Before we can state the paper's decomposition, we need eight pieces of the covering-space / hyperbolic-geometry setup. Each is given a three-field recall — formal statement, plain-language paraphrase, and a concrete example the reader can hold. **A floor reader can read the recalls below and end with a picture of the setup, without opening any other file.**

> [!recall]- The hyperbolic plane $\mathbb{H}^2$
> **Formally:** $\mathbb{H}^2 := \{z = x+iy \in \mathbb{C} : y>0\}$ is the *upper half-plane* with the Riemannian metric $ds^2 = (dx^2+dy^2)/y^2$; distances shrink as $y$ grows and blow up as $y \to 0$. Its isometry group is $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$, acting by Möbius transformations $z \mapsto (az+b)/(cz+d)$, $ad-bc=1$.
> **In words:** the same set of complex numbers you know (the upper half-plane), but with a rescaled ruler that makes it a model of a curved, negatively-curved "surface" — the negative-curvature analogue of a sphere. Geodesics (shortest paths) are vertical straight lines and half-circles perpendicular to the real axis.
> **Concretely:** compute the distance from $z = i$ to $z = 2i$ along the vertical geodesic $x = 0$: it equals $\int_1^2 dy/y = \log 2$. Points near the real axis are effectively "at infinity" — a Euclidean-tiny circle drawn at $y = 0.001$ actually has huge hyperbolic circumference. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic Plane]].

> [!recall]- Fuchsian group $\Gamma$ and the quotient surface $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$
> **Formally:** a *Fuchsian group* $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ is a discrete subgroup of hyperbolic isometries. Assume $\Gamma$ is *torsion-free* (no non-identity element $h$ has $h^n = \text{id}$). Its action on $\mathbb{H}^2$ has no fixed points, and the *quotient* $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ — points identified if a $\Gamma$-element moves one to the other — inherits a smooth hyperbolic metric. $X$ is a *hyperbolic surface*; the paper takes it *geometrically finite* (a technical condition that bounds its complexity).
> **In words:** take a huge symmetric wallpaper on $\mathbb{H}^2$; the group $\Gamma$ is the wallpaper's symmetry group; glue together any two points that a wallpaper symmetry maps to each other; the result is a surface $X$ with the same local geometry as $\mathbb{H}^2$ but a nontrivial *global* shape (holes, handles, cusps — puncture-shaped ends of infinite length but finite area).
> **Concretely:** the standard example: take $\Gamma$ generated by two translations, $z \mapsto z+1$ and $z \mapsto z+i$, acting on $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$ (this is a *Euclidean* analogue, not hyperbolic, but the picture is identical). The quotient is the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ — one unit square with opposite edges glued. In the hyperbolic case, a genus-2 surface (a two-holed pretzel) arises as $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ for a specific 4-generator Fuchsian $\Gamma$. Full detail: [[Def - Fuchsian Group and the Hyperbolic Quotient Surface]].

> [!recall]- Universal cover $\pi:\mathbb{H}^2 \to X$ and its deck moves
> **Formally:** the *covering projection* $\pi:\mathbb{H}^2 \to X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ sends each point in the upper half-plane to its equivalence class under $\Gamma$. It is a local isometry: every point $x \in X$ has a neighbourhood $U$ such that $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is a disjoint union of copies of $U$, each mapped isometrically by $\pi$. The *fibre* $\pi^{-1}(x)$ over a point $x$ is exactly the $\Gamma$-orbit $\{h\tilde x : h \in \Gamma\}$ of any lift $\tilde x$. The *deck transformation group* — isometries $h$ of $\mathbb{H}^2$ with $\pi \circ h = \pi$ — is exactly $\Gamma$ itself, so also $\pi_1(X, x) \cong \Gamma$ (the fundamental group of $X$).
> **In words:** $\mathbb{H}^2$ is the "infinite unrolled" version of $X$: standing on $\mathbb{H}^2$, you can walk in any direction forever; standing on $X$, you eventually come back to where you started. The map $\pi$ collapses the infinite unrolled version down to the finite surface. Every point on $X$ has infinitely many lifts on $\mathbb{H}^2$ — one for each element of $\Gamma$ — and the deck moves $\Gamma$ are the rigid motions of $\mathbb{H}^2$ that permute those lifts among themselves.
> **Concretely:** for the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, the universal cover is $\pi:\mathbb{R}^2 \to T^2$ sending $(x, y) \mapsto (x \bmod 1, y \bmod 1)$. The deck group is $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^2$ acting by integer translations. The lift of the single torus point $(0.3, 0.7)$ is the infinite lattice $\{(0.3+m, 0.7+n) : (m, n) \in \mathbb{Z}^2\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, and the deck moves shuffle those lattice points among themselves. Full detail: [[Def - Universal Cover]].

> [!recall]- Path lifting and the monodromy $h_\omega$ of a loop
> **Formally:** the *path-lifting lemma*: given a continuous path $\omega:[0, t] \to X$ and a chosen lift $\tilde x \in \pi^{-1}(\omega(0))$ of its starting point, there is a *unique* continuous path $\tilde\omega:[0, t] \to \mathbb{H}^2$ with $\pi \circ \tilde\omega = \omega$ and $\tilde\omega(0) = \tilde x$. When $\omega$ is a *loop* (i.e. $\omega(t) = \omega(0)$), the endpoint $\tilde\omega(t)$ still lies in the same fibre $\pi^{-1}(\omega(0))$, so there is a *unique* deck transformation $h_\omega \in \Gamma$ with $\tilde\omega(t) = h_\omega \tilde x$. This $h_\omega$ is the **monodromy** of the loop.
> **In words:** lift a downstairs loop to $\mathbb{H}^2$ starting at a chosen lift; the loop unrolls to an arc; its endpoint is a *different* lift of the starting point (unless the loop is contractible, in which case the endpoint is the same lift). The deck move carrying the starting lift to the ending lift is a single group element $h_\omega$, and it records which topological "type" of loop you traversed.
> **Concretely:** on the torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, a loop that goes once around horizontally starting from the origin lifts to an arc from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 0)$; its monodromy is $(1, 0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$. A loop that goes twice around vertically has monodromy $(0, 2)$. A contractible loop (e.g. a small circle around the point $(0.5, 0.5)$) lifts to a small closed arc; its monodromy is $(0, 0)$.

> [!recall]- Free homotopy classes on $X$ ↔ conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$
> **Formally:** two oriented closed loops on $X$ are *freely homotopic* if one deforms continuously into the other in $X$ **with the basepoint allowed to move**. The set of free homotopy classes is in bijection with the set of conjugacy classes $[h]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \{qhq^{-1} : q \in \Gamma\}$ of $\Gamma$. The trivial class (contractible loops) corresponds to the singleton conjugacy class $\{\mathrm{id}\}$.
> **In words:** every loop on the surface "goes around some holes in some pattern"; two loops that go around the same holes in the same pattern are in one free homotopy class. On the algebraic side, forgetting the basepoint changes the recorded monodromy by conjugation, so a class of loops corresponds to a class of group elements (a conjugacy class).
> **Concretely:** on the torus $T^2$, $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian, so every conjugacy class $\{qhq^{-1}\}$ is a singleton $\{h\}$. Free homotopy classes are therefore in bijection with $\mathbb{Z}^2$ itself: the class of $(a, b)$ consists of every loop that goes $a$ times around horizontally and $b$ times around vertically. On a hyperbolic surface, $\Gamma$ is *not* abelian, so a conjugacy class is a genuine equivalence class of many group elements — but the picture is the same: one class per topological type of loop.

> [!recall]- Peripheral class (loops around a cusp)
> **Formally:** a *cusp* of the finite-area hyperbolic surface $X$ is a puncture whose neighbourhood is isometric to $\{z : \mathrm{Im}\,z > c\}/\langle z \mapsto z+1\rangle$ for large $c$ — a "funnel" shrinking to a puncture. A free homotopy class is *peripheral* if its loops can be pushed entirely into a cusp neighbourhood (equivalently: its conjugacy class in $\Gamma$ is generated by a *parabolic* element, one fixing a single point on the boundary circle $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$).
> **In words:** a peripheral loop is one that just wraps around a puncture. You can push it arbitrarily close to the puncture and its length shrinks to zero, so it has no shortest representative — no closed geodesic.
> **Concretely:** on the once-punctured torus (a torus with one point removed), any loop that encircles the missing point *once* is peripheral: as you push it toward the puncture, its length goes to $0$. Any loop that instead runs "around the handle" is *not* peripheral: it cannot be shrunk past a fixed positive length, and it has a closed-geodesic representative.

> [!recall]- Primitive hyperbolic element $\tau \in \Gamma$ and the closed geodesic $\gamma$
> **Formally:** an element $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ is *hyperbolic* if it is conjugate in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ to the standard form $\tau_0: z \mapsto e^\ell z$ for some $\ell > 0$ (equivalently, $|\mathrm{tr}\,\tau| > 2$). The number $\ell > 0$ is the **translation length**; it is the hyperbolic distance $\tau$ moves points along its *axis* (the geodesic invariant under $\tau$; for $\tau_0$ the axis is the imaginary half-line $\{iy: y>0\}$). An element $\tau \in \Gamma$ is **primitive** if it cannot be written as $\sigma^k$ for any $\sigma \in \Gamma$ and $k \ge 2$. **Fact:** every non-trivial, non-peripheral free homotopy class on $X$ contains a unique closed geodesic $\gamma$, whose length equals the translation length $\ell_\gamma$ of the primitive hyperbolic $\tau$ representing that class; the class of the $m$-th power $\tau^m$ contains the geodesic $\gamma$ *traced $m$ times*.
> **In words:** among the isometries of $\mathbb{H}^2$, the hyperbolic ones behave like "translations along a specific line" — they pick a geodesic axis and slide everything along it by a fixed distance $\ell$. In $\Gamma$, a *primitive* such element is one not obtained by iterating a smaller element. Every interesting (non-trivial, non-peripheral) loop-class on $X$ is represented by a primitive $\tau$, and the taut version of that loop — its shortest curve — is a closed geodesic of length exactly $\ell$. Winding around it $m$ times gives the class of $\tau^m$.
> **Concretely:** with $\tau_0: z \mapsto e^\ell z$, the axis is the imaginary axis $\{iy : y>0\}$; the distance from $i$ to $\tau_0(i) = e^\ell i$ along it is $\int_1^{e^\ell} dy/y = \ell$ — so $\tau_0$ translates points along the axis by exactly $\ell$. In the quotient surface $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ with $\Gamma = \langle\tau_0\rangle$, the axis projects to a *closed loop* (going once from $y=1$ up to $y=e^\ell$ takes you back to your start), which is the closed geodesic $\gamma$ of length $\ell$. Iterating $\tau_0$ gives $\tau_0^m: z \mapsto e^{m\ell}z$; its axis is the same imaginary half-line, and its "closed loop on $X$" is $\gamma$ traversed $m$ times. Full detail: [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- Fundamental strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{z \in \mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$
> **Formally:** for a hyperbolic $\tau: z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$, the *cyclic subgroup* $\langle\tau\rangle = \{\tau^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ acts on $\mathbb{H}^2$ with orbits $\{e^{k\ell_\gamma}z : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. A *fundamental region* for $\langle\tau\rangle$ is a subset of $\mathbb{H}^2$ containing exactly one representative from each orbit. The set $\mathcal{F}_\tau := \{z \in \mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ is such a fundamental region — the *fundamental strip*.
> **In words:** $\tau$ scales the imaginary part by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$; so within any orbit $\{e^{k\ell_\gamma}z : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, exactly one point has its imaginary part in the half-open range $[1, e^{\ell_\gamma})$. The strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is *one period* of that scaling: quotienting $\mathbb{H}^2$ by $\langle\tau\rangle$ is the same as taking the strip and gluing its top edge $\mathrm{Im}\,z = e^{\ell_\gamma}$ to its bottom edge $\mathrm{Im}\,z = 1$ by the map $z \mapsto z/e^{\ell_\gamma}$. The result is a cylinder — the geometric picture of $\langle\tau\rangle\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$.
> **Concretely:** for $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$ (so $\tau: z \mapsto 2z$), the strip is $\{z : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < 2\}$. The point $z = 1+i$ has orbit $\{1+i, 2(1+i), 4(1+i), \ldots, (1+i)/2, (1+i)/4, \ldots\}$; only $1+i$ itself lies in the strip. The point $z = 3+3i$ has $\mathrm{Im}\,z = 3 \ge 2$, so it lies outside the strip — its representative in the strip is $\tau^{-1}(3+3i) = (3+3i)/2 = 1.5 + 1.5i$, which has imaginary part $1.5 \in [1, 2)$.

## Concept map

### §3.0 — The lifting picture (setup)

- **[[Remark - Standard Form as Coordinates on the Axis|Standard form as coordinates on the axis]]** — Every primitive hyperbolic $\tau \in \Gamma$ with translation length $\ell_\gamma$ is $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$-conjugate to $\tau_0 : z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma} z$, whose axis is the imaginary half-line. In this coordinate, $\tau_0$ acts on imaginary parts by rescaling $\mathrm{Im}(\tau_0 z) = e^{\ell_\gamma}\mathrm{Im}(z)$, turning every $\langle\tau_0\rangle$-orbit into a geometric sequence in $\mathrm{Im}(z)$ and every fundamental region into a horizontal band — the strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau$. The choice of coordinate is invisible in the strip-integral formula (both the region and the integrand transform compatibly under $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$-conjugation), so the paper computes in standard form throughout §3 and §7 without loss.
- **[[Remark - Collapsing the Conjugacy Sum via the Centraliser|Collapsing the conjugacy sum via the centraliser]]** — For a torsion-free Fuchsian $\Gamma$ and a primitive hyperbolic $\tau \in \Gamma$, the centraliser of every power $\tau^m$ is $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ (anything commuting with $\tau^m$ must preserve $\tau$'s axis; discreteness forces the axis-stabiliser to be cyclic; primitivity of $\tau$ names the generator). The orbit–stabiliser bijection then enumerates the conjugacy class one-to-one by cosets: $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \bigsqcup_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\{r\tau^m r^{-1}\}$ (paper's identity (10)). This is what makes Thm 3.2's coset unfolding count each contribution exactly once — without $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ the double structure "sum over class × integrate over $X$" would over-count.
- **[[Remark - Descent of the Heat Kernel by Periodisation|Descent of the heat kernel by periodisation]]** — The downstairs kernel on $X$ is the sum of the upstairs kernel over the deck group: $p^E_X(t, z, w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, \tilde z, h\tilde w)$ (paper's identity (11)). $\Gamma$-invariance of $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ makes the sum independent of the choice of lifts; geometric finiteness of $\Gamma$ makes it converge (orbit growth beaten by kernel decay). The resulting $p^E_X$ is the heat kernel of the descended Dirichlet form, and the sum is *pre-sorted by homotopy class*: restricting to $h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ picks out precisely the loops of free homotopy type $C_X(\gamma^m)$. This periodisation is the mechanism the whole homotopy decomposition of §3 rests on.
- **[[Remark - Homotopy Classes for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1 — Homotopy classes for jump processes]]** — An $\alpha$-stable or general subordinate loop $Y = B\circ S$ is a càdlàg path with deleted Brownian arcs, so it has no honest universal-cover lift and no intrinsic free homotopy class. The paper *defines* the class-mass for jump processes by pulling the periodised formula (isolate $h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ in the group-sum kernel) through to the jump kernel $p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^2}$: the class of $Y$ is set to be the class of the underlying Brownian arc $B$. This is a definition, not a theorem — a fact worth flagging, because everything downstream reads the same in the jump case as in the Brownian case, but its interpretation is definitional.

### §3.1 — The mass of a free homotopy class

- **[[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2 — Mass of a free homotopy class]]** — For a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $\mathbb{H}^2$ with kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}$ and a primitive geodesic $\gamma$ with hyperbolic representative $\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$, the loop-measure mass of the class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is $\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, \tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z)$. **Mechanism:** isolate the conjugacy class in the group-sum kernel, lift the base integral to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$, unfold the conjugacy class over cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ (each coset contributes one integrand $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ over one $\Gamma$-translate of the fundamental region), reassemble the translates onto the strip. The whole group-sum collapses into a single term on a single strip — the identity that makes each topological type's mass finite and computable.
  - Uses $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel *twice* (well-definedness downstairs + conjugation to standard form), one isometry substitution (Jacobian 1), and $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance of the integrand $w\mapsto p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, w, \tau^m w)$. The centraliser identification $Z_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ makes cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ enumerate the conjugacy class one-to-one.

- **[[Remark - Relation to the Path Integral|Remark 3.3 — Relation to the path integral]]** — The loop measure's decomposition over free homotopy classes is a Wick-rotated, character-trivial, unrooted version of the sum-over-homotopy-classes for a quantum-mechanical propagator on a multiply-connected space $X$: $K(t; x, y) = \sum_{[\alpha]\in\pi_1(X,x)}\chi(\alpha)K_\alpha(t; x, y)$ with $\chi:\pi_1(X, x)\to U(1)$ a unitary character; class-masses $\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ are the analogues of the partial amplitudes $K_\alpha$, with $\chi\equiv 1$ and conjugacy classes replacing based classes. Twisting by a non-trivial character reproduces the character-weighted decomposition — this is what [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §6 A Probability Measure on Classes|§6]] does with homology characters.
  - **Contains the paper's full §3.2 QM digression** — the Wick-rotation dictionary, the Feynman–Kac formula (external input), and the identity $Z^\kappa_X\propto \exp(\frac12|\mu^\kappa_X|_{\mathrm{reg}})$ linking the killed loop measure to the free-scalar-field partition function; the divergent total mass $|\mu^\kappa_X|_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is what [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §5 Renormalising the Total Mass|§5]] regularises into the zeta-determinant.

- **[[Lemma - Wang-Xue Strip Integral|Lemma 3.4 — Wang–Xue strip integral]]** — External input: for the Brownian heat kernel on $\mathbb{H}^2$ and $\tau^m : z\mapsto e^Lz$, $\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau}p_{\mathbb{H}^2}(s, z, \tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)}\cdot\frac{e^{-s/4}e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}$. **Mechanism (Wang–Xue [WX25]):** Fermi coordinates on the strip decompose the integral into an axis contribution (1-D Gaussian in $L$) and a cross-axis contribution (geometric width $\ell_\gamma/[2\sinh(L/2)]$ from the sinh Jacobian); the $e^{-s/4}$ is the spectral-bottom correction of the $\mathbb{H}^2$ heat kernel ($1/4 = (\frac12)^2$).

- **[[Thm - Mass of a Subordinate Brownian Loop Class|Theorem 3.5 — Mass of a subordinate loop class]]** — For a Bernstein $\phi$, $\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X$, and $L = m\ell_\gamma$: $\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)}I_\phi(L)$, where $I_\phi(L)$ is the loop-length integral. **Mechanism:** Theorem 3.2 with $p^E := p^\phi$ + subordination formula + Wang–Xue's Lemma 3.4 to evaluate the spatial strip integral + Lemma 2.11 to collapse the $t$-integral into $V_\phi$. Consequently every subordinate class-mass reduces to a single 1-D integral in the subordination variable.
  - **Computed cases (§3.1.1–3.1.4):** the atomic subpage carries the four closed forms as worked-example collapsibles: **Brownian** $\phi = \lambda$ gives $\mu_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = 1/(m(e^L - 1))$; **killing** $\phi = \lambda + \kappa$ gives $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(\frac12 - \sqrt{1/4+\kappa})L}}{e^L - 1}$; **$\alpha$-stable** $\phi = \lambda^{\alpha/2}$ gives $\mu^\alpha_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = (\alpha/2)\mu_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ (scale-invariance forces this collapse); **shifted $\alpha$-stable** $\phi = (\lambda + \kappa)^{\alpha/2}$ gives $(\alpha/2)\cdot\frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(\frac12 - \sqrt{1/4+\kappa})L}}{e^L - 1}$ (killing restores the geometry-sensitive exponent). All four follow from the Gaussian-type identity $\int_0^\infty s^{-3/2}e^{-as - b/s}ds = \sqrt{\pi/b}\,e^{-2\sqrt{ab}}$.

- **Definition 3.6 — The loop-length integral $I_\phi(L)$ ([[Def - The Loop-Length Integral]])** — $I_\phi(L) := \int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{e^{-s/4}e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}V_\phi(ds)$, a purely 1-D integral in the subordination variable $s$ that packages all of $\phi$'s dependence in the class-mass; the geometry enters only through $L = m\ell_\gamma$. Reduces to $e^{-L/2}/L$ (Brownian), $e^{-L\sqrt{1/4+\kappa}}/L$ (killed), etc. Studied in [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §4 Zeta Functions and Total Mass|§4]] to sum the class-masses into a Selberg zeta function.

- **Remark 3.7 — The range $\kappa\ge -\frac14$ ([[Remark - The Range of the Killing Parameter]])** — Although $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda + \kappa$ is Bernstein only for $\kappa\ge 0$, the class-mass formula extends analytically to $\kappa \ge -\frac14$. Introducing the **spectral parameter** $\mathfrak{s} = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$ (equivalently, $\kappa = \mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{s} - 1)$), the condition $\kappa\ge -\frac14$ is exactly what keeps $\mathfrak{s}$ real, with the boundary $\kappa = -\frac14$ giving $\mathfrak{s} = \frac12$ — precisely the bottom of the $L^2$-spectrum of $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^2}$. This dictionary $\kappa\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{s}$ is the bridge to the Selberg zeta variable in §4.

### §3.3 — The loop soup and its Poissonian structure

- **[[Prop - Poissonian Structure of Homotopy Classes|Proposition 3.8 — Poissonian structure of homotopy classes]]** — The loop soup $\mathcal{L}_c$ at intensity $c$ is the Poisson point process on loop space with intensity measure $c\mu^\phi_X$. The number of soup loops in the class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is $\mathrm{Poisson}(c\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)))$; counts over pairwise-distinct classes are jointly independent. **Mechanism:** each free homotopy class is a Borel measurable set of loops, and distinct classes are disjoint (equivalence-class partition), so the claim is the Poisson-count-and-independent-scattering axioms of the PPP applied to those disjoint sets. Upgrades every class-mass from an *expectation* to a full *distribution*.

### §3.4 — Length-spectrum identities and metric rigidity

- **[[Thm - Wang-Xue Length Spectrum Identity|Theorem 3.9 — Wang–Xue length-spectrum identity]]** — External input: for a complete hyperbolic surface $X$ and non-empty closed discrete polar $P\subset X$, with $X' = X\setminus P$ carrying its unique complete hyperbolic metric, the Brownian mass of $C_X(\gamma^m)$ on $X$ equals the sum of the Brownian masses of the $X'$-classes that fuse into $C_X(\gamma^m)$: $\frac{1}{m(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1)} = \sum_{\gamma'^{m'}\simeq_X\gamma^m}\frac{1}{m'(e^{m'\ell_{\gamma'}} - 1)}$. **Mechanism (Wang–Xue [WX25]):** polar-set removal leaves the Brownian process unchanged; conformal invariance of the Brownian loop measure lets the metric $g|_{X\setminus P}$ be swapped for $X'$'s hyperbolic metric $g'$; $\sigma$-additivity over the finer $X'$-partition sums to $C_X(\gamma^m)$. **Not used structurally by the paper** — conformal invariance fails for subordinate/killed processes because $\phi$ doesn't commute with the conformal rescaling of $\Delta$; the paper's own §3.4 arguments use only the weaker polar-set-only identity.

- **[[Def - Marked Length Spectrum|Definition 3.10 — Marked length spectrum]]** — $\mathrm{MLS}(C_X(\gamma^m)) := \inf_{\eta\in C_X(\gamma^m)}\ell_g(\eta) = m\ell_\gamma$ on a hyperbolic surface. The **marking** — the class label $C_X(\gamma^m)$ — is essential: the unmarked length spectrum fails to determine the metric (Vignéras's isospectral-non-isometric examples), but the marked spectrum does (Otal–Croke rigidity on closed negatively-curved surfaces).

- **[[Prop - Loop Masses Determine the Length Spectrum|Proposition 3.11 — Loop masses determine the length spectrum]]** — For every $\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_X$, $\ell_\gamma = \log(1 + 1/\mu_X(C_X(\gamma)))$ (algebraic inversion of the Brownian closed form). For the killed measure with $\kappa\ge -\frac14$, $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma))$ is strictly decreasing in $\ell_\gamma$ (log-derivative $\le -\frac12$), so injective. Either way, the loop-masses determine the marked length spectrum $\mathrm{MLS}$.

- **[[Cor - Loop Masses Determine the Hyperbolic Surface|Corollary 3.12 — Loop masses determine the surface]]** — On a closed hyperbolic surface (genus $g\ge 2$), if two hyperbolic metrics $g_1, g_2$ give equal killed loop masses on every free homotopy class (fixed $\kappa\ge -\frac14$), then $(X, g_1)\cong(X, g_2)$ by an isometry isotopic to the identity — the same point of Teichmüller space. **Mechanism:** loop masses ⇒ MLS (Prop 3.11) + MLS ⇒ metric (Otal–Croke rigidity on closed negatively-curved surfaces). The killed Brownian loop measure is a *complete* geometric invariant.
