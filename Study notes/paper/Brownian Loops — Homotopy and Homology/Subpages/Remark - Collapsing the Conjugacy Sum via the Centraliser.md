# Notation

- $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ — a torsion-free (geometrically finite) Fuchsian group.
- $\tau \in \Gamma$ — a primitive hyperbolic element (not a proper power of any element of $\Gamma$), representing a primitive closed geodesic $\gamma$ of length $\ell_\gamma > 0$; standard form $\tau : z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma} z$, axis the imaginary half-line.
- $\tau^m$ — the $m$-th power of $\tau$, for $m \ge 1$; also hyperbolic, translation length $m\ell_\gamma$, same axis as $\tau$.
- **$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$** — the *conjugacy class* of $\tau^m$ in $\Gamma$: $\{q \tau^m q^{-1} : q \in \Gamma\} \subset \Gamma$.
- **$C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$** — the *centraliser* of $\tau^m$ in $\Gamma$: $\{h \in \Gamma : h\tau^m = \tau^m h\}$.
- **$\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$** — the set of left cosets $r\langle\tau\rangle$ of the cyclic subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle = \{\tau^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ in $\Gamma$; a set of coset representatives is any transversal.
- $C_X(\gamma^m)$ — the free homotopy class on $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ corresponding to $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ (loops winding $m$ times around $\gamma$).

> [!recall]- Conjugacy class and centraliser (basic group theory)
> **Formally:** for a group $G$ and $g \in G$, the *conjugacy class* is $[g]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \{qgq^{-1} : q \in G\}$; the *centraliser* is $C_G(g) = \{h \in G : hg = gh\}$. The orbit–stabiliser theorem for the conjugation action gives a bijection $G / C_G(g) \leftrightarrow [g]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ via $q C_G(g) \mapsto q g q^{-1}$; in particular the class size $|[g]_{\mathrm{conj}}| = [G : C_G(g)]$.
> **In words:** conjugating $g$ by $q$ and by $q'$ gives the same element iff $q^{-1}q' \in C_G(g)$; so the conjugates of $g$ are indexed one-to-one by *cosets* of the centraliser, not by all of $G$. The centraliser is the source of double-counting in the "sum over $q \in \Gamma$ of $q g q^{-1}$".
> **Concretely:** in the symmetric group $S_3$, the transposition $(12)$ has centraliser $\{e, (12)\}$ of order $2$, so $[G : C_G] = 6/2 = 3$; the three conjugates of $(12)$ are exactly the three transpositions.

> [!recall]- Primitive hyperbolic and axis-stabiliser structure in $\Gamma$
> **Formally:** an element $\tau \in \Gamma$ is *primitive* if $\tau \ne \sigma^k$ for any $\sigma \in \Gamma$ and $k \ge 2$. For $\tau$ hyperbolic with axis $A_\tau$, the *axis-stabiliser* $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A_\tau) := \{h \in \Gamma : h A_\tau = A_\tau\}$ (as a set) is an infinite cyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$: because $\Gamma$ is discrete in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$, the subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$-elements preserving $A_\tau$ is $\mathbb{R} \times \{\pm 1\}$ (translations along the axis + a reflection), and its intersection with a torsion-free discrete $\Gamma$ is a discrete subgroup of the translation part $\mathbb{R}$ — hence cyclic $\cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the *shortest* translation, which is exactly the primitive $\tau$.
> **In words:** in a torsion-free Fuchsian group, the only elements that can commute with a hyperbolic $\tau$ (or with any of its powers) are those that share its axis and translate along it. The primitive $\tau$ generates that whole cyclic group of shared-axis translations.
> **Concretely:** consider a compact hyperbolic surface with a single closed geodesic $\gamma$ of length $\ell$, represented by the primitive $\tau : z \mapsto e^\ell z$. The powers $\{\tau^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ all preserve the imaginary axis; any hypothetical $\sigma \in \Gamma \setminus \langle\tau\rangle$ commuting with $\tau^m$ would have to preserve the axis too and act as a translation of length $\alpha < \ell$ — but this contradicts primitivity of $\tau$ (some power $\sigma^k$ would then translate by less than $\ell$, and $\tau$ would fail to generate the axis-stabiliser). See [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- Left cosets of a subgroup (partitioning a group)
> **Formally:** for $H \le G$, the *left cosets* $\{qH : q \in G\}$ partition $G$: two elements $q_1, q_2 \in G$ lie in the same left coset iff $q_1^{-1} q_2 \in H$. A *transversal* (or system of representatives) is a set $R \subset G$ containing exactly one element from each coset, so $G = \bigsqcup_{r \in R} rH$.
> **In words:** cosets are "$H$ shifted by $q$"; they group the elements of $G$ into equivalence classes, one class per element of the quotient $G/H$.
> **Concretely:** in $\mathbb{Z}$, the subgroup $2\mathbb{Z}$ has two cosets $\{0, 2\mathbb{Z}\} = \{\text{evens}\}$ and $\{1 + 2\mathbb{Z}\} = \{\text{odds}\}$; a transversal is $\{0, 1\}$. In $S_3$, the subgroup $\langle(12)\rangle$ has three cosets $\{e, (12)\}, \{(13), (132)\}, \{(23), (123)\}$; a transversal is $\{e, (13), (23)\}$.

# Claim / Identity

> **Claim (centraliser identification + coset enumeration of the conjugacy class).** For a torsion-free Fuchsian $\Gamma$ and a primitive hyperbolic $\tau \in \Gamma$, the centraliser of every power $\tau^m$ ($m \ge 1$) is
> $$C_\Gamma(\tau^m) \;=\; \langle\tau\rangle \;=\; \{\tau^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}. \tag{$\clubsuit$}$$
> Consequently the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is enumerated **without repetition** by cosets of $\langle\tau\rangle$ in $\Gamma$:
> $$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} \;=\; \bigsqcup_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} \{r \tau^m r^{-1}\}, \tag{10}$$
> with one distinct conjugate per left coset. Two elements $h_1, h_2 \in \Gamma$ produce the same conjugate $h_1 \tau^m h_1^{-1} = h_2 \tau^m h_2^{-1}$ iff $h_1^{-1} h_2 \in \langle\tau\rangle$, i.e. iff $h_1, h_2$ lie in the same left coset of $\langle\tau\rangle$.

---

# In One Line

The natural "double structure" summing $q \tau^m q^{-1}$ over $q \in \Gamma$ over-counts the conjugacy class exactly by the centraliser; for a torsion-free Fuchsian group the centraliser of a primitive hyperbolic $\tau$'s power is the cyclic subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$ itself, so the class is enumerated one-to-one by cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ — which is what makes [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]]'s coset unfolding correctly count each contribution once.

---

# Why It's True

**Mechanism (one sentence).** *Anything in $\Gamma$ that commutes with $\tau^m$ must preserve $\tau$'s axis; discreteness of $\Gamma$ forces the axis-stabiliser to be infinite cyclic; primitivity of $\tau$ forces its generator to be $\tau$ itself — hence $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$, and the standard orbit–stabiliser bijection lifts to the coset enumeration.*

There are two facts at play:

1. **The general orbit–stabiliser story.** For any $g$ in any group $G$, conjugates $q g q^{-1}$ and $q' g q'^{-1}$ coincide iff $q^{-1} q' \in C_G(g)$; so left cosets $G/C_G(g)$ index the conjugacy class bijectively. This is pure group theory, no geometry.

2. **The geometry pins down the centraliser.** In an arbitrary group, computing $C_G(g)$ is a task with no shortcuts. But for $\Gamma$ a torsion-free Fuchsian group and $g = \tau^m$ a power of a primitive hyperbolic element, the centraliser has a geometric description: anything commuting with $\tau^m$ must send the axis of $\tau^m$ (= axis of $\tau$) to itself; discreteness of $\Gamma$ makes the axis-stabiliser cyclic; primitivity of $\tau$ names its generator as $\tau$ itself. So $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ — the *smallest* possible centraliser, and independent of the power $m$.

The coset enumeration (10) is then just fact 1 applied with $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$.

# Derivation

> [!note]- Gap-free derivation
> **Step 1 — the orbit–stabiliser bijection.** For any $g \in \Gamma$, the map $\Phi : \Gamma \to [g]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, $q \mapsto q g q^{-1}$ has fibres $\Phi^{-1}(qgq^{-1}) = q\,C_\Gamma(g)$: indeed $q'gq'^{-1} = qgq^{-1}$ iff $q^{-1}q' g (q^{-1}q')^{-1} = g$ iff $q^{-1} q' \in C_\Gamma(g)$. So $\Phi$ descends to a bijection $\bar\Phi : \Gamma/C_\Gamma(g) \to [g]_{\mathrm{conj}}$, $qC_\Gamma(g) \mapsto qgq^{-1}$, and
> $$[g]_{\mathrm{conj}} \;=\; \bigsqcup_{q\,C_\Gamma(g) \in \Gamma/C_\Gamma(g)} \{q g q^{-1}\}. \tag{orbit-stabiliser}$$
> This is the general fact.
>
> **Step 2 — every element of $C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$ preserves $\tau$'s axis.** Suppose $h \in \Gamma$ satisfies $h \tau^m = \tau^m h$. Apply both sides to a point $z$ on the axis $A$ of $\tau$: $h(\tau^m z) = \tau^m(hz)$. Since $\tau^m$ preserves $A$ and translates by $m\ell_\gamma$ along it, and $\tau^m z \in A$, we have $h(\tau^m z) \in hA$ and $\tau^m(hz) \in \tau^m(hA)$; the identity of the two forces $\tau^m(hA) = hA$ (as sets in $\mathbb{H}^2$). But the axis of $\tau^m$ is the *unique* geodesic in $\mathbb{H}^2$ preserved by $\tau^m$ and containing all fixed points on the boundary of the extended action; so $hA = A$. (Alternative argument: $h$ conjugates $\tau^m$ into $h\tau^m h^{-1} = \tau^m$, and the fixed-point set on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$ of $\tau^m$ is $\{p_-, p_+\}$, so $h$ must permute $\{p_-, p_+\}$; the geodesic $A$ joining them is therefore preserved.)
>
> **Step 3 — the axis-stabiliser in $\Gamma$ is $\langle\tau\rangle$.** Let $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A) := \{h \in \Gamma : hA = A\}$. Elements of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ preserving $A$ form a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{R} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2$ (translations along $A$ + one reflection swapping the two ends). Since $\Gamma$ is torsion-free, no reflection lies in $\Gamma$ (any reflection has order $2$), so $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A)$ lies inside the translation part $\mathbb{R}$. Discreteness of $\Gamma$ inside $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ forces $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A)$ to be a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$, hence cyclic $\cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the *shortest positive translation* $\sigma$. Now $\tau \in \mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A)$ (it translates by $\ell_\gamma$), so $\tau = \sigma^k$ for some $k \ge 1$; by primitivity of $\tau$ in $\Gamma$, $k = 1$, i.e. $\sigma = \tau$. Therefore
> $$\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A) \;=\; \langle\tau\rangle. \tag{axis-stabiliser}$$
>
> **Step 4 — the centraliser identification.** From Step 2, $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) \subseteq \mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A)$; from Step 3, $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A) = \langle\tau\rangle$; and every $\tau^k$ commutes with $\tau^m$ (powers of the same element commute), so $\langle\tau\rangle \subseteq C_\Gamma(\tau^m)$. Combining,
> $$C_\Gamma(\tau^m) \;=\; \langle\tau\rangle. \tag{$\clubsuit$}$$
>
> **Step 5 — the coset enumeration of $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$.** Apply the orbit-stabiliser bijection (Step 1) with $g := \tau^m$ and $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ (Step 4):
> $$[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} \;=\; \bigsqcup_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} \{r \tau^m r^{-1}\}, \tag{10}$$
> with one distinct conjugate per left coset — the paper's identity (10). $\blacksquare$

**The two ingredients that make this work.**

- **Torsion-freeness of $\Gamma$** is what excludes the boundary-swapping reflection from $\mathrm{Stab}_\Gamma(A)$ (Step 3). If $\Gamma$ had a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ boundary-swap of $A$ (an "elliptic" preserving $A$), the axis-stabiliser could be $\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2$ (infinite dihedral), and the centraliser identity would fail — the class enumeration would need to be by cosets of this larger stabiliser.
- **Primitivity of $\tau$** is what makes the axis-generator equal to $\tau$ (Step 3). If $\tau$ were $\sigma^2$ for some $\sigma \in \Gamma$, the axis-stabiliser would be $\langle\sigma\rangle$ (strictly larger than $\langle\tau\rangle$), and cosets $\Gamma/\langle\sigma\rangle$ (not $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$) would enumerate the class — different coset structure, different count.

# Where the paper uses this

**Structural role in [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]]'s proof.** After [[Remark - Descent of the Heat Kernel by Periodisation|descent of the kernel]] and lifting the base integral to a $\Gamma$-fundamental region $F$, the class-mass takes the form
$$\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_F \sum_{h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, hz)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z).$$
The centraliser identification ($\clubsuit$) and its coset enumeration (10) reindex the inner sum as $\sum_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, r\tau^m r^{-1} z)$; the substitution $w = r^{-1}z$ (Jacobian 1, isometry) turns the sum-of-integrals $\sum_r \int_F$ into a single integral over $\bigsqcup_r r^{-1}F$; and that union is precisely a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$ — of which the cleanest is the strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ ([[Remark - Standard Form as Coordinates on the Axis]]). Without ($\clubsuit$), the coset unfolding would over-count each conjugate by a factor of $|C_\Gamma(\tau^m)|/|\langle\tau\rangle|$, and the strip integral would carry the wrong weight.

**Downstream use.** The same centraliser identification underlies every homotopy-class mass formula in [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §3 Decomposition over Homotopy Classes|§3]] and every zeta-function identity in [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §4 Zeta Functions and Total Mass|§4]] (the Selberg zeta $\prod_\gamma \prod_k (1 - e^{-(\mathfrak{s} + k)\ell_\gamma})$ has one factor *per primitive geodesic*, precisely because each primitive hyperbolic conjugacy class contributes one $\gamma$). It reappears in [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §7 Hyperbolic 3-Manifolds|§7]] for hyperbolic 3-manifolds ([[Thm - Homotopy Decomposition for 3-Manifolds|Theorem 7.1]]) where $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is Kleinian and the axis-stabiliser argument is the same, giving $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ for primitive loxodromic $\tau$.
