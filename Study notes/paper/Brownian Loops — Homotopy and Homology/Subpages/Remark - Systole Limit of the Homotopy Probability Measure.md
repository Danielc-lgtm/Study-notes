# Notation

- $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ a geometrically finite hyperbolic surface with $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ discrete and *torsion-free*.
- $\mathcal P_X$ the set of oriented primitive closed geodesics on $X$; $\ell_\gamma > 0$ the length of $\gamma \in \mathcal P_X$.
- $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} := \min_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\ell_\gamma > 0$ the *systolic length* (shortest primitive geodesic).
- $N_{\mathrm{sys}} := \#\{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X : \ell_\gamma = \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}\}$ its *multiplicity* (number of oriented primitive geodesics realising the minimum).
- $\kappa > 0$ the killing rate, $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa} > \delta$ the spectral parameter.
- $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$ the killed loop mass (§3.1.2).
- $F(s) := -\log Z_X(s) = \sum_{\gamma, m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$; $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) := \mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))/F(s)$.
- $L = m\ell_\gamma$ the length random variable, $\mathbb{E}_s$ its expectation under $\mathbb{P}_s$.

> [!recall]- Torsion-free Fuchsian group $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$
> **Formally:** $\Gamma$ is *Fuchsian* if it is a discrete subgroup of the orientation-preserving isometry group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ of $\mathbb{H}^2$; *torsion-free* if the only element of finite order is the identity. Every non-identity element is then either hyperbolic ($|\operatorname{tr}| > 2$, two fixed points on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$, translates its unique axis by a positive length), parabolic (one fixed point on $\partial\mathbb{H}^2$, no axis), or the identity — elliptic elements (which would have finite order) are excluded.
> **In words:** the group of deck transformations of the covering $\mathbb{H}^2 \to X$ is discrete (its orbits don't accumulate in $\mathbb{H}^2$) and has no rotations (otherwise the quotient $X$ would have cone-point singularities, not be a smooth hyperbolic surface). The elements are labelled by whether they move points along a geodesic axis (hyperbolic) or slide them toward a cusp (parabolic).
> **Concretely:** for the closed genus-$2$ surface, $\Gamma$ is generated by $4$ hyperbolic elements $a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2$ with the single relation $[a_1, b_1][a_2, b_2] = 1$; no parabolics because the surface has no cusps; torsion-free because the surface has no cone points. On a modular surface $\Gamma = \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$, torsion is present (the elements of orders $2$ and $3$), so the modular surface has cone points and $\Gamma$ is *not* torsion-free. Full detail: [[Def - Fuchsian Group and the Hyperbolic Quotient Surface]].

> [!recall]- Hyperbolic element in standard form $\tau : z \mapsto e^\ell z$
> **Formally:** every hyperbolic element $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ of translation length $\ell > 0$ is conjugate inside $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ (not necessarily inside $\Gamma$) to the *standard form* $\tau_0 : z \mapsto e^\ell z$, whose axis is the imaginary half-line $\{iy : y > 0\}$ and whose direction of translation is *upward* (from $i$ to $e^\ell i$). Its inverse $\tau_0^{-1} : z \mapsto e^{-\ell}z$ has the same axis but translates *downward*.
> **In words:** a hyperbolic element is a translation along a geodesic (its axis), by a specific length (its translation length), in a specific direction. Reversing the direction gives its inverse. Two hyperbolic elements with the same axis and length but opposite direction are inverses of each other.
> **Concretely:** for $\ell = \log 2$, $\tau_0(i) = 2i$, $\tau_0^{-1}(i) = i/2$; the axis is the positive imaginary axis, and the two elements translate points along it in opposite directions. Full detail: [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- $\mathbb{P}_s$ and its length random variable $L$
> **Formally:** for $\kappa > 0$ and $s = \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$, $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) := \mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))/F(s)$, a probability measure on the countable index set $\{(\gamma, m) : \gamma \in \mathcal P_X, m \ge 1\}$; $L := m\ell_\gamma$ its length random variable.
> **In words:** the killed loop mass, normalised to a probability. Each topological type $(\gamma, m)$ gets weight proportional to its killed loop mass; longer types (large $L$) are exponentially penalised by $e^{-sL}$.
> **Concretely:** with one primitive geodesic of length $\ell = 1$ and $s = 2$, $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma)) \approx 0.214/0.224 \approx 0.955$, $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^2)) \approx 0.009/0.224 \approx 0.040$, etc. Full detail: [[Def - Probability Measure on Homotopy Classes]].

# Claim / Identity

> **Claim (systole limit as $s \to \infty$).** Let $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ be a geometrically finite hyperbolic surface with $\Gamma$ torsion-free.
>
> **(a) The systole is realised by at least two oriented primitive classes:** $N_{\mathrm{sys}} \ge 2$.
>
> **(b) Concentration of $\mathbb{P}_s$ on the systolic set:** for each $\gamma \in \mathcal P_X$ with $\ell_\gamma = \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$,
> $$\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma)) \xrightarrow{s \to \infty} \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{sys}}},\qquad \mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) \xrightarrow{s \to \infty} 0\quad\text{for every other } (\gamma, m).$$
>
> **(c) Mean length converges to the systole:** $\mathbb{E}_s[L] \xrightarrow{s \to \infty} \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$.
>
> **(d) Analytic large-$s$ asymptotic:**
> $$F(s) = -\log Z_X(s) \sim \frac{N_{\mathrm{sys}}}{1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}}\,e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}\qquad (s \to \infty).\qquad(\dagger)$$
>
> **(e) Recovery formulas for $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ from the Selberg zeta:**
> $$\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} = -\lim_{s \to \infty}\frac{1}{s}\log\!\big(-\log Z_X(s)\big),\qquad N_{\mathrm{sys}} = (1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})\lim_{s \to \infty}e^{s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}\!\big(-\log Z_X(s)\big).\qquad(\ddagger)$$

---

# In One Line

Under heavy killing ($s \to \infty$), the probability measure $\mathbb{P}_s$ concentrates uniformly on the finitely many oriented primitive geodesics of shortest length; the leading large-$s$ asymptotics of the Selberg zeta value $-\log Z_X(s)$ contain both the systolic length (in the exponential decay rate) and its multiplicity (in the prefactor).

---

# Why It's True

**Mechanism.** *The killed loop mass $\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) \sim \frac{1}{m}e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}$ for large $s$ is dominated exponentially by the smallest exponent, which is $-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and is achieved only by primitive ($m = 1$) systolic classes. In a torsion-free Fuchsian group, a hyperbolic element is never conjugate to its inverse — so every oriented primitive systolic class comes with a distinct reversed-orientation systolic class, giving $N_{\mathrm{sys}} \ge 2$. All non-systolic-primitive contributions to $F(s)$ are exponentially subdominant, so $\mathbb{P}_s$ concentrates uniformly on the $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ leading terms.*

The Selberg zeta value at large $s$ is a *tropical* limit of its Euler product: only the shortest primitive geodesic survives, and its multiplicity determines the prefactor.

# Derivation

> [!note]- Gap-free derivation
>
> **Step 1 — mass asymptotic at fixed $(\gamma, m)$.** For large $s$,
> $$\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1} = \frac{e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}}{m(1 - e^{-m\ell_\gamma})} = \frac{e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}}{m(1 - e^{-m\ell_\gamma})},$$
> using $e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}/(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1) = e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}/(1 - e^{-m\ell_\gamma})$ (multiplying numerator and denominator by $e^{-m\ell_\gamma}$). The prefactor $1/[m(1 - e^{-m\ell_\gamma})]$ is $s$-free, positive, bounded above by $1/[m(1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})]$ and bounded below by $1/m$.
>
> **Step 2 — the shortest-exponent term dominates.** The exponent of the leading exponential is $-sm\ell_\gamma$. Among all $(\gamma, m)$ with $\gamma \in \mathcal P_X$ and $m \ge 1$:
> - if $m \ge 2$: the exponent is $\le -s\cdot 2\ell_\gamma \le -2s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, so smaller than $-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ by at least $s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$;
> - if $m = 1$ and $\ell_\gamma > \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$: the exponent is $-s\ell_\gamma < -s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, smaller by at least $s(\ell_\gamma - \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}) > 0$.
>
> In both cases the exponent gap to the leading $-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ grows linearly in $s$; the corresponding mass is $O(e^{-s(\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} + \varepsilon)})$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$ depending on the class. Summing over all such subleading classes and using that the sum over $(\gamma, m)$ converges absolutely at *any* $s' > \delta$, one may bound the subleading total by (a constant times) $e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}\cdot e^{-s\varepsilon_0}$ for some $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ (take $\varepsilon_0$ to be the smallest positive gap; this is positive because the length spectrum is discrete). ⚠️ Discreteness of the length spectrum uses that $\Gamma$ acts properly discontinuously; it is a standard fact for geometrically finite Fuchsian groups.
>
> **Step 3 — $N_{\mathrm{sys}} \ge 2$ via non-conjugacy of $\tau$ to $\tau^{-1}$.** Let $\gamma \in \mathcal P_X$ be systolic, and let $\tau \in \Gamma$ be a primitive hyperbolic element representing it (translation length $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$). We show that $\tau$ and $\tau^{-1}$ represent *different* free homotopy classes, i.e. are non-conjugate in $\Gamma$, so the oriented systolic set contains both $\gamma$ (represented by $[\tau]_{\mathrm{conj}}$) and its reverse $\gamma^{-1}$ (represented by $[\tau^{-1}]_{\mathrm{conj}}$).
>
> Conjugate $\tau$ inside $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ to the standard form $\tau_0 : z \mapsto e^{\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}} z$; then $\tau_0^{-1} : z \mapsto e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}z$ shares the same axis (the imaginary half-line) but translates in the opposite direction. Any $q \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ with $q\tau_0 q^{-1} = \tau_0^{-1}$ must send the axis of $\tau_0$ to the axis of $\tau_0^{-1}$, i.e. to itself, and must reverse the direction of translation — but every element of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ is orientation-preserving (it is a Möbius transformation with real coefficients and positive determinant), and any Möbius map preserving the imaginary half-line as a set either preserves its orientation (translation along it) or reverses both the axis's orientation and the upper-half-plane's orientation (a rotation through $\pi$ swapping the two ends of the axis while flipping the plane upside down). The first preserves the direction of $\tau_0$; the second flips the plane, so it is not in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ acting on $\mathbb{H}^2$ (it would swap $\mathbb{H}^2$ with the lower half-plane). Therefore no $q \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ satisfies $q\tau_0 q^{-1} = \tau_0^{-1}$; conjugating back to $\tau$, no $q \in \Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ satisfies $q\tau q^{-1} = \tau^{-1}$.
>
> Hence $[\tau]_{\mathrm{conj}} \ne [\tau^{-1}]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ in $\Gamma$; the oriented systolic classes come in reverse-orientation pairs, and $N_{\mathrm{sys}} \ge 2$. ⚠️ The "no orientation-reversing element of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$" is the arithmetic fact $\det > 0$ for elements of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$; the torsion-free hypothesis is used to exclude an *elliptic* element of $\Gamma$ that could implement the swap (an elliptic of order $2$ can flip an axis it does not lie on), but such an element has finite order and is excluded.
>
> **Step 4 — asymptotic of $F(s)$ (proof of $(\dagger)$).** By Steps 1–2, the leading contribution to $F(s) = \sum_{\gamma, m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ comes from the $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ primitive systolic classes; each contributes
> $$\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma)) = \frac{e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}}{1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}},\qquad \gamma\ \text{systolic}, m = 1.$$
> Summing over the $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ leading terms and folding the exponentially smaller remainder from Step 2 into an $O(e^{-s(\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} + \varepsilon_0)})$ error,
> $$F(s) = \frac{N_{\mathrm{sys}}}{1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}}\,e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}\big(1 + O(e^{-s\varepsilon_0})\big),$$
> which is $(\dagger)$.
>
> **Step 5 — concentration and mean (proof of (b), (c)).** For any $\gamma$ systolic (so $\ell_\gamma = \ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, $m = 1$),
> $$\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma)) = \frac{\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma))}{F(s)} = \frac{e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}/(1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})}{[N_{\mathrm{sys}}/(1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})]e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}(1 + o(1))} = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{sys}}}(1 + o(1)),$$
> proving the first half of (b). For a non-systolic-primitive $(\gamma, m)$, its mass is $O(e^{-s(\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} + \varepsilon)})$ while $F(s)$ is $\Theta(e^{-s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})$, so $\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) = O(e^{-s\varepsilon}) \to 0$, the second half. Then
> $$\mathbb{E}_s[L] = \sum_{\gamma, m}L\,\mathbb{P}_s(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \sum_{\gamma\ \text{systolic}}\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}\cdot\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{sys}}} + o(1) = \ell_{\mathrm{sys}} + o(1),$$
> proving (c). Interchanging the limit with the sum uses uniform integrability, which follows from the exponential domination of Step 2.
>
> **Step 6 — recovery formulas (proof of $(\ddagger)$).** Take $\log$ of $(\dagger)$:
> $$\log F(s) = \log\frac{N_{\mathrm{sys}}}{1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}} - s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} + O(e^{-s\varepsilon_0}),$$
> divide by $s$ and let $s \to \infty$: $s^{-1}\log F(s) \to -\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$, giving the first identity. Multiply $(\dagger)$ by $e^{s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}$ and let $s \to \infty$: $e^{s\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}}F(s) \to N_{\mathrm{sys}}/(1 - e^{-\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}})$, solve for $N_{\mathrm{sys}}$ using $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}}$ just recovered. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Sanity check.** On a toy $\Gamma = \langle \tau_0\rangle$ (an infinite cyclic hyperbolic group, so $X$ a hyperbolic cylinder with a single primitive geodesic of length $\ell$ and its reverse): $N_{\mathrm{sys}} = 2$, $\ell_{\mathrm{sys}} = \ell$, $F(s) = 2\cdot\sum_{m \ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{-sm\ell}}{1 - e^{-m\ell}}$; the leading two $m = 1$ terms give $F(s) \sim 2 e^{-s\ell}/(1 - e^{-\ell})$, exactly $(\dagger)$.
