# Notation

$(X,g)$ a [[Def - Riemannian Surface, Laplace-Beltrami Operator, and Volume Measure|Riemannian surface]], $\Delta_X$ its positive Laplacian, $p_X(t,x,y)$ its [[Def - Heat Kernel and Heat Semigroup|heat kernel]]. $C([0,t],X)$ is the space of continuous paths $\omega:[0,t]\to X$; a point of it is a whole trajectory.

---

# Axiom Motivation

Brownian motion is the mathematical model of a particle buffeted by countless tiny independent random kicks — pollen in water, an electron in noise. On the line it is the process whose increments are independent, Gaussian, and whose variance grows linearly in time. The reason it belongs in this paper is a single dictionary entry: **the probability density for a Brownian particle to go from $x$ to $y$ in time $t$ is exactly the heat kernel $p(t,x,y)$.** Heat diffusing and a particle wandering are the same phenomenon read two ways — analytically (a PDE, the heat equation) and probabilistically (a random path). This is why the paper can freely translate between the operator $e^{-t\Delta_X}$ and "the law of a random loop": they are the same object.

To lift this to a curved surface one cannot just add Gaussian increments — "straight line" and "Gaussian" are not intrinsic. The intrinsic definition instead *starts* from the generator: Brownian motion on $(X,g)$ is the random process whose transition densities are the heat kernel of $\Delta_X$. The generator $-\Delta_X$ plays the role that $\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ plays on the line (up to the speed convention).

# The Definition

> **Definition (Brownian motion on $(X,g)$).** **Brownian motion on $X$, run at speed 2**, is the continuous-time Markov process $(B_t)_{t\ge0}$ with values in $X$ whose transition probabilities have density the heat kernel: for a starting point $x$ and a Borel set $A\subseteq X$,
> $$\mathbb{P}_x\big(B_t\in A\big) \;=\; \int_A p_X(t,x,y)\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y).$$
> Equivalently, it is the diffusion generated by $-\Delta_X$ (so $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}_x[f(B_t)]\big|_{t=0}=-\Delta_X f(x)$). Its **law started at $x$**, written $\mathbb{W}^t_x$, is the probability measure on the path space $C([0,t],X)$ assigning to each set of trajectories the probability that the particle's path lies in it. When $X$ has a boundary, one imposes **Dirichlet (killing) conditions**: the particle is killed the instant it hits $\partial X$, so $p_X$ is the heat kernel of $\Delta_X$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions and $\int_X p_X(t,x,y)\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y)<1$ (mass is lost to the boundary).

**Concrete unpacking.** On $\mathbb{R}^2$, "run at speed 2" means $B_t=(W^1_{2t},W^2_{2t})$ for independent standard 1-D Brownian motions — increments are Gaussian with variance $2t$ per coordinate, matching the density $\frac{1}{4\pi t}e^{-|x-y|^2/4t}$. The factor 2 is why the generator is $-\Delta$ rather than $-\frac12\Delta$; it is pure convention and only rescales time. The law $\mathbb{W}^t_x$ is a measure on *entire paths*, not on points — a single draw is a whole continuous curve.

**Standard names.** $(B_t)$ is **Brownian motion** (the *Wiener process* on the line); $\mathbb{W}^t_x$ is the **Wiener measure** started at $x$. "Generated by $-\Delta_X$" names $-\Delta_X$ the **infinitesimal generator**.

# Examples and Non-Examples

**Is an instance.** Standard planar Brownian motion (on $\mathbb{R}^2$). Brownian motion on the flat torus (planar Brownian motion read modulo $\mathbb{Z}^2$). Killed Brownian motion on a disc (killed on the boundary circle).

**Is NOT an instance.** A process with *jumps* — e.g. an $\alpha$-stable process — is **not** Brownian motion: its paths are discontinuous (càdlàg, not continuous), and its generator is a fractional power $\Delta_X^{\alpha/2}$, not $\Delta_X$. Such processes appear later in the paper via [[Def - Bernstein Function, Subordinator, and Subordination|subordination]] and require càdlàg path spaces.

**Calibration check.** (1) Verify that "transition density $=p(t,x,y)$" plus Chapman–Kolmogorov ([[Def - Heat Kernel and Heat Semigroup|heat-kernel page]]) is exactly the Markov (memoryless) property. (2) On $\mathbb{R}^2$, confirm $\mathbb{E}_x[|B_t-x|^2]=4t$. (3) Explain why killing on a boundary makes $\int_A p\,d\operatorname{vol}_g$ sum to less than 1.

# Where the paper uses this

The paper's loops are Brownian paths that return to their start. The law $\mathbb{W}^t_x$ and its conditioned version, the [[Def - Disintegration and the Bridge Measure|bridge measure]] $\mathbb{W}^t_{x\to y}$, are the raw material of the [[Def - Brownian Loop Measure|Brownian loop measure]]. **[[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology — §2 Preliminaries|§2]]**.
