# Notation

- $\mathbb{H}^3 = \{(z, y) : z \in \mathbb{C},\, y > 0\}$ — hyperbolic 3-space (upper half-space model) with metric $ds^2 = (|dz|^2 + dy^2)/y^2$ and volume form $d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3} = y^{-3}\,dA(z)\,dy$.
- $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ — a **Kleinian group**: a discrete, torsion-free subgroup of the isometry group of $\mathbb{H}^3$, assumed geometrically finite; $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$ is a hyperbolic 3-manifold. The covering projection is $\pi : \mathbb{H}^3 \to X$.
- $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ — a *primitive* closed geodesic on $X$, with translation length $\ell_\gamma > 0$ and holonomy angle $\theta_\gamma \in \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$; complex length $L_\gamma := \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma \in \mathbb{C}$.
- $\tau \in \Gamma$ — a primitive **loxodromic** representative of $\gamma$; after conjugation in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ take $\tau$ in **standard form** $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z,\, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$, with axis the vertical geodesic from $(0, 0)$ to $\infty$.
- $C_X(\gamma^m)$ — the free homotopy class of loops on $X$ corresponding to the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} \subset \Gamma$ (loops winding $m$ times around $\gamma$).
- $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{(z, y) \in \mathbb{H}^3 : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ — the **fundamental slab** of $\langle\tau\rangle$ (each $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit meets it once, because $\tau$ scales $y$ by the real factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$).
- $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ — a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^3, \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3})$ with $\Gamma$-invariant transition kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$ (so $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, hz, hw) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$ for every $h \in \Gamma$); the downstairs kernel on $X$ is $p^E_X(t, z, w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h\tilde w)$.
- $\mu^E_X$ — the Dirichlet-form loop measure on $X$ built from $p^E_X$: $\mu^E_X(A) = \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_X \mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to z, X}(A)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_X(z)$, where $\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to z, X}$ is the bridge measure of the process at $z$ over time $t$.

> [!recall]- Hyperbolic 3-space $\mathbb{H}^3$
> **Formally:** $\mathbb{H}^3 := \{(z, y) : z \in \mathbb{C},\, y > 0\}$ with Riemannian metric $ds^2 = (|dz|^2 + dy^2)/y^2$ and volume $d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3} = y^{-3}\,dA(z)\,dy$; isometry group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$.
> **In words:** the 3D analogue of the hyperbolic upper half-plane you know from the surface theory. Points are indexed by a complex "horizontal" coordinate $z$ and a positive "height" $y$; the ruler shrinks as height grows, giving constant negative curvature.
> **Concretely:** the vertical geodesic from $(0, 1)$ to $(0, 2)$ has hyperbolic length $\int_1^2 dy/y = \log 2$. A Euclidean-tiny box of side $\varepsilon$ centred at height $y$ has hyperbolic volume $\varepsilon^3/y^3$ — much smaller high up. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic 3-Space, Kleinian Groups, and Complex Length]].

> [!recall]- Kleinian group $\Gamma$ and the hyperbolic 3-manifold $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$
> **Formally:** a **Kleinian group** $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is a discrete, torsion-free subgroup of the isometry group of $\mathbb{H}^3$. Its action on $\mathbb{H}^3$ has no fixed points; the *quotient* $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$ inherits a smooth hyperbolic metric of constant curvature $-1$ and is a **hyperbolic 3-manifold**. Geometrically finite: a technical finiteness condition bounding $X$'s topological complexity (finitely-generated fundamental group with a well-behaved boundary).
> **In words:** the 3D analogue of a Fuchsian group / hyperbolic surface. $\Gamma$ is the symmetry group of an infinite hyperbolic 3D wallpaper; fold $\mathbb{H}^3$ by that symmetry group and you get a 3-manifold with the same local geometry as $\mathbb{H}^3$ but a nontrivial global shape (handles, cusps, knotted structure).
> **Concretely:** the smallest example is the figure-eight knot complement $S^3 \setminus K_{8}$, which admits a complete hyperbolic 3-manifold structure with $\Gamma$ generated by two elements of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z}[\omega])$ ($\omega$ a primitive cube root of $1$); its finite hyperbolic volume is $2 \cdot 1.014\ldots$ (twice Catalan's constant). A simpler Euclidean-analogue is $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^3$ acting on $\mathbb{R}^3$ by integer translations, quotient the flat 3-torus. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic 3-Space, Kleinian Groups, and Complex Length]].

> [!recall]- Loxodromic isometry, complex length $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$
> **Formally:** a non-parabolic, non-elliptic element $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is **loxodromic**: it fixes exactly two boundary points, preserves the geodesic between them (its *axis*), and after conjugation in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ takes the *standard form* $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z,\, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$ with $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$, $\ell_\gamma > 0$. It **translates** length $\ell_\gamma$ along its axis and simultaneously **rotates** by angle $\theta_\gamma$ about it. The complex length $L_\gamma$ packages these two real numbers.
> **In words:** A hyperbolic isometry of $\mathbb{H}^2$ is a Möbius map that fixes a geodesic axis (a specific line) and slides points along it by a definite distance — in 2D that is all it does; in 3D the analogue (a loxodromic isometry) also rotates the extra dimension around the axis, so it acts as a screw motion. The complex length $L_\gamma$ is a bookkeeping trick: its real part is the slide, its imaginary part is the twist, and packaging them as one complex number makes the surface formulas carry over verbatim to 3D with $\ell_\gamma$ replaced by $L_\gamma$.
> **Concretely:** $\tau(z, y) = (2 i z, 2 y)$ has $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$, $\theta_\gamma = \pi/2$: the point $(1, 1)$ maps to $(2i, 2)$, then $(-4, 4)$ — height doubling, horizontal rotating $90°$ per step. Points on the axis $(0, y)$ just double in height with no rotation because there is nothing to rotate about themselves. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic 3-Space, Kleinian Groups, and Complex Length]].

> [!recall]- Free homotopy classes on $X$ ↔ loxodromic conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$
> **Formally:** two oriented closed curves on $X$ are *freely homotopic* if one can be continuously deformed into the other in $X$ with the basepoint allowed to move. The set of free homotopy classes is in bijection with the set of conjugacy classes $[h]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \{q h q^{-1} : q \in \Gamma\}$; the non-trivial non-peripheral classes correspond exactly to loxodromic conjugacy classes (each carrying a unique closed geodesic representative of complex length $L_\gamma$). $C_X(\gamma^m) \leftrightarrow [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is the class of loops winding $m$ times around $\gamma$.
> **In words:** each loop on the 3-manifold "goes around some topological structure (handles, cusps, knotted pieces) in some pattern"; two loops that go around the same structure the same number of times (with the starting point allowed to slide) are in the same class. On the algebraic side, moving a loop's basepoint by $q \in \Gamma$ conjugates its recorded deck move — so classes of loops correspond to classes of group elements.
> **Concretely:** on the 3-torus $T^3 = \mathbb{R}^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$ (a Euclidean analogue), $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^3$ is abelian, so free homotopy classes are just triples $(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ ("net winding around each of the three axes"). On the figure-eight knot complement the fundamental group is non-abelian and a conjugacy class is a genuine multi-element equivalence class of group elements — but the picture is the same: one class per topological loop type.

> [!recall]- Primitive geodesic $\gamma$; the loxodromic representative $\tau$
> **Formally:** an element $\tau \in \Gamma$ is **primitive** if it is not a proper power $\sigma^k$ ($k \ge 2$) of any other $\sigma \in \Gamma$. Every non-trivial, non-peripheral free homotopy class on $X$ is represented by a primitive loxodromic $\tau$ (with translation length $\ell_\gamma$ and holonomy $\theta_\gamma$); the class contains a unique closed geodesic $\gamma$ obtained as the projection of $\tau$'s axis to $X$, of length $\ell_\gamma$. The set of all such primitive geodesics is written $\mathcal{P}_X$; the class of $\tau^m$ contains $\gamma$ traversed $m$ times.
> **In words:** among the loxodromic elements of $\Gamma$, the *primitive* ones are those that cannot be written as an iterate of a smaller element. Each is the shortest-length representative of its own class of loops; iterating it $m$ times gives a loop that winds around $\gamma$ exactly $m$ times.
> **Concretely:** if $\Gamma$ contains $\tau$ with $L_\gamma = \log 2 + i\pi/4$ and no other element $\sigma$ with $\sigma^k = \tau$, then $\tau$ is primitive. Its iterate $\tau^2$ has complex length $2 L_\gamma = 2 \log 2 + i\pi/2$ and represents the same geodesic $\gamma$ traversed twice.

> [!recall]- Fundamental slab $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{(z, y) : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$
> **Formally:** for the loxodromic $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z,\, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$, the cyclic subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle \subset \Gamma$ acts on $\mathbb{H}^3$ with orbits $\{(e^{k L_\gamma} z, e^{k \ell_\gamma} y) : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Because $y$ scales by the real factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, each orbit meets $\mathcal{F}_\tau := \{(z, y) : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ exactly once — the slab is a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$.
> **In words:** slice $\mathbb{H}^3$ into horizontal layers of height $y$. $\tau$ scales heights by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, so one full period of $\tau$'s action carries a layer at height $y$ to one at height $e^{\ell_\gamma} y$. The slab $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is one full period — every $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit crosses it once. This is the 3D analogue of the 2D fundamental *strip* $\{1 \le \operatorname{Im} z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$.
> **Concretely:** if $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$, the slab is $\{(z, y) : 1 \le y < 2\}$ — an infinite horizontal 3D layer of hyperbolic thickness $\log 2$. The point $(1 + i, 1.5)$ is in the slab; the point $(1 + i, 5)$ has $\tau^{-2}(1 + i, 5) = (e^{-2 L_\gamma}(1+i), 5/4)$, which is in the slab.

> [!recall]- $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form and its heat kernel on $\mathbb{H}^3$
> **Formally:** a Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^3, \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3})$ generates a symmetric Markov semigroup $(P^E_t)_{t \ge 0}$ with integral kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$. It is **$\Gamma$-invariant** if $\mathcal{E}(f \circ h, g \circ h) = \mathcal{E}(f, g)$ for every $h \in \Gamma$, which forces $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, h z, h w) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$ for every $h \in \Gamma$. The downstairs kernel on $X$ is $p^E_X(t, z, w) = \sum_{h \in \Gamma} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h\tilde w)$ — the periodisation.
> **In words:** a Dirichlet form is a bilinear "energy" functional on functions, whose associated Markov process has transition density $p^E$. $\Gamma$-invariance means the form treats $\Gamma$-related points identically, so the process descends to the quotient 3-manifold. The downstairs kernel is a sum over all lifts of the endpoint.
> **Concretely:** the archetype is $\mathcal{E}(f, f) = \int_{\mathbb{H}^3} |\nabla f|^2 \,d\!\operatorname{vol}$ generated by the Laplace–Beltrami $-\frac12\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^3}$; its kernel is the explicit $\mathbb{H}^3$ heat kernel $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w) = (4\pi t)^{-3/2} \frac{u}{\sinh u}\,e^{-t - u^2/4t}$ with $u = d(z, w)$. Subordinate variants (α-stable, killed) are the ones the paper uses. Full detail: [[Def - Dirichlet Form and its Operator and Semigroup]].

> [!recall]- Loop measure $\mu^E_X$ and the bridge measure $\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to z, X}$
> **Formally:** for the Markov process associated with $E$, the **bridge measure** $\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to w, X}$ is the conditional law of the path over $[0, t]$ from $z$ to $w$, multiplied by the transition density (so it has total mass $p^E_X(t, z, w)$). The **loop measure** on $X$ is $\mu^E_X(A) := \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_X \mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to z, X}(A)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_X(z)$: integrate the bridge measure of loops of duration $t$ starting and ending at $z$, over all base points $z$ and durations $t$, with the scale-invariant weight $dt/t$.
> **In words:** the loop measure gives an infinite measure on loop-space by summing "loops at every basepoint over every duration" with a Le Jan weight $dt/t$. Its restriction to loops in a fixed free homotopy class is a positive measure whose total mass is the class's Le Jan mass.
> **Concretely:** for Brownian motion on the flat torus $T^d = \mathbb{R}^d/\mathbb{Z}^d$, $\mathbb{W}^t_{0 \to 0}$ has total mass $p(t, 0, 0)$ (a sum of Gaussians summed over lattice translates); the mass of loops of *class* $n \in \mathbb{Z}^d$ (winding number) is $\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \cdot (4\pi t)^{-d/2} e^{-|n|^2/(4t)} = \frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{2\pi^{d/2}|n|^d}$ — Le Jan's identity for the flat torus. Full detail: [[Def - Dirichlet Form Loop Measure]] and [[Def - Disintegration and the Bridge Measure]].

> [!recall]- From earlier sections — [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]] (surface case)
> **Formally:** on a hyperbolic *surface* $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ with primitive hyperbolic $\tau : z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma} z$, $\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, \tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z)$ where $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{z : 1 \le \operatorname{Im} z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$.
> **In words:** The 2D analogue of the theorem below — on the surface, the class-mass is a single integral of the upstairs transition density (heat kernel) from a point to its $\tau^m$-image over one fundamental strip (one period of the cyclic translation $\langle\tau\rangle$). The proof uses only that $\Gamma$ moves the density unchanged, that the elements of $\Gamma$ commuting with $\tau^m$ are exactly the powers $\langle\tau\rangle$ (so orbit-summing over cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ collapses to one strip integral), and that the integrand is periodic under $\langle\tau\rangle$. Every step transfers to 3D verbatim with $\mathbb{H}^2 \to \mathbb{H}^3$ and hyperbolic-standard-form $\to$ screw-motion standard form.
> **Concretely:** for the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ and $\tau : x \mapsto x + n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}^2$), the strip is $[0, 1) \times \mathbb{R}$, the kernel is $(4\pi t)^{-1} e^{-|z - (z + n)|^2/(4t)} = (4\pi t)^{-1} e^{-|n|^2/(4t)}$ (constant in $z$), and the strip integral gives $(4\pi t)^{-1} e^{-|n|^2/(4t)}$ — reproducing the winding-number decomposition of the torus heat kernel. See [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class]].

# Statement

> **Theorem (homotopy-class decomposition, 3-manifolds; Belyaev–Huseynli 7.1).** Let $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$ be a geometrically finite hyperbolic 3-manifold, $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ a primitive closed geodesic with loxodromic representative $\tau$ in standard form ($\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z,\, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$, complex length $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$), and $m \ge 1$. Let $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ be a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^3, \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3})$ with $\Gamma$-invariant transition kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}$. Then the loop-measure mass of the free homotopy class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ equals
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t,\, w,\, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w),$$
> where $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{(z, y) \in \mathbb{H}^3 : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ is the fundamental slab of $\langle\tau\rangle$.

---

# In One Line

The whole apparatus of loop-classes on a hyperbolic 3-manifold collapses, for each topological class, into a single kernel value $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ (upstairs kernel from a point to its $\tau^m$-image) integrated over one geodesic period — the exact 3D analogue of [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]], with the surface's strip replaced by the 3-manifold's slab and the hyperbolic standard form replaced by the loxodromic one.

---

# Why It's True

**Mechanism (one sentence).** *Nothing in the surface proof used two-dimensionality: the argument turned only on $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel, the identification $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ of the centraliser, coset enumeration $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ of the conjugacy class, one isometry substitution, and the $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance of the integrand — every one of which transfers verbatim from the 2D setting to the 3D setting once "hyperbolic $\tau : z \mapsto e^\ell z$" is replaced by "loxodromic $\tau : (z, y) \mapsto (e^L z, e^\ell y)$" and "strip" by "slab".*

The starting observation is the same lifting picture as in 2D: every continuous downstairs loop $\omega$ rooted at $z \in X$ lifts uniquely to an $\mathbb{H}^3$-arc from a chosen lift $\tilde z \in \pi^{-1}(z)$ to some $h \tilde z$; the deck element $h \in \Gamma$ is the loop's monodromy and its conjugacy class is the free homotopy class of $\omega$. So the loop measure at $z$ decomposes as a sum over $h \in \Gamma$ of upstairs bridge measures from $\tilde z$ to $h \tilde z$, pushed down. Restricting to loops of type $C_X(\gamma^m)$ keeps exactly the $h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$.

The mass is then a triple integral: over time $t$ with weight $dt/t$, over basepoints $z \in X$ with $\operatorname{vol}_X$, and over the conjugacy class. Passing $\int_X \cdots d\!\operatorname{vol}_X$ upstairs to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$ and enumerating the conjugacy class as one representative per coset $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ turns the double sum-and-integral into a *single* integrand $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ integrated over a union of $\Gamma$-translates of the fundamental region. These translates fill a fundamental region for the *smaller* subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$; because the integrand is $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant, that integral equals its value on any fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$, and the cleanest such region is the slab $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ — the 3D analogue of the 2D fundamental strip.

**Why the slab is the natural fundamental region.** In standard form $\tau$ scales the *real* coordinate $y$ (height) by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$ and rotates the horizontal $\mathbb{C}$-plane by $e^{i\theta_\gamma}$ at each height. The rotation stays within a given horizontal layer, so orbits are indexed by height modulo the multiplicative factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$; one period is exactly the slab $\{1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$, and each $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit meets it once. The horizontal rotation, though present, does not participate in choosing the fundamental region — it will show up only inside the integrand, as the horizontal piece of $\tau^m w$.

**The one genuinely new feature.** In 2D the fundamental region is a strip in a 2D space; in 3D it is a 3D slab. This does not complicate the proof, but it does make the *evaluation* of the slab integral in later sections richer, because the extra horizontal $\mathbb{C}$-plane at each height contributes a polar integration that becomes the "cross-section" factor in the closed forms of §7.2.

# Proof

> [!note]- Gap-free proof
> **Setup.** Fix the loop measure $\mu^E_X$ built from the $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet-form kernel $p^E_X(t, z, w) = \sum_{h \in \Gamma} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h \tilde w)$. Its bridge decomposition at a basepoint $z \in X$ is
> $$\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z \to z, X} \;=\; \sum_{h \in \Gamma} \pi_*\,\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{\tilde z \to h \tilde z,\,\mathbb{H}^3}, \tag{$\ast$}$$
> where $\pi_*$ is the pushforward under $\pi : \mathbb{H}^3 \to X$ and each summand is the (positive) bridge measure of the upstairs process from $\tilde z$ to $h \tilde z$. This is the standard universal-cover decomposition: every downstairs bridge is the disjoint union, over deck elements $h$, of pushforwards of upstairs bridges to the various lifts of $z$; the total masses match by the periodisation identity for $p^E_X$. Taking total masses gives $|\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{\tilde z \to h \tilde z}|_{\mathrm{tv}} = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h \tilde z)$.
>
> **Step 1 — isolate the conjugacy class.** A loop rooted at $z$ belongs to $C_X(\gamma^m)$ if and only if its monodromy $h$ lies in $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. So $\mu^E_X$ restricted to $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is obtained by keeping only those summands of ($\ast$) with $h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. Taking total masses and integrating $z$ over $X$ with $\operatorname{vol}_X$,
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_X \sum_{h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h\tilde z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_X(z). \tag{$\dagger$}$$
> (For a jump process this restriction is the *definition* of the class-mass, exactly as in [[Remark - Homotopy Classes for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1]]; the formula that follows depends only on the right-hand side of ($\dagger$), so it holds in both the diffusive and jump cases.)
>
> **Step 2 — lift the base integral to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$.** Let $F \subset \mathbb{H}^3$ be a fundamental region for $\Gamma$: $F$ meets each $\Gamma$-orbit in exactly one point (up to a volume-null boundary). The projection $\pi : F \to X$ is a volume-preserving bijection almost everywhere, so for any $\Gamma$-invariant measurable $f : X \to [0, \infty]$,
> $$\int_X f\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_X \;=\; \int_F (f \circ \pi)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}.$$
> Apply this with $f(z) := \sum_{h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h \tilde z)$. The value at $z$ does not depend on the choice of lift $\tilde z$: for any $q \in \Gamma$, $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, q \tilde z, h q \tilde z) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, q^{-1} h q \tilde z)$ by $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel (applied with the deck element $q^{-1}$ to both slots), and as $h$ ranges over the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ so does $q^{-1} h q$. Hence $f$ is well-defined on $X$, and
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_F \sum_{h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, h z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(z). \tag{$\ddagger$}$$
>
> **Step 3 — unfold the conjugacy class over cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$.** *Centraliser identification*: the centraliser of $\tau^m$ in $\Gamma$ is exactly $\langle\tau\rangle$, i.e. $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$. Reason: any element $\sigma \in \Gamma$ commuting with a primitive loxodromic $\tau^m$ must preserve its axis (the axis is the unique $\tau^m$-invariant geodesic in $\mathbb{H}^3$); among torsion-free discrete Kleinian groups, elements preserving a given geodesic form a cyclic group, and the primitivity of $\tau$ forces this cyclic group to be $\langle\tau\rangle$. Therefore each element of $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is uniquely of the form $r \tau^m r^{-1}$ for a coset representative $r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$:
> $$\sum_{h \in [\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, h z) \;=\; \sum_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, r \tau^m r^{-1} z). \tag{$\S$}$$
>
> For each coset $r$, apply $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel with $r^{-1}$ to both slots:
> $$p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, r \tau^m r^{-1} z) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, r^{-1} z,\, r^{-1} \cdot r \tau^m r^{-1} z) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, r^{-1} z,\, \tau^m r^{-1} z).$$
> Substitute $w := r^{-1} z$ in the spatial integral over $F$. Because $r^{-1}$ is a hyperbolic isometry, its Jacobian on $d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}$ is $1$, and the region becomes $r^{-1} F$:
> $$\int_F p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, r \tau^m r^{-1} z)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(z) \;=\; \int_{r^{-1} F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w).$$
> Sum over $r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$: the sets $\{r^{-1} F : r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\}$ are pairwise disjoint (up to volume-null boundaries) because $\{q F : q \in \Gamma\}$ tiles $\mathbb{H}^3$ and picking one $r$ per coset picks one $\Gamma$-translate per coset. Therefore
> $$\sum_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} \int_{r^{-1} F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w) \;=\; \int_{\bigcup_r r^{-1} F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w). \tag{$\P$}$$
>
> **Step 4 — replace the tiled union by the slab.** The union $\bigcup_{r \in \Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle} r^{-1} F$ is a **fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$**. Reason: the cosets partition $\Gamma = \bigsqcup_r r \langle\tau\rangle$, and $\bigcup_r r^{-1} F$ picks one representative of each $\Gamma$-orbit from each coset, which is the same as picking one representative of each $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit.
>
> The integrand $w \mapsto p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ is $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant: for any $k \in \mathbb{Z}$,
> $$p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tau^k w,\, \tau^m \tau^k w) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tau^k w,\, \tau^k \tau^m w) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w),$$
> using $\tau^k \tau^m = \tau^m \tau^k$ (abelian since both are in $\langle\tau\rangle$) and $\Gamma$-invariance applied with $\tau^k$ to both slots. A non-negative $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant function has the *same* integral over every fundamental region of $\langle\tau\rangle$; the cleanest such region is the slab
> $$\mathcal{F}_\tau \;=\; \{(z, y) \in \mathbb{H}^3 : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\},$$
> which is fundamental because $\tau$ scales heights by the real factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$ (Step 4 of the recall above). Therefore
> $$\int_{\bigcup_r r^{-1} F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w) \;=\; \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w). \tag{$\|$}$$
>
> **Conclusion.** Chaining ($\ddagger$), ($\S$), ($\P$), ($\|$):
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w). \qquad \blacksquare$$
>
> **Remark (structural equivalence with the surface proof).** Steps 1–4 are structurally identical to the corresponding steps in the proof of [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]] with $\mathbb{H}^2 \to \mathbb{H}^3$, $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R}) \to \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$, hyperbolic $\to$ loxodromic, strip $\to$ slab, $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2} \to \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}$. Nothing in the argument used two-dimensionality — the transfer is verbatim.
