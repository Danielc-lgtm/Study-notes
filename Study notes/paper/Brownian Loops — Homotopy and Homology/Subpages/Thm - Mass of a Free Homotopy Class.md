# Notation

- $\mathbb{H}^2 = \{x + iy : y > 0\}$ — the upper-half-plane, equipped with the hyperbolic metric $ds^2 = (dx^2 + dy^2)/y^2$; isometry group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$.
- $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ — a discrete, torsion-free (geometrically finite) subgroup; $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ is a hyperbolic surface; $\pi : \mathbb{H}^2 \to X$ is the covering projection.
- $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ — a *primitive* closed geodesic (element of the set $\mathcal{P}_X$ of primitive geodesics), with translation length $\ell_\gamma > 0$.
- $\tau \in \Gamma$ — a primitive hyperbolic element representing $\gamma$; after conjugation in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ one may take $\tau : z \mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma} z$.
- $C_X(\gamma^m)$ — the free homotopy class of loops on $X$ corresponding to the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}} \subset \Gamma$ (winding $m$ times around $\gamma$).
- $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{z \in \mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ — the fundamental strip of $\langle\tau\rangle$.
- $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ — a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^2, \rho_{\mathbb{H}^2})$ with $\Gamma$-invariant transition kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$ satisfying $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,hz,hw) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$ for every $h\in\Gamma$; the downstairs kernel is $p^E_X(t,z,w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z,h\tilde w)$.
- $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$, $\rho_X$ — hyperbolic area measures on $\mathbb{H}^2$ and $X$ (the pushforward of $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}|_F$ under $\pi$ for any fundamental region $F$).
- $\mu^E_X$ — the loop measure on $X$ built from $p^E_X$: $\mu^E_X(A) = \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_X \mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to z,X}(A)\,d\rho_X(z)$, where $\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to z}$ is the bridge measure of the process at $z$ over time $t$.

> [!recall]- The hyperbolic plane $\mathbb{H}^2$
> **Formally:** $\mathbb{H}^2 := \{z = x+iy \in \mathbb{C} : y > 0\}$ with the Riemannian metric $ds^2 = (dx^2 + dy^2)/y^2$. Isometry group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, acting by Möbius maps $z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)$, $ad-bc=1$. Geodesics: vertical rays and half-circles perpendicular to the real axis.
> **In words:** the upper half-plane with a rescaled ruler that makes it negatively curved — the "negative-curvature analogue of a sphere". Points near the real axis are at effectively infinite distance from anything at height $y=1$.
> **Concretely:** the hyperbolic distance from $i$ to $2i$ along the vertical geodesic $x=0$ equals $\int_1^2 dy/y = \log 2$. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic Plane]].

> [!recall]- Fuchsian group $\Gamma$ and $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$
> **Formally:** a *Fuchsian group* is a discrete $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$. Torsion-free means no non-identity element has finite order; the quotient $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ (points identified when a $\Gamma$-element moves one to the other) is then a smooth hyperbolic surface. Geometrically finite: a technical finiteness condition bounding $X$'s topology.
> **In words:** the group $\Gamma$ is a set of symmetries of $\mathbb{H}^2$; folding $\mathbb{H}^2$ by that symmetry gives a surface with the same local geometry as $\mathbb{H}^2$ but a genuine global shape (handles, cusps — puncture-shaped ends of infinite length but finite area).
> **Concretely:** take $\Gamma$ generated by $z\mapsto z+1$ and $z\mapsto z+i$ acting on $\mathbb{R}^2$; the quotient is the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ — one unit square with opposite edges glued. In the hyperbolic case a genus-2 surface arises from a specific 4-generator $\Gamma$. Full detail: [[Def - Fuchsian Group and the Hyperbolic Quotient Surface]].

> [!recall]- Primitive hyperbolic $\tau$, translation length $\ell_\gamma$, closed geodesic $\gamma$
> **Formally:** $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ is *hyperbolic* if it is conjugate in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ to $\tau_0 : z\mapsto e^\ell z$ for some $\ell > 0$; the *translation length* is $\ell = \ell_\tau$. It is *primitive* in $\Gamma$ if it is not a proper power $\sigma^k$ ($k\ge 2$) of any other $\sigma\in\Gamma$. Every non-trivial, non-peripheral free homotopy class on $X$ contains a unique closed geodesic $\gamma$ of length $\ell_\gamma$ equal to the translation length of its primitive representative; the class of $\tau^m$ contains $\gamma$ traversed $m$ times.
> **In words:** hyperbolic elements of $\Gamma$ are "translations along a geodesic axis by a fixed hyperbolic distance". Primitive means not obtained by iterating a smaller element. Each non-trivial loop-type on $X$ has a shortest representative — a closed geodesic — whose length equals the translation length of the corresponding primitive $\tau$.
> **Concretely:** $\tau_0 : z\mapsto e^\ell z$ has axis the imaginary half-line $\{iy : y>0\}$; the distance from $i$ to $e^\ell i$ along it is $\int_1^{e^\ell}dy/y = \ell$. In the quotient $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ with $\Gamma = \langle\tau_0\rangle$, the axis projects to a closed loop of length $\ell$ — the geodesic $\gamma$. Full detail: [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- Fundamental strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau$
> **Formally:** for $\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$, $\mathcal{F}_\tau := \{z\in\mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ is a fundamental region for the cyclic subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle \subset \Gamma$: it contains exactly one point from every $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit $\{e^{k\ell_\gamma}z : k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$.
> **In words:** $\tau$ rescales imaginary parts by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$; the strip is "one period" of that rescaling. Quotienting $\mathbb{H}^2$ by $\langle\tau\rangle$ is the same as taking $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ and gluing its top edge to its bottom edge — producing a cylinder.
> **Concretely:** if $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$ (so $\tau : z\mapsto 2z$), the strip is $\{z : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < 2\}$. The orbit of $z = 1+i$ is $\{1+i, 2+2i, 4+4i, \ldots, (1+i)/2, \ldots\}$ and only $1+i$ lies in the strip; the point $3+3i$ has its representative at $\tau^{-1}(3+3i) = 1.5 + 1.5i \in \mathcal{F}_\tau$.

> [!recall]- Free homotopy classes on $X$ ↔ conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$
> **Formally:** two oriented closed curves on $X$ are *freely homotopic* if one continuously deforms into the other with the basepoint allowed to move. The set of free homotopy classes is in bijection with the set of conjugacy classes $[h]_{\mathrm{conj}} = \{qhq^{-1} : q\in\Gamma\}$ of $\Gamma$; the class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ corresponds to $C_X(\gamma^m)$.
> **In words:** each loop "goes around some holes in some pattern"; two loops in the same pattern (with the starting point allowed to drift) are in one class. The *monodromy* of a loop is the group element of $\Gamma$ that carries the starting lift on $\mathbb{H}^2$ to its ending lift, and it records which topological type the loop has. Moving a loop's basepoint by $q\in\Gamma$ conjugates its recorded monodromy $h \mapsto qhq^{-1}$, so basepoint-free classes on $X$ correspond to conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$.
> **Concretely:** on the torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian, so conjugacy classes are singletons; free homotopy classes are in bijection with $\mathbb{Z}^2$, one per integer pair $(a,b)$ ("$a$ times horizontally, $b$ times vertically"). In the hyperbolic case $\Gamma$ is non-abelian, so a conjugacy class is a genuine equivalence class — one per topological loop-type.

> [!recall]- $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form and its kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}$
> **Formally:** a Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^2, \rho_{\mathbb{H}^2})$ generates a symmetric Markov semigroup $(P^E_t)_{t\ge 0}$ with integral kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$. It is *$\Gamma$-invariant* if $\mathcal{E}(f\circ h, g\circ h) = \mathcal{E}(f,g)$ for every $h\in\Gamma$, which forces the kernel to satisfy $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,hz,hw) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,w)$. The downstairs Dirichlet form on $X$ has kernel $p^E_X(t,z,w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde w)$.
> **In words:** a Dirichlet form is a bilinear energy functional on functions, generating a Markov process whose "heat kernel" $p^E$ gives transition densities. $\Gamma$-invariance means the form treats $\Gamma$-related points identically; the surface's kernel is the sum of upstairs contributions over all $\Gamma$-copies of the endpoint.
> **Concretely:** on the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$, the ordinary (Euclidean) Laplacian defines a $\mathbb{Z}^2$-invariant Dirichlet form; the downstairs heat kernel is $p_{T^2}(t,x,y) = \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^2}(4\pi t)^{-1}\exp(-|x-y-n|^2/4t)$ — the Gaussian summed over lattice translates. This is the same periodisation identity, upgraded to $\mathbb{H}^2$ with $\Gamma$ replacing $\mathbb{Z}^2$. Full detail: [[Def - Dirichlet Form and its Operator and Semigroup]].

> [!recall]- Bridge measure $\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to w}$
> **Formally:** given the Markov process $(X_s)_{s\in[0,t]}$ associated with the Dirichlet form $E$ starting from $z$, the *bridge measure* $\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to w}$ is the conditional law of the path $(X_s)_{s\in[0,t]}$ given $X_t = w$, multiplied by the transition density: it has total mass $p^E(t,z,w)$. When $w = z$ it is a *loop measure* on paths starting and ending at $z$.
> **In words:** the bridge measure is the "how the process goes from $z$ to $w$ in time $t$" measure — a positive measure on path space concentrated on paths with the prescribed endpoints, whose total mass is exactly the transition density.
> **Concretely:** for Brownian motion on $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{W}^{t}_{0\to 0}$ (a Brownian bridge from $0$ to $0$ over time $t$) has total mass $p(t,0,0) = 1/\sqrt{2\pi t}$; sampled from this measure normalised to a probability, the path is the classical Brownian bridge on $[0,t]$. On the surface $X$, the loop measure restricted to loops in class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ has total mass $\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m))$ — the quantity Theorem 3.2 evaluates. Full detail: [[Def - Disintegration and the Bridge Measure]] and [[Def - Dirichlet Form Loop Measure]].

# Statement

> **Theorem (mass of a free homotopy class; Belyaev–Huseynli 3.2).** Let $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$ be a geometrically finite hyperbolic surface, $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ a primitive closed geodesic with hyperbolic representative $\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$, and $m\ge 1$. Let $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ be a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $L^2(\mathbb{H}^2, \rho_{\mathbb{H}^2})$ with $\Gamma$-invariant kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}$. Then the loop-measure mass of the free homotopy class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ equals
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) = \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}\big(t, z, \tau^m z\big)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z),$$
> where $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{z\in\mathbb{H}^2 : 1 \le \mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ is the fundamental strip of $\langle\tau\rangle$.

---

# In One Line

The whole apparatus of loop-classes-over-a-hyperbolic-surface collapses, for each topological class, into a single kernel value $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, \tau^m z)$ (upstairs heat kernel from a point to its $\tau^m$-image) integrated over one geodesic period — the identity that makes the mass of each topological type finite and computable.

---

# Why It's True

**Mechanism (one sentence).** *A downstairs loop of class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ lifts to an upstairs arc from $\tilde z$ to $\tau^m \tilde z$; summing the bridge masses of such arcs over all basepoints, and then unwrapping the coset $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ redundancy, leaves exactly one integrand over one $\langle\tau\rangle$-period, namely the strip.*

The starting observation is the **lifting picture**: every continuous downstairs loop $\omega$ rooted at $z\in X$ lifts uniquely to an $\mathbb{H}^2$-arc from a chosen $\tilde z \in \pi^{-1}(z)$ to some $h\tilde z$; the deck element $h\in\Gamma$ is the loop's monodromy and its conjugacy class is the free homotopy class of $\omega$. So the loop measure at $z$ decomposes as a sum over $h\in\Gamma$ of upstairs bridge measures from $\tilde z$ to $h\tilde z$, pushed down. Restricting to loops of type $C_X(\gamma^m)$ keeps exactly the $h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ — one term of the group-sum per group element in the conjugacy class.

The mass is then a triple integral: over time $t$ with the loop-measure weight $dt/t$, over basepoints $z\in X$ with the area measure $\rho_X$, and over the conjugacy class. Passing $\int_X\cdots d\rho_X$ upstairs to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$ (using the descent formula) and then enumerating the conjugacy class as one representative per coset $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ turns the double sum-and-integral into a *single* integrand $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ integrated over a union of translates of the fundamental region. These translates precisely fill a fundamental region for the smaller subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$; since the integrand is $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant, any such region gives the same integral, and the cleanest is the strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau$.

**Why the strip is the natural fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$.** $\tau : z\mapsto e^{\ell_\gamma}z$ rescales imaginary parts by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, so within each orbit $\{e^{k\ell_\gamma}z : k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ exactly one point has $\mathrm{Im}\,z\in[1,e^{\ell_\gamma})$: the strip. This is the geometric picture behind the algebraic step "one integrand per coset of $\langle\tau\rangle$ in $\Gamma$".

# Proof

> [!note]- Gap-free proof
> **Setup.** Fix the loop measure $\mu^E_X$ built from the $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet-form kernel $p^E_X(t,z,w) = \sum_{h\in\Gamma}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde w)$. Its bridge decomposition at a base point $z\in X$ is
> $$\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{z\to z, X} \;=\; \sum_{h\in\Gamma}\pi_*\,\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{\tilde z\to h\tilde z,\,\mathbb{H}^2}, \tag{$\ast$}$$
> where $\pi_*$ is the pushforward under $\pi$ and each summand on the right is the (positive) bridge measure of the upstairs process from $\tilde z$ to $h\tilde z$. (This is the standard universal-cover decomposition: every downstairs bridge is the disjoint union, over deck elements $h$, of the pushforwards of upstairs bridges to the various lifts of $z$; the total masses match by the periodisation identity for $p^E_X$.) Taking total masses of the two sides of ($\ast$), $|\mathbb{W}^{t,E}_{\tilde z\to h\tilde z}|_{\mathrm{tv}} = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde z)$.
>
> **Step 1 — isolate the conjugacy class.** A loop rooted at $z$ belongs to $C_X(\gamma^m)$ iff its monodromy $h$ lies in $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. So $\mu^E_X$ restricted to $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is obtained by keeping only those summands of ($\ast$) with $h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$. Taking total masses and integrating $z$ over $X$ with $\rho_X$,
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_X\sum_{h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde z)\,d\rho_X(z). \tag{16}$$
> (For a jump process this restriction is the *definition* of the class-mass; see [[Remark - Homotopy Classes for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1]]. The formula that follows depends only on the right-hand side of (16), so it holds in both cases.)
>
> **Step 2 — lift the base integral to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$.** Let $F\subset\mathbb{H}^2$ be a fundamental region for $\Gamma$ (i.e. $F$ meets each $\Gamma$-orbit in exactly one point, up to a $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$-null boundary). The map $\pi : F\to X$ is a $\rho$-measure-preserving bijection (almost everywhere), so for any $\Gamma$-invariant measurable $f : X\to[0,\infty]$ (which the integrand of (16) is, as a function of $z\in X$), $\int_X f\,d\rho_X = \int_F (f\circ\pi)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$. Applying this with $f(z) := \sum_{h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, h\tilde z)$ (its value at $z$ does not depend on the choice of lift $\tilde z$, since $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,q\tilde z, hq\tilde z) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tilde z, q^{-1}hq\tilde z)$ by $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel, and $q^{-1}hq$ ranges over $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ as $h$ does):
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_F \sum_{h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z, hz)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z). \tag{17}$$
>
> **Step 3 — unfold the conjugacy class over cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$.** The centraliser of $\tau^m$ in $\Gamma$ is exactly $\langle\tau\rangle$ (standard fact: any element commuting with a primitive hyperbolic $\tau$ shares its axis, and among torsion-free discrete groups this forces membership of $\langle\tau\rangle$). Therefore each element of the conjugacy class $[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}$ is written uniquely as $r\tau^m r^{-1}$ for a coset representative $r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$. Fix such a set of representatives and enumerate the sum:
> $$\sum_{h\in[\tau^m]_{\mathrm{conj}}}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z, hz) \;=\; \sum_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z, r\tau^m r^{-1}z). \tag{$\dagger$}$$
> For each coset $r$, apply $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel with $h := r^{-1}$ to both slots:
> $$p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z, r\tau^m r^{-1}z) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, r^{-1}z, r^{-1}\cdot r\tau^m r^{-1}z) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, r^{-1}z, \tau^m r^{-1}z).$$
> Substitute $w := r^{-1}z$ in the spatial integral over $F$; because $r^{-1}$ is a hyperbolic isometry, the Jacobian of the substitution is $1$ (isometries preserve $\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}$), and the region of integration becomes $r^{-1}F$:
> $$\int_F p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z, r\tau^m r^{-1}z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z) \;=\; \int_{r^{-1}F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(w).$$
> Sum over $r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$: the sets $\{r^{-1}F : r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle\}$ are pairwise disjoint (up to $\rho$-null boundaries) because the $\Gamma$-translates $\{qF\}_{q\in\Gamma}$ tile $\mathbb{H}^2$, and the map $r\mapsto r^{-1}F$ picks one translate per coset. So
> $$\sum_{r\in\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle}\int_{r^{-1}F}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,w,\tau^m w)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(w) \;=\; \int_{\bigcup_r r^{-1}F} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,w,\tau^m w)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(w). \tag{18}$$
>
> **Step 4 — replace the tiled union by the strip.** The cosets partition $\Gamma = \bigsqcup_r r\langle\tau\rangle$, and so the union $\bigcup_r r^{-1}F = \bigcup_r (r^{-1}F)$ contains, for each $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit in $\mathbb{H}^2$, exactly one representative from each $\Gamma$-orbit under the tiling; regrouping, it is a **fundamental region for the subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$**. (Reason: $F$ picks one representative per $\Gamma$-orbit; unioning $r^{-1}F$ over one representative $r$ from each coset $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ picks one representative per $\Gamma$-orbit *per coset*, which is one representative per $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit.)
>
> The integrand $w\mapsto p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,w,\tau^m w)$ is $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant: for any $k\in\mathbb{Z}$,
> $$p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tau^k w, \tau^m \tau^k w) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,\tau^k w, \tau^k \tau^m w) \;=\; p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, w, \tau^m w),$$
> using $\tau^k\tau^m = \tau^m\tau^k$ (abelian since inside $\langle\tau\rangle$) and $\Gamma$-invariance applied with $h := \tau^k$. A non-negative $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariant function has the *same* integral over every fundamental region of $\langle\tau\rangle$; the standard cleanest such region is the strip $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{z : 1\le\mathrm{Im}\,z < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$. Therefore
> $$\int_{\bigcup_r r^{-1}F}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,w,\tau^m w)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(w) \;=\; \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau}p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,w,\tau^m w)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(w). \tag{19}$$
>
> **Conclusion.** Chaining (17), ($\dagger$), (18), (19):
> $$\mu^E_X\big(C_X(\gamma^m)\big) \;=\; \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t,z,\tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z). \qquad\blacksquare$$

The proof uses $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel *twice* (once in Step 2 to check the integrand is well-defined downstairs; once in Step 3 to conjugate $r\tau^m r^{-1}$), one isometry substitution ($w = r^{-1}z$, Jacobian $1$), and one $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance argument (Step 4). The centraliser identification $Z_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ is what makes cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ enumerate the conjugacy class one-to-one; without it Step 3 would over-count.
