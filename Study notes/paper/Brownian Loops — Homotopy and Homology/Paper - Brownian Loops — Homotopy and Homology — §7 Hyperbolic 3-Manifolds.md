# §7 — Brownian loops on hyperbolic 3-manifolds

Back to the [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology|hub]]. The whole construction repeats one dimension up. §2's loop measure used $X$ only through its heat kernel, bridge measures, and the weights $dt/t$ and $\operatorname{vol}_g$ — all of which exist on any complete Riemannian manifold — and §3's decomposition used only descent-and-unfold over a cyclic centraliser. The single thing that tied the paper to surfaces was *conformal invariance* (needed for Polyakov and the length-spectrum identity in §3.4); with a killing rate or any non-linear subordination it plays no role, so nothing obstructs 3D. This section takes $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$ for a **Kleinian group** $\Gamma$ (the 3D analogue of a Fuchsian group) and re-derives the mass formulas. The one genuinely new feature is that a closed geodesic on a 3-manifold carries a **complex length** $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$: real part the translation, imaginary part the holonomy angle of a "screw-motion" isometry. And because the $\mathbb{H}^3$ heat kernel has explicit closed form (unlike the $\mathbb{H}^2$ one), the strip integral is *derived* here rather than cited from Wang–Xue.

## Prerequisites recap

A reader landing on this section cold — without having read §2–§6 — finds below every above-floor concept the section uses, either as a three-field `> [!recall]-` callout or as a wikilinked one-line reminder to a paper-item subpage from an earlier section. Everything used in §7 is here.

### From earlier sections (paper items)

- **Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$ and its kernel $p^E$ ([[Def - Dirichlet Form and its Operator and Semigroup]])** (§2) — a bilinear energy functional on functions in $L^2(X, \rho_X)$ that generates a symmetric Markov semigroup $P^E_t$ with integral kernel $p^E$. Its $\Gamma$-invariance is the key hypothesis of §7: $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, h z, h w) = p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$ for every $h \in \Gamma$.
- **Bernstein function $\phi$ and its subordinator ([[Def - Bernstein Function, Subordinator, and Subordination]])** (§2) — $\phi : (0, \infty) \to (0, \infty)$ concave, non-decreasing, with completely monotone derivative; the subordinator $(S_t)$ is a non-decreasing càdlàg process with $\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda S_t}] = e^{-t \phi(\lambda)}$. Concrete cases: $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda$ (Brownian), $\lambda + \kappa$ (killed), $\lambda^{\alpha/2}$ ($\alpha$-stable).
- **Subordinate Brownian loop measure $\mu^\phi_X$ ([[Def - Subordinate Brownian Loop Measure]])** (§2) — the loop measure of $Y = B \circ S$, with kernel $p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w) = \int p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(s, z, w)\,\psi^\phi_t(ds)$.
- **Weighted potential measure $V_\phi$ ([[Def - Weighted Potential Measure]])** (Def 2.9) — $V_\phi(ds) = \int_0^\infty \psi^\phi_t(ds)\,dt/t$, packaging all $\phi$-dependence in a single measure on $(0, \infty)$.
- **[[Lemma - Collapsing the Time Integral of the Subordinate Kernel|Lemma 2.11 (time-integral collapse)]]** — $\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int h(s)\,\psi^\phi_t(ds) = \int h(s)\,V_\phi(ds)$: the $t$-integral against $(t, s)$ with weight $dt/t$ reduces to a single integral against $V_\phi$.
- **Dirichlet-form loop measure $\mu^E_X$ ([[Def - Dirichlet Form Loop Measure]])** (§2) — the general loop measure of a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $X$; specialised to $\mu^\phi_X$ in the subordinate case.
- **Brownian loop measure $\mu_X$ ([[Def - Brownian Loop Measure]])** — the loop measure of ordinary Brownian motion, the case $\phi(\lambda) = \lambda$; the archetype whose 3-manifold class-masses give the $1/(m|e^{m L_\gamma} - 1|^2)$ formula.
- **Bridge measure $\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z\to w}$ ([[Def - Disintegration and the Bridge Measure]])** (§2) — conditional law of the process from $z$ to $w$ over $[0, t]$, with total mass $p^E(t, z, w)$; the loop measure at a base point is the total-mass of $\mathbb{W}^{t, E}_{z\to z}$ integrated against $dt/t$.
- **[[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2 (surface case)]]** — on a hyperbolic *surface* $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^2$, $\mu^E_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^2}(t, z, \tau^m z)\,d\rho_{\mathbb{H}^2}(z)$. §7.1's Theorem 7.1 is the verbatim 3D lift.
- **[[Lemma - Wang-Xue Strip Integral|Lemma 3.4 (Wang–Xue surface strip integral)]]** — the 2D closed form for the strip integral; §7.2 derives the 3D analogue instead of importing.
- **[[Remark - Homotopy Classes for Jump Processes|Remark 3.1 (homotopy classes for jump processes)]]** — for a subordinate jump process the class-mass is *defined* by isolating the conjugacy-class summand of the periodised kernel; carries over to 3D verbatim.

### Above-floor prerequisites — the §7.0 3D lifting picture

The four pieces of the covering-space / 3D-hyperbolic-geometry setup a reader needs before we can state Theorem 7.1. Each has a three-field recall — formal statement, plain-language paraphrase (no above-floor jargon), and a concrete example the reader can hold. **A floor reader can read the four recalls below and end with a picture of the setup, without opening any other file.**

> [!recall]- Hyperbolic 3-space $\mathbb{H}^3$ (upper half-space model)
> **Formally:** $\mathbb{H}^3 := \{(z, y) : z \in \mathbb{C},\, y > 0\}$ (the *upper half-space* — the complex plane times the positive reals) with Riemannian metric $ds^2 = (|dz|^2 + dy^2)/y^2$ and volume form $d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3} = y^{-3}\,dA(z)\,dy$ ($dA$ Euclidean 2-D area on $\mathbb{C}$); its isometry group is $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$. Hyperbolic distance satisfies $\cosh d((z_1, y_1), (z_2, y_2)) = 1 + \frac{|z_1 - z_2|^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}{2 y_1 y_2}$.
> **In words:** the 3-dimensional analogue of the hyperbolic upper half-plane you know from the surface theory. Points are labelled by a complex "horizontal" coordinate $z$ and a positive "height" $y$; the same set of numbers as ordinary 3D upper half-space, but with a rescaled ruler that shrinks Euclidean distances as height grows, giving constant negative curvature. Volumes shrink as $1/y^3$ high up. The isometry group is now the *complex* Möbius group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ (matrices with complex entries instead of real), which is bigger than the surface's $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$.
> **Concretely:** the vertical geodesic from $(0, 1)$ to $(0, 2)$ has hyperbolic length $\int_1^2 dy/y = \log 2$. A Euclidean-tiny 3D ball of side $\varepsilon$ centred at height $y$ has hyperbolic volume $\varepsilon^3/y^3$ — much smaller high up, exactly mirroring the 2D $\mathbb{H}^2$ picture. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic 3-Space, Kleinian Groups, and Complex Length]].

> [!recall]- Kleinian group $\Gamma$ and the hyperbolic 3-manifold $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$
> **Formally:** a **Kleinian group** $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is a discrete, torsion-free subgroup of the isometry group of $\mathbb{H}^3$; **geometrically finite** is a technical finiteness condition bounding $X$'s topological complexity. The action of $\Gamma$ on $\mathbb{H}^3$ has no fixed points and the quotient $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$ (points identified when a $\Gamma$-element moves one to the other) inherits a smooth hyperbolic metric of constant curvature $-1$ — a **hyperbolic 3-manifold**.
> **In words:** take a huge symmetric 3D wallpaper on $\mathbb{H}^3$; the group $\Gamma$ is the wallpaper's symmetry group; glue any two points that a wallpaper symmetry maps to each other; the result is a 3-manifold $X$ with the same local geometry as $\mathbb{H}^3$ but a non-trivial global topology (handles, cusps, knotted structure). This is the 3D analogue of the Fuchsian setup from §3.
> **Concretely:** the figure-eight knot complement $S^3 \setminus K_{8}$ is a finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold; its $\Gamma$ is generated by two elements of $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z}[\omega])$ ($\omega$ a primitive cube root of $1$), and its hyperbolic volume is $2 \cdot \mathrm{Cl}(\pi/3) \approx 2.03$ (twice Gieseking's constant). A Euclidean analogue: $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}^3$ acting on $\mathbb{R}^3$ by integer translations, quotient the flat 3-torus.

> [!recall]- Loxodromic isometry and complex length $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$
> **Formally:** an isometry $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is **loxodromic** if it is neither parabolic (one boundary fixed point) nor elliptic (finite order). It fixes exactly two boundary points, preserves the geodesic between them (its **axis**), and after conjugation in $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ takes the **standard form** $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z,\, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$ with $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$, $\ell_\gamma > 0$. Along its axis it translates by hyperbolic distance $\ell_\gamma$ (the **translation length**); off the axis it also rotates by angle $\theta_\gamma$ (the **holonomy angle**) about the axis. The **complex length** $L_\gamma$ packages the two real numbers into one. Non-trivial non-peripheral free homotopy classes on $X$ correspond to loxodromic conjugacy classes, each with a unique closed geodesic representative; centralisers are cyclic ($C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$) exactly as in 2D.
> **In words:** the 3D analogue of a 2D hyperbolic isometry. In 2D a hyperbolic $\tau$ only slid points along its axis; in 3D there is a whole extra dimension around the axis, and the isometry can rotate that dimension as it slides. Picture it as a screw motion: slide along a fixed line by $\ell_\gamma$ *and* twist around it by $\theta_\gamma$. The complex length $L_\gamma$ bundles slide and twist so that "$e^{L_\gamma}$" acts on the horizontal $\mathbb{C}$-plane at each height as multiplication by a complex number of modulus $e^{\ell_\gamma}$ and argument $\theta_\gamma$ — simultaneous scaling *and* rotation. When $\theta_\gamma = 0$ everything reduces to the surface case.
> **Concretely:** $\tau(z, y) = (2 i z, 2 y)$ has $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$ and $\theta_\gamma = \pi/2$: the point $(1, 1)$ maps to $(2 i, 2)$, then $(-4, 4)$, then $(-8 i, 8)$ — height doubling each step, horizontal coordinate rotating $90°$ per step while its modulus doubles. The point $(0, 1)$ on the axis just doubles in height: pure translation. A **hyperbolic** element is the special case $\theta_\gamma = 0$; a general loxodromic has nonzero twist. Full detail: [[Def - Hyperbolic 3-Space, Kleinian Groups, and Complex Length]].

> [!recall]- Fundamental slab $\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{(z, y) : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$
> **Formally:** for the loxodromic $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$, the cyclic subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle = \{\tau^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ acts on $\mathbb{H}^3$ with orbits $\{(e^{k L_\gamma} z, e^{k \ell_\gamma} y) : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Because $y$ scales by the *real* factor $e^{\ell_\gamma}$, each orbit meets $\mathcal{F}_\tau := \{(z, y) : 1 \le y < e^{\ell_\gamma}\}$ exactly once — the slab is a fundamental region for $\langle\tau\rangle$. (The horizontal rotation $e^{i\theta_\gamma}$ stays within a given horizontal layer and does not affect which slab one lands in.)
> **In words:** slice $\mathbb{H}^3$ into horizontal layers of height $y$. $\tau$ multiplies heights by $e^{\ell_\gamma}$ and separately rotates the horizontal $\mathbb{C}$-coordinate — the rotation is *intrinsic to each height*, and does not carry a point out of its height layer, so one full period of $\tau$ carries a layer at height $y$ to one at height $e^{\ell_\gamma} y$. The slab $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is one full period. Quotienting $\mathbb{H}^3$ by $\langle\tau\rangle$ glues the top face $y = e^{\ell_\gamma}$ to the bottom face $y = 1$ by the map $(z, e^{\ell_\gamma}) \mapsto (e^{-L_\gamma} z, 1)$, producing a "solid torus with a twist". This is the 3D analogue of the surface's fundamental strip.
> **Concretely:** with $\ell_\gamma = \log 2$, $\theta_\gamma = 0$, the slab is $\{(z, y) : 1 \le y < 2\}$ — an infinite horizontal 3D layer of hyperbolic thickness $\log 2$. The point $(1 + i, 1)$ lies in the slab; the point $(2 + 2i, 4)$ has representative $\tau^{-2}(2 + 2i, 4) = ((2+2i)/4, 1) = ((1+i)/2, 1)$ in the slab. With $\theta_\gamma = \pi/2$ the horizontal rotation shows up when identifying top and bottom: $(1 + i, 2) \sim (\tau^{-1}(1+i, 2))$, i.e. we identify $(1+i, 2)$ with $((1+i)/2 i, 1) = ((1-i)/2, 1)$.

> [!recall]- The $\mathbb{H}^3$ heat kernel $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w)$
> **Formally:** the fundamental solution of $\partial_t f = \frac12 \Delta_{\mathbb{H}^3} f$ (Brownian motion generator on $\mathbb{H}^3$) has the closed form $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, z, w) = \frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}\frac{u}{\sinh u}\,e^{-t - u^2/(4t)}$ where $u = d(z, w)$ is hyperbolic distance. Depends on $(z, w)$ only through $u$; the $e^{-t}$ factor is the spectral shift $e^{-((n-1)/2)^2 t}$ at $n = 3$ (bottom of the $L^2$ spectrum of $-\frac12\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^3}$ is $1$).
> **In words:** the probability density that a Brownian particle on $\mathbb{H}^3$ started at $z$ is near $w$ at time $t$. In flat 3D this would be the Gaussian $(4\pi t)^{-3/2}\,e^{-u^2/4t}$; on $\mathbb{H}^3$ two curvature corrections multiply it. The $u/\sinh u$ suppresses long paths — negative curvature "spreads paths thin". The $e^{-t}$ is uniform-in-space exponential decay — hyperbolic Brownian motion escapes to infinity almost surely. Crucially the kernel is **explicit** — unlike in 2D, where the analogous formula is an integral against a Selberg transform.
> **Concretely:** at $t = 1$, $u = 1$: $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(1) = (4\pi)^{-3/2}\,(1/\sinh 1)\,e^{-1 - 1/4} \approx 0.0141$, vs. the flat-space value $(4\pi)^{-3/2}\,e^{-1/4} \approx 0.0350$ — curvature suppresses by about $60\%$. At $u \to 0$ the corrections $u/\sinh u \to 1$ and $e^{-u^2/(4t)} \to 1$, leaving the on-diagonal value $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(1, z, z) = (4\pi)^{-3/2} e^{-1} \approx 0.0165$ — smaller than flat by exactly the spectral-shift factor $e^{-1}$.

**Symbols.** $\mathbb{H}^3$ hyperbolic 3-space; $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ Kleinian; primitive closed geodesic $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$ with **complex length** $L_\gamma = \ell_\gamma + i\theta_\gamma$ (translation $+ i$ holonomy); $\tau$ the loxodromic representative in standard form; $L := m L_\gamma$; $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ the fundamental slab.

**Descent of the heat kernel (the periodisation).** The downstairs kernel is the sum of the upstairs kernel over the group — the standard way a $\Gamma$-invariant object on $\mathbb{H}^3$ becomes one on $X$: $p^E_X(t, z, w) = \sum_{h \in \Gamma} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, \tilde z, h \tilde w)$, convergent because the geometrically-finite $\Gamma$'s orbit grows slower than the kernel decays. Restricting the sum to a conjugacy class picks out loops of the corresponding type.

## Concept map

Two sub-sections in the paper: §7.1 sets up the homotopy-class decomposition (a verbatim transfer of Theorem 3.2 to 3D); §7.2 evaluates the resulting slab integral in closed form using the explicit $\mathbb{H}^3$ heat kernel, then specialises to Brownian motion.

### §7.1 — The homotopy-class decomposition

- **[[Thm - Homotopy Decomposition for 3-Manifolds|Theorem 7.1 (homotopy-class decomposition, 3-manifolds)]]** — For a $\Gamma$-invariant Dirichlet form on $\mathbb{H}^3$ with kernel $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}$ and a primitive geodesic $\gamma$ with loxodromic representative $\tau(z, y) = (e^{L_\gamma} z, e^{\ell_\gamma} y)$, the loop-measure mass of $C_X(\gamma^m)$ is $\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{t} \int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w)$. **Mechanism:** isolate the conjugacy class in the group-sum kernel, lift the base integral to a fundamental region for $\Gamma$, unfold the conjugacy class over cosets $\Gamma/\langle\tau\rangle$ (each coset contributes one integrand $p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)$ over one $\Gamma$-translate of the fundamental region), reassemble the translates onto the slab. **Proof:** structurally identical to [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]] (isolate conjugacy class; unfold over cosets using $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel; reassemble on the slab using $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance of the integrand), now with the loxodromic standard form in place of the hyperbolic one — nothing in the surface argument used two-dimensionality.
  - Uses $\Gamma$-invariance of the kernel *twice* (well-definedness downstairs and conjugation of $r\tau^m r^{-1}$), one isometry substitution (Jacobian $1$ because loxodromics preserve $\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}$), $\langle\tau\rangle$-invariance of the integrand $w \mapsto p^E_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)$, and the centraliser identification $C_\Gamma(\tau^m) = \langle\tau\rangle$ (any element commuting with a primitive loxodromic preserves its axis, and the axis-preserving subgroup of a torsion-free discrete Kleinian group is cyclic).

### §7.2 — The subordinate mass and the closed-form Brownian mass

- **The $\mathbb{H}^3$ strip integral (unnumbered; derived in §7.2) ([[Lemma - Hyperbolic 3-Space Strip Integral]])** — For the Brownian heat kernel on $\mathbb{H}^3$ and $\tau^m(z, y) = (e^L z, e^{m\ell_\gamma} y)$, $\int_{\mathcal{F}_\tau} p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(t, w, \tau^m w)\,d\!\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{H}^3}(w) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2(\cosh(m\ell_\gamma) - \cos(m\theta_\gamma))}\cdot\frac{e^{-t - (m\ell_\gamma)^2/(4t)}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}$. **Mechanism:** in standard form the distance $u = d(w, \tau^m w)$ depends on the horizontal coordinate $z$ only through $|z| = r$, so polar coordinates give a $2\pi$ from the angular integral; the change of variables $r \to u$ produces a factor $\sinh u\,du$ that cancels the $1/\sinh u$ in $p_{\mathbb{H}^3}$; the resulting $\int_{m\ell_\gamma}^\infty u\,e^{-u^2/(4t)}\,du = 2t\,e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/(4t)}$ is elementary; the $y$-integral of $y^{-1}$ over the slab gives $\ell_\gamma$. **Not cited from Wang–Xue** — because the $\mathbb{H}^3$ heat kernel is explicit, the identity is derived in-house. The 3D analogue of [[Lemma - Wang-Xue Strip Integral|Lemma 3.4]].
  - The auxiliary identities are: $1 + \frac{(1 - e^a)^2}{2 e^a} = \cosh a$ (used in the distance formula) and $|e^{a + ib} - 1|^2 = 2 e^a(\cosh a - \cos b)$ (used to move between the compact form and the $\cosh - \cos$ form). Both are elementary.

- **[[Thm - Mass of Subordinate Loops on 3-Manifolds|Theorem 7.2 (subordinate mass, 3-manifolds)]]** — For a Bernstein $\phi$, $\gamma \in \mathcal{P}_X$, $m \ge 1$ (with $L = m L_\gamma = m\ell_\gamma + i m\theta_\gamma$), $\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = 2\pi\,\frac{e^{m\ell_\gamma}\ell_\gamma}{|e^L - 1|^2}\int_{(0, \infty)} \frac{2 s\,e^{-s}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}}\,e^{-(m\ell_\gamma)^2/(4s)}\,V_\phi(ds)$. **Mechanism:** Theorem 7.1 with $p^E := p^\phi_{\mathbb{H}^3}$; expand the subordinate kernel as an $s$-mixture $\int p_{\mathbb{H}^3}(s)\,\psi^\phi_t(ds)$; swap the $w$- and $s$-integrals (Tonelli, non-negative integrand); evaluate the spatial integral via the $\mathbb{H}^3$ strip integral; collapse the $t$-integral against $V_\phi$ via [[Lemma - Collapsing the Time Integral of the Subordinate Kernel|Lemma 2.11]]. Consequently every subordinate 3-manifold class-mass reduces to a single 1-D integral in the subordination variable.
  - The complex length enters through $|e^L - 1|^2 = 2 e^{m\ell_\gamma}(\cosh(m\ell_\gamma) - \cos(m\theta_\gamma))$; when $\theta_\gamma = 0$ (no twist) this is $(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1)^2$ — the *square* of the surface counterpart, the extra factor being the "cross-section" of the geodesic in the extra transverse dimension.

- **[[Cor - Brownian Mass on 3-Manifolds|Corollary 7.3 (Brownian mass, 3-manifolds)]]** — For pure Brownian motion, $\mu_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{1}{m}\frac{1}{|e^{m L_\gamma} - 1|^2} = \frac{e^{-m\ell_\gamma}}{2m(\cosh(m\ell_\gamma) - \cos(m\theta_\gamma))} = \frac{1}{m}\Big[(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1)^2 + 4 e^{m\ell_\gamma}\sin^2\frac{m\theta_\gamma}{2}\Big]^{-1}$. **Mechanism:** Theorem 7.2 with $V_\phi(ds) = ds/s$; the resulting integral $\int_0^\infty s^{-3/2}\,e^{-s - (m\ell_\gamma)^2/(4s)}\,ds$ evaluates by the standard identity $\int_0^\infty s^{-3/2}\,e^{-as - b/s}\,ds = \sqrt{\pi/b}\,e^{-2\sqrt{ab}}$; the constants align exactly so that $\frac{2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 2\sqrt{\pi}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}} = 1$. The three equivalent forms come from the identities $|e^{a+ib} - 1|^2 = 2e^a(\cosh a - \cos b)$ and $1 - \cos c = 2\sin^2(c/2)$.
  - Cross-check with the surface case: at $\theta_\gamma = 0$, the mass collapses to $\frac{1}{m(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1)^2}$ — the *square* of the surface answer $\frac{1}{m(e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1)}$. This closes §7: the same random-loop weights, now on a 3-manifold, packaged by the complex length.

### Beyond §7 (the paper's own pointer, not developed)

The paper notes that §4–§6's zeta/probability apparatus — the Selberg zeta identity, the killed-mass formula, the probability measure on classes, the homology-class distribution — carries over to 3-manifolds with $\ell_\gamma \to L_\gamma$ and $\sinh^2 \to |e^L - 1|^2$ throughout, though it develops that extension only for surfaces. This is stated as a pointer, not a theorem, and is flagged as intuition-not-yet-formalised in the verification log below.

## Section verification log (§7)

**Verified.** The $\mathbb{H}^3$ strip integral (eqs. 88–89 of the paper) is derived in full from the explicit heat kernel in the dedicated atomic [[Lemma - Hyperbolic 3-Space Strip Integral|Lemma]]: the change of variables $r \to u$, the cancellation of $\sinh u$, the Gaussian $\int_a^\infty u\,e^{-u^2/(4t)}\,du = 2t\,e^{-a^2/(4t)}$, and the constant bookkeeping $2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 2\sqrt{\pi} = (4\pi)^{3/2}$ are all explicit. Theorem 7.1 is proved gap-free as a verbatim 3D transfer of the [[Thm - Mass of a Free Homotopy Class|Theorem 3.2]] proof (steps 1–4 restated with $\mathbb{H}^3$, $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{C})$, loxodromic, slab). Theorem 7.2 follows from Theorem 7.1 by expanding the subordinate kernel and applying the strip integral and Lemma 2.11. Corollary 7.3 follows by specialisation with $V_\phi(ds) = ds/s$ and the Gaussian-type integral; all three equivalent forms of the answer are checked, and the $\theta_\gamma = 0$ limit matches the square of the surface answer.

**Flagged / uncertain.** None outstanding for §7. The two auxiliary identities the strip-integral derivation rests on ($1 + \frac{(1 - e^a)^2}{2 e^a} = \cosh a$ and $|e^{a + ib} - 1|^2 = 2 e^a(\cosh a - \cos b)$) are verified by direct computation in the [[Lemma - Hyperbolic 3-Space Strip Integral|Lemma]]'s proof.

**Intuition not yet formalised.** The paper's remark that §4–§6's zeta/probability apparatus carries over to 3-manifolds with $\ell_\gamma \to L_\gamma$ and $\sinh^2 \to |e^L - 1|^2$ is stated as a pointer but not developed — flagged as intuition, not a proved extension.
