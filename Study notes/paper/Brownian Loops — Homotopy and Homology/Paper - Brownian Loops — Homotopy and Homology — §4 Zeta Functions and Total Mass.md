# §4 — Zeta functions and the total mass of Brownian loops

Back to the [[Paper - Brownian Loops — Homotopy and Homology|hub]]. §3 gave each free homotopy class a finite, closed-form mass; this section **sums those masses over all non-trivial non-peripheral classes** (equivalently: over all free homotopy classes that genuinely wrap at least one hole — throwing away both the contractible class and any "peripheral" class that just circles a puncture / cusp, i.e. a puncture-shaped end of the surface whose encircling loops shrink to zero length) and recognises the sum as a value of a classical spectral object — the **Selberg zeta function**. That identification is the paper's bridge from probability to spectral geometry, and it comes with a companion (the **twisted Ruelle zeta**, which §6 will specialise to characters for homology) and a sharp convergence criterion (the total mass is finite exactly when the loops' decay rate beats the geodesics' proliferation rate). Throughout, *total mass* means the sum over non-trivial non-peripheral classes only — the trivial (contractible) class contributes an infinite mass on any surface (there are arbitrarily short contractible loops of every length) and is treated in §5.

**Section variables.** The zeta variable is $s \in \mathbb C$ (distinct from the §2 subordination internal-clock variable, also traditionally called $s$); $\delta \in (0, 1]$ is the critical exponent; $Z_X$ and $R_X$ are the Selberg and Ruelle zeta functions; $I_\phi$ is the [[Def - The Loop-Length Integral|loop-length integral]] of §3; $L = m\ell_\gamma$ is the total translation length.

## Prerequisites recap

Every above-floor concept §4 uses appears here — as a three-field `> [!recall]-` callout, or as a "from earlier sections" reminder of a paper item developed elsewhere. A reader landing on this section without having read §2 or §3 finds every prerequisite here.

> [!recall]- The hyperbolic surface X = Γ\ℍ²
> **Formally:** $\mathbb H^2 = \{x + iy \in \mathbb C : y > 0\}$ with metric $ds^2 = (dx^2 + dy^2)/y^2$; isometry group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb R)$ acting by Möbius transformations. A **Fuchsian group** $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb R)$ is discrete and torsion-free; $X = \Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^2$ (glue $z$ to $hz$ for every $h \in \Gamma$) is a smooth hyperbolic surface. Geometrically finite: a technical bound on complexity (finitely many geometric ends).
> **In words:** the upper half-plane with a curved ruler, quotiented by a discrete group of rigid motions with no fixed points, giving a curved surface with a nontrivial global shape (handles, holes, cusps — puncture-shaped ends of infinite length but finite area) but the same local geometry as $\mathbb H^2$.
> **Concretely:** the Euclidean analogue is the flat torus $T^2 = \mathbb R^2 / \mathbb Z^2$ (take $\Gamma = \mathbb Z^2$). Hyperbolic examples: a compact genus-2 surface (a two-holed pretzel; finite area, $\delta = 1$); a "3-funnel sphere" (a sphere with three infinite-area trumpets; infinite area, $\delta \in (0, 1)$ tunable). Full detail: [[Def - Fuchsian Group and the Hyperbolic Quotient Surface]].

> [!recall]- Primitive closed geodesic γ and length ℓ_γ; the winding class C_X(γ^m)
> **Formally:** an element $\tau \in \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb R)$ is **hyperbolic** if it is conjugate to $\tau_0 : z \mapsto e^\ell z$ for some $\ell > 0$; the number $\ell$ is $\tau$'s **translation length** — the hyperbolic distance $\tau$ slides points along its **axis**. $\tau \in \Gamma$ is **primitive** if $\tau \ne \sigma^k$ for any $\sigma \in \Gamma$ and $k \ge 2$. Every non-trivial non-peripheral free homotopy class on $X$ contains a unique closed geodesic $\gamma$ of length $\ell_\gamma$ = the translation length of a primitive hyperbolic $\tau$ representing it; $\tau^m$ represents the class $C_X(\gamma^m)$ of $\gamma$ traversed $m$ times. Set $L := m\ell_\gamma$ throughout.
> **In words:** the hyperbolic isometries are "translations along a specific line" — pick a geodesic, slide by a fixed distance $\ell$. On the surface, every genuinely-wrapping-a-hole loop is homotopic to a unique shortest closed geodesic of a definite length $\ell_\gamma$; iterating gives longer loops of length $m\ell_\gamma$.
> **Concretely:** on $T^2 = \mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ with $\Gamma = \mathbb Z^2$: free homotopy classes are in bijection with $\mathbb Z^2$ itself; the primitive class $(1, 0)$ (once around horizontally) has "geodesic length" $1$; the winding-$m$ class $(m, 0)$ has $L = m$. In the hyperbolic setting, non-abelian $\Gamma$ gives a genuine class of many group elements per topological type. Full detail: [[Def - Closed Geodesics, Conjugacy Classes, and Translation Length]].

> [!recall]- Critical exponent δ (the proliferation rate)
> **Formally:** $\delta := \inf\{s > 0 : \sum_{h \in \Gamma} e^{-s\,d(z, hz)} < \infty\}$, independent of $z \in \mathbb H^2$ (Poincaré-series exponent of convergence). Equivalent descriptions: the Hausdorff dimension — a fractal-size measurement — of the limit set $\Lambda(\Gamma) \subset \partial\mathbb H^2$ (the set of boundary limits of the $\Gamma$-orbit); the topological entropy — a growth-rate — of the geodesic flow (the flow that sends a point along its geodesic). Finite area $\Rightarrow \delta = 1$; infinite area $\Rightarrow \delta < 1$.
> **In words:** a single number that measures how fast the group orbit $\Gamma z$ spreads out on the boundary — equivalently (by the prime geodesic theorem below), how fast closed geodesics of length $\le R$ multiply as $R$ grows.
> **Concretely:** on a compact genus-$g$ surface ($g \ge 2$), $\delta = 1$. On a 3-funnel sphere with wide funnels, $\delta$ can be close to $1$; with thin funnels, close to $0$ — it is tunable in $(0, 1)$ by the funnel geometry. This is the single parameter that decides finiteness of the total loop mass. Full detail: [[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent]].

> [!recall]- Selberg zeta function Z_X(s) and its log-expansion
> **Formally:** for $\operatorname{Re} s > \delta$, $Z_X(s) := \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\prod_{k \ge 0}(1 - e^{-(s + k)\ell_\gamma})$; log-expansion $-\log Z_X(s) = \sum_\gamma\sum_{m \ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\frac{e^{(1 - s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$, obtained by applying $-\log(1 - x) = \sum_m x^m/m$ to each factor and doing the geometric $k$-sum.
> **In words:** an infinite product with one factor per closed geodesic $\gamma$ and per non-negative integer $k$, of the simple shape $1 - e^{-(s + k)\ell_\gamma}$. It is the generating function of the length spectrum $\{\ell_\gamma\}$, analogous to how the Riemann zeta $\prod_p(1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$ generates the primes.
> **Concretely:** for a single-generator toy $\Gamma = \langle\tau_0 : z \mapsto e^\ell z\rangle$ (infinite cylinder with one geodesic of length $\ell$), $Z_X(s) = \prod_{k \ge 0}(1 - e^{-(s+k)\ell})$; at $s = 1$, $\ell = 1$: $Z_X(1) = (1 - e^{-1})(1 - e^{-2})\cdots \approx 0.521$. The log-expansion at $s = 1$: $-\log Z_X(1) = \sum_m \frac{1}{m(e^m - 1)} \approx 0.652$. Full detail: [[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent]].

> [!recall]- Ruelle zeta R_X(s) and the twisted version R_X(s, ρ)
> **Formally:** for $\operatorname{Re} s > \delta$, the **Ruelle zeta** is $R_X(s) := \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}(1 - e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$; related to Selberg by $R_X(s) = Z_X(s)/Z_X(s + 1)$ and $Z_X(s) = \prod_{k \ge 0}R_X(s + k)$. Given a finite-dimensional complex representation $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}(V_\rho)$ with $\tau$ representing $\gamma$, the **twisted Ruelle zeta** is $R_X(s, \rho) := \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\det(I - \rho(\tau)e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$; converges for $\operatorname{Re} s > c_\rho$, and $c_\rho = \delta$ when $\rho$ is unitary.
> **In words:** the Ruelle zeta is simpler than Selberg — a single product over geodesics, no $\prod_k$. In the twisted version, each scalar factor $1 - e^{-s\ell_\gamma}$ is replaced by a matrix determinant $\det(I - \rho(\tau)e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$ — the matrix records how the representation "sees" the geodesic. Twisting is a device to *sort* loops by a group-theoretic label; §6 uses this with characters to project onto individual homology classes.
> **Concretely:** the trivial representation ($\rho(\tau) = 1$ for all $\tau$; $V_\rho = \mathbb C$) recovers the untwisted case $R_X(s, \rho) = R_X(s)$. On $T^2$ with a character $\chi_{(u,v)}(a, b) = e^{2\pi i(au + bv)}$, $R_X(s, \chi_{(u,v)}) = \prod_\gamma(1 - e^{2\pi i(a_\gamma u + b_\gamma v)}e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$: each geodesic weighted by a phase encoding its winding numbers. Integrating $(u, v)$ over the torus extracts the mass of a specific homology class. Full detail: [[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent]].

> [!recall]- Prime geodesic theorem: N_X(R) ∼ e^{δR}/(δR)
> **Formally:** the counting function $N_X(R) := \#\{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X : \ell_\gamma \le R\}$ satisfies $N_X(R) \sim e^{\delta R}/(\delta R)$ as $R \to \infty$.
> **In words:** the number of primitive closed geodesics of length up to $R$ grows exponentially at exact rate $\delta$, with a $1/R$ correction — the same shape as the prime number theorem $\pi(x) \sim x/\log x$, with length replacing $\log x$ and $\delta$ setting the rate.
> **Concretely:** on a finite-area surface ($\delta = 1$): $N_X(10) \approx e^{10}/10 \approx 2200$; $N_X(20) \approx e^{20}/20 \approx 2.4 \times 10^7$. On an infinite-area surface with $\delta = 1/2$: $N_X(10) \approx 2e^5/10 \approx 30$. Geodesics multiply exponentially fast, and this is what makes the finiteness of the total loop mass a race between $s$ and $\delta$. Full detail: [[Thm - Prime Geodesic Theorem]].

> [!recall]- Loop-length integral I_φ(L) and the class-mass factorisation (Theorem 3.5 recall)
> **Formally:** for a Bernstein function $\phi$ with weighted potential measure $V_\phi$ on $(0, \infty)$, and $L > 0$, $I_\phi(L) := \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-s/4}\,e^{-L^2/(4s)}}{2\sqrt{\pi s}}\,V_\phi(ds)$ ($s$ here is the *subordination* variable, not the zeta variable). Theorem 3.5 states $\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = \frac{\ell_\gamma}{2\sinh(L/2)}I_\phi(L)$ for $L = m\ell_\gamma$.
> **In words:** all the process-dependent content of the class-mass is packed into one 1-D integral in a dummy internal-clock variable; the class-mass then splits as a geometric prefactor $\ell_\gamma/[2\sinh(L/2)]$ times this integral. The four Bernstein functions the paper uses each have a closed form for $I_\phi(L)$.
> **Concretely:** four cases — Brownian ($\phi = \lambda$, $I_\phi = e^{-L/2}/L$), killed ($\phi = \lambda + \kappa$, $I_\kappa = e^{-L\sqrt{1/4 + \kappa}}/L$), $\alpha$-stable ($\phi = \lambda^{\alpha/2}$, $I_\alpha = (\alpha/2)e^{-L/2}/L$), shifted stable ($\phi = (\lambda + \kappa)^{\alpha/2}$, $I_\phi = (\alpha/2)e^{-L\sqrt{1/4 + \kappa}}/L$). Full detail: [[Def - The Loop-Length Integral]] and [[Ex - The Four Bernstein Functions of the Paper]].

### §4.1 — Selberg zeta identities

- **[[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent|Definition 4.1 — Selberg zeta function Z_X(s)]]** — Introduces $Z_X(s) := \prod_{\gamma \in \mathcal P_X}\prod_{k \ge 0}(1 - e^{-(s + k)\ell_\gamma})$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \delta$, the generating function of the length spectrum. Its log-expansion $-\log Z_X(s) = \sum_{\gamma}\sum_{m \ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$ is what §4.1 will recognise inside a random-loop sum. Convergence for $\operatorname{Re} s > \delta$ is the whole point of the critical exponent. Used throughout §4 (as the target of the Selberg zeta criterion, the value of the killed loop mass), in §5 (renormalisation → zeta-regularised determinant of $\Delta_X$), and in §6 (character-twisted analogue for homology).
    - The critical exponent $\delta$ is defined on the same page (Poincaré-series exponent of convergence; equivalently, Hausdorff dimension of the limit set; equivalently, geodesic-flow entropy). Finite area $\Rightarrow \delta = 1$; infinite area $\Rightarrow \delta < 1$.

- **[[Lemma - Selberg Zeta Criterion|Lemma 4.2 — Selberg zeta criterion]]** — A shape-matching lemma: if $\frac{L}{2\sinh(L/2)}I_\phi(L) = C \cdot \frac{e^{(1 - s)L}}{e^L - 1}$ for constants $C > 0$ and $s > \delta$, then $\sum_{\gamma, m}\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = -C\log Z_X(s)$. The proof is a three-step calculation: rewrite each class-mass via Theorem 3.5, substitute the hypothesised shape to get a $\frac{C}{m}\cdot\frac{e^{(1-s)m\ell_\gamma}}{e^{m\ell_\gamma} - 1}$ summand, and recognise the log-expansion of $Z_X$. This is the engine of §4.1: every zeta identity the paper proves factors through it.

- **[[Thm - Selberg Zeta Identity for the Total Loop Mass|Corollary 4.3 — Selberg zeta identity for the killed total loop mass]]** — For $\kappa \ge -\frac14$ with $s(\kappa) := \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa} > \delta$, $\sum_{\gamma, m}\mu^\kappa_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) = -\log Z_X(s(\kappa))$; in particular at $\kappa = 0$ (plain Brownian, $s = 1$) the total mass is $-\log Z_X(1)$. Immediate from the Selberg zeta criterion once one checks (a two-line manipulation) that the killed $I_\kappa(L) = e^{-L\sqrt{1/4 + \kappa}}/L$ turns into the canonical shape with $C = 1$ and $s = s(\kappa)$. On a finite-area surface, $\delta = 1$: at $\kappa = 0$ the sum diverges (needs $\kappa > 0$ to reach $s > 1$); on an infinite-area surface, $-\log Z_X(1)$ is a finite number.

- **[[Remark - Bosonic Partition Function Interpretation|Remark 4.4 — bosonic partition function interpretation]]** — Reads Corollary 4.3 in the vocabulary of statistical mechanics: each factor $(1 - e^{-(s + k)\ell_\gamma})^{-1}$ has the shape of a free-boson mode partition function with energy $(s + k)\ell_\gamma$; the product $\mathcal Z(s) := \prod_\gamma\prod_k(1 - e^{-(s+k)\ell_\gamma})^{-1} = Z_X(s)^{-1}$ is the grand canonical partition function of a free Bose gas with one boson tower per geodesic; and the total loop mass equals $\log \mathcal Z(s)$. ⚠️ Physical interpretation only; the mathematical content is Corollary 4.3.

- **[[Def - Selberg and Ruelle Zeta Functions and the Critical Exponent|Definition 4.5 — Ruelle zeta and twisted Ruelle zeta]]** — Also lives on the Selberg/Ruelle zeta atomic page (the paper treats Definitions 4.1 and 4.5 together). $R_X(s) := \prod_\gamma(1 - e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$ is the untwisted Ruelle zeta; for a representation $\rho : \Gamma \to \mathrm{GL}(V_\rho)$ (not necessarily unitary), the twisted Ruelle zeta is $R_X(s, \rho) := \prod_\gamma\det(I - \rho(\tau)e^{-s\ell_\gamma})$, a product of matrix determinants — one $\dim V_\rho \times \dim V_\rho$ block per geodesic. The trivial representation recovers $R_X(s)$; characters $\chi : \Gamma \to S^1$ give the version §6 will use for homology.

- **[[Thm - Twisted Ruelle Zeta Identity|Corollary 4.6 — twisted Ruelle zeta identity]]** — With $\kappa_\pm(s) := s(s \pm 1)$ (chosen so $\sigma(\kappa_-(s)) = s$ and $\sigma(\kappa_+(s)) = s + 1$), for $\operatorname{Re} s > \max(c_\rho, \frac12)$, $-\log R_X(s, \rho) = \sum_{\gamma, m}\operatorname{tr}\rho(\tau^m)\big[\mu^{\kappa_-(s)}_X - \mu^{\kappa_+(s)}_X\big](C_X(\gamma^m)) = \sum_{\gamma, m}\operatorname{tr}\rho(\tau^m)\cdot e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}/m$. The proof runs on two independent expansions: (i) the determinant expansion $-\log\det(I - M) = \sum_m \operatorname{tr}(M^m)/m$ turns the twisted product into a trace-weighted geodesic sum; (ii) the difference of killed class-masses at $\kappa_-$ and $\kappa_+$ **telescopes** algebraically to $e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}/m$ (using $e^{(1-s)L} - e^{-sL} = e^{-sL}(e^L - 1)$, which cancels the $1/(e^L - 1)$ denominator). This is the engine of the §6 homology decomposition — the clean $e^{-sm\ell_\gamma}/m$ shape is what Fourier analysis on the character group can handle.

### §4.2 — Finiteness of the total mass

- **[[Thm - Finiteness of the Total Loop Mass|Corollary 4.7 — finiteness of the total loop mass]]** — For any of the paper's four Bernstein functions, if the spectral parameter $s(\phi)$ ($= 1$ for Brownian/$\alpha$-stable; $= \frac12 + \sqrt{\frac14 + \kappa}$ for killed/shifted stable) exceeds $\delta$, then $\sum_{\gamma, m}\mu^\phi_X(C_X(\gamma^m)) < \infty$; and the threshold is sharp ($Z_X(s) \to 0$ as $s \downarrow \delta$). The proof is a **race** between two exponentials: the class-mass carries $e^{-s\ell_\gamma}$ per geodesic; the prime geodesic theorem says the geodesic counting function grows like $e^{\delta R}/R$; the sum $\sum_\gamma e^{-s\ell_\gamma}$ converges iff $s > \delta$. Bounding the winding sum $\sum_m$ against the systole reduces the double sum to this single-geodesic sum; integration by parts against $N_X(R)$ finishes it.
    - On a finite-area surface ($\delta = 1$), plain Brownian ($s = 1$) sits at the threshold and diverges; a strictly positive killing rate $\kappa > 0$ pushes $s > 1$ and gives finiteness. This is the divergence §5 renormalises: the contractible class is the culprit, and subtracting it off gives a finite zeta-regularised determinant of $\Delta_X$.
