## §1.2 Normal Subgroups and Isomorphism Theorems — Exercises

- [[Ex - Index-two subgroups are normal]] — prove normality cheaply by enumerating cosets: a subgroup of index two has exactly two left cosets and two right cosets, both partitions being $\{H,\ G\setminus H\}$, so $gH = Hg$ for all $g$ ([[Def - Subgroup]], [[Def - Coset]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Def - Order of a Group and of an Element]])
- [[Ex - Identifying a quotient with the first isomorphism theorem]] — the master method for naming a quotient: build a surjection out of $G$ with kernel exactly $N$, here $\det : \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}) \to \mathbb{F}^\times$, to get $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})/\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{F}) \cong \mathbb{F}^\times$ ([[Def - Homomorphism]], [[Def - Kernel and Image]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Def - Quotient Group]], [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Homomorphisms out of a simple group]] — turn a normal-subgroup hypothesis into a statement about maps via the kernel: $\ker\varphi \trianglelefteq G$ and simplicity force it to be $\{e\}$ or $G$, so $\varphi$ is injective or trivial ([[Def - Homomorphism]], [[Def - Kernel and Image]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Def - Simple Group]], [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem]])
- [[Ex - The product HK and the second isomorphism theorem]] — derive the second isomorphism theorem and the order formula $|HN| = |H||N|/|H\cap N|$ by restricting the quotient map $\pi : G \to G/N$ to $H$ and applying the first isomorphism theorem ([[Def - Subgroup]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Def - Quotient Group]], [[Def - Homomorphism]], [[Def - Kernel and Image]], [[Def - Coset]], [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem]], [[Thm - Second Isomorphism Theorem]], [[Thm - Lagrange's Theorem]])
