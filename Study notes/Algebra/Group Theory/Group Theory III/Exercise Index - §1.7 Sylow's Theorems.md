## §1.7 Sylow's Theorems — Exercises

The exercises of §1.7 drill the central application of [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems|Sylow's theorems]]: deciding, from nothing but the integer $|G|$, whether a group of that order can be [[Def - Simple Group|simple]]. The standard playbook runs three tactics in order — force a unique Sylow subgroup and invoke [[Thm - A Unique Sylow Subgroup is Normal|its normality]]; count elements of prime order; act by conjugation on the set of Sylow subgroups — and these problems exercise each tactic and their combination. Each entry below names the exercise, summarizes the technique it practices in one line, and lists in parentheses every definition and theorem invoked in that exercise's solution — the per-exercise prerequisites, the precise set of concepts that exercise rehearses.

- [[Ex - Counting elements of prime order with Sylow subgroups]] — the infrastructural lemma: a prime dividing $|G|$ to the first power has Sylow subgroups of prime order, which intersect trivially, so $G$ has exactly $n_p(p-1)$ elements of order $p$ — the engine of every element-counting non-simplicity proof ([[Def - Sylow p-Subgroup]], [[Def - p-group]], [[Def - Order of a Group and of an Element]], [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems]], [[Thm - Lagrange's Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Groups of order pq are not simple]] — the cheapest non-simplicity argument: for $|G| = pq$ with $p < q$, the Sylow constraints on the *larger* prime $q$ admit only $n_q = 1$, so the Sylow $q$-subgroup is normal ([[Def - Sylow p-Subgroup]], [[Def - Simple Group]], [[Def - Normal Subgroup]], [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems]], [[Thm - A Unique Sylow Subgroup is Normal]])
- [[Ex - No group of order 30 is simple]] — the overflow form of element-counting: for the square-free order $30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, assuming simplicity pins $n_5 = 6$ and $n_3 = 10$, and the $24 + 20 = 44$ elements of orders $5$ and $3$ exceed $|G|$ ([[Def - Sylow p-Subgroup]], [[Def - Simple Group]], [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems]], [[Thm - A Unique Sylow Subgroup is Normal]], [[Ex - Counting elements of prime order with Sylow subgroups]])
- [[Ex - No group of order 56 is simple]] — the leftover-room form of element-counting: for $56 = 2^3 \cdot 7$, the case $n_7 = 8$ leaves exactly $8$ non-order-$7$ elements, which must be the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup, forcing $n_2 = 1$ ([[Def - Sylow p-Subgroup]], [[Def - Simple Group]], [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems]], [[Thm - A Unique Sylow Subgroup is Normal]], [[Ex - Counting elements of prime order with Sylow subgroups]])
- [[Ex - No group of order 132 is simple]] — all three tactics chained: for $132 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 11$, uniqueness pins $n_{11} = 12$, the conjugation action on the $4$ Sylow $3$-subgroups embeds a simple $G$ into $S_4$ to kill $n_3 = 4$, and element-counting then overflows $|G|$ with $120 + 44$ elements ([[Def - Sylow p-Subgroup]], [[Def - Simple Group]], [[Def - Normaliser]], [[Thm - Sylow's Theorems]], [[Thm - A Unique Sylow Subgroup is Normal]], [[Thm - Coset Action and the Normal Core]], [[Thm - First Isomorphism Theorem]], [[Ex - Counting elements of prime order with Sylow subgroups]])
