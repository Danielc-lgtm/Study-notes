## §3.1 Modules, Submodules and Quotients — Exercises

The exercises of §3.1 drill the foundational module theory. The identification "module over $\mathbb{Z}$ = abelian group" calibrates the intuition; annihilator computations turn an abstract submodule condition into a concrete arithmetic divisibility; the cyclic submodule $Rm$ is identified with the quotient $R/\operatorname{Ann}(m)$ by the first isomorphism theorem; and the polynomial counterexample shows finitely generated modules can have non-finitely-generated submodules — the failure of Noetherianity over a non-Noetherian base.

- [[Ex - A module over the integers is an abelian group]] (⭐) — use the unital axiom plus scalar-distributivity to force the $\mathbb{Z}$-action to be repeated addition, prove every abelian group carries this action uniquely, and deduce $\mathbb{Z}$-module homomorphisms are exactly group homomorphisms — an isomorphism of categories ([[Def - Module]], [[Def - Module Homomorphism]], [[Def - Abelian Group]])
- [[Ex - Computing annihilators]] (⭐) — unwind the condition $k\cdot\bar a=\bar 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ into the divisibility $n\mid ka$, divide out the gcd to expose a coprime divisibility, and read off the principal annihilator $\operatorname{Ann}(\bar a)=(n/\gcd(a,n))$ — the order of $\bar a$ ([[Def - Module]], [[Def - Annihilator]], [[Def - Ideal]])
- [[Ex - The submodule generated by an element]] (⭐⭐) — apply the first isomorphism theorem to the orbit map $r\mapsto rm$ to identify $Rm\cong R/\operatorname{Ann}(m)$, then deduce that a simple module over a commutative ring is $R/\mathfrak{m}$ for a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ via the submodule correspondence ([[Def - Module]], [[Def - Submodule]], [[Def - Quotient Module]], [[Def - Module Homomorphism]], [[Def - Annihilator]], [[Thm - Isomorphism Theorems for Modules]], [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal]])
- [[Ex - A submodule of a finitely generated module need not be finitely generated]] (⭐⭐⭐) — exhibit the cyclic module $R=\mathbb{C}[X_1,X_2,\dots]$ whose submodule $(X_1,X_2,\dots)$ resists every finite generating set, proven by bounding the variables of a putative generating set and projecting an identity onto its degree-$1$ homogeneous component ([[Def - Module]], [[Def - Submodule]], [[Def - Finitely Generated Module]], [[Def - Ideal]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]])
