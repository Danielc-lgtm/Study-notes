## §3.4 Normal Forms for Matrices — Exercises

The exercises of §3.4 drill the application of the structure theorem to linear operators. The $\mathbb{F}[X]$-module of a linear operator $A$ on $V$ is the cokernel of $XI - A: V[X] \to V[X]$; the rational canonical form is the Smith normal form of this module presentation; the Jordan normal form is the corresponding primary decomposition over an algebraically closed field. Each exercise practices computing one of these forms, identifying the minimal and characteristic polynomials as consequences, and confirming similarity is precisely $\mathbb{F}[X]$-module isomorphism.

- [[Ex - Computing the rational canonical form]] (⭐⭐) — given a concrete $3\times 3$ matrix over $\mathbb{Q}$, compute its rational canonical form by reducing the characteristic matrix $XI-A$ to Smith normal form over $\mathbb{Q}[X]$ (pivot on constant entries, use division with remainder, apply the gcd-lcm correction), read the invariant factors $f_1\mid\cdots\mid f_s$ off the diagonal, assemble the companion-matrix blocks $c(f_i)$, and extract the minimal polynomial $f_s$ and characteristic polynomial $\prod f_i$ with a $\det(XI-A)$ cross-check ([[Def - The Module of a Linear Operator]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Euclidean Domain]], [[Thm - Smith Normal Form]], [[Thm - Structure Theorem for Finitely Generated Modules over a Euclidean Domain]], [[Thm - Rational Canonical Form]]).

- [[Ex - Computing the Jordan normal form]] (⭐⭐) — compute the Jordan normal form of a concrete matrix over $\mathbb{C}$ two ways: factor the characteristic polynomial into linear powers, then find the elementary divisors $(X-\lambda)^m$ either by splitting the Smith normal form invariant factors of $XI-A$ into prime powers or by computing generalised-eigenspace dimensions $\dim\ker(A-\lambda I)^k$, confirm the routes agree, assemble the Jordan blocks $J_m(\lambda)$, and read off the minimal polynomial — illustrating that the Jordan form is the primary decomposition of $V_A$ over the algebraically closed field $\mathbb{C}$ ([[Def - The Module of a Linear Operator]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Thm - Smith Normal Form]], [[Thm - Primary Decomposition Theorem]], [[Thm - Rational Canonical Form]], [[Thm - Jordan Normal Form]]).

- [[Ex - Possible Jordan forms from the minimal and characteristic polynomials]] (⭐⭐) — given characteristic polynomial $(X-2)^3(X-5)^2$ and minimal polynomial $(X-2)^2(X-5)$, enumerate every consistent Jordan normal form by translating each eigenvalue's polynomial exponents into a block-size partition (the characteristic polynomial fixes the sum, the minimal polynomial fixes the largest part), then show $(\chi_\alpha,m_\alpha)$ is an incomplete similarity invariant — sum and maximum fail to determine a partition once the multiplicity is large enough, the missing datum being the per-eigenvalue block count $\dim\ker(\alpha-\lambda I)$ ([[Def - The Module of a Linear Operator]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Thm - Primary Decomposition Theorem]], [[Thm - Jordan Normal Form]]).

- [[Ex - Matrix similarity via the structure theorem]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove that two $n\times n$ matrices $A,B$ over a field $F$ are similar if and only if the $F[X]$-modules $V_A,V_B$ are isomorphic, equivalently the characteristic matrices $XI-A,XI-B$ are equivalent over $F[X]$, via a cycle of implications (a similarity is exactly an $F[X]$-module isomorphism intertwining the $X$-actions; $V_A$ is the cokernel of $XI-A$; a module isomorphism lifts through the free presentations to a conjugacy of characteristic matrices), and conclude that the rational canonical form is a complete invariant for similarity ([[Def - The Module of a Linear Operator]], [[Def - Module]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Euclidean Domain]], [[Thm - Smith Normal Form]], [[Thm - Structure Theorem for Finitely Generated Modules over a Euclidean Domain]], [[Thm - Rational Canonical Form]]).
