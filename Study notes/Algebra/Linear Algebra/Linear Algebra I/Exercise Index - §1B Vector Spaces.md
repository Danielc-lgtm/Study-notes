## §1B Definition of Vector Space — Exercises

LADR §1B states the abstract definition of a vector space, derives the immediate corollaries $0 \cdot v = 0$, $a \cdot 0 = 0$, $(-1) v = -v$, and introduces the function-space $\mathbb{F}^S$ and sequence-space $\mathbb{F}^\infty$ as the canonical infinite-dimensional examples. The exercises drill **axiom-trading and corollary derivation**: confirming that a candidate space satisfies all the vector-space axioms, exploring which axioms can replace which, and checking that natural function-space constructions inherit vector-space structure. The unifying frame is that the eight axioms package a finite checklist into a clean structural notion, and once verified, the entire infinite range of vector-space machinery applies for free.

- [[Ex - F^infinity and F^S as vector spaces]] (⭐) — verifying that the space of sequences and the space of functions $S \to \mathbb{F}$ are vector spaces under pointwise operations; the prototype function-space construction ([[Def - Vector Space]]).
- [[Ex - Additive inverse axiom replaced by 0v=0]] (⭐⭐) — showing that the inverse axiom can be replaced by $0 \cdot v = 0$; illustrates that axiom sets admit reformulations and that the corollary $0 \cdot v = 0$ is doing real structural work ([[Def - Vector Space]], [[Thm - Uniqueness of Additive Identity and Inverses]]).
- [[Ex - F^infinity is not the union of finite-dimensional subspaces]] (⭐⭐⭐) — using a diagonalization argument to show $\mathbb{F}^\infty$ over an infinite field cannot be written as a countable union of finite-dimensional subspaces; classic qualifying-exam result on the structural distinction between finite- and infinite-dimensional spaces ([[Def - Vector Space]], [[Def - Subspace]]).
