## §7A Self-Adjoint and Normal Operators — Exercises

The exercises of §7A drill the manipulation of the adjoint and the characterisations of self-adjoint and normal operators. The recurring technique is the "compute one inner product two ways" pattern: pick a strategic inner product involving $T$, manipulate it once using the eigenvalue equation or matrix description of $T$, manipulate it again using self-adjointness or the conjugation pairing, and equate to extract a constraint. Each entry below names the exercise, summarises the technique it practises in one line, and lists in parentheses every definition and theorem invoked in that exercise's solution.

- [[Ex - Adjoint of differentiation is negative differentiation with boundary conditions]] (⭐⭐) — compute the adjoint of a differential operator via integration by parts, identifying the boundary subspace on which the formal adjoint $-D$ is the true adjoint ([[Def - Adjoint of a Linear Map]], [[Def - Inner Product Space]])
- [[Ex - Self-adjoint operators have real eigenvalues]] (⭐) — compute $\langle Tv, v \rangle$ in two ways using self-adjointness to force $\lambda = \overline{\lambda}$ ([[Def - Self-Adjoint Operator]], [[Def - Adjoint of a Linear Map]])
- [[Ex - Eigenvectors of a normal operator with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal]] (⭐⭐) — use the conjugation pairing $T^*w = \overline{\mu} w$ together with $Tv = \lambda v$ to force $\langle v, w \rangle = 0$ when $\lambda \neq \mu$ ([[Def - Normal Operator]], [[Thm - Normal Operators Commute with Their Adjoint]], [[Def - Adjoint of a Linear Map]])
