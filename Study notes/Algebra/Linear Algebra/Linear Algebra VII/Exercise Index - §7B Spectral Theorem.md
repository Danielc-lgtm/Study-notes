## §7B Spectral Theorem — Exercises

The exercises of §7B drill the application of the spectral theorem to concrete operators: computing eigenvalue decompositions for normal matrices, applying functional calculus to self-adjoint operators, and exploiting commutation to simultaneously diagonalise. The recurring technique is: spectrally decompose first, then read off the answer from the eigenvalues and projections. Each entry below names the exercise, summarises the technique it drills in one line, and lists in parentheses every definition and theorem invoked.

- [[Ex - Spectral decomposition of a 2x2 normal matrix]] (⭐) — concrete eigenvalue computation: verify normality, compute eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors, write spectral decomposition ([[Def - Normal Operator]], [[Thm - Complex Spectral Theorem]])
- [[Ex - Functional calculus on a self-adjoint operator]] (⭐⭐) — establish that $f \mapsto f(T)$ is a unital $*$-homomorphism via spectral expansion and orthogonality of projections; derive the positive square root as a consequence ([[Thm - Complex Spectral Theorem]], [[Thm - Real Spectral Theorem]], [[Def - Self-Adjoint Operator]])
- [[Ex - Commuting self-adjoint operators simultaneously diagonalizable]] (⭐⭐) — extend the spectral theorem to commuting pairs: eigenspaces of $S$ are $T$-invariant when $ST = TS$, so the spectral theorem applies to $T|_{E_\lambda}$ within each eigenspace ([[Def - Self-Adjoint Operator]], [[Thm - Complex Spectral Theorem]], [[Thm - Real Spectral Theorem]])
