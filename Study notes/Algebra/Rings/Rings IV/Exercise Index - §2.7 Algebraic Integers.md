## §2.7 Algebraic Integers — Exercises

- [[Ex - Finding the minimal polynomial of an algebraic integer]] — eliminate radicals by isolate-and-square to find the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt2 + \sqrt3$, namely $X^4 - 10X^2 + 1$, then certify it irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by excluding both factor shapes ([[Def - Algebraic Integer and Minimal Polynomial]], [[Thm - The Minimal Polynomial Generates the Kernel Ideal]], [[Thm - Gauss's Lemma]], [[Thm - Eisenstein's Criterion]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Irreducible and Prime Elements]])
- [[Ex - Deciding whether a number is an algebraic integer]] — decide a yes/no algebraic-integer question by computing the minimal polynomial and inspecting its coefficients: $\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)$ is an algebraic integer, $\tfrac12(1+\sqrt3)$ is not ([[Def - Algebraic Integer and Minimal Polynomial]], [[Thm - The Minimal Polynomial Generates the Kernel Ideal]], [[Thm - Rational Algebraic Integers are Integers]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Irreducible and Prime Elements]])
- [[Ex - A rational algebraic integer is an integer]] — prove the rigidity theorem $\mathbb{Q} \cap \{\text{algebraic integers}\} = \mathbb{Z}$ via the rational root theorem: lowest terms, clear denominators, conclude $q \mid 1$ ([[Def - Algebraic Integer and Minimal Polynomial]], [[Thm - Rational Algebraic Integers are Integers]], [[Thm - Gauss's Lemma]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Greatest Common Divisor and Least Common Multiple]])
