## §2.6 Gaussian Integers — Exercises

- [[Ex - Factoring a Gaussian integer into primes]] — factor a rational integer and a genuine Gaussian integer into Gaussian primes by computing the norm, factoring it over $\mathbb{Z}$, and reading off prime factors from the splitting trichotomy ([[Def - Gaussian Integers]], [[Thm - Classification of Gaussian Primes]], [[Def - Irreducible and Prime Elements]], [[Def - Unique Factorization Domain]], [[Def - Principal Ideal Domain]]).
- [[Ex - Writing an integer as a sum of two squares]] — decide whether $65$, $2026$, $2023$ are sums of two squares via the prime-exponent criterion, then construct the representations by factoring the split primes in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and multiplying ([[Def - Gaussian Integers]], [[Thm - Sum of Two Squares]], [[Thm - Classification of Gaussian Primes]]).
- [[Ex - Which rational primes remain prime in the Gaussian integers]] — prove the inert/split/ramified trichotomy: a rational prime stays prime in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ iff $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$, relating splitting to $-1$ being a quadratic residue mod $p$ ([[Def - Gaussian Integers]], [[Def - Irreducible and Prime Elements]], [[Thm - The Unit Group of a Finite Field is Cyclic]], [[Thm - Classification of Gaussian Primes]], [[Def - Unique Factorization Domain]], [[Def - Principal Ideal Domain]]).
- [[Ex - The number of ways to write n as a sum of two squares]] — count representations of $n$ as a sum of two squares using unique factorization in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$: $65=1^2+8^2=4^2+7^2$ from the conjugate choices in $5$ and $13$, the count governed by the exponents of primes $\equiv 1\pmod 4$ ([[Def - Gaussian Integers]], [[Def - Unique Factorization Domain]], [[Thm - Classification of Gaussian Primes]], [[Thm - Sum of Two Squares]]).
