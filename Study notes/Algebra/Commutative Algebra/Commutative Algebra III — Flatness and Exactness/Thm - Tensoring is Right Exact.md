# Notation

All rings are commutative with $1$; all modules unital. Fix an $R$-module $M$ and write $T_M = M \otimes_R (-)$ for the [[Def - Tensor Product of Modules|tensor-with-$M$ functor]], sending a module $N$ to $M \otimes N$ and an $R$-linear $f : N \to N'$ to $\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f$. A sequence $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C$ is [[Def - Exact Sequence and Short Exact Sequence|exact at $B$]] when $\operatorname{im} f = \ker g$. We write $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-, P)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, -)$ for the [[Def - The Hom Functor and Left Exactness|Hom functors]], $\iota : Q \hookrightarrow P$ for an inclusion of a submodule, and $\operatorname{coker} f = B/\operatorname{im} f$ for the cokernel. The full registry is on [[Commutative Algebra III — Flatness and Exactness]].

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (Tensoring is right exact).** Let $M$ be an $R$-module. If
> $$A \xrightarrow{\ f\ } B \xrightarrow{\ g\ } C \to 0$$
> is an exact sequence of $R$-modules, then
> $$M \otimes_R A \xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{id}_M \otimes f\ } M \otimes_R B \xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{id}_M \otimes g\ } M \otimes_R C \to 0$$
> is exact. Equivalently, the functor $T_M = M \otimes_R(-)$ is **right exact**.

> **Corollary (quotients).** For a submodule $Q \subseteq P$ with inclusion $\iota$ and quotient $\pi : P \to P/Q$,
> $$M \otimes (P/Q) \;\cong\; (M \otimes P)\big/\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota),$$
> and in particular $M \otimes_R (R/I) \cong M/IM$ for any ideal $I$. For algebras $S, T$ over $R$ and an ideal $I \trianglelefteq T$, $S \otimes_R (T/I) \cong (S \otimes_R T)/I^e$, where $I^e$ is the ideal generated by the image of $I$.

The leading map $\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f$ is **not** asserted to be injective even when $f$ is; that is precisely the content of [[Def - Flat Module|flatness]], and its failure is the warning that opens the next section.

---

# Motivation

Tensoring is a functor, and the first thing one asks of any functor on modules is whether it respects exact sequences — the language in which every relation between modules (injection, surjection, kernel, quotient) is written. The honest answer is "half of it", and this theorem pins down *which* half. It is the positive half, the good news, and it is what makes tensor products computable at all.

The role this theorem plays is that of a *licence to tensor quotients*. Almost every concrete tensor computation one ever does is an instance of the corollary $M \otimes (R/I) \cong M/IM$: this is what lets you compute $M \otimes \kappa(\mathfrak p) = M/\mathfrak p M$, the fibre of a module at a point; it is the engine behind $S^{-1}M \cong S^{-1}R \otimes M$; it is why base change of an algebra $S \otimes_R (T/I) = (S\otimes T)/I^e$ extends ideals. Without right-exactness, none of these identities would hold and the tensor product would be a black box. With it, tensoring becomes a calculational tool: present a module as a quotient, and right-exactness computes the tensor product as the corresponding quotient.

The theorem also sets up the chapter's central tension. It tells you the surjection at the *back* of an exact sequence always survives tensoring. The next question — does the injection at the *front* survive? — is the one it deliberately does *not* answer, and the answer being "not always" is what forces the definition of flatness. So this theorem is both a positive result and the precise statement of what remains to be controlled.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The theorem's precondition is "an exact sequence ending in $\to 0$", but it is invoked from several disguises.

The first disguised source is **a surjection $g : B \twoheadrightarrow C$**, with no explicit sequence in sight. The property $B$ is "$g$ is onto." The bridge: extend $g$ to the exact sequence $\ker g \hookrightarrow B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ and apply the theorem to conclude $\operatorname{id}_M \otimes g$ is onto. The non-obvious value is that surjectivity is preserved by tensoring *for free*, whereas injectivity is not — so any time you tensor a surjection, you may assert the result is a surjection. *Example problem:* given $R^n \twoheadrightarrow M$, deduce $P^n = P \otimes R^n \twoheadrightarrow P \otimes M$ to bound generators of $P \otimes M$.

The second disguised source is **a quotient module $P/Q$**. The property $B$ is "I must tensor a quotient." The bridge: $Q \hookrightarrow P \to P/Q \to 0$ is exact, so the corollary gives $M \otimes (P/Q) = (M \otimes P)/\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota)$. The non-obviousness is that the image of $M \otimes Q$ — which may not be all of what you would naively subtract, since $\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota$ need not be injective — is exactly the right thing to quotient by. *Example problem:* compute $M \otimes_R (R/I)$ and get $M/IM$ by recognising $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota) = IM$.

The third disguised source is **a finite presentation $R^m \to R^n \to M \to 0$**. The property $B$ is "$M$ is finitely presented." The bridge: tensoring the presentation stays right exact, giving $M' \otimes M$ as the cokernel of a known map between free modules $ (M')^m \to (M')^n$. The non-obvious payoff is a completely explicit description of $M' \otimes M$ by generators and relations. *Example problem:* compute $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ from the presentations and recover $\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The conclusion is "the tensored sequence is exact at the last two spots, ending in $\to 0$."

Combine the conclusion with **flatness of $M$**. Right-exactness gives the back half; if $M$ is moreover [[Def - Flat Module|flat]], the front injection survives too, and the result $E$ is that the *entire* short exact sequence tensors to a short exact sequence — full exactness of $T_M$. This is non-obvious because right-exactness alone never delivers the front, and it is the combination that powers localization being exact. *Use:* localizing or base-changing an exact sequence along a flat map.

Combine the conclusion with **a second presentation to compute a tensor product explicitly**. Right-exactness says $M \otimes M' = \operatorname{coker}(\text{tensored relation map})$; combining the cokernel description with the relations of $M'$ gives a presentation of $M \otimes M'$. The result $E$ is the standard computation $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ and, generally, an algorithm for tensoring finitely presented modules. The combination is useful because it turns an abstract universal object into a concrete quotient.

Combine the conclusion with **the algebra structure** to extend ideals. For algebras, $S \otimes_R (T/I) \cong (S \otimes T)/I^e$ shows that base-changing a quotient algebra extends the ideal. The result $E$ is that $\mathbb{C} \otimes_\mathbb{R} (\mathbb{R}[T]/I) \cong \mathbb{C}[T]/I^e$ — complexifying a real variety extends its ideal — the algebraic skeleton of base change in geometry. This is non-obvious because it identifies a tensor of algebras with an ideal extension.

# Why Is It True

The intuition is that **tensoring is built to turn bilinear data into linear data, and the universal property that does this is a statement about maps *out of* the tensor product — which is exactly the data $\operatorname{Hom}$ records, and $\operatorname{Hom}$ is left exact.** Run the slogan: a sequence $X \to Y \to Z \to 0$ being right-exact is *the same information* as, for every test module $L$, the sequence $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(Z, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(Y, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(X, L)$ being left-exact — this is a general fact dualizing exactness into $\operatorname{Hom}$. So to prove the tensored sequence is right exact, it suffices to show that after applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ it becomes left exact, for every $L$. But $\operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes (-), L) = \operatorname{Hom}(-, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$ by the adjunction, so applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ to the *tensored* sequence is the same as applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$ to the *original* sequence — and *that* is left exact by [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of Hom]], because the original sequence was right exact. The right-exactness of tensor is the left-exactness of Hom, read through the adjunction.

**The whole mechanism in one sentence: tensoring is right exact because it is a left adjoint, and left adjoints preserve cokernels.** Surjections and cokernels are colimits; the tensor functor, being a left adjoint to $\operatorname{Hom}(M, -)$, commutes with all colimits; so it preserves the cokernel-data $\operatorname{coker} f = \ker g$, $g$ surjective — which is exactly right-exactness.

The geometric why for the corollary $M \otimes R/I = M/IM$: tensoring with $R/I$ is "set $I$ to zero," and setting $I$ to zero in $M$ means killing $IM$. The surjection $M \to M/IM$ survives because killing a submodule is a quotient construction, and quotients are precisely the colimits tensoring respects.

# What Makes This Hard

The hard part is not any computation but seeing that the *right* tool is the [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of Hom]] together with the tensor–Hom adjunction, rather than a direct element chase on tensors (which is painful because elements of $M \otimes B$ are sums of pure tensors with non-unique representations). The non-obvious step is the "dualize into Hom" move: replacing "show the tensored sequence is right exact" with "show $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ of it is left exact for all $L$," which feels like it goes the wrong way until the adjunction collapses it. The most common error is to try to prove the leading map $\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f$ is injective — it is *not* part of the claim, and attempting it is the trap that flatness exists to flag.

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:**
Convert the tensor statement into a Hom statement using the adjunction $\operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes N, L) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(N, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$. Use that "$X \to Y \to Z \to 0$ is exact" is equivalent to "$0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(Z, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(Y, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(X, L)$ is exact for all $L$." Apply [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of Hom]] to the original sequence with test module $\operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$, then translate back through the adjunction.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Reduce to a Hom criterion.** Show: $X \xrightarrow{u} Y \xrightarrow{v} Z \to 0$ is exact $\iff$ for every $L$, $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(Z, L) \xrightarrow{v^*} \operatorname{Hom}(Y, L) \xrightarrow{u^*} \operatorname{Hom}(X, L)$ is exact.
   - *Hint:* This is the converse-lemma direction of [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|Hom-left-exactness]]; surjectivity of $v$ dualizes to injectivity of $v^*$, and $\operatorname{im} u = \ker v$ dualizes to $\ker u^* = \operatorname{im} v^*$.
   - *Why needed:* It is the bridge that lets a Hom fact prove a tensor fact.

2. **Apply the adjunction.** Show that $\operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes A, L) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(A, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$ naturally, and similarly for $B, C$, compatibly with the induced maps.
   - *Hint:* $\Phi \mapsto (a \mapsto (m \mapsto \Phi(m \otimes a)))$; check naturality so the three isomorphisms form a morphism of sequences.
   - *Why needed:* It identifies "$\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ of the tensored sequence" with "$\operatorname{Hom}(-, \operatorname{Hom}(M,L))$ of the original."

3. **Invoke left-exactness of Hom.** Apply [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness]] of the contravariant $\operatorname{Hom}(-, P)$ with $P = \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$ to the right-exact original sequence.
   - *Hint:* $A \to B \to C \to 0$ exact gives $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(C, P) \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, P) \to \operatorname{Hom}(A, P)$ exact, for $P = \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$.
   - *Why needed:* This is the actual exactness input; the adjunction transports it to the tensored sequence.

4. **Translate back.** Conclude via Subgoal 1 (applied to the tensored sequence) that $M \otimes A \to M \otimes B \to M \otimes C \to 0$ is exact, since its $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ is left exact for all $L$.
   - *Hint:* Run Subgoal 1 in reverse on $X = M\otimes A$, $Y = M \otimes B$, $Z = M \otimes C$.
   - *Why needed:* It delivers the conclusion in tensor form.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Right-exactness dualizes to left-exactness of Hom
> **Statement:** A sequence $X \xrightarrow{u} Y \xrightarrow{v} Z \to 0$ is exact if and only if, for *every* $R$-module $L$, the sequence $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(Z, L) \xrightarrow{v^*} \operatorname{Hom}(Y, L) \xrightarrow{u^*} \operatorname{Hom}(X, L)$ is exact.
>
> **Hint:** Surjectivity of $v$ $\iff$ injectivity of $v^*$ for all $L$ (a map out of $Z$ killed by precomposition with a surjection is zero); and $\operatorname{im} u = \ker v$ $\iff$ $\ker u^* = \operatorname{im} v^*$ for all $L$.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the two-way bridge between right-exact tensor statements and left-exact Hom statements; the "only if" is [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of Hom]], the "if" is its converse (the analogue of [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|Lemma 3.36]]).
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > ($\Rightarrow$) is [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of the contravariant Hom]]. ($\Leftarrow$) Suppose the Hom sequence is exact for all $L$. Take $L = \operatorname{coker} v = Z/\operatorname{im} v$ and the projection $p : Z \to L$. Then $v^*(p) = p \circ v = 0$, so injectivity of $v^*$ forces $p = 0$, i.e. $\operatorname{im} v = Z$: $v$ is onto. For exactness in the middle: $u^* v^* = (vu)^* $, and $\ker u^* = \operatorname{im} v^*$ for all $L$ gives $vu = 0$ (take $L = Z$, $\operatorname{id}_Z$) hence $\operatorname{im} u \subseteq \ker v$; the reverse uses $L = Y/\operatorname{im} u$ and the quotient map, whose vanishing under $u^*$ forces it to factor through $v$, giving $\ker v \subseteq \operatorname{im} u$.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: The tensor–Hom adjunction is natural
> **Statement:** For $R$-modules $N, L$ and fixed $M$, the map $\Theta_N : \operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes N, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(N, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$, $\Phi \mapsto (n \mapsto (m \mapsto \Phi(m \otimes n)))$, is an isomorphism, natural in $N$.
>
> **Hint:** Build the inverse from the universal property of $\otimes$: a map $N \to \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$ is a bilinear map $M \times N \to L$, hence a map $M \otimes N \to L$.
>
> **Why needed:** It identifies $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L) \circ T_M$ with $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L))$, so a Hom-fact about the original sequence becomes a Hom-fact about the tensored one.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > Given $\Phi : M \otimes N \to L$, the assignment $n \mapsto (m \mapsto \Phi(m \otimes n))$ is well-defined and $R$-linear in $n$. Conversely a map $\psi : N \to \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$ defines a bilinear map $M \times N \to L$, $(m, n) \mapsto \psi(n)(m)$, which factors uniquely through $M \otimes N$ by the [[Def - Tensor Product of Modules|universal property]], giving $\Phi$. These are mutually inverse. Naturality in $N$: for $f : N \to N'$, both routes around the naturality square send $\Phi$ to $n \mapsto (m \mapsto \Phi(m \otimes f(n)))$, using $(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f)(m \otimes n) = m \otimes f(n)$.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Let $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ be exact and fix an $R$-module $M$. We show $M \otimes A \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f} M \otimes B \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_M \otimes g} M \otimes C \to 0$ is exact, using the criterion of Lemma 1: it suffices to show that for every $R$-module $L$,
> $$0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes C, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes B, L) \to \operatorname{Hom}(M \otimes A, L)$$
> is exact.
>
> By Lemma 2 (the adjunction, natural in the module variable), this sequence is isomorphic, as a sequence, to
> $$0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(C, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)) \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)) \to \operatorname{Hom}(A, \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)),$$
> the three vertical isomorphisms $\Theta_C, \Theta_B, \Theta_A$ forming a morphism of sequences (naturality of Lemma 2). Set $P := \operatorname{Hom}(M, L)$. The original sequence $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ is exact, so by [[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|left-exactness of the contravariant functor $\operatorname{Hom}(-, P)$]] the sequence
> $$0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(C, P) \to \operatorname{Hom}(B, P) \to \operatorname{Hom}(A, P)$$
> is exact. Since the two displayed sequences are isomorphic, the first is exact too. As this holds for *every* $L$, Lemma 1 (the "if" direction) applied to $X = M\otimes A$, $Y = M\otimes B$, $Z = M\otimes C$ gives that
> $$M \otimes A \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_M \otimes f} M \otimes B \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_M \otimes g} M \otimes C \to 0$$
> is exact. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Corollary.** For $Q \hookrightarrow P \xrightarrow{\pi} P/Q \to 0$, tensoring gives $M \otimes Q \to M \otimes P \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\pi} M \otimes(P/Q) \to 0$ exact, so $\operatorname{id} \otimes \pi$ is surjective with kernel $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota)$, whence $M \otimes (P/Q) \cong (M \otimes P)/\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota)$. For $Q = I$, $P = R$: $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_M \otimes \iota) = IM$ under $M \otimes R \cong M$, giving $M \otimes (R/I) \cong M/IM$. The algebra version follows by checking $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{id}_S \otimes \iota)$ is the ideal $I^e$ generated by $\{1 \otimes x : x \in I\}$.

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Computing $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ in number theory.** Present $\mathbb{Z}/n = \operatorname{coker}(\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z})$ and tensor with $\mathbb{Z}/m$. Right-exactness gives $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n = \operatorname{coker}(\mathbb{Z}/m \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z}/m) = (\mathbb{Z}/m)/(n) = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. The application is nonobvious because the gcd emerges from a cokernel computation, and it is the cleanest demonstration that the theorem turns abstract tensors into arithmetic.

**Base change and complexification in algebraic geometry.** For a real affine variety with coordinate ring $\mathbb{R}[T_1, \dots, T_n]/I$, complexifying is $\mathbb{C} \otimes_\mathbb{R} (\mathbb{R}[T]/I) \cong \mathbb{C}[T]/I^e$ by the algebra corollary — the complex points are cut out by the extended ideal. The application is nonobvious because it reframes "complexify the variety" as "extend the ideal", and right-exactness is exactly what guarantees the quotient survives the base change.

**Fibres of a module at a point.** For a module $M$ over $R$ and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, the fibre is $M \otimes_R \kappa(\mathfrak m) = M/\mathfrak m M$, a vector space over the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak m) = R/\mathfrak m$. Right-exactness is what makes "tensor with the residue field $=$ reduce mod $\mathfrak m$" hold. The application is nonobvious because it is the algebraic definition of the fibre dimension whose constancy is exactly flatness — connecting this theorem to the geometric meaning of the next.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Hom is Left Exact|Hom is Left Exact]]** — the mirror theorem and the actual tool of the proof. Tensoring keeps the back surjection; Hom keeps the front injection. The proof here *is* the left-exactness of Hom, transported across the tensor–Hom adjunction: applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, L)$ to a tensored sequence is the same as applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \operatorname{Hom}(M,L))$ to the original, so the one exactness fact yields the other. The two theorems are a single fact viewed through the adjunction.

- **[[Def - Flat Module|Flat Module]]** — the theorem's deliberate gap. Right-exactness gives the back two-thirds of a short exact sequence; flatness is the extra hypothesis that recovers the front injection, upgrading $T_M$ from right exact to fully exact. The whole notion of flatness exists to name "when does the part this theorem does *not* guarantee also hold?"

- **[[Def - Restriction and Extension of Scalars|Extension of Scalars]]** — base change $S \otimes_R(-)$ is a special tensor functor, so it is right exact; the corollary $S \otimes_R (T/I) = (S \otimes T)/I^e$ is how extension of scalars interacts with quotient algebras, the algebraic skeleton of geometric base change.

- **Cokernels are colimits; tensoring is a left adjoint** — the categorical bridge. Right-exactness is the instance, for the cokernel colimit, of the general theorem that a left adjoint preserves all colimits. The same principle gives $M \otimes (A \oplus B) \cong (M\otimes A)\oplus(M\otimes B)$ (direct sum is a coproduct, a colimit) and is the reason tensoring commutes with direct limits.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Right-derived vs left-derived functors *(from Homological Algebra)*
> Because $T_M$ is right exact but not exact, it has **left-derived functors** $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, -)$ extending it to the left, measuring the failure of the front injection to survive — exactly the failure flatness forbids. This is the dual of how the left-exact $\operatorname{Hom}$ is right-derived into $\operatorname{Ext}^n$, and the two derived theories are linked by the same adjunction that proves this theorem.

> [!tip] Base change of schemes *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> The algebra corollary $S \otimes_R (T/I) \cong (S \otimes_R T)/I^e$ is the affine form of **base change** of schemes: pulling back a closed subscheme $V(I)$ along $\operatorname{Spec} S \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ cuts out $V(I^e)$. Right-exactness guarantees the closed-subscheme structure survives the pullback, the foundational compatibility making fibre products of schemes well-behaved.
