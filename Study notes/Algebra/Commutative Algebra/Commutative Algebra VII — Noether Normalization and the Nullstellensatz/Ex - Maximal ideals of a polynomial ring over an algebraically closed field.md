# Problem Statement

Let $\Omega$ be an **algebraically closed** field and $R = \Omega[T_1, \dots, T_n]$. Prove that every maximal ideal of $R$ has the form
$$\mathfrak m_x = (T_1 - x_1, \dots, T_n - x_n) \qquad \text{for a unique } x = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in \Omega^n,$$
and that $x \mapsto \mathfrak m_x$ is a bijection $\Omega^n \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \operatorname{mSpec} R$.

Then show by example that the hypothesis "$\Omega$ algebraically closed" is essential: exhibit a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{R}[T]$ that is *not* of the form $(T - a)$.

**Recall:**

The objects in play are the polynomial ring, maximal ideals, the weak Nullstellensatz, and Zariski's lemma.

![[Thm - The Weak Nullstellensatz#Statement]]

![[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients#Statement]]

A polynomial $f \in \Omega[T_1, \dots, T_n]$ evaluates at $x \in \Omega^n$ to $f(x) \in \Omega$; **evaluation at $x$** is the $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_x : R \to \Omega$, $f \mapsto f(x)$, surjective with kernel exactly $\mathfrak m_x$. A field is **algebraically closed** if every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root in it; consequently it has no proper finite (algebraic) extensions. [[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]] says a field that is finitely generated as an $\Omega$-algebra is a finite extension of $\Omega$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *classify-the-maximal-ideals* problem, the prototype of "compute $\operatorname{mSpec}$ of a finitely generated algebra". It is the concrete face of the [[Thm - The Weak Nullstellensatz|weak Nullstellensatz]]'s point form, and the same routine — pass to the residue field, use Zariski's lemma, read off a point — classifies maximal ideals in any finitely generated $\Omega$-algebra. The topic page's problem-solving strategy files this under "translate a question about maximal ideals into a question about residue fields".

**Assumption pattern.** Two assumptions carry the whole argument. First, $R$ is a *finitely generated $\Omega$-algebra* (generated by $T_1, \dots, T_n$), so its residue fields are finitely generated $\Omega$-algebras and Zariski's lemma applies. Second, $\Omega$ is *algebraically closed*, so the only finite extension of $\Omega$ is $\Omega$ itself — this is what collapses the residue field to $\Omega$ and pins the ideal to a point. Removing either assumption breaks the conclusion: drop finite generation and residue fields can be huge; drop closure and the residue field can be a proper finite extension.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ $\Rightarrow$ residue field $R/\mathfrak m$ ([[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients|maximal ⟹ field quotient]]) $\Rightarrow$ $R/\mathfrak m$ finite over $\Omega$ ([[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]]) $\Rightarrow$ $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ (algebraic closure) $\Rightarrow$ the quotient map is $\operatorname{ev}_x$ for $x$ given by the images of the $T_i$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathfrak m = \ker \operatorname{ev}_x = \mathfrak m_x$. This is exactly the chain inside the [[Thm - The Weak Nullstellensatz|weak Nullstellensatz]] proof, applied with $k = \Omega$.

**Key decision point.** The non-obvious move is to *pass to the residue field $R/\mathfrak m$ and recognise it as a finitely generated $\Omega$-algebra* — at which point Zariski's lemma is available. A reader who tries to attack $\mathfrak m$ directly (listing generators, factoring) gets nowhere; the leverage is entirely in the quotient. The second subtlety is identifying the quotient map $R \to R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ with *evaluation at a point* — seeing that an $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism $R \to \Omega$ is the same data as a point $x = (\varphi(T_i))$, so its kernel is automatically $\mathfrak m_x$.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations from [[Commutative Algebra VII — Noether Normalization and the Nullstellensatz#Legal Operations|the topic page's Legal Operations]]:

1. **Pass to the residue field at a maximal ideal.** Replace $\mathfrak m$ by the field $R/\mathfrak m$, where field-theoretic tools apply.

2. **Apply Zariski's lemma to a finitely generated algebra that is a field.** $R/\mathfrak m$ is a field finitely generated as an $\Omega$-algebra, hence a finite extension of $\Omega$.

3. **Collapse a finite extension of an algebraically closed field.** A finite extension of $\Omega$ algebraically closed is $\Omega$ itself.

4. **Identify an $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism out of a polynomial ring with a point.** The map $R \to \Omega$ is $\operatorname{ev}_x$ for $x = (\varphi(T_i))$, with kernel $\mathfrak m_x$.

5. **Produce a non-closed counterexample by exhibiting an irreducible polynomial of degree $> 1$.** Over $\mathbb{R}$, $(T^2 + 1)$ is maximal with residue field $\mathbb{C} \neq \mathbb{R}$.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> You cannot say much about a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ directly. But $R/\mathfrak m$ is a *field* — and a very special one, because $R$ is generated as an $\Omega$-algebra by finitely many elements. What theorem governs fields that are finitely generated as algebras over $\Omega$?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> [[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]]: $R/\mathfrak m$ is a *finite* extension of $\Omega$. Now use that $\Omega$ is algebraically closed — how big can a finite extension of an algebraically closed field be?

> [!note]- Hint 3
> $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$. So the quotient map $\varphi : R \to R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ is an $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism. Set $x_i := \varphi(T_i) \in \Omega$. What is $\varphi(f)$ in terms of $f$ and $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$? And what is $\ker \varphi$?

> [!note]- Hint 4
> $\varphi(f) = f(x)$, so $\varphi = \operatorname{ev}_x$ and $\ker \varphi = \mathfrak m = \{f : f(x) = 0\} = \mathfrak m_x$. For the counterexample, over $\mathbb{R}$ pick a polynomial with *no real root* but irreducible — $(T^2 + 1)$ is maximal because $\mathbb{R}[T]/(T^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C}$ is a field, yet it is not $(T - a)$ for any $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

# Solution

The proof passes to the residue field, applies Zariski's lemma to learn it is a finite extension of $\Omega$, collapses that to $\Omega$ by algebraic closure, and reads the resulting quotient map as evaluation at a point. The counterexample over $\mathbb{R}$ shows that without closure the residue field can be a proper extension ($\mathbb{C}$), so the maximal ideal need not be a point.

**Step 1: The residue field $R/\mathfrak m$ is a finite extension of $\Omega$.**

For a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, $R/\mathfrak m$ is a field, finitely generated as an $\Omega$-algebra; by Zariski's lemma it is a finite extension of $\Omega$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal of $R = \Omega[T_1, \dots, T_n]$. By [[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients|maximal ⟺ field quotient]], $R/\mathfrak m$ is a field. It is generated as an $\Omega$-algebra by the images $\bar T_1, \dots, \bar T_n$ of the indeterminates, so it is a *finitely generated $\Omega$-algebra that is a field*. By [[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]], $R/\mathfrak m$ is a **finite** extension of $\Omega$: $\dim_\Omega (R/\mathfrak m) < \infty$.

**Step 2: Algebraic closure forces $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$.**

A finite (hence algebraic) extension of the algebraically closed field $\Omega$ must equal $\Omega$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> $R/\mathfrak m$ is a finite, hence algebraic, extension of $\Omega$. Let $\alpha \in R/\mathfrak m$; it is algebraic over $\Omega$, so it is a root of its minimal polynomial $p \in \Omega[T]$. Since $\Omega$ is **algebraically closed**, $p$ splits into linear factors over $\Omega$, so its only roots lie in $\Omega$; as $p$ is irreducible over $\Omega$ it must be linear, $p = T - c$, giving $\alpha = c \in \Omega$. Hence every element of $R/\mathfrak m$ lies in $\Omega$, so $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$. (Equivalently: an algebraically closed field has no proper finite extension.)

**Step 3: The quotient map is evaluation at a point, so $\mathfrak m = \mathfrak m_x$.**

The composite $\varphi : R \twoheadrightarrow R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ is $\operatorname{ev}_x$ for $x = (\varphi(T_1), \dots, \varphi(T_n))$, with kernel $\mathfrak m_x$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $\varphi : R \to R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ be the quotient map, an $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism, and set $x_i := \varphi(T_i) \in \Omega$, $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$. Since $\varphi$ fixes $\Omega$ and is a ring homomorphism, for $f = \sum_\alpha a_\alpha T^\alpha$ we have $\varphi(f) = \sum_\alpha a_\alpha x^\alpha = f(x)$. So $\varphi = \operatorname{ev}_x$, and
> $$\mathfrak m = \ker \varphi = \{f \in R : f(x) = 0\}.$$
> Now $\mathfrak m_x = (T_1 - x_1, \dots, T_n - x_n) \subseteq \ker \operatorname{ev}_x$ (each $T_i - x_i$ vanishes at $x$), and $\mathfrak m_x$ is itself maximal — indeed $R/\mathfrak m_x \cong \Omega$ via $\operatorname{ev}_x$, a field. Since $\mathfrak m_x$ is maximal and $\mathfrak m_x \subseteq \mathfrak m \subsetneq R$, we get $\mathfrak m = \mathfrak m_x$.

**Step 4: The map $x \mapsto \mathfrak m_x$ is a bijection.**

Distinct points give distinct ideals (injectivity), and Steps 1–3 show every maximal ideal arises (surjectivity).

> [!note]- Derivation
> *Surjectivity* is Steps 1–3: every maximal ideal is some $\mathfrak m_x$. *Injectivity:* if $\mathfrak m_x = \mathfrak m_y$, then $T_i - x_i \in \mathfrak m_y$, so $(T_i - x_i)(y) = y_i - x_i = 0$, giving $x_i = y_i$ for all $i$, i.e. $x = y$. (Equivalently, $\operatorname{ev}_x = \operatorname{ev}_y$ on the $T_i$ forces $x = y$.) Hence $x \mapsto \mathfrak m_x$ is a bijection $\Omega^n \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{mSpec} R$.

**Step 5: The counterexample over $\mathbb{R}$.**

Over the non-closed field $\mathbb{R}$, the ideal $(T^2 + 1) \trianglelefteq \mathbb{R}[T]$ is maximal but is not $(T - a)$ for any $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> The polynomial $T^2 + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$ (no real root), so $\mathbb{R}[T]/(T^2 + 1) \cong \mathbb{C}$ is a *field*; by [[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients|the quotient criterion]], $(T^2 + 1)$ is **maximal**. But it is not of the form $(T - a)$: those have residue field $\mathbb{R}[T]/(T - a) \cong \mathbb{R}$, whereas $\mathbb{R}[T]/(T^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C} \neq \mathbb{R}$. Concretely, $(T^2+1) = (T - a)$ would force $T^2 + 1 = (T - a)g$, impossible since $T^2 + 1$ has no real root. So the classification *fails* over $\mathbb{R}$: the failure is exactly that Step 2 breaks — the residue field $\mathbb{C}$ is a proper finite extension of $\mathbb{R}$, because $\mathbb{R}$ is not algebraically closed. (Geometrically, $(T^2+1)$ corresponds to the conjugate pair $\{i, -i\} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, a *Galois orbit*, not a single $\mathbb{R}$-point.)

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** For $\Omega$ algebraically closed, every maximal ideal of $R = \Omega[T_1, \dots, T_n]$ is $\mathfrak m_x = (T_1 - x_1, \dots, T_n - x_n)$ for a unique $x \in \Omega^n$, and $x \mapsto \mathfrak m_x$ is a bijection $\Omega^n \to \operatorname{mSpec} R$.
>
> Let $\mathfrak m$ be maximal. Then $R/\mathfrak m$ is a field ([[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients|maximal ⟹ field quotient]]), finitely generated as an $\Omega$-algebra by $\bar T_1, \dots, \bar T_n$, hence finite over $\Omega$ ([[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]]). A finite extension of the algebraically closed $\Omega$ equals $\Omega$, so $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$. The quotient map $\varphi : R \to \Omega$ has $\varphi(f) = f(x)$ for $x := (\varphi(T_1), \dots, \varphi(T_n))$, so $\mathfrak m = \ker \varphi = \{f : f(x) = 0\}$. Since $\mathfrak m_x \subseteq \mathfrak m$ with $\mathfrak m_x$ maximal and $\mathfrak m$ proper, $\mathfrak m = \mathfrak m_x$.
>
> Injectivity: $\mathfrak m_x = \mathfrak m_y \Rightarrow y_i - x_i = (T_i - x_i)(y) = 0 \Rightarrow x = y$. Surjectivity: shown above. So $x \mapsto \mathfrak m_x$ is a bijection.
>
> **Counterexample (non-closed base).** Over $\mathbb{R}$, $(T^2 + 1)$ is maximal ($\mathbb{R}[T]/(T^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C}$ a field) but not $(T - a)$ for any $a \in \mathbb{R}$ (its residue field $\mathbb{C} \neq \mathbb{R}$). Algebraic closure is essential. $\blacksquare$

> [!warning] Illegal but tempting: concluding $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$ without algebraic closure
> It is tempting to think "$R/\mathfrak m$ is a finite extension of $\Omega$, and finite extensions are small, so $R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$". The collapse "finite extension $= \Omega$" uses *algebraic closure* and nothing else. Over $\mathbb{R}$, Zariski's lemma still gives a *finite* extension — but it can be $\mathbb{C}$, a genuine degree-$2$ extension, so the maximal ideal is the ideal of a conjugate pair, not a point. The repair is exactly the hypothesis "$\Omega$ algebraically closed", which removes all proper finite extensions.

# Key Takeaways

**To classify maximal ideals of a finitely generated algebra, pass to the residue field and squeeze it with Zariski's lemma.** The universal move whenever you face "describe $\operatorname{mSpec} A$" for $A$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra is: a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ gives a residue field $A/\mathfrak m$ that is *finitely generated as a $k$-algebra*, so [[Thm - Zariski's Lemma|Zariski's lemma]] forces it to be a *finite* extension of $k$. Over an algebraically closed $k$ this residue field is $k$ itself and $\mathfrak m$ is a point; over a general $k$ it is some finite extension and $\mathfrak m$ corresponds to a Galois orbit of points. The trigger is "maximal ideal of a finitely generated algebra"; the reaction is "look at the residue field, it is finite over $k$". This single pattern computes maximal spectra throughout commutative algebra and is the concrete content of the weak Nullstellensatz.

**A homomorphism out of a polynomial ring is a point — internalise the identification.** The step that turns the algebraic conclusion ($R/\mathfrak m = \Omega$, so there is a map $R \to \Omega$) into the geometric one ($\mathfrak m = \mathfrak m_x$) is the recognition that an $\Omega$-algebra homomorphism $\varphi : \Omega[T_1, \dots, T_n] \to \Omega$ is *nothing but a point* $x = (\varphi(T_i))$, with $\varphi = \operatorname{ev}_x$ and $\ker \varphi = \mathfrak m_x$. This is the bedrock of the variety–ring dictionary: maps out of coordinate rings are points (and more generally maps between coordinate rings are regular maps of varieties). Whenever you see a homomorphism from a polynomial ring to a field, immediately read off the image-tuple as a point; this reflex converts ideal-theoretic data into geometry.

**Algebraic closure is the hypothesis that collapses residue fields to the ground field — and its failure is a feature, not a bug.** The counterexample $(T^2+1) \trianglelefteq \mathbb{R}[T]$ shows precisely where closure is used: Zariski's lemma is *closure-independent* (it gives a finite extension over any base), but the final collapse "finite extension $= \Omega$" needs closure. Over a non-closed field, maximal ideals correspond to Galois orbits, and the residue field $A/\mathfrak m$ is the **field of definition** of the orbit — its degree counts the points in the orbit. This is not a defect to be avoided but the entry to arithmetic geometry: working over $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$, the residue fields *are* the interesting data, encoding which extension is needed to "see" a point. Recognising that "closure makes points simple, non-closure makes points arithmetic" is the diagnostic that tells you when you are doing geometry versus number theory. See the companion [[Ex - Why algebraic closure is needed in the Nullstellensatz]] for the dual failure on the variety side.
