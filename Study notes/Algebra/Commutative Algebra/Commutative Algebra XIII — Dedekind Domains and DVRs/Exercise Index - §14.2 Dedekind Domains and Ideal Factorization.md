## §14.2 Dedekind Domains and Ideal Factorization — Exercises

The exercises of §14.2 drill the global theory: how the local DVR structure at each prime assembles into unique factorization of ideals, how fractional ideals form a group, and how the failure of that group to be "all principal" — the class group — measures the failure of unique factorization of elements. The unifying skill is to think of an ideal as an exponent-vector over the primes and of the class group as the cokernel of "take the principal ideal". The first exercise establishes the group structure of fractional ideals (the Dedekind miracle of invertibility, proved by localizing to DVRs); the second is the canonical worked computation in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, factoring $(6)$ into prime ideals and watching the two element-factorizations regroup; the third proves the headline equivalence "trivial class group $\iff$ PID $\iff$ UFD" and computes a nontrivial class group. The recurring techniques are localization to DVRs to verify ideal identities, reduction of the defining polynomial modulo $p$ to factor $(p)$ (Dedekind's criterion), and the norm obstruction to principality.

- [[Ex - Fractional ideals form a group]] (⭐⭐) — prove the nonzero fractional ideals of a Dedekind domain form an abelian group, with the only nontrivial axiom (invertibility, $\mathfrak{a}(A:\mathfrak{a}) = A$) verified *locally* — at each prime the equation reduces to the trivial DVR cancellation $(\pi^n)(\pi^{-n}) = A_\mathfrak{p}$ — and identify the group as free abelian on the primes, with invertibility characterizing Dedekind domains ([[Def - Fractional Ideal and the Ideal Class Group]], [[Def - Dedekind Domain]], [[Thm - Localization of a Dedekind Domain is a DVR]], [[Thm - A Dedekind Domain has Unique Factorization of Ideals]], [[Thm - Characterization of Discrete Valuation Rings]]).

- [[Ex - Unique factorization of ideals in Z[sqrt -5] into primes]] (⭐⭐) — factor $(2) = \mathfrak{p}^2$, $(3) = \mathfrak{q}\bar{\mathfrak{q}}$, $(1\pm\sqrt{-5}) = \mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q},\,\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{q}}$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ by reducing $x^2+5$ modulo $2$ and $3$ (Dedekind's criterion), verify primality via the residue fields $\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{F}_3$, and watch both factorizations of $6$ regroup to the single ideal factorization $(6) = \mathfrak{p}^2\mathfrak{q}\bar{\mathfrak{q}}$ ([[Def - Dedekind Domain]], [[Thm - A Dedekind Domain has Unique Factorization of Ideals]], [[Thm - The Ring of Integers of a Number Field is Dedekind]], [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal]]).

- [[Ex - The class group measures failure of unique factorization]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove the equivalence "trivial class group $\iff$ PID $\iff$ UFD" (the hard arrow UFD ⇒ PID using that dimension one makes every nonzero prime minimal, hence principal in a UFD), then compute $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ by showing $[\mathfrak{p}]$ has order $2$ — $\mathfrak{p}^2 = (2)$ principal but $\mathfrak{p}$ non-principal by the norm obstruction $a^2+5b^2 \neq 2$ ([[Def - Fractional Ideal and the Ideal Class Group]], [[Def - Dedekind Domain]], [[Def - Unique Factorization Domain]], [[Def - Principal Ideal Domain]], [[Thm - A Dedekind Domain has Unique Factorization of Ideals]]).
