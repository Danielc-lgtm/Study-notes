# Problem Statement

Construct a **Noetherian ring of infinite Krull dimension** (Nagata's example, ES4 Q13). This shows that, although Krull's height theorem makes every individual prime of a Noetherian ring have *finite* height, Noetherianity does *not* bound the dimension $\dim R = \sup_\mathfrak{p} \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}$.

The construction. Let $A = k[T_1, T_2, T_3, \dots]$ be the polynomial ring in countably many variables over a field $k$. Group the variables into consecutive blocks of growing length and let, for $i \geq 1$,
$$\mathfrak{p}_i = \big(T_{i^2}, T_{i^2 + 1}, \dots, T_{(i+1)^2 - 1}\big),$$
the prime generated by the $i$-th block of $2i + 1$ variables. Let $S = A \setminus \bigcup_{i \geq 1} \mathfrak{p}_i$. Prove:

1. $S$ is a multiplicative subset of $A$.
2. In the localization $R = S^{-1}A$, the extended ideal $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$ has height $2i + 1$, so $\dim R \geq 2i+1$ for all $i$, hence $\dim R = \infty$.
3. (Stated; the proof uses a local Noetherianity criterion) $R = S^{-1}A$ is Noetherian.

So $R$ is Noetherian with $\dim R = \infty$.

**Recall:**

The objects in play are Krull dimension and height, localization at a multiplicative set, and Krull's height theorem.

![[Def - Krull Dimension and Height#Krull dimension]]

![[Thm - Krull's Height Theorem (Principal Ideal Theorem)#Statement]]

Localization at a [[Def - Multiplicative Set and Localization|multiplicative set]] $S$: the primes of $S^{-1}A$ correspond bijectively, via extension $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto S^{-1}\mathfrak{q}$ and contraction, to the primes $\mathfrak{q}$ of $A$ with $\mathfrak{q} \cap S = \varnothing$. This correspondence preserves inclusions, so $\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{q} = \operatorname{ht}_A \mathfrak{q}$ whenever the chain below $\mathfrak{q}$ also avoids $S$.

The key tension to resolve — *Krull's height theorem bounds each height, not the dimension*: in a Noetherian ring $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} < \infty$ for every $\mathfrak{p}$ (a prime minimal over an $r$-generated ideal has height $\leq r$), yet $\dim R = \sup_\mathfrak{p} \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}$ can still be $\infty$ if the heights are unbounded.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *construct-a-counterexample* problem — exhibit a single object witnessing that two properties (Noetherian; finite-dimensional) are independent. The design principle is **engineering, not deduction**: you build a ring whose prime spectrum contains primes of every height $2i+1$ but is glued together so cleverly (by localizing away from the union of the blocks) that it remains Noetherian. The exercise is a sharp lesson in the *limits* of the structural theorems: Krull's height theorem gives a finite bound on each prime separately, and the construction shows that "each finite" does not upgrade to "uniformly bounded."

**Assumption pattern.** The construction is driven by a tension between two facts. Krull's height theorem says heights are finite *individually*; the supremum defining dimension can still diverge. The recognisable design trigger is "make a sequence of primes $\mathfrak{p}_i$ with $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}_i \to \infty$, but ensure the ring around them is Noetherian." The growing block sizes $2i+1$ are chosen so that $\mathfrak{p}_i$, generated by $2i+1$ variables in a polynomial ring, has height exactly $2i+1$ (a regular sequence of variables). Localizing at $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ makes each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ a maximal ideal of $R$ — it isolates the blocks so that the resulting local rings $R_{\mathfrak{p}_i} \cong (A_{\mathfrak{p}_i})$ are each Noetherian polynomial-ring localizations of finite dimension, while their dimensions are unbounded across $i$.

**Theorem routing.** The route is: (1) check $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ is multiplicative — equivalently, its complement $\bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ is closed under "being a non-product," which works because each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is prime and a product lands in $\bigcup \mathfrak{p}_i$ only if a factor does; (2) compute $\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = \operatorname{ht}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}} \mathfrak{p}_i A_{\mathfrak{p}_i} = 2i+1$, since $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is generated by $2i+1$ variables forming a regular sequence and is the localization at a "coordinate" prime of a polynomial ring; (3) conclude $\dim R \geq 2i+1$ for all $i$, so $\dim R = \infty$; (4) cite the local Noetherianity criterion (a ring is Noetherian if it is locally Noetherian and each element lies in finitely many maximal ideals) to get $R$ Noetherian — the part that requires the non-examinable lemma.

**Key decision point.** The crucial design insight is *why localization is the right move and growing blocks the right sizes*. The polynomial ring $A = k[T_1, T_2, \dots]$ in infinitely many variables already has infinite dimension, but it is *not* Noetherian (the ideal $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$ is not finitely generated). Localizing at $S$ surgically removes the bad maximal ideal $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$ — which meets $S$ and so disappears in $R$ — while keeping each finite block $\mathfrak{p}_i$ as a genuine maximal ideal of finite height. The growing sizes $2i+1$ (any sequence $\to \infty$ would do; $2i+1$ is chosen so the blocks partition $\{T_2, T_3, \dots\}$ cleanly) guarantee unbounded heights. Recognising that "localize to kill the infinitely-generated maximal ideal while preserving the finite-height primes" is the entire trick is the conceptual crux.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations from [[Commutative Algebra XII — Dimension Theory#Legal Operations|the topic page's Legal Operations]]:

1. **Form a multiplicative set as the complement of a union of primes.** $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ is multiplicative because each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is prime.

2. **Use the prime correspondence under localization.** Primes of $S^{-1}A$ are the primes of $A$ disjoint from $S$; this preserves inclusions and heights of avoiding chains.

3. **Compute the height of a coordinate prime in a polynomial ring.** $\mathfrak{p}_i$, generated by $2i+1$ variables, has height exactly $2i+1$ (regular sequence of variables; Krull's height theorem gives $\leq$, the chain of prefixes gives $\geq$).

4. **Bound dimension from below by a single prime's height.** $\dim R \geq \operatorname{ht} S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i+1$ for each $i$.

5. **Take a supremum over an unbounded family.** $\dim R = \sup_i \operatorname{ht} S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = \sup_i (2i+1) = \infty$.

6. **Invoke a local-to-global Noetherianity criterion.** Locally Noetherian plus "each element in finitely many maximal ideals" gives Noetherian.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> The polynomial ring $A = k[T_1, T_2, \dots]$ in infinitely many variables already has $\dim A = \infty$ (chains of coordinate primes grow without bound), but it fails to be Noetherian. The problem is the maximal ideal $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$, which is not finitely generated. How can you *remove* this bad ideal while keeping primes of unbounded but finite height?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Localize. Choose a family of primes $\mathfrak{p}_i$ of finite but unbounded height — the blocks of variables $\mathfrak{p}_i = (T_{i^2}, \dots, T_{(i+1)^2-1})$, of size $2i+1$ — and localize at $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$. Why is $S$ multiplicative? Use that each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is prime.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> After localizing, the surviving primes are those disjoint from $S$, i.e. contained in some $\mathfrak{p}_i$. The ideal $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$ is a maximal ideal of $R$. Its height: $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is generated by $2i+1$ variables, which form a regular sequence in a polynomial ring, so $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i+1$. Localizing does not change this height (the chain of prefix primes below $\mathfrak{p}_i$ also avoids $S$).

> [!note]- Hint 4
> So $\dim R \geq \operatorname{ht} S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i+1$ for *every* $i$, hence $\dim R = \infty$. The remaining (harder, non-examinable) point is that $R$ is Noetherian: each localization $R_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i}$ is a Noetherian local ring of finite dimension, every element of $R$ lies in only finitely many of the $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$, and a local Noetherianity criterion then upgrades "locally Noetherian" to "Noetherian."

# Solution

The construction is a piece of engineering: take the infinite-dimensional but non-Noetherian polynomial ring in countably many variables, and surgically localize away the single offending maximal ideal $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$, leaving a ring whose maximal ideals are the finite blocks $\mathfrak{p}_i$ of heights $2i+1 \to \infty$. The result is Noetherian (each local piece is a finite-dimensional polynomial localization) yet infinite-dimensional (the heights are unbounded).

**Step 1: $S = A \setminus \bigcup_{i \geq 1} \mathfrak{p}_i$ is a multiplicative set.**

Because each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is prime, the complement of their union is closed under multiplication and contains $1$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> $1 \in S$: the constant $1$ lies in no proper ideal, so in no $\mathfrak{p}_i$, hence $1 \in S$. For closure under products, take $s, t \in S$ and suppose, for contradiction, $st \notin S$, i.e. $st \in \mathfrak{p}_i$ for some $i$. Since $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is **prime**, $st \in \mathfrak{p}_i$ forces $s \in \mathfrak{p}_i$ or $t \in \mathfrak{p}_i$, contradicting $s, t \in S = A \setminus \bigcup_j \mathfrak{p}_j$. Hence $st \in S$. So $S$ is multiplicative. (This is the standard fact that a complement of a union of primes is multiplicative — exactly ES4 Q13(a).)

**Step 2: The localized blocks have height $\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i + 1$.**

Each $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is a coordinate prime generated by $2i+1$ variables; its height is $2i+1$, unchanged by localization.

> [!note]- Derivation
> First, $\mathfrak{p}_i \cap S = \varnothing$ (since $\mathfrak{p}_i \subseteq \bigcup_j \mathfrak{p}_j$, whose complement is $S$), so $\mathfrak{p}_i$ survives in $R$ and $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$ is prime, indeed maximal in $R$ (it is maximal among primes avoiding $S$). By the prime correspondence under [[Def - Multiplicative Set and Localization|localization]],
> $$\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = \sup\{\,d : \mathfrak{q}_0 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{q}_d = \mathfrak{p}_i,\ \mathfrak{q}_j \cap S = \varnothing\,\}.$$
> The block $\mathfrak{p}_i = (T_{i^2}, \dots, T_{(i+1)^2 - 1})$ is generated by exactly $2i+1$ variables (the indices run from $i^2$ to $(i+1)^2 - 1$, a count of $(i+1)^2 - i^2 = 2i+1$). In the polynomial ring $A$, these variables form a *regular sequence*, and the prefix chain
> $$(T_{i^2}) \subsetneq (T_{i^2}, T_{i^2+1}) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_i$$
> is a strictly increasing chain of primes of length $2i$ below $\mathfrak{p}_i$, augmented by $(0) \subsetneq (T_{i^2})$ to length $2i+1$; each of these prefix primes is contained in $\mathfrak{p}_i$, hence avoids $S$. So $\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i \geq 2i+1$. Conversely, $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$ is generated by the $2i+1$ images of the variables, so by [[Thm - Krull's Height Theorem (Principal Ideal Theorem)|Krull's height theorem]] (a prime that is minimal over — here equal to — a $(2i+1)$-generated ideal has height $\leq 2i+1$), $\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i \leq 2i+1$. Hence
> $$\operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i + 1.$$

**Step 3: $\dim R = \infty$.**

The heights $2i+1$ are unbounded, so their supremum — the dimension — is infinite.

> [!note]- Derivation
> By the definition of [[Def - Krull Dimension and Height|Krull dimension]] as a supremum of heights,
> $$\dim R = \sup_{\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{Spec} R} \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{q} \;\geq\; \sup_{i \geq 1} \operatorname{ht}_R S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i \;=\; \sup_{i \geq 1} (2i + 1) \;=\; \infty.$$
> So $\dim R = \infty$. Note that *each individual* prime still has finite height (consistent with Krull's height theorem); it is only the supremum across the family that diverges.

**Step 4: $R$ is Noetherian (cited).**

A local Noetherianity criterion upgrades the finite-dimensional Noetherian local pieces to a Noetherian ring.

> [!note]- Derivation
> This is the non-examinable part ES4 Q13(c). The maximal ideals of $R$ are exactly the $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$. For each $i$, the localization $R_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i} = A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ is the localization of the polynomial ring $A$ at the prime $\mathfrak{p}_i$; since $\mathfrak{p}_i$ involves only finitely many variables, $A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ is a **Noetherian local ring of finite dimension $2i+1$** (it is essentially a localization of a finitely-generated polynomial ring in the relevant variables, the others becoming units). Moreover every nonzero element of $A$ involves only finitely many variables, hence lies in only finitely many blocks $\mathfrak{p}_i$, so every element of $R$ lies in only finitely many maximal ideals $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$. The criterion (proved via a local condition for Noetherianity, as in Example Class 2): *a ring all of whose localizations at maximal ideals are Noetherian, and in which every element lies in only finitely many maximal ideals, is Noetherian* — applies and gives that $R$ is Noetherian. $\blacksquare$

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** $R = S^{-1}A$, with $A = k[T_1, T_2, \dots]$, $\mathfrak{p}_i = (T_{i^2}, \dots, T_{(i+1)^2-1})$, $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$, is Noetherian with $\dim R = \infty$.
>
> *(Multiplicative.)* $1 \in S$, and if $s,t \in S$ but $st \in \mathfrak{p}_i$, primeness gives $s$ or $t \in \mathfrak{p}_i$, a contradiction; so $st \in S$.
>
> *(Heights.)* $\mathfrak{p}_i$ avoids $S$, so $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i \in \operatorname{Spec} R$. It is generated by $2i+1$ variables; the prefix chain of coordinate primes below it has length $2i+1$, so $\operatorname{ht} S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i \geq 2i+1$; Krull's height theorem gives $\leq 2i+1$. Hence $\operatorname{ht} S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i = 2i+1$.
>
> *(Dimension.)* $\dim R \geq \sup_i (2i+1) = \infty$.
>
> *(Noetherian.)* Each $R_{S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i} = A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ is Noetherian local of finite dimension, and every element of $R$ lies in finitely many maximal ideals $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}_i$; by the local Noetherianity criterion, $R$ is Noetherian. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**"Each prime has finite height" does not upgrade to "the ring is finite-dimensional" — the supremum can diverge.** This is the precise content of Nagata's example and the reason it matters. Krull's height theorem is a genuinely strong finiteness result: in *any* Noetherian ring, every prime has finite height, because it is minimal over a finitely generated ideal and the number of generators caps the height. One might hope this forces $\dim R < \infty$. It does not: $\dim R = \sup_\mathfrak{p} \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}$ is a supremum of finite numbers, and a supremum of finite numbers can be infinite. The example realizes this by manufacturing primes $\mathfrak{p}_i$ of height $2i+1 \to \infty$. The lesson for spaced practice — and a general logical hygiene point — is to never conflate "each member of a family is finite" with "the family is bounded"; the gap between pointwise and uniform finiteness is exactly where this counterexample lives, and the same gap appears throughout analysis and logic.

**Localization is surgery on the spectrum: it deletes the primes that meet $S$ and keeps the rest, with heights intact.** The single most reusable technique here is *localizing to remove a bad prime*. The polynomial ring $A = k[T_1, T_2, \dots]$ is infinite-dimensional but non-Noetherian, the obstruction being the infinitely-generated maximal ideal $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$. Localizing at $S = A \setminus \bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ *deletes* every prime meeting $S$ — in particular $(T_1, T_2, \dots)$, which contains elements of $S$ — while *preserving* every prime contained in some $\mathfrak{p}_i$, with its height unchanged (the chains below it also avoid $S$). The result is a ring whose only maximal ideals are the well-behaved finite blocks. This "localize to excise the pathology" move is ubiquitous: it is how one passes to the smooth locus, how one inverts a non-zero-divisor to remove a component, and how one studies a variety near a point. The structural principle to remember: $\operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}A)$ is the subspace of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ of primes disjoint from $S$, and this subspace inherits all height relations among its members.

**Counterexamples are built, not found: engineer the spectrum you want, then localize to enforce Noetherianity.** The methodology of this problem is worth abstracting. To show two properties are independent, you design an object with the spectrum you want — here, primes of every height $2i+1$ — and then impose the second property (Noetherianity) by a controlled construction (localization at $S$) that does not disturb the first. The growing block sizes are a deliberate choice: any sequence tending to infinity would give unbounded heights; the squares $i^2$ are chosen only so the blocks tile $\{T_2, T_3, \dots\}$ exactly. This is the design philosophy behind most famous counterexamples in commutative algebra (non-catenary rings, non-finitely-generated integral closures, etc.): identify the obstruction, build a spectrum exhibiting it, and tune the construction so the desired good properties survive. When you encounter "does Noetherian imply $X$?", the productive instinct is to ask "can I build a spectrum violating $X$ and then localize/complete/quotient to restore Noetherianity?"

**This sharpens the geometric meaning of dimension and previews where it is well-behaved.** Geometrically, $\dim R = \infty$ would be impossible for a variety: any **bold plain text — finite-type scheme over a field** has finite dimension, because Noether normalization bounds it by the transcendence degree (see [[Ex - Dimension equals transcendence degree for a finitely generated domain]]). Nagata's ring is *not* of finite type over a field — it is a localization of a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables — which is exactly why it escapes the finiteness. So the example draws the boundary of good behaviour: dimension is finite and geometric for **bold plain text — finite-type schemes**, and the pathologies require leaving that category (infinitely many variables, then localizing). It also previews the subtlety of **bold plain text — catenary rings** and the dimension formula $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} + \dim R/\mathfrak{p} = \dim R$ (see [[Ex - Height plus dimension of the quotient equals dimension]]): that identity *fails* in general Noetherian rings precisely because of pathologies in the same family as Nagata's, and holds only for the well-behaved finitely-generated-algebra case. The example is the cautionary boundary marker for the whole theory.
