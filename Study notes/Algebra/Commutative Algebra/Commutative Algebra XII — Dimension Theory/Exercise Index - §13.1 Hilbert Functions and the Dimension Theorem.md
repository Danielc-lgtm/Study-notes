## §13.1 Hilbert Functions and the Dimension Theorem — Exercises

The exercises of §13.1 drill the *analytic* face of dimension: the Hilbert function $n \mapsto \ell(M_n)$, its generating function (the Poincaré series), the polynomial it eventually equals (the Hilbert polynomial), and the way the **degree of that polynomial recovers the dimension**. The central computational skill is reading the two geometric numbers — dimension (the degree of $\mathrm{HP}$) and degree (its normalized leading coefficient) — off an asymptotic length-count, and the central conceptual skill is the [[Thm - The Dimension Theorem for Noetherian Local Rings|dimension theorem]]'s identification of three a priori unrelated integers: the chain-length $\dim A$, the Hilbert–Samuel growth degree $d(G_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))$, and the minimal number $\delta(A)$ of generators of an $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal. The exercises also establish the *geometric* identification $\dim A = \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$ that makes dimension computable by counting independent coordinates, the bridge from the local Hilbert-function machinery to the global theory of varieties.

- [[Ex - The Hilbert polynomial of projective space]] (⭐⭐) — compute the Hilbert function, Hilbert polynomial, and Poincaré series of $S = k[T_0,\dots,T_n]$ and read off the geometry: count degree-$n$ monomials by stars and bars to get $\dim_k S_d = \binom{n+d}{n}$, recognise this as the degree-$n$ numerical polynomial $\mathrm{HP}_S(T) = \binom{T+n}{n}$ with leading coefficient $1/n!$, sum the binomial series to $P(S,T) = (1-T)^{-(n+1)}$ with pole order $n+1 = \dim S$, and decode $\deg \mathrm{HP}_S = n = \dim \mathbb{P}^n$ and $\deg \mathbb{P}^n = 1$ — keeping straight the off-by-one between the affine cone ($\dim n+1$) and the projective variety ($\dim n$) ([[Def - The Hilbert Function and Hilbert Polynomial]], [[Thm - The Hilbert Polynomial]], [[Thm - Hilbert-Serre and Rationality of the Poincare Series]], [[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module]], [[Def - Composition Series and Length]]).

- [[Ex - Dimension equals transcendence degree for a finitely generated domain]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove $\dim A = \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$ for a finitely generated $k$-algebra domain by routing both invariants through a polynomial subring: Noether-normalize to get a finite integral injection $k[Y_1,\dots,Y_d] \hookrightarrow A$, observe integrality forces $d = \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$ (the fraction field is algebraic over $k(Y_1,\dots,Y_d)$) and $\dim A = \dim k[Y_1,\dots,Y_d] = d$ (lying-over, going-up, incomparability), then chain the equalities — the technique being "find a common normal-form model on which both invariants are obvious and a structural relation that transports both unchanged" ([[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Thm - Noether Normalization]], [[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]], [[Thm - Dimension of a Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]]).

- [[Ex - The dimension of a polynomial ring is n]] (⭐⭐) — establish $\dim k[T_1,\dots,T_n] = n$ by a two-sided squeeze: the prefix chain $(0) \subsetneq (T_1) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (T_1,\dots,T_n)$ (the algebraic shadow of the coordinate-subspace flag) gives $\dim \geq n$, and the inequality $\dim A \leq \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$ applied with the variables as a transcendence basis gives $\dim \leq n$ — the foundational dimension computation against which the entire subject is calibrated, and the base case that, via Noether normalization, computes the dimension of every finitely generated $k$-algebra ([[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Thm - Dimension of a Polynomial Ring]], [[Thm - Noether Normalization]], [[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]]).
