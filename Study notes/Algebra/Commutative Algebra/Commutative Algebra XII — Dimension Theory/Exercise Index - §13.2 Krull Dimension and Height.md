## §13.2 Krull Dimension and Height — Exercises

The exercises of §13.2 drill the *combinatorial* face of dimension — chains of prime ideals — and its geometric reading: **dimension is the length of the longest chain, height is codimension, and one equation drops dimension by exactly one**. The central skill is the two-sided squeeze that pins a height or dimension to an exact value, with [[Thm - Krull's Height Theorem (Principal Ideal Theorem)|Krull's height theorem]] supplying the upper bound (number of generators caps height) and an explicit chain or a non-zero-divisor argument supplying the lower bound. The exercises run from the foundational computation $\dim k[T_1,\dots,T_n] = n$, through the principal ideal theorem (the algebraic seed of divisor theory), to the dimension formula $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} + \dim A/\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ that makes height literally codimension — and they mark the boundary of good behaviour with Nagata's Noetherian ring of *infinite* dimension, which shows that "every height is finite" does not upgrade to "the dimension is finite."

- [[Ex - The dimension of a polynomial ring is n]] (⭐⭐) — prove $\dim k[T_1,\dots,T_n] = n$ by squeezing the explicit prefix chain $(0) \subsetneq (T_1) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (T_1,\dots,T_n)$ (lower bound, the coordinate-subspace flag) against the inequality $\dim A \leq \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$ with the variables as a transcendence basis (upper bound), the calibrating dimension computation of the whole subject ([[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Thm - Dimension of a Polynomial Ring]], [[Thm - Noether Normalization]], [[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]]).

- [[Ex - Krull's principal ideal theorem and hypersurfaces]] (⭐⭐) — show a non-zero-divisor's minimal primes have height *exactly* $1$ by a two-hypothesis squeeze: Krull's height theorem with $r = 1$ gives $\operatorname{ht} \leq 1$, and the non-zero-divisor hypothesis — via "every element of a minimal prime is a zero divisor" — forces the prime to sit strictly above a minimal prime, giving $\operatorname{ht} \geq 1$, the geometric statement that a hypersurface has pure codimension one and the algebraic seed of divisor theory ([[Thm - Krull's Height Theorem (Principal Ideal Theorem)]], [[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Def - Associated and Minimal Primes]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field]]).

- [[Ex - Height plus dimension of the quotient equals dimension]] (⭐⭐) — prove $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} + \dim A/\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ for finitely generated $k$-algebra domains, identifying height with codimension: the free inequality $\leq$ comes from concatenating a chain below $\mathfrak{p}$ with one above, and the content $\geq$ is the **catenary** property (ES4 Q3a) that every saturated chain reaches the full length $\dim A$, proved via $\dim = \operatorname{trdeg}$ and going-down — with the cautionary failure in $\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}[T]$ showing the hypotheses are load-bearing ([[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Ex - Dimension equals transcendence degree for a finitely generated domain]], [[Thm - Noether Normalization]], [[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]]).

- [[Ex - A Noetherian ring of infinite dimension]] (⭐⭐⭐) — construct Nagata's example, a Noetherian ring with $\dim R = \infty$, by localizing the polynomial ring $k[T_1, T_2, \dots]$ at the complement of $\bigcup_i \mathfrak{p}_i$ (blocks of $2i+1$ variables) to excise the infinitely-generated bad maximal ideal while preserving coordinate primes of unbounded height $2i+1$, the sharp lesson that "each height finite" (Krull's height theorem) does not give "dimension finite" because a supremum of finite numbers can diverge ([[Def - Krull Dimension and Height]], [[Thm - Krull's Height Theorem (Principal Ideal Theorem)]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Multiplicative Set and Localization]], [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal]]).
