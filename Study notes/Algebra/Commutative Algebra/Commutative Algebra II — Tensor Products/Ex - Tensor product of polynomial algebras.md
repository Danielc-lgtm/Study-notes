# Problem Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Prove the $R$-algebra isomorphism
$$R[X_1,\dots,X_n]\ \otimes_R\ R[T_1,\dots,T_r]\ \cong\ R[X_1,\dots,X_n, T_1,\dots,T_r],$$
given on pure tensors by $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, in **two ways**: (i) by exhibiting an $R$-module isomorphism on the monomial basis and upgrading it to an algebra map; (ii) by recognising the right-hand side as the **coproduct** of the two polynomial algebras and invoking the universal property.

Deduce the **quotient version**: for ideals $I\trianglelefteq R[X_*]$, $J\trianglelefteq R[T_*]$,
$$R[X_*]/I\ \otimes_R\ R[T_*]/J\ \cong\ R[X_*, T_*]/(I^e + J^e),$$
where $I^e, J^e$ denote the extensions of $I, J$ to $R[X_*, T_*]$. As a concrete instance, $\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]/(f,g)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[W,U]/(h)\cong\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z,W,U]/(f,g,h)$.

**Recall:**

![[Def - Tensor Product of Algebras#The Definition]]

A **monomial** in $R[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ is a product $X_1^{e_1}\cdots X_n^{e_n}$ (no coefficient); the monomials form an $R$-basis of the polynomial ring ([[Def - Free Module|free module]] of countable rank). The **upgrading lemma**: an $R$-linear map $\varphi : A\to A'$ between $R$-algebras is an $R$-algebra homomorphism provided $\varphi(1) = 1$ and $\varphi$ is multiplicative on a set generating $A$ as an $R$-module.

![[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Algebras#Statement]]

For the quotient version, $I^e$ is the ideal of $R[X_*, T_*]$ generated by the image of $I$, and the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient rule]] for tensor products carries $L$ (the submodule generated by $I\otimes\text{-} $ and $\text{-}\otimes J$) onto $I^e + J^e$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is the headline *identify-a-tensor-of-algebras* computation: tensoring polynomial algebras glues their variable sets. As the [[Commutative Algebra II — Tensor Products#Problem-Solving Strategy|topic strategy]] records, algebra problems route through the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Algebras|coproduct universal property]], and the upgrading lemma reduces algebra-map checks to generators.

**Assumption pattern.** The trigger is *both factors are polynomial algebras (or quotients of them)* over a common base $R$. A polynomial algebra is free on monomials and is the free $R$-algebra on its variables — two facts that drive the two proofs: freeness on monomials gives the module isomorphism (route i), and "free on variables" makes the joint polynomial ring the coproduct (route ii).

**Theorem routing.** Route (i): the monomials $a\otimes b$ (with $a, b$ monomials) form an $R$-basis of the LHS ([[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|free tensor]]), and the monomials of $R[X_*, T_*]$ form an $R$-basis of the RHS; the assignment $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$ is a bijection of bases, hence an $R$-module isomorphism, upgraded to an algebra map by the upgrading lemma. Route (ii): the joint polynomial ring receives maps from each factor (universal property of polynomial rings) and is universal among such, so it *is* the coproduct, hence $\cong$ the tensor product by [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Algebras|uniqueness of the coproduct]] — and this route hands over the formula $\varphi(a\otimes b) = ab$ for free. The quotient version applies the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient rule]] and tracks $L\mapsto I^e + J^e$.

**Key decision point.** The non-obvious recognition is that *the right-hand polynomial ring is literally the coproduct* — a fact that, once seen, makes route (ii) almost contentless and is the "right" proof. Route (i) is more hands-on but requires checking multiplicativity ($\varphi(a_1 a_2\otimes b_1 b_2) = a_1 a_2 b_1 b_2 = (a_1 b_1)(a_2 b_2) = \varphi(a_1\otimes b_1)\varphi(a_2\otimes b_2)$) on monomial generators, which is where the upgrading lemma earns its place. The genuine insight is that "gluing variable sets" and "taking the coproduct of algebras" are the same operation, and geometrically both are "taking the product of affine spaces" $\mathbb{A}^n\times\mathbb{A}^r = \mathbb{A}^{n+r}$.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations from [[Commutative Algebra II — Tensor Products#Legal Operations|the topic page's Legal Operations]]:

1. **Invoke the coproduct universal property for algebras (operation 9).** Route (ii) identifies $R[X_*, T_*]$ as the coproduct directly.

2. **Upgrade an $R$-linear map to an algebra map on generators (operation 10).** Route (i) checks multiplicativity on monomials only.

3. **Use that pure tensors of generators (monomials) form a basis (operation 6).** The monomials $a\otimes b$ are an $R$-basis of the LHS.

4. **Push $\otimes$ through a quotient (operation 5).** The quotient version applies $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ and tracks $L\mapsto I^e + J^e$.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> The polynomial ring $R[X_*]$ is free over $R$ on the monomials $X_1^{a_1}\cdots X_n^{a_n}$. So the LHS $R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]$ is free on the tensors $a\otimes b$ with $a, b$ monomials. What is an obvious $R$-basis of the RHS $R[X_*, T_*]$?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> The monomials $X^a T^b$ form an $R$-basis of $R[X_*, T_*]$. Map $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$ (a monomial in the $X$'s times a monomial in the $T$'s = a monomial in all variables). This is a bijection of bases, hence an $R$-module isomorphism. Now upgrade it: check $1\otimes1\mapsto 1$ and multiplicativity on monomial generators.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> Slicker: $R[X_*, T_*]$ is the *free $R$-algebra on the variables $X_*, T_*$*. A map out of it is a free choice of images of all variables — which is exactly a pair of maps, one from $R[X_*]$ and one from $R[T_*]$. So $R[X_*, T_*]$ is the coproduct of $R[X_*]$ and $R[T_*]$, hence $\cong$ their tensor product.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> For the quotient version, tensor the quotients: $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ with $L$ generated by $I\otimes R[T_*]$ and $R[X_*]\otimes J$. Under the isomorphism $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, this $L$ maps onto the ideal generated by $I$ and $J$ in $R[X_*, T_*]$, namely $I^e + J^e$.

# Solution

The proof gives two constructions of the same isomorphism and then the quotient corollary. Step 1 (route i) builds the module isomorphism on monomials and upgrades it. Step 2 (route ii) recognises the coproduct and reads off the formula. Step 3 derives the quotient version. The non-obvious move is seeing the joint polynomial ring *as* the coproduct, which makes route (ii) the conceptually right proof and hands over the explicit map.

**Step 1 (route i): Monomial-basis isomorphism, upgraded.**

$\varphi : a\otimes b\mapsto ab$ is an $R$-module isomorphism on monomials, and an $R$-algebra isomorphism by the upgrading lemma.

> [!note]- Derivation
> The monomials $\{X^a := X_1^{a_1}\cdots X_n^{a_n}\}$ form an $R$-basis of $R[X_*]$, and $\{T^b\}$ of $R[T_*]$. By the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|free-tensor corollary]], $\{X^a\otimes T^b\}$ is an $R$-basis of $R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]$. The monomials $\{X^a T^b\}$ form an $R$-basis of $R[X_*, T_*]$.
>
> Define $\varphi$ on the basis by $\varphi(X^a\otimes T^b) = X^a T^b$, extended $R$-linearly. This sends a basis bijectively to a basis, so $\varphi$ is an $R$-**module** isomorphism.
>
> *Upgrade to an algebra map.* $\varphi(1\otimes 1) = 1$. For monomial generators $a_1, a_2\in R[X_*]$, $b_1, b_2\in R[T_*]$,
> $$\varphi\big((a_1\otimes b_1)(a_2\otimes b_2)\big) = \varphi(a_1 a_2\otimes b_1 b_2) = a_1 a_2 b_1 b_2 = (a_1 b_1)(a_2 b_2) = \varphi(a_1\otimes b_1)\varphi(a_2\otimes b_2),$$
> using commutativity to reorder $a_1 a_2 b_1 b_2 = a_1 b_1 a_2 b_2$. Since the monomial tensors $a\otimes b$ generate $R[X_*]\otimes R[T_*]$ as an $R$-module, the upgrading lemma makes $\varphi$ an $R$-**algebra** homomorphism, hence (being a bijection) an isomorphism.

**Step 2 (route ii): The joint polynomial ring is the coproduct.**

$R[X_*, T_*]$ satisfies the coproduct universal property of $R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]$, so they are isomorphic by $p\otimes q\mapsto pq$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $j_A : R[X_*]\hookrightarrow R[X_*, T_*]$ and $j_B : R[T_*]\hookrightarrow R[X_*, T_*]$ be the inclusions. Given any $R$-algebra $C$ and $R$-algebra maps $f_1 : R[X_*]\to C$, $f_2 : R[T_*]\to C$, the data is exactly the images $f_1(X_i), f_2(T_j)\in C$. By the [[Def - Polynomial Ring|universal property of the polynomial ring]] $R[X_*, T_*]$ (a free $R$-algebra), there is a *unique* $R$-algebra map $h : R[X_*, T_*]\to C$ with $h(X_i) = f_1(X_i)$ and $h(T_j) = f_2(T_j)$ — equivalently $h\circ j_A = f_1$, $h\circ j_B = f_2$.
>
> So $(R[X_*, T_*], j_A, j_B)$ is a coproduct of $R[X_*]$ and $R[T_*]$ over $R$. By the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Algebras|uniqueness of the coproduct]], the unique $R$-algebra isomorphism $\varphi : R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]\to R[X_*, T_*]$ satisfies $\varphi(p\otimes q) = j_A(p)j_B(q) = pq$. This *both* proves the isomorphism *and* hands over its formula on pure tensors — the advantage of the coproduct route over hand computation.

**Step 3: The quotient version.**

$R[X_*]/I\otimes_R R[T_*]/J\cong R[X_*, T_*]/(I^e + J^e)$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Apply the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient rule]] $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ with $M = R[X_*]$, $M' = I$, $N = R[T_*]$, $N' = J$. Here $L\leq R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]$ is generated by
> $$\{p\otimes q : p\in I, q\in R[T_*]\}\ \cup\ \{g\otimes h : g\in R[X_*], h\in J\}.$$
> Under the isomorphism $\varphi(a\otimes b) = ab$ from Step 1/2, the first set maps onto $\{pq : p\in I\}$, generating the extension $I^e$ (the ideal of $R[X_*, T_*]$ generated by $I$), and the second onto $J^e$. So $\varphi(L) = I^e + J^e$, and
> $$R[X_*]/I\otimes_R R[T_*]/J\cong\big(R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]\big)/L\cong R[X_*, T_*]/(I^e + J^e).$$
> *Concrete instance.* With $R = \mathbb{C}$, $I = (f, g)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]$, $J = (h)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[W,U]$:
> $$\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]/(f,g)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[W,U]/(h)\cong\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z,W,U]/(f,g,h),$$
> the pure tensor $(p+(f,g))\otimes(q+(h))\mapsto pq + (f,g,h)$.

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** $R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]\cong R[X_*, T_*]$ as $R$-algebras, $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$.
>
> *Module isomorphism.* Monomials $X^a$ form an $R$-basis of $R[X_*]$ and $T^b$ of $R[T_*]$, so $\{X^a\otimes T^b\}$ is a basis of the tensor product; $\varphi(X^a\otimes T^b) = X^a T^b$ maps it bijectively to the monomial basis of $R[X_*, T_*]$, an $R$-module isomorphism.
>
> *Algebra isomorphism.* $\varphi(1\otimes1) = 1$ and $\varphi(a_1 a_2\otimes b_1 b_2) = a_1 a_2 b_1 b_2 = \varphi(a_1\otimes b_1)\varphi(a_2\otimes b_2)$ on monomials; the upgrading lemma makes $\varphi$ an algebra isomorphism. (Equivalently: $R[X_*, T_*]$ is the coproduct of $R[X_*]$ and $R[T_*]$ — a map out of it is a free choice of images of all variables, i.e. a pair of algebra maps — so $\cong R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]$ with $p\otimes q\mapsto pq$.)
>
> *Quotient version.* The quotient rule gives $R[X_*]/I\otimes R[T_*]/J\cong(R[X_*]\otimes R[T_*])/L$ with $L = \langle I\otimes R[T_*], R[X_*]\otimes J\rangle$; under $\varphi$, $L\mapsto I^e + J^e$, so the left side $\cong R[X_*, T_*]/(I^e+J^e)$. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**Tensoring polynomial algebras glues their variable sets — and that is the coproduct, hence the product of affine spaces.** The cleanest lesson is the slogan $R[X_*]\otimes_R R[T_*]\cong R[X_*, T_*]$: the tensor product over $R$ *unions the variables*. The conceptual upgrade is recognising *why* — the joint polynomial ring is the **coproduct** of the two factors, because a polynomial ring is the free $R$-algebra on its variables, so a map out of it is a free choice of variable images, i.e. a pair of algebra maps. The trigger for spaced recall: whenever you tensor free algebras (polynomial rings, free commutative algebras), the answer is the free algebra on the disjoint union of generators, no computation required. Geometrically this is $\mathbb{A}^n\times\mathbb{A}^r = \mathbb{A}^{n+r}$, and via $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R B) = \operatorname{Spec}A\times_{\operatorname{Spec}R}\operatorname{Spec}B$ it is the statement that the product of affine spaces is again affine space.

**The coproduct route hands over the formula; the monomial route confirms it concretely.** This exercise is a clean illustration that universal properties do more than prove existence — route (ii) gives not just "there is an isomorphism" but the explicit $\varphi(p\otimes q) = pq$, because the uniqueness clause of the coproduct property pins the map down. The transferable principle: when an isomorphism is wanted *with a specific formula on generators*, the universal-property route delivers both at once, whereas the hands-on basis route delivers the formula by construction but costs an upgrading check. The two routes are worth holding together — the basis route makes the gluing visible and is the right tool when you need to *count* (dimensions, ranks), while the coproduct route is the right tool when you need to *map out* of the tensor product or recognise it abstractly.

**Tensoring quotient algebras adds the relation ideals: $L\mapsto I^e + J^e$.** The quotient version is the workhorse for geometry: $R[X_*]/I\otimes R[T_*]/J\cong R[X_*, T_*]/(I^e + J^e)$ says the product variety is cut out by the *union* of the two defining systems. The mechanism mirrors the cyclic case $R/I\otimes R/J\cong R/(I+J)$ — tensoring *adds the ideals* — now with the extensions $I^e, J^e$ because the variables live in disjoint sets. The diagnostic for spaced practice: to find the coordinate ring of a product of varieties $V(f_1,\dots)\times V(g_1,\dots)$, tensor the coordinate rings and read off the relations as the *combined* system $(f_1,\dots,g_1,\dots)$. This is the algebraic computation behind products of affine varieties (see [[Ex - C tensor R C is not a field]] for the subtler case where base change *splits* a factor, and [[Commutative Algebra VII — Noether Normalization and the Nullstellensatz|the Nullstellensatz chapter]] for the variety dictionary).
