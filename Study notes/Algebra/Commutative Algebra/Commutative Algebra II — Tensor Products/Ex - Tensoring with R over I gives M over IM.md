# Problem Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $I\trianglelefteq R$ an [[Def - Ideal|ideal]], and $M$ an $R$-module. Let $IM = \{\sum_k a_k m_k : a_k\in I,\ m_k\in M\}$ be the submodule of $M$ generated by products $am$ ($a\in I$, $m\in M$). Prove that there is an isomorphism of $R$-modules (indeed of $R/I$-modules)
$$(R/I)\otimes_R M\ \cong\ M/IM, \qquad (\bar r\otimes m)\mapsto\overline{rm}.$$
Deduce the special cases $(\mathbb{Z}/n)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^k\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n)^k$ and, for the localization-style quotient, $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^n\cong\mathbb{C}^n$ via a different ideal.

**Recall:**

The objects in play are the tensor product, the quotient module, the submodule $IM$, and the quotient isomorphism for tensor products.

![[Def - Tensor Product of Modules#The Definition]]

![[Def - Quotient Module#The Definition]]

The submodule $IM\leq M$ is generated by all $am$ with $a\in I$, $m\in M$; the quotient $M/IM$ has the property that $\overline{am} = 0$ for every $a\in I$, $m\in M$. The tensor relations used are $r(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar r\otimes m = \bar1\otimes rm$ (scalars slide), where $\bar r$ denotes $r + I$.

This is the simplest instance of [[Def - Restriction and Extension of Scalars|extension of scalars]] along the quotient map $R\to R/I$: "tensoring $M$ with the residue ring $R/I$" is the same as "reducing $M$ modulo $I$". The general [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient isomorphism]] specialises here.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is an *identify-a-tensor-product-concretely* problem, and it produces the single most-used computation in commutative algebra: tensoring with $R/I$ reduces a module mod $I$. As the [[Commutative Algebra II — Tensor Products#Problem-Solving Strategy|topic strategy]] records, a factor presented as a quotient ($R/I$) routes through the quotient isomorphism; here the result is a clean recognition formula.

**Assumption pattern.** The recognisable trigger is *one factor is $R/I$*, a quotient ring. The element $\bar1$ generates $R/I$, so $(R/I)\otimes_R M$ is generated by tensors $\bar1\otimes m$, and scalar-sliding gives $\bar r\otimes m = r(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar1\otimes rm$. The whole tensor product is thus "$M$ with the $I$-action collapsed", because $\bar a\otimes m = a(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar1\otimes am$ but $\bar a = 0$ for $a\in I$ — exactly the relations cut by $IM$.

**Theorem routing.** Two routes. The *bare-hands* route builds the isomorphism directly: a bilinear map $(R/I)\times M\to M/IM$, $(\bar r, m)\mapsto\overline{rm}$, and an inverse $M/IM\to(R/I)\otimes M$, $\overline m\mapsto\bar1\otimes m$, via the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property]] and the universal property of the [[Def - Quotient Module|quotient]]. The *structural* route specialises the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient isomorphism]] $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ with $M = R$, $M' = I$, $N = M$, $N' = 0$, then uses $R\otimes_R M\cong M$.

**Key decision point.** The non-obvious moves are two. First, *the inverse map must kill $IM$*: $\overline m\mapsto\bar1\otimes m$ is only well-defined on $M/IM$ because $\bar1\otimes am = \bar a\otimes m = \bar 0\otimes m = 0$ for $a\in I$ — so $IM$ maps to $0$, exactly licensing the descent to the quotient. Second, *the forward map must kill the tensor relations*: $(\bar r, m)\mapsto\overline{rm}$ being well-defined on $R/I$ (not just $R$) needs $\overline{am} = 0$ for $a\in I$, which is true precisely in $M/IM$. The two well-definedness checks are mirror images, and recognising that $IM$ on one side corresponds to "$\bar a = 0$" on the other is the heart of the matching.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations from [[Commutative Algebra II — Tensor Products#Legal Operations|the topic page's Legal Operations]]:

1. **Push $\otimes$ through a quotient (operation 5).** This *is* the content: $R/I\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$ is the quotient isomorphism in its most useful special case.

2. **Replace a bilinear map by a linear map out of the tensor product (operation 2).** Build the forward map from the bilinear $(\bar r, m)\mapsto\overline{rm}$ via the universal property.

3. **Slide scalars across the $\otimes$ (operation 1).** $\bar r\otimes m = r(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar1\otimes rm$, and $\bar a\otimes m = \bar1\otimes am$ for $a\in I$ shows the $I$-action is what collapses.

4. **Build an isomorphism by maps both ways on pure tensors (operation 4).** Check the forward and inverse maps compose to the identity on $\bar1\otimes m$ and on $\overline m$.

# Hints

> [!note]- Hint 1
> The factor $R/I$ is cyclic, generated by $\bar1$. So every element of $(R/I)\otimes_R M$ is a sum of tensors $\bar1\otimes m$ (slide the scalar: $\bar r\otimes m = \bar1\otimes rm$). Guess that the tensor product is just $M$ with something killed — which $m$'s should become $0$?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> For $a\in I$, $\bar a = 0$ in $R/I$, so $\bar1\otimes am = \bar a\otimes m = \bar0\otimes m = 0$. Thus every element of $IM$ becomes $0$. This suggests $(R/I)\otimes M\cong M/IM$. Build the map $\overline m\mapsto\bar1\otimes m$ and check it kills $IM$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> For the reverse direction, define $b : (R/I)\times M\to M/IM$ by $b(\bar r, m) = \overline{rm}$. Check it is well-defined on $R/I$ (needs $\overline{am} = 0$ for $a\in I$, true in $M/IM$) and bilinear, then invoke the universal property to get $(R/I)\otimes M\to M/IM$. Verify the two maps are mutually inverse on generators.

> [!note]- Hint 4
> Slick route: the quotient isomorphism $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ with $M = R$, $M' = I$, $N = M$, $N' = 0$ gives $(R/I)\otimes M\cong(R\otimes M)/L$ where $L$ is generated by $\{a\otimes m : a\in I\}$. Now use $R\otimes_R M\cong M$, under which $L\mapsto IM$.

# Solution

The proof builds the isomorphism in both directions and checks they invert, then records the structural one-liner. Step 1 establishes that the tensor product is generated by tensors $\bar1\otimes m$ and that $IM$ is killed; Step 2 builds the forward map $(R/I)\otimes M\to M/IM$; Step 3 builds the inverse $M/IM\to(R/I)\otimes M$ and checks the round trips; Step 4 gives the quotient-isomorphism derivation and the corollaries. The non-obvious move is recognising that the two well-definedness checks — "the forward map kills the tensor relations on $R/I$" and "the inverse kills $IM$" — are the same fact $\bar a\otimes m = \bar1\otimes am$ read both ways.

**Step 1: Every element is a sum of $\bar1\otimes m$, and $IM$ dies.**

$(R/I)\otimes_R M = \{\bar1\otimes m' : m'\in M\}^{\text{-span}}$, and $\bar1\otimes(am) = 0$ for $a\in I$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Pure tensors $\bar r\otimes m$ generate. Sliding the scalar, $\bar r\otimes m = r(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar1\otimes rm$, so every pure tensor equals $\bar1\otimes(rm)$ for some element $rm\in M$. As these are closed under addition ($\bar1\otimes m_1 + \bar1\otimes m_2 = \bar1\otimes(m_1+m_2)$), every element of $(R/I)\otimes M$ has the form $\bar1\otimes m'$ for a single $m'\in M$.
>
> For $a\in I$ and $m\in M$, $\bar a = 0$ in $R/I$, so
> $$\bar1\otimes(am) = (\bar a)\otimes m = \bar0\otimes m = 0.$$
> By additivity, $\bar1\otimes x = 0$ for every $x\in IM$. So the map $m\mapsto\bar1\otimes m$ factors through $M/IM$.

**Step 2: The forward map $(R/I)\otimes_R M\to M/IM$.**

$(\bar r, m)\mapsto\overline{rm}$ is well-defined and bilinear, inducing $\varphi$ with $\varphi(\bar r\otimes m) = \overline{rm}$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Define $b : (R/I)\times M\to M/IM$ by $b(\bar r, m) = \overline{rm}$ (the class of $rm$ in $M/IM$).
>
> *Well-defined on $R/I$.* If $\bar r = \bar r'$, then $r - r'\in I$, so $(r-r')m\in IM$, hence $\overline{rm} = \overline{r'm}$ in $M/IM$. So $b$ does not depend on the representative of $\bar r$.
>
> *Bilinear.* $b(\bar r+\bar r', m) = \overline{(r+r')m} = \overline{rm}+\overline{r'm}$; $b(\bar r, m+m') = \overline{r(m+m')} = \overline{rm}+\overline{rm'}$; and $b(s\bar r, m) = \overline{srm} = s\,\overline{rm} = b(\bar r, sm)$ for $s\in R$.
>
> By the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property]], $b$ induces an $R$-linear $\varphi : (R/I)\otimes_R M\to M/IM$ with $\varphi(\bar r\otimes m) = \overline{rm}$.

**Step 3: The inverse map and the round trips.**

$\psi : M/IM\to(R/I)\otimes_R M$, $\overline m\mapsto\bar1\otimes m$, is a well-defined inverse of $\varphi$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Define $\psi_0 : M\to(R/I)\otimes_R M$, $\psi_0(m) = \bar1\otimes m$ ($R$-linear). By Step 1, $\psi_0(IM) = 0$, so $\psi_0$ factors through $M/IM$, giving $\psi : M/IM\to(R/I)\otimes M$, $\psi(\overline m) = \bar1\otimes m$.
>
> *Round trip 1.* $\varphi\psi(\overline m) = \varphi(\bar1\otimes m) = \overline{1\cdot m} = \overline m$. Since the $\overline m$ generate $M/IM$, $\varphi\psi = \operatorname{id}_{M/IM}$.
>
> *Round trip 2.* On a generating tensor $\bar1\otimes m$ (Step 1 shows these generate): $\psi\varphi(\bar1\otimes m) = \psi(\overline m) = \bar1\otimes m$. So $\psi\varphi = \operatorname{id}_{(R/I)\otimes M}$.
>
> Hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\psi$. It is $R/I$-linear: $\varphi(\bar s\cdot(\bar r\otimes m)) = \varphi(\overline{sr}\otimes m) = \overline{srm} = \bar s\cdot\overline{rm}$.

**Step 4: The structural derivation and corollaries.**

The quotient isomorphism gives the same result in one line, and the corollaries follow.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Apply the [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient isomorphism]] $(M_0/M_0')\otimes(N_0/N_0')\cong(M_0\otimes N_0)/L$ with $M_0 = R$, $M_0' = I$, $N_0 = M$, $N_0' = 0$. Here $L\leq R\otimes_R M$ is generated by $\{a\otimes m : a\in I, m\in M\}$. Under the identity isomorphism $R\otimes_R M\cong M$, $r\otimes m\mapsto rm$, the generator $a\otimes m\mapsto am$, so $L$ maps onto $IM$. Therefore
> $$(R/I)\otimes_R M\cong(R\otimes_R M)/L\cong M/IM.$$
>
> *Corollary 1.* Take $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $I = n\mathbb{Z}$, $M = \mathbb{Z}^k$. Then $IM = n\mathbb{Z}^k$, so $(\mathbb{Z}/n)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^k\cong\mathbb{Z}^k/n\mathbb{Z}^k\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n)^k$.
>
> *Corollary 2.* For $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^n$, this is extension along $\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$, not a quotient — but distributivity plus $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{C}$ gives $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^n\cong\mathbb{C}^n$ (see [[Ex - Extension of scalars of a free module]]). The two corollaries illustrate the two faces of extension of scalars: *reducing* (along $R\to R/I$, $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n$) and *enlarging* (along $\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$).

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** $(R/I)\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$ via $\bar r\otimes m\mapsto\overline{rm}$.
>
> *Forward.* $b(\bar r, m) = \overline{rm}$ is well-defined on $R/I$ (if $r-r'\in I$ then $(r-r')m\in IM$) and bilinear, so induces $\varphi : (R/I)\otimes M\to M/IM$, $\varphi(\bar r\otimes m) = \overline{rm}$.
>
> *Inverse.* $\psi_0(m) = \bar1\otimes m$ is $R$-linear and kills $IM$ (for $a\in I$: $\bar1\otimes am = \bar a\otimes m = 0$), so descends to $\psi : M/IM\to(R/I)\otimes M$, $\psi(\overline m) = \bar1\otimes m$.
>
> *Inverses.* $\varphi\psi(\overline m) = \overline m$ and $\psi\varphi(\bar1\otimes m) = \bar1\otimes m$ on generators (every element of $(R/I)\otimes M$ is $\bar1\otimes m'$), so $\varphi, \psi$ are mutually inverse $R/I$-linear isomorphisms.
>
> *Corollaries.* $(\mathbb{Z}/n)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^k\cong\mathbb{Z}^k/n\mathbb{Z}^k = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^k$; and $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^n\cong\mathbb{C}^n$ by distributivity and $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{C}$. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**"Tensor with $R/I$" means "reduce mod $I$" — the single most useful tensor computation.** This isomorphism is the workhorse of the entire subject, and it deserves to be a reflex: the instant you see $(R/I)\otimes_R M$, you write $M/IM$. The mechanism is that $R/I$ contributes nothing but the relation "kill the $I$-action": every $\bar a\otimes m$ with $a\in I$ slides to $\bar1\otimes am = 0$, which is exactly the relation that defines $M/IM$. The trigger to watch for is *any quotient ring as a tensor factor* — $R/I$, $R/\mathfrak{p}$, a residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ — at which point "tensoring evaluates / reduces the module at that ideal" is the right picture. This is how one computes the fibre of a module at a point (tensor with the residue field), how the [[Commutative Algebra IV — Localization|local–global]] machinery evaluates $M$ at $\mathfrak{p}$, and how Nakayama's lemma is set up ($M/\mathfrak{m}M = \kappa(\mathfrak{m})\otimes M$).

**The two well-definedness checks are mirror images, and recognising this is the proof.** A common stumbling block is treating the forward map (kill the tensor relations on $R/I$) and the inverse map (kill $IM$ in $M/IM$) as two separate verifications. They are the *same* fact viewed from both sides: $\bar a\otimes m = \bar1\otimes am$ for $a\in I$. From the tensor side it says "$\bar a = 0$ forces $\bar a\otimes m = 0$" (forward well-definedness); from the module side it says "$\bar1\otimes am = 0$, so $IM$ maps to $0$" (inverse well-definedness). The transferable lesson: when proving a quotient-of-tensor isomorphism, find the *one* relation that both maps must respect, and check it once — the two well-definedness obligations are not independent, they are dual readings of a single identity. This same mirror-image structure appears in the general [[Thm - Standard Isomorphisms of Tensor Products|quotient isomorphism]] $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$.

**Extension of scalars has two faces — reducing and enlarging — and both are this isomorphism.** The corollaries are deliberately chosen to show the same operation pointing in opposite size-directions. Along $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n$, extension *shrinks*: $(\mathbb{Z}/n)\otimes\mathbb{Z}^k = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^k$ reduces the free module modulo $n$. Along $\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$, extension *grows*: $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{R}^n = \mathbb{C}^n$ complexifies. Both are "$S\otimes_R(-)$ replaces the scalars by $S$", and the unifying picture is that extension of scalars *rebases* a module — keeping its generators/relations but reading them over the new ring. The diagnostic for spaced practice: whenever a problem changes the base ring (reduce mod $p$, complexify, pass to a residue field, localize), it is an extension of scalars, and for free modules it does nothing but reinterpret the matrices over the new ring (see [[Ex - Extension of scalars of a free module]]). The general theory — the two-step factorisation and the extension–restriction adjunction — is on [[Thm - Extension of Scalars and the Adjunction]].
