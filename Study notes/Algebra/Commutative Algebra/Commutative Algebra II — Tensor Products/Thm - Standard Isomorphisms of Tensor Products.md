# Notation

All rings are commutative with $1$; all modules unital. Let $R$ be a ring and $M, N, P$ be [[Def - Module|$R$-modules]], with [[Def - Submodule|submodules]] $M'\leq M$, $N'\leq N$, and a family $\{M_i\}_{i\in I}$. We write $M\otimes_R N$ for the [[Def - Tensor Product of Modules|tensor product]], $\bigoplus_i M_i$ for the [[Def - Direct Sum of Modules|direct sum]], $M/M'$ for the [[Def - Quotient Module|quotient]], and $I, J\trianglelefteq R$ for ideals with sum $I+J$. The full registry is on [[Commutative Algebra II — Tensor Products]].

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (Standard isomorphisms).** Let $M, N, P$ be $R$-modules. There are *natural* $R$-module isomorphisms, given on pure tensors by the indicated maps:
>
> 1. **Commutativity.** $M\otimes_R N\ \cong\ N\otimes_R M$, $\quad m\otimes n\mapsto n\otimes m$.
> 2. **Associativity.** $(M\otimes_R N)\otimes_R P\ \cong\ M\otimes_R(N\otimes_R P)\ \cong\ M\otimes_R N\otimes_R P$, $\quad (m\otimes n)\otimes p\mapsto m\otimes(n\otimes p)\mapsto m\otimes n\otimes p$ (the last via trilinear maps).
> 3. **Distributivity.** $\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i\big)\otimes_R P\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_i\otimes_R P)$, $\quad (m_i)_i\otimes p\mapsto (m_i\otimes p)_i$.
> 4. **Identity.** $R\otimes_R M\ \cong\ M$, $\quad r\otimes m\mapsto rm$.
> 5. **Quotients.** For submodules $M'\leq M$, $N'\leq N$, $\quad (M/M')\otimes_R(N/N')\ \cong\ (M\otimes_R N)/L$, $\quad (m+M')\otimes(n+N')\mapsto m\otimes n + L$, where $L\leq M\otimes_R N$ is generated by $\{m'\otimes n\}\cup\{m\otimes n'\}$ ($m'\in M'$, $n'\in N'$).

> **Corollary (free and cyclic computations).** $R^m\otimes_R R^n\cong R^{mn}$ with basis $\{e_i\otimes f_j\}$ (hence $R^m\otimes R^n\cong R^{mn}$ for any ring, and $\dim_k(V\otimes W) = \dim V\cdot\dim W$ over a field). For ideals $I, J\trianglelefteq R$, $\;R/I\otimes_R R/J\cong R/(I+J)\;$ via $(r_1+I)\otimes(r_2+J)\mapsto r_1 r_2 + (I+J)$; the kernel of $R\to R/I\otimes R/J$ is $I+J$ (contrast: the kernel of $R\to R/I\times R/J$ is $I\cap J$).

---

# Motivation

These five isomorphisms are what let you *manipulate* tensor products: they say $\otimes$ behaves like a well-mannered product. Commutativity and associativity let you reorder and regroup factors freely, so a tensor of several modules is unambiguous; distributivity lets you split a [[Def - Direct Sum of Modules|direct sum]] factor and reduce any tensor of free modules to a sum of copies of $R$; the identity isomorphism is the unit law; and the quotient rule lets you push $\otimes$ through presentations by relations. Without them, $M\otimes N$ would be an inert symbol; with them, it is computable.

The two consequences in the corollary are the everyday workhorses. $R^m\otimes R^n\cong R^{mn}$ — proved purely from distributivity and the identity law — is why tensoring free modules multiplies ranks, and is the source of $\dim(V\otimes W) = \dim V\dim W$. The cyclic rule $R/I\otimes R/J\cong R/(I+J)$ is the single most-used concrete computation in the subject: it is how one shows $\mathbb{Z}/m\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ (with $I+J = \gcd(m,n)\mathbb{Z}$), and its special case $R/I\otimes M\cong M/IM$ is the engine of extension of scalars. The contrast with the product — kernel $I+J$ for $\otimes$ versus $I\cap J$ for $\times$ — is itself illuminating: tensoring imposes "both sets of relations" ($I+J$), while the product imposes "neither extra relation but record both" ($I\cap J$).

All five are proved the same way, and the value of seeing this is that it is *the* method of the chapter: build maps in both directions using the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property]], then check the composites are the identity on pure tensors. The proofs are not individually interesting; the *technique* is.

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The precondition $A$ is "I have a tensor product and want to rearrange or compute it".

The first disguised source is **a tensor of free or direct-sum modules**. The property $B$ is "a factor is $\bigoplus_i M_i$ (in particular $R^n = \bigoplus_1^n R$)". The bridge is distributivity plus identity: $\big(\bigoplus M_i\big)\otimes P\cong\bigoplus(M_i\otimes P)$ and $R\otimes M\cong M$ reduce the tensor to a sum of copies of $P$. *Example problem:* $R^m\otimes R^n\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(R\otimes R)\cong R^{mn}$. See [[Ex - Extension of scalars of a free module]].

The second disguised source is **a factor presented as a quotient**. The property $B$ is "$M = M''/M'$ or $M = R/I$". The bridge is the quotient rule, specialising to $R/I\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$ — "tensoring with $R/I$ reduces mod $I$". This converts an abstract tensor into a concrete quotient module. *Example problem:* $\mathbb{Z}/m\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/(m\mathbb{Z}+n\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. See [[Ex - Z mod m tensor Z mod n is Z mod gcd]] and [[Ex - Tensoring with R over I gives M over IM]].

The third disguised source is **a need to reorder factors in a multi-fold tensor**. The property $B$ is "I have $(M\otimes N)\otimes P$ but want $M\otimes(N\otimes P)$, or to swap two factors". The bridge is associativity and commutativity, which make any bracketing and ordering equivalent, so $M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_k$ is well-defined. *Example problem:* defining the tensor algebra $\bigoplus_k M^{\otimes k}$ requires associativity to make the multiplication $M^{\otimes p}\otimes M^{\otimes q}\to M^{\otimes(p+q)}$ unambiguous.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The conclusions $C$ are the five isomorphisms.

Combine the **identity and distributivity isomorphisms** with **a basis of a free module**. They give $R^m\otimes R^n\cong R^{mn}$ with explicit basis $\{e_i\otimes f_j\}$. The result $E$ is that tensoring free modules multiplies ranks *and* hands you a basis — so dimension counts, rank computations, and the linear independence of $\{e_i\otimes f_j\}$ all follow. Nonobvious because it converts an abstract universal object into an explicitly based free module.

Combine the **quotient isomorphism** with **two ideals $I, J$**. It gives $R/I\otimes R/J\cong R/(I+J)$, whose kernel description $\ker(R\to R/I\otimes R/J) = I+J$ contrasts with $\ker(R\to R/I\times R/J) = I\cap J$. The result $E$ is a clean dichotomy: $\otimes$ encodes "impose both ideals' relations" ($I+J$), $\times$ encodes "the simultaneous-congruence relations" ($I\cap J$). Nonobvious because the *product* of ideals $IJ$ has no such kernel description — only $\otimes$ and $\times$ do.

Combine **commutativity and associativity** with **a bilinear/trilinear pairing**. They make $M\otimes N\otimes P$ symmetric enough to support symmetric or alternating quotients. The result $E$ is the construction of $\operatorname{Sym}^k$ and $\Lambda^k$ as quotients of $M^{\otimes k}$, and ultimately the basis-free determinant. Nonobvious because the *symmetry* of $\otimes$ is what allows imposing further symmetry by quotient.

# Why Is It True

Every part is the same three-line argument, and the unifying observation is worth stating once: **to prove $X\otimes Y\cong Z$, exhibit a bilinear map $X\times Y\to Z$ and a map back $Z\to X\otimes Y$ via the universal property, then check the round trips fix pure tensors (which generate, so fixing them is enough).** The isomorphisms differ only in which bilinear maps you write down.

**Commutativity.** The swap $(m,n)\mapsto n\otimes m$ is bilinear $M\times N\to N\otimes M$, so induces $\sigma : M\otimes N\to N\otimes M$. The reverse swap induces $\tau : N\otimes M\to M\otimes N$. On pure tensors $\tau\sigma(m\otimes n) = \tau(n\otimes m) = m\otimes n$, so $\tau\sigma = \operatorname{id}$; symmetrically $\sigma\tau = \operatorname{id}$. Done.

**Associativity.** Both $(M\otimes N)\otimes P$ and $M\otimes(N\otimes P)$ are the universal receptacle for *trilinear* maps $M\times N\times P\to L$ (a bilinear map valued in a tensor product, iterated), so both satisfy the same universal property and are uniquely isomorphic by $(m\otimes n)\otimes p\mapsto m\otimes(n\otimes p)$.

**Distributivity** is the one part Becker proves in full, and it is the template. Define $\varphi : \big(\bigoplus M_i\big)\otimes P\to\bigoplus(M_i\otimes P)$ from the bilinear map $((m_i)_i, p)\mapsto(m_i\otimes p)_i$. In the other direction, for each $i$ the bilinear $M_i\times P\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$, $(m,p)\mapsto e_i(m)\otimes p$ (where $e_i$ is the inclusion), induces $\psi_i : M_i\otimes P\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$; the universal property of the direct sum assembles the $\psi_i$ into $\psi : \bigoplus(M_i\otimes P)\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$. Then $\psi\varphi$ and $\varphi\psi$ fix the (generating) pure tensors, so both are identities.

**Identity.** The bilinear $(r,m)\mapsto rm$ induces $R\otimes M\to M$; the linear $m\mapsto 1\otimes m$ goes back. Round trips: $1\otimes m\mapsto m\mapsto 1\otimes m$, and $r\otimes m = r(1\otimes m)\mapsto rm\mapsto 1\otimes rm = r\otimes m$. (Here $r\otimes m = r(1\otimes m) = 1\otimes rm$ uses scalar-sliding — the reason $R$ is the unit.)

**Quotients.** The bilinear $(m+M', n+N')\mapsto m\otimes n + L$ is well-defined into $(M\otimes N)/L$ precisely because $L$ swallows $m'\otimes n$ and $m\otimes n'$, so changing representatives changes $m\otimes n$ by an element of $L$. It induces a map down; the inverse comes from $m\otimes n\mapsto(m+M')\otimes(n+N')$, which kills $L$. Round trips fix pure tensors.

**The corollary.** $R^m\otimes R^n\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(R\otimes R)\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}R = R^{mn}$ by distributivity (twice) and identity; the basis tensors $e_i\otimes f_j$ map to the standard basis. And $R/I\otimes R/J\cong(R/I)/J(R/I)$ by the quotient rule (taking $M = R$, $M' = I$, and using $R/I\otimes R/J\cong(R/I)/(\text{image of }J)$) $= R/(I+J)$, since $J(R/I) = (I+J)/I$.

# What Makes This Hard

There is no single hard step; the difficulty is *organisational* — recognising that all five are one technique (maps both ways, check on pure tensors) and not memorising five separate proofs. The one genuinely non-obvious point is in distributivity: the reverse map must be assembled from the $\psi_i$ via the **universal property of the direct sum**, not built directly, because a map *out of* a direct sum is exactly a compatible family of maps out of the summands. The most common error is in the quotient rule, where one forgets to check that $L$ is *exactly* the right submodule — large enough to make the map well-defined, small enough that the inverse exists — and in the corollary, confusing the kernel $I+J$ (for $\otimes$) with $I\cap J$ (for $\times$).

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:** For each isomorphism, write a bilinear (or trilinear) map one way and another back, both via the universal property; verify the two composites fix pure tensors, which generate, hence are identities. For associativity, observe both sides are universal for trilinear maps. Derive the corollary by iterating distributivity + identity (free case) and applying the quotient rule with $M = R$ (cyclic case).

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Commutativity.** Build the swap and its inverse.
   - *Hint:* $(m,n)\mapsto n\otimes m$ is bilinear; the reverse swap inverts it on pure tensors.
   - *Why needed:* It is the base symmetry; associativity and the multi-fold tensor rely on it.

2. **Distributivity.** Build $\varphi$ from the obvious bilinear map and $\psi$ from the $\psi_i$ via the direct-sum universal property.
   - *Hint:* A map *out of* $\bigoplus M_i$ is a family of maps out of the $M_i$.
   - *Why needed:* It reduces free tensors to sums of copies of $R$; it is the template proof.

3. **Identity.** $(r,m)\mapsto rm$ and $m\mapsto 1\otimes m$.
   - *Hint:* $r\otimes m = r(1\otimes m) = 1\otimes rm$ by scalar-sliding.
   - *Why needed:* It is the unit law; combined with distributivity it gives $R^m\otimes R^n\cong R^{mn}$.

4. **Associativity.** Match universal properties for trilinear maps.
   - *Hint:* Both bracketings represent the trilinear-maps functor.
   - *Why needed:* It makes multi-fold tensors well-defined.

5. **Quotients, then corollary.** Build the quotient isomorphism; specialise to $R/I\otimes R/J\cong R/(I+J)$ and $R/I\otimes M\cong M/IM$.
   - *Hint:* $L$ is exactly what makes the descended map well-defined; $J(R/I) = (I+J)/I$.
   - *Why needed:* It is the workhorse computation, and gives the $\gcd$ result.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Maps-both-ways criterion
> **Statement:** To prove an isomorphism $X\otimes Y\cong Z$, it suffices to give $R$-linear maps $\varphi : X\otimes Y\to Z$, $\psi : Z\to X\otimes Y$ such that $\psi\varphi$ and $\varphi\psi$ fix all pure tensors (resp. all generators of $Z$).
>
> **Hint:** Pure tensors generate $X\otimes Y$, so a linear map fixing them is the identity; cite [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|the universal property]] for existence of $\varphi, \psi$.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the single technique behind all five isomorphisms; every proof below is an instance.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > By the [[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|universal property]], $\varphi$ is obtained from a bilinear map and $\psi$ is given (or built from a universal property of the target). If $\psi\varphi$ fixes every pure tensor $x\otimes y$, then since pure tensors generate $X\otimes Y$ as an $R$-module and $\psi\varphi$ is $R$-linear, $\psi\varphi = \operatorname{id}_{X\otimes Y}$. Symmetrically, if $\varphi\psi$ fixes a generating set of $Z$ and is linear, $\varphi\psi = \operatorname{id}_Z$. Hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\psi$.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: Distributivity (the template proof)
> **Statement:** $\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i\big)\otimes_R P\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_i\otimes_R P)$ via $(m_i)_i\otimes p\mapsto(m_i\otimes p)_i$.
>
> **Hint:** Build the forward map from a bilinear map; build the inverse by assembling per-summand maps via the direct-sum universal property.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the most-used isomorphism (it reduces free tensors) and the model for the maps-both-ways method.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > **Forward.** The map $\big(\bigoplus M_i\big)\times P\to\bigoplus(M_i\otimes P)$, $((m_i)_i, p)\mapsto(m_i\otimes p)_i$, is $R$-bilinear (additivity and scalar-sliding hold coordinatewise), so induces $R$-linear $\varphi$ with $\varphi((m_i)_i\otimes p) = (m_i\otimes p)_i$.
> >
> > **Backward.** For each $i$, let $\iota_i : M_i\to\bigoplus_j M_j$ be the inclusion. The map $M_i\times P\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$, $(m, p)\mapsto\iota_i(m)\otimes p$, is bilinear, inducing $\psi_i : M_i\otimes P\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$ with $\psi_i(m\otimes p) = \iota_i(m)\otimes p$. By the [[Def - Direct Sum of Modules|universal property of the direct sum]], the family $(\psi_i)$ assembles into a single $R$-linear $\psi : \bigoplus(M_i\otimes P)\to\big(\bigoplus M_j\big)\otimes P$ with $\psi((m_i\otimes p)_i) = (m_i)_i\otimes p$ on elements pure in each coordinate.
> >
> > **Round trips.** $\psi\varphi((m_i)_i\otimes p) = \psi((m_i\otimes p)_i) = (m_i)_i\otimes p$; pure tensors $(m_i)_i\otimes p$ generate the source, so $\psi\varphi = \operatorname{id}$. Likewise $\varphi\psi((m_i\otimes p)_i) = (m_i\otimes p)_i$ on the generating coordinate-pure tensors, so $\varphi\psi = \operatorname{id}$. By Lemma 1, $\varphi$ is an isomorphism.

> [!note]- Lemma 3: Identity law
> **Statement:** $R\otimes_R M\cong M$ via $r\otimes m\mapsto rm$, inverse $m\mapsto 1\otimes m$.
>
> **Hint:** Use $r\otimes m = r(1\otimes m) = 1\otimes rm$ from scalar-sliding to check the round trips.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the unit law and, with distributivity, yields $R^m\otimes R^n\cong R^{mn}$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > The bilinear $(r,m)\mapsto rm$ induces $\varphi : R\otimes M\to M$, $\varphi(r\otimes m) = rm$. Let $\psi : M\to R\otimes M$, $\psi(m) = 1\otimes m$ ($R$-linear). Then $\varphi\psi(m) = \varphi(1\otimes m) = m$, so $\varphi\psi = \operatorname{id}_M$. And $\psi\varphi(r\otimes m) = \psi(rm) = 1\otimes rm = r(1\otimes m) = r\otimes m$, using $1\otimes rm = r\otimes m$; pure tensors generate, so $\psi\varphi = \operatorname{id}_{R\otimes M}$. By Lemma 1, isomorphism.

> [!note]- Lemma 4: Quotient rule and the cyclic corollary
> **Statement:** $(M/M')\otimes(N/N')\cong(M\otimes N)/L$ with $L = \langle m'\otimes n, m\otimes n'\rangle$; in particular $R/I\otimes_R R/J\cong R/(I+J)$ and $R/I\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$.
>
> **Hint:** $L$ is exactly what is needed to make $(m+M', n+N')\mapsto m\otimes n + L$ well-defined; for the cyclic case use $J(R/I) = (I+J)/I$.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the workhorse computation; it gives $\mathbb{Z}/m\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > **Forward.** Define $\bar b : (M/M')\times(N/N')\to(M\otimes N)/L$ by $\bar b(m+M', n+N') = m\otimes n + L$. Well-defined: replacing $m$ by $m + m'_0$ ($m'_0\in M'$) changes $m\otimes n$ by $m'_0\otimes n\in L$, and similarly in the second slot, so the class mod $L$ is unchanged. It is bilinear, inducing $\varphi : (M/M')\otimes(N/N')\to(M\otimes N)/L$.
> >
> > **Backward.** The bilinear $(m,n)\mapsto(m+M')\otimes(n+N')$ induces a map $M\otimes N\to(M/M')\otimes(N/N')$ that kills the generators $m'\otimes n$ and $m\otimes n'$ of $L$ (since $m'+M' = 0$, $n'+N' = 0$), hence factors through $(M\otimes N)/L$ to give $\psi$. Round trips fix pure tensors, so by Lemma 1 it is an isomorphism.
> >
> > **Cyclic case.** Take $M = N = R$, $M' = I$, $N' = J$. Then $L$ is generated by $\{x\otimes 1 : x\in I\}\cup\{1\otimes y : y\in J\}$. Under $R\otimes_R R\cong R$ (Lemma 3) this $L$ corresponds to $I + J$, so $R/I\otimes R/J\cong R/(I+J)$, with $(r_1+I)\otimes(r_2+J)\mapsto r_1 r_2 + (I+J)$. Taking instead $N' = 0$, $N = R$, $M' = I$ gives $R/I\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$ after $R\otimes M\cong M$.

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> All five use **Lemma 1** (maps both ways, check on pure tensors).
>
> **(1) Commutativity.** The bilinear $(m,n)\mapsto n\otimes m$ induces $\sigma : M\otimes N\to N\otimes M$; the reverse swap induces $\tau$. On pure tensors $\tau\sigma(m\otimes n) = m\otimes n$ and $\sigma\tau(n\otimes m) = n\otimes m$, so $\sigma$ is an isomorphism.
>
> **(2) Associativity.** For any $L$, an $R$-linear map $(M\otimes N)\otimes P\to L$ corresponds (by the universal property, twice) to a trilinear map $M\times N\times P\to L$, and so does an $R$-linear map $M\otimes(N\otimes P)\to L$. Both objects thus represent the same trilinear-maps functor, so are uniquely isomorphic via $(m\otimes n)\otimes p\mapsto m\otimes(n\otimes p)$; both equal the receptacle $M\otimes N\otimes P$ for trilinear maps.
>
> **(3) Distributivity.** This is **Lemma 2**.
>
> **(4) Identity.** This is **Lemma 3**.
>
> **(5) Quotients.** This is **Lemma 4**.
>
> ---
> **Corollary.** Iterating (3) and (4): $R^m\otimes_R R^n = \big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^m R\big)\otimes\big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^n R\big)\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(R\otimes_R R)\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}R = R^{mn}$, with $e_i\otimes f_j$ mapping to the standard basis vectors — so $\{e_i\otimes f_j\}$ is a basis. Over a field this gives $\dim_k(V\otimes W) = \dim V\cdot\dim W$.
>
> By **Lemma 4**, $R/I\otimes_R R/J\cong R/(I+J)$, so the structure map $R\to R/I\otimes_R R/J$, $r\mapsto(1+I)\otimes(1+J) = r\cdot((1+I)\otimes(1+J))$, is $r\mapsto r + (I+J)$, with kernel $I+J$. Contrast the kernel of $R\to R/I\times R/J$, $r\mapsto(r+I, r+J)$, which is $\{r : r\in I\text{ and }r\in J\} = I\cap J$. $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**The Chinese Remainder Theorem from two sides (number theory).** Comparing $\mathbb{Z}/m\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ with $\mathbb{Z}/m\times\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$ (when coprime, $\cong\mathbb{Z}/mn$) makes the kernels $I+J$ versus $I\cap J$ concrete: $\gcd$ from $\otimes$, $\operatorname{lcm}$ from $\times$. Nonobvious because the *same* two ideals produce $\gcd$ or $\operatorname{lcm}$ depending on whether you tensor or take a product. See [[Ex - Z mod m tensor Z mod n is Z mod gcd]].

**Dimension of a space of matrices (linear algebra).** $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V, W)\cong V^*\otimes W$ has dimension $\dim V\cdot\dim W$ by the free-tensor corollary, recovering the count of entries in an $m\times n$ matrix without choosing bases. Nonobvious because "the space of linear maps has dimension $mn$" becomes an instance of "tensoring multiplies dimensions". See [[Ex - The Kronecker product of matrices]].

**Coordinate ring of a product variety (algebraic geometry).** The quotient rule upgraded to algebras gives $k[X_*]/I\otimes_k k[Y_*]/J\cong k[X_*, Y_*]/(I^e + J^e)$ — the coordinate ring of the product variety, with relations $I+J$. Nonobvious because the *sum* of the (extended) ideals, exactly the $\otimes$-kernel here, is what cuts out the product. See [[Ex - Tensor product of polynomial algebras]].

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Universal Property of the Tensor Product of Modules|Universal Property]]** — all five isomorphisms are proved by it: each is "build bilinear maps both ways, get linear maps, check composites on pure tensors". The universal property of the [[Def - Direct Sum of Modules|direct sum]] additionally supplies the reverse map in distributivity. This theorem is the universal property used as a computational engine.

- **[[Thm - Functoriality of the Tensor Product|Functoriality]]** — functoriality is what makes these isomorphisms *natural*: the commutativity isomorphism is a natural transformation $M\otimes N\to N\otimes M$, and naturality is exactly compatibility with $f\otimes g$. Distributivity's component maps are instances of $\iota_i\otimes\operatorname{id}$.

- **Extension of scalars** — the special case $R/I\otimes_R M\cong M/IM$ *is* extension of scalars along $R\to R/I$, and the free case $S\otimes_R R^n\cong S^n$ is extension of a free module. So the standard isomorphisms specialise directly to [[Thm - Extension of Scalars and the Adjunction|the base-change theorems]], and to [[Ex - Tensoring with R over I gives M over IM]] and [[Ex - Extension of scalars of a free module]].

- **The product ideal has no kernel description** — the corollary's kernels are $I+J$ (for $\otimes$) and $I\cap J$ (for $\times$); notably the *product* ideal $IJ$, generated by products $xy$ with $x\in I$, $y\in J$, is *not* the kernel of any such natural map. This asymmetry — $\otimes\leftrightarrow I+J$, $\times\leftrightarrow I\cap J$, $IJ\leftrightarrow$ nothing canonical — is a recurring subtlety in ideal theory and in the [[Commutative Algebra IV — Localization|localization]] computations.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The tensor, symmetric, and exterior algebras *(from Multilinear Algebra)*
> Associativity makes $\bigoplus_k M^{\otimes k}$ an associative algebra (the **tensor algebra** $T(M)$) with unambiguous multiplication $M^{\otimes p}\otimes M^{\otimes q}\to M^{\otimes(p+q)}$; commutativity lets one further quotient to the **symmetric algebra** $\operatorname{Sym}(M)$ (a polynomial ring when $M$ is free) and the **exterior algebra** $\Lambda(M)$. The free-tensor corollary computes their dimensions, and $\Lambda^n$ of an $n$-dimensional space is $1$-dimensional — the home of the determinant.

> [!tip] The Künneth formula and external products *(from Algebraic Topology)*
> The naturality and distributivity here make the cross product $H_*(X)\otimes H_*(Y)\to H_*(X\times Y)$ well-defined, and the free-module computation $\dim(V\otimes W) = \dim V\dim W$ is the dimension count behind the **Künneth formula** for the homology of a product (up to the $\operatorname{Tor}$ correction that appears because $\otimes$ is only right exact).
