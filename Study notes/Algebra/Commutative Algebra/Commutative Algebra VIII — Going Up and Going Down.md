# Notation Registry

**Standing convention.** Throughout these commutative algebra notes, *ring* means a commutative ring with a multiplicative identity $1$, and a ring homomorphism sends $1 \mapsto 1$. This chapter studies a single map: given an [[Def - Integral Element and Integral Extension|integral extension]] of rings $A \subseteq B$, the inclusion $\iota : A \hookrightarrow B$ induces a **contraction map** $\iota^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto \mathfrak{q} \cap A$. The whole chapter is the study of $\iota^*$ — whether it is onto (**lying over**), how it lifts inclusions of primes (**going up** and **going down**), and how big its fibres are (**incomparability**). The geometric slogan, stated again in Motivation: **$\iota^*$ is a finite map of spaces, and the four Cohen–Seidenberg theorems are the algebra of how a finite map moves points, chains, and fibres.**

- $A, B$ — commutative rings with $1$, usually with $A \subseteq B$ an integral extension
- $\iota : A \hookrightarrow B$ — the inclusion; more generally $f : A \to B$ a ring homomorphism
- $\iota^* = f^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ — the **induced (contraction) map**, $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto \mathfrak{q} \cap A = \iota^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$
- $\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{p}_1, \mathfrak{p}_2$ — prime ideals of $A$; $\mathfrak{q}, \mathfrak{q}_1, \mathfrak{q}_2$ — prime ideals of $B$
- $\mathfrak{q} \text{ lies over } \mathfrak{p}$ — means $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$, i.e. $\iota^*(\mathfrak{q}) = \mathfrak{p}$
- $\operatorname{Spec} R$ — the **prime spectrum** of $R$, the set of prime ideals with the Zariski topology; $\operatorname{mSpec} R$ — the set of maximal ideals
- $V(I) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} R : I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}\}$ — the closed set cut out by an ideal $I$
- $\text{fibre of } \iota^* \text{ over } \mathfrak{p} = (\iota^*)^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}) = \{\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{Spec} B : \mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}\}$
- $S = A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ — the multiplicative set complementary to $\mathfrak{p}$; $A_{\mathfrak{p}} = S^{-1}A$ — the localization (a local ring)
- $B_{\mathfrak{p}} := (A \setminus \mathfrak{p})^{-1}B = S^{-1}B$ — $B$ localized at the elements of $S \subseteq A$; **not** in general a local ring, and **not** "$B$ at a prime of $B$"
- $\kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}} = \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak{p})$ — the **residue field** of $A$ at $\mathfrak{p}$
- $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \cong (B/\mathfrak{p}B)_{\mathfrak{p}}$ — the **fibre ring** over $\mathfrak{p}$; its primes are exactly the fibre $(\iota^*)^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})$
- $\dim R$ — the **Krull dimension**, the supremum of lengths $n$ of chains $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_n$ of primes
- $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak{p}$ — the **height** of $\mathfrak{p}$, the supremum of lengths of chains $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d = \mathfrak{p}$ descending from $\mathfrak{p}$; $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}$
- a map $f : A \to B$ is **finite** if $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-module; **integral** if every element of $B$ is integral over $A$; **flat** if $B$ is a flat $A$-module

# Motivation

Here is the whole chapter in one sentence: **an integral extension $A \subseteq B$ is the algebra of a finite map of spaces, and the Cohen–Seidenberg theorems say exactly how that map behaves — it is onto, it carries chains of primes up and (when $A$ is normal) down, and its fibres are finite.** The previous chapter, [[Commutative Algebra VI — Integral Extensions|integral extensions]], built the algebraic object: $B$ is integral over $A$ when every $b \in B$ satisfies a monic equation $b^n + a_1 b^{n-1} + \cdots + a_n = 0$ with $a_i \in A$. This chapter asks the geometric question that integrality was invented to answer. A ring map $A \to B$ induces a map of spectra in the *opposite* direction, $\iota^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, sending a prime $\mathfrak{q}$ to its contraction $\mathfrak{q} \cap A$; geometrically this is a map of spaces $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$. For a generic ring map this induced map can be wild — its image need not be closed, points can disappear, fibres can have varying dimension. The miracle of integrality is that it tames $\iota^*$ completely.

To see why a finite map is the right picture, hold the simplest example in mind throughout: $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}[i]$, where $i$ satisfies $i^2 + 1 = 0$. The induced map $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ sends a Gaussian prime to the ordinary prime it sits over. Above each rational prime $(p)$ sit one or two Gaussian primes — but *never* none: above every $(p)$ there is at least one Gaussian prime, so the fibre is never empty (this is **lying over**); the fibre is finite, with one or two primes (this is **incomparability**, forcing fibres to be zero-dimensional); and as $p$ ranges over the primes, the number in the fibre is governed by how $X^2 + 1$ factors mod $p$. The map "looks like" a branched two-sheeted cover of the line $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$, ramified at $p = 2$. Every theorem in this chapter is a feature of that picture made into an algebraic statement about contractions of primes.

The four theorems organise into a hierarchy of how faithfully $\iota^*$ reflects the order structure of primes. They are best held as one display:

$$\underbrace{\text{Lying Over}}_{\iota^* \text{ surjective}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{Going Up}}_{\iota^* \text{ lifts } \mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2 \text{ upward}}, \qquad \underbrace{\text{Incomparability}}_{\text{fibres are antichains}}, \qquad \underbrace{\text{Going Down}}_{\text{lifts } \mathfrak{p}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_2 \text{ downward, needs } A \text{ normal}}.$$

Lying Over says the map is *onto*: every point of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is hit. Going Up says that if you have a prime $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$ and you *enlarge* $\mathfrak{p}_1$ to $\mathfrak{p}_2$, you can enlarge $\mathfrak{q}_1$ to a $\mathfrak{q}_2$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$ — chains of primes lift *upward*. Incomparability says that within a single fibre no two primes are comparable: $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}'$ with the same contraction forces $\mathfrak{q} = \mathfrak{q}'$, so the fibre has no internal vertical structure — it is finite and zero-dimensional. Going Down is the mirror of Going Up: it lifts chains *downward*, but unlike the other three it is *not free* — it requires $A$ to be an [[Def - Integral Closure and Normal Domain|integrally closed (normal) domain]], and it genuinely fails without that hypothesis. The payoff of the package is the headline corollary that **an integral extension preserves Krull dimension**, $\dim A = \dim B$: a finite map does not change the dimension of a space, and chains downstairs and upstairs match length-for-length.

This chapter assumes you are fluent with [[Def - Integral Element and Integral Extension|integral extensions]] and the [[Thm - Integral Extensions and Fields (Domain Criterion)|domain/field criterion]] (an integral extension of domains has $A$ a field iff $B$ is a field) from [[Commutative Algebra VI — Integral Extensions|the integral-extensions chapter]]; with [[Def - The Prime Spectrum (Spec)|the prime spectrum]], [[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field|local rings and residue fields]], and the [[Thm - Prime Ideals of a Localization|prime-correspondence theorem for localizations]] from [[Commutative Algebra IV — Localization|the localization chapter]]; and with [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal|prime and maximal ideals]] and the [[Thm - Maximal and Prime Ideals via Quotients|quotient criteria]] from the [[Rings II — §2.3–2.4|Rings]] notes. The notion of [[Def - Krull Dimension and Height|Krull dimension and height]] is developed fully in [[Commutative Algebra XII — Dimension Theory|the dimension chapter]], but the definition (longest chain of primes) is all that is needed here.

# Concept Map

## §9.1 Lying Over and Going Up

- **[[Def - The Induced Map on Spectra]]**
	- Any ring homomorphism $f : A \to B$ induces $f^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) = \mathfrak{q}^c$, the **contraction** map; it is continuous for the Zariski topology, with $(f^*)^{-1}(V(I)) = V(f(I)B)$. For the inclusion $A \subseteq B$ this is $\mathfrak{q} \mapsto \mathfrak{q} \cap A$. The **fibre** over $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$ is the set of primes lying over $\mathfrak{p}$, and it is canonically the prime spectrum of the **fibre ring** $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \cong (B/\mathfrak{p}B)_{\mathfrak{p}}$, so that geometry of the fibre is algebra of one ring. This is the single object the whole chapter studies.

- **[[Def - Lying Over, Going Up, Going Down]]**
	- A ring map $f : A \to B$ **satisfies lying over** if $f^*$ is surjective (every $\mathfrak{p}$ is the contraction of some $\mathfrak{q}$); **satisfies going up** if any inclusion $\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ in $A$ with a prime $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$ can be matched by $\mathfrak{q}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$; **satisfies going down** if the same holds with all inclusions reversed ($\mathfrak{p}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ matched by $\mathfrak{q}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_2$). These are the three lifting properties of $f^*$. Going up forces lying over; quotient maps satisfy all three; integral maps satisfy lying over and going up always, but going down only under normality.

- **[[Thm - Lying Over]]**
	- For an integral extension $A \subseteq B$ and any $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$, there is a prime $\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{Spec} B$ with $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$. Equivalently $\iota^*$ is surjective and the fibre over every point is non-empty. The one-line proof: the fibre is $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, and $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = (A \setminus \mathfrak{p})^{-1}B \neq 0$ because $0 \notin A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, so it has a maximal ideal. Geometrically the finite map $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is *onto* — no point of the base is missed.

- **[[Thm - Going Up]]**
	- For an integral extension $A \subseteq B$, given $\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, there is $\mathfrak{q}_2 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$. The proof passes to the quotient: $A/\mathfrak{p}_1 \hookrightarrow B/\mathfrak{q}_1$ is again integral, and lying over for *it* produces the prime $\mathfrak{q}_2/\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2/\mathfrak{p}_1$. Going up is the statement that chains of primes lift upward, the engine behind $\dim B \geq \dim A$; geometrically, as the image point specialises, a preimage point specialises with it.

- **[[Thm - Incomparability]]**
	- For an integral extension $A \subseteq B$, if $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}'$ are primes of $B$ with $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{q}' \cap A$, then $\mathfrak{q} = \mathfrak{q}'$: no two distinct comparable primes lie over the same prime. Equivalently, the fibre $(\iota^*)^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})$ is an *antichain*, the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ has Krull dimension zero, and (when $B$ is module-finite) the fibre is *finite*. This is the algebraic form of "the fibres of a finite map are zero-dimensional", and it is what forces $\dim B \leq \dim A$.

> [!tip] Unlocked: A finite map of affine varieties *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> A module-finite integral extension $A \subseteq B$ of finitely generated $k$-algebras is exactly the algebraic data of a **finite morphism of affine varieties** $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$. **Lying Over** says this morphism is *surjective*; **Going Up** says it is *closed* (it carries closed sets to closed sets, with no component of the image escaping to a non-closed limit); and **Incomparability** says its fibres are *finite* (zero-dimensional). The branched cover $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ and the projection of a plane curve to a coordinate axis after **Noether normalization** are the prototypes. The general theory of finite morphisms — properness, the finiteness of fibres, the structure of ramification — is the geometric upgrade of this section, developed once one has **schemes** and the **structure sheaf**.

## §9.2 Going Down and Dimension

- **[[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains]]**
	- For an integral extension $A \subseteq B$ of *integral domains* with $A$ **integrally closed** in $\operatorname{Frac} A$, given $\mathfrak{p}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, there is $\mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$. Chains lift *downward* — but only because $A$ is normal: the proof shows $\mathfrak{p}_2$ is contracted from the localization $B_{\mathfrak{q}_1}$, using that for normal $A$ the minimal equation of an element of $\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}_2 B}$ has all coefficients in $\mathfrak{p}_2$. Without normality the conclusion genuinely fails. Geometrically: a finite map *from* or *onto* a normal variety has no jump in fibre dimension as you specialise — equidimensionality.

- **[[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]]**
	- For an integral extension $A \subseteq B$, $\dim A = \dim B$. The inequality $\dim B \leq \dim A$ comes from **incomparability** (a strict chain in $B$ contracts to a strict chain in $A$ of the same length); the inequality $\dim A \leq \dim B$ comes from **lying over** plus **going up** (a chain in $A$ lifts to a chain in $B$ of the same length). Notably, dimension equality needs only the first three theorems — *not* going down, hence *not* normality. It is the cleanest expression of "a finite map preserves dimension", and with **Noether normalization** it computes $\dim k[X_1, \dots, X_n] = n$.

> [!tip] Unlocked: Equidimensional fibres and the dimension formula *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> **Going Down** is the algebra of *no fibre-dimension jumping*: for a finite (or, more generally, flat) map onto a normal base, the fibres do not suddenly grow in dimension as the target point specialises, which is the property that makes a family of varieties **equidimensional**. The downstream payoff is the dimension formula $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ for a finitely generated domain over a field (codimension plus dimension of a subvariety equals the dimension of the ambient space), whose proof runs on Going Down, Incomparability, and Noether normalization. This is the entry point to the dimension theory of varieties and **schemes**, taken up in [[Commutative Algebra XII — Dimension Theory|the dimension chapter]].

> [!tip] Unlocked: Going up means the map of spectra is closed *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> A ring map $f : A \to B$ satisfies going up **if and only if** the induced map $f^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is a *closed map* in the Zariski topology — it sends closed sets to closed sets. This is what distinguishes integral maps from generic ones: the projection $\{ab = 1\} \to \mathbb{A}^1$, $(a,b) \mapsto a$, has image $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$, which is *not* closed, and correspondingly the algebra map $k[T_1] \to k[T_1, T_2]/(T_1 T_2 - 1)$ is *not* integral. The geometric meaning of going up is precisely the closedness — properness in disguise — of finite morphisms.

### Exercises across both sections

- **[[Ex - Primes of Z[i] over a rational prime]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Compute the fibre of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ over each rational prime $(p)$: ramified at $2$ (one prime $(1+i)$), split when $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ (two conjugate primes), inert when $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ (one prime). The mechanism is $\mathbb{Z}[i]/p\mathbb{Z}[i] \cong \mathbb{F}_p[X]/(X^2+1)$ and how $X^2+1$ factors. This is **lying over** and **incomparability** made fully explicit on the prototype branched cover.

- **[[Ex - The induced map on Spec is surjective for integral extensions]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Prove directly that $\iota^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is surjective for an integral extension, by showing $B_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0$ and translating a maximal ideal of $B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ back to a prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak{p}$. This *is* lying over, stated as surjectivity of the geometric map, and it drills the fibre-equals-$\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ dictionary.

- **[[Ex - Going down can fail without normality]]** (⭐⭐⭐)
	- Exhibit an integral extension where going down fails: $B = k[u] \times k[v]$ (two disjoint lines) and $A = \{(f,g) \in B : f(0) = g(0)\}$ (the two lines glued at a point, a non-normal ring). A chain $\mathfrak{p}_2 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1$ in $A$ and a prime $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$ admit *no* $\mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$. The gluing destroys the downward-lifting that normality would guarantee — the sharpest illustration of why Going Down needs its hypothesis.

- **[[Ex - Fibres of a finite map are finite]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Show that for a *finite* (module-finite) extension $A \subseteq B$, every fibre $(\iota^*)^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})$ is a finite set. The fibre is $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, a finite-dimensional $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$-algebra, hence Artinian, hence with finitely many primes — all maximal by incomparability. This is the algebraic content of "a finite map has finite fibres".

- **[[Ex - A chain of primes lifts along a finite extension]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Given a strict chain $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_n$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and a prime $\mathfrak{q}_0$ over $\mathfrak{p}_0$, build a strict chain $\mathfrak{q}_0 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{q}_n$ in $\operatorname{Spec} B$ with $\mathfrak{q}_i \cap A = \mathfrak{p}_i$ by iterating **going up**, the strictness being supplied by **incomparability**. This is the half of dimension-preservation that lifts chains upward.

- **[[Ex - Dimension is preserved under integral extension]]** (⭐⭐)
	- Prove $\dim A = \dim B$ for an integral extension, combining the chain-lifting exercise ($\dim A \leq \dim B$) with the contraction-of-chains argument ($\dim B \leq \dim A$). Deduce, with **Noether normalization**, that $\dim k[X_1,\dots,X_n] = n$ and that $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ for a finitely generated $k$-domain (ES4 Q3). The capstone computation of the chapter.

# Sources and Targets

**Targets — what do we usually try to prove?**

The recurring goals of this chapter cluster into five types, and all five concern the contraction map $\iota^*$. The first is **showing a prime lies over a given prime** — producing $\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$ (existence) or showing the fibre is exactly some set (a complete description). The standard route is the fibre-ring dictionary: the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}))$, so the question reduces to finding the primes of a single explicit ring, often a quotient of a polynomial ring over a field. The second is **lifting a chain or an inclusion of primes** — given a containment $\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ (or $\supseteq$) and a partial lift, complete it upstairs. This routes through [[Thm - Going Up|going up]] (upward) or [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]] (downward, normal case), usually iterated to lift a whole chain. The third, the chapter's signature, is **computing or comparing Krull dimensions** — proving $\dim B \leq \dim A$ (via [[Thm - Incomparability|incomparability]]), $\dim A \leq \dim B$ (via lying over and going up), or the identity $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$. The fourth is **establishing finiteness or zero-dimensionality of a fibre** — that a finite map has finite fibres, settled by incomparability plus the Artinian-ness of the fibre ring. The fifth is **diagnosing when going down fails** — recognising that a non-normal base permits the kind of "gluing" that breaks downward lifting, and constructing or recognising the counterexample. These are the natural targets because the chapter is fundamentally about *one geometric map* and the only questions one can ask of a map are: is it onto, how does it move chains, and how big are its fibres.

**Sources — what assumptions do we usually leverage?**

The hypotheses are equally stereotyped. **An integral extension is named**, $A \subseteq B$ — this is the master hypothesis, and it unlocks lying over, going up, and incomparability *unconditionally*; the first reflex on seeing it is "the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ is $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and is non-empty". **A prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of the base is given**, switching on the localization $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = (A\setminus\mathfrak{p})^{-1}B$, the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, and the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$. **The base $A$ is an integrally closed domain (normal)** — this is the extra ingredient that, and *only* that, buys [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]]; recognising its presence or absence tells you whether downward lifting is available. **The extension is module-finite** ($B$ a finitely generated $A$-module), which strengthens incomparability to *finiteness* of fibres because the fibre ring is then finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$. **A chain of primes is in play** — and the move is to lift it one link at a time, alternating going up (or down) with incomparability to keep strictness. The discipline is to route source to target along the chapter's spine: integrality alone routes to surjectivity, upward chains, and the dimension inequality $\dim B \le \dim A$; integrality plus normality routes to downward chains and the dimension *formula*; module-finiteness routes to finite fibres. The [[Commutative Algebra VIII — Going Up and Going Down#Problem-Solving Strategy|Problem-Solving Strategy]] makes these routes explicit.

# Legal Operations

These are the moves nearly every going-up/going-down problem is assembled from. When stuck, scan the list. Everything is self-contained.

1. **Translate the fibre into $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$.** The primes of $B$ lying over $\mathfrak{p}$ correspond bijectively to the *maximal* ideals of the localized ring $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = (A \setminus \mathfrak{p})^{-1}B$, via extension and contraction along $B \to B_{\mathfrak{p}}$. So any question about a fibre becomes a question about $\operatorname{mSpec}$ of one explicit ring. *Trigger:* "describe / count / show non-empty the primes over $\mathfrak{p}$." *Pattern:* "the fibre is $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$; now study $B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, which is integral over the local ring $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$."

2. **Force a prime into existence by non-vanishing of a localization.** To produce *some* prime over $\mathfrak{p}$, show $B_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0$ — automatic because $0 \notin A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ — and take any maximal ideal of the non-zero ring, then contract. This is the engine of [[Thm - Lying Over|lying over]]. *Trigger:* "show the fibre is non-empty / the map is surjective." *Pattern:* "$B_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0$, so it has a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$; the contraction of $\mathfrak{m}$ to $B$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}$."

3. **Pass to the quotient to reduce going up to lying over.** Given $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, the inclusion $A/\mathfrak{p}_1 \hookrightarrow B/\mathfrak{q}_1$ is again an integral extension; applying [[Thm - Lying Over|lying over]] *there* to $\mathfrak{p}_2/\mathfrak{p}_1$ produces $\mathfrak{q}_2/\mathfrak{q}_1$ over it, and $\mathfrak{q}_2 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ contracts to $\mathfrak{p}_2$. *Trigger:* "lift the inclusion $\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ upward from a known $\mathfrak{q}_1$." *Pattern:* "quotient by $\mathfrak{q}_1$ and $\mathfrak{p}_1$, then lying over in the quotient."

4. **Use that integrality passes to quotients and localizations.** If $A \subseteq B$ is integral, then so are $A/(\mathfrak{q}\cap A) \subseteq B/\mathfrak{q}$ for any ideal and $S^{-1}A \subseteq S^{-1}B$ for any multiplicative $S \subseteq A$. This is what lets every proof move freely between $B$, its quotients, and its localizations without losing the integrality hypothesis. *Trigger:* you want to quotient or localize but must keep "integral." *Pattern:* "integrality is stable under quotients and localization, so the reduced/localized extension is still integral."

5. **Collapse comparable primes in one fibre.** If $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}'$ both contract to $\mathfrak{p}$, localize at $\mathfrak{p}$: in $B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ they become maximal ideals (by the [[Thm - Integral Extensions and Fields (Domain Criterion)|domain criterion]], contraction of maximal is maximal for an integral extension), and one maximal ideal inside another forces equality. This is [[Thm - Incomparability|incomparability]]. *Trigger:* "two primes over the same prime, one inside the other." *Pattern:* "after localizing they are maximal; comparable maximal ideals coincide."

6. **Reduce maximality to the base via the domain criterion.** For an integral extension, $\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{mSpec} B \iff \mathfrak{q} \cap A \in \operatorname{mSpec} A$ — because $A/(\mathfrak{q}\cap A) \subseteq B/\mathfrak{q}$ is an integral extension of domains, and there one is a field iff the other is ([[Thm - Integral Extensions and Fields (Domain Criterion)|domain criterion]]). *Trigger:* "is this prime maximal?" or "this prime over a maximal ideal." *Pattern:* "maximal upstairs $\iff$ maximal downstairs, by the field/domain criterion on the quotient."

7. **Contract a chain to get the dimension inequality $\dim B \leq \dim A$.** A strict chain $\mathfrak{q}_0 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{q}_n$ in $B$ contracts to a chain $\mathfrak{q}_0\cap A \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_n \cap A$ in $A$ that is again *strict* by incomparability. Hence chains in $B$ are no longer than chains in $A$. *Trigger:* "bound $\dim B$ above." *Pattern:* "contract and invoke incomparability to keep the inclusions strict."

8. **Lift a chain to get the inequality $\dim A \leq \dim B$.** A strict chain in $A$ is lifted link-by-link by lying over (the bottom) and going up (each step), producing a strict chain in $B$ of the same length. *Trigger:* "bound $\dim B$ below" or "$\dim A \leq \dim B$." *Pattern:* "lying over starts the chain, going up extends it, incomparability keeps it strict."

9. **Recognise a finite fibre via the fibre ring.** When $B$ is module-finite over $A$, the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is a finite-dimensional algebra over the field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, hence Artinian, hence has finitely many primes — all maximal by incomparability. *Trigger:* "how many primes over $\mathfrak{p}$?" *Pattern:* "fibre ring is finite over a field $\Rightarrow$ Artinian $\Rightarrow$ finite spectrum."

**Illegal but tempting operations:**

> [!warning] 1. Assuming going down holds for every integral extension
> Lying over, going up, and incomparability hold for *any* integral extension, with no hypothesis on the rings — so one is lured into assuming [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]] is equally free. It is not. Take $B = k[u] \times k[v]$ (two disjoint lines) and the *non-normal* subring $A = \{(f,g) : f(0) = g(0)\}$ (the lines glued at a point); $A \subseteq B$ is integral, yet there is a chain $\mathfrak{p}_2 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1$ in $A$ and a $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$ with *no* $\mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$ (see [[Ex - Going down can fail without normality]]). Going down becomes legal **exactly when $A$ is an [[Def - Integral Closure and Normal Domain|integrally closed domain]]**; the gluing in the counterexample is precisely the non-normality that the hypothesis rules out.

> [!warning] 2. Believing the fibre $\operatorname{Spec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, not $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, is the fibre
> The localization $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = (A\setminus\mathfrak{p})^{-1}B$ has *more* primes than the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$: by the prime-correspondence theorem its primes are all $\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{q} \cap (A\setminus\mathfrak{p}) = \varnothing$, i.e. $\mathfrak{q} \cap A \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ — those lying over *any* prime $\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, not just $\mathfrak{p}$ itself. The fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ is the smaller set of $\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{q}\cap A = \mathfrak{p}$ *exactly*, and these are the *maximal* ideals of $B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ (using the domain criterion). The fix: it is $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, not $\operatorname{Spec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, that is the fibre — a point where the unfamiliarity of "$B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is not a local ring" bites.

> [!warning] 3. Thinking the contraction of a chain stays strict automatically
> Contracting primes is order-preserving, so a strict chain $\mathfrak{q}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{q}_1$ in $B$ certainly gives $\mathfrak{q}_0\cap A \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1 \cap A$ in $A$ — but for a *general* ring map the contraction can collapse, $\mathfrak{q}_0\cap A = \mathfrak{q}_1\cap A$, so the contracted "chain" is shorter. (For instance $k \hookrightarrow k[X]$ contracts both $(0)$ and $(X)$ to $(0)$.) What rescues the dimension argument is precisely [[Thm - Incomparability|incomparability]]: for an *integral* extension, two distinct comparable primes cannot share a contraction, so strictness is preserved. The repair condition is integrality — without it, contracted chains may collapse and the inequality $\dim B \leq \dim A$ is false.

> [!warning] 4. Forgetting that $\dim A = \dim B$ needs only three of the four theorems
> Because going down is the subtle theorem and dimension is the headline result, it is tempting to think dimension preservation *uses* going down (and hence needs normality). It does not. $\dim B \leq \dim A$ is pure incomparability; $\dim A \leq \dim B$ is pure lying over plus going up. None of these requires $A$ normal, so **$\dim A = \dim B$ holds for every integral extension**, normal or not (see [[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension]]). Going down is needed not for the *equality of dimensions* but for the finer *catenary* statement $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$, where the position of $\mathfrak{p}$ in the chain matters.

# Problem-Solving Strategy

The first decision in any problem of this chapter is to identify which feature of the contraction map $\iota^*$ is being probed, because there are only three — surjectivity, chain-lifting, fibre-size — and each has a characteristic route.

If the problem **asks you to produce a prime over a given prime, or to show the map is onto**, you are using [[Thm - Lying Over|lying over]], and the route is fixed: the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ is $\operatorname{mSpec} B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, and $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = (A\setminus\mathfrak{p})^{-1}B$ is non-zero because $0 \notin A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, so it has a maximal ideal, which contracts to a prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak{p}$. The entire content is the translation "fibre $= \operatorname{mSpec}$ of a localization" plus "a non-zero ring has a maximal ideal". When the problem instead asks to *describe* the fibre completely — as with $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ over a rational prime — replace $B_{\mathfrak{p}}$ by the cleaner *fibre ring* $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \cong (B/\mathfrak{p}B)_{\mathfrak{p}}$, which over a field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is usually an explicit finite-dimensional algebra whose primes you can list by factoring a polynomial.

If the problem **asks you to lift an inclusion of primes**, decide the *direction*. Upward — given $\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, find $\mathfrak{q}_2 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$ — is always available: pass to the integral extension $A/\mathfrak{p}_1 \hookrightarrow B/\mathfrak{q}_1$ and apply lying over there. This is [[Thm - Going Up|going up]], and to lift a whole chain you iterate it, using [[Thm - Incomparability|incomparability]] at each stage to be sure the lifted inclusions stay strict (distinct contractions force distinct primes). Downward — given $\mathfrak{p}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_2$ and $\mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_1$, find $\mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_1$ over $\mathfrak{p}_2$ — is *conditional*: first check that $A$ is an [[Def - Integral Closure and Normal Domain|integrally closed domain]]. If it is, [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]] applies, and the mechanism is to show $\mathfrak{p}_2$ is contracted from the localization $B_{\mathfrak{q}_1}$, using the normality of $A$ to control the minimal equations of elements of $\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}_2 B}$. If $A$ is *not* normal, do not reach for going down — and if the problem is to *disprove* it, look for a "gluing" of the base that creates two branches over one point with no shared specialisation downstairs.

If the problem **concerns dimension**, the strategy is a two-sided chain comparison, and the key realisation is that the two inequalities use different theorems. For $\dim B \leq \dim A$, take a chain in $B$, contract it, and invoke incomparability to keep it strict — a chain upstairs is no longer than its image downstairs. For $\dim A \leq \dim B$, take a chain in $A$, start it upstairs with lying over, and extend it with repeated going up, again using incomparability for strictness. Putting the two together gives $\dim A = \dim B$, and the entire argument never touches going down. When the finer identity $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ is wanted (for a finitely generated $k$-domain), *then* going down enters, together with [[Commutative Algebra VII — Noether Normalization and the Nullstellensatz|Noether normalization]]: normalize to a polynomial subring, where every maximal chain has the same length, and transport chains across the integral extension using going down to splice a chain below $\mathfrak{p}$ to one above it.

If the problem **concerns finiteness of a fibre**, demand module-finiteness and use the fibre ring. When $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-module, $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is finite-dimensional over the field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, hence Artinian; an Artinian ring has finitely many primes, all maximal — and incomparability already told you they are maximal (zero-dimensional fibre). So "finite map $\Rightarrow$ finite fibres."

The meta-strategy that threads all of this: **every question in this chapter is a question about the single map $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$, and the only three things you can ask of a map are whether it is onto, how it moves chains, and how big its fibres are.** Lying over answers "onto"; going up and going down answer "how it moves chains, upward and downward"; incomparability answers "fibres are zero-dimensional"; and module-finiteness upgrades the last to "fibres are finite". Decide which of the three you are being asked, and the right theorem is determined — with the single proviso that the downward direction costs you the normality of $A$.

# Most Reusable Properties

- **[[Thm - Lying Over|Lying Over]]**: $\iota^*$ is surjective for an integral extension. This is the most reused fact because it is the *base case* of every chain-lifting argument — going up starts by laying a prime over the bottom of a chain, and the dimension inequality $\dim A \leq \dim B$ begins here. Its standard uses are existence of a prime over a given prime, surjectivity of finite maps, and (via the quotient reduction) the proof of going up itself. The trigger is any need to find *some* prime with a prescribed contraction; the mechanism is always "$B_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0$, take a maximal ideal".

- **[[Thm - Incomparability|Incomparability]]**: distinct comparable primes never share a contraction. Its reusability is as the *strictness guarantee* in every dimension argument: contracting a strict chain keeps it strict, and lifting a strict chain keeps it strict, *only* because incomparability forbids two links from collapsing onto one contraction. It is also what makes fibres zero-dimensional, hence (with finiteness) finite. Reach for it whenever a chain of primes must be shown to remain strict after contraction or lifting.

- **[[Thm - Going Up|Going Up]]**: chains lift upward along an integral extension. Its reusability is as the *engine* of the inequality $\dim A \leq \dim B$ and of all "complete this partial lift" problems. Combined with lying over (for the start) and incomparability (for strictness), it lifts an arbitrary chain of $A$ into a chain of $B$ of equal length. The standard recognition: you have a prime over the bottom of an ascending chain and want to follow the chain up.

- **[[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|Going Down]]**: chains lift downward when $A$ is normal. Its reusability is narrower but sharper — it is the tool for the *catenary* dimension formula and for any argument that needs to specialise a preimage point downward. The crucial reflex it installs is to *check normality first*: going down is the one theorem of the chapter that can fail, and recognising whether $A$ is integrally closed is the gate to using it.

- **[[Thm - Integral Extensions Preserve Dimension|Dimension preservation]]**: $\dim A = \dim B$. This is the chapter's headline reusable result, and its standard use is to *transport a known dimension across an integral extension* — most importantly, to compute $\dim k[X_1,\dots,X_n] = n$ by [[Commutative Algebra VII — Noether Normalization and the Nullstellensatz|Noether-normalizing]] any finitely generated $k$-domain down to a polynomial subring of the same dimension. Whenever a ring is integral over a simpler ring of known dimension, this fact hands you its dimension for free.

# Bridges

1. **Algebraic geometry — an integral extension is a finite map, and the Cohen–Seidenberg theorems are how a finite map moves points.** To a finitely generated $k$-algebra $A$ one attaches the affine variety $\operatorname{Spec} A$ (or its classical points $\operatorname{mSpec} A$), and a module-finite integral extension $A \subseteq B$ corresponds to a **finite morphism of varieties** $\pi : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ — a map whose fibres are finite and which is "the same size everywhere". [[Thm - Lying Over|Lying over]] is the statement that $\pi$ is *surjective*: every point of the base has a preimage. [[Thm - Going Up|Going up]] is the statement that $\pi$ is a *closed map*: it carries closed subvarieties to closed subvarieties, so the image of a variety is again a variety, with no points escaping to a non-closed limit (this is the algebraic shadow of *properness*). [[Thm - Incomparability|Incomparability]] is the statement that the fibres are *zero-dimensional*, and with module-finiteness, *finite*. The chapter is thus the complete local-to-global dictionary for finite morphisms; the prototype $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ is a two-sheeted cover branched at $2$.

2. **Number theory — the splitting of primes in a number field is exactly the fibre of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_K \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$.** For a number field $K$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, the extension $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$ is integral (indeed module-finite), so all four theorems apply. A rational prime $p$ "splits", "ramifies", or "is inert" according to how the fibre of the finite map $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_K \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ over $(p)$ decomposes — and lying over guarantees the fibre is non-empty, incomparability that the primes above $p$ are incomparable (zero-dimensional), and module-finiteness that there are finitely many. The case $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[i]$, worked out in [[Ex - Primes of Z[i] over a rational prime]], is the model: $p$ splits when $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, is inert when $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and ramifies at $p = 2$ — read off from the factorisation of $X^2 + 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$. The general fact $\dim \mathcal{O}_K = \dim \mathbb{Z} = 1$ is dimension preservation, and it is why $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a one-dimensional ring (a [[Commutative Algebra XIII — Dedekind Domains and DVRs|Dedekind domain]]).

3. **Dimension theory — preservation of dimension under finite maps is the foundation of the dimension of varieties.** The identity $\dim A = \dim B$ for an integral extension is what makes Krull dimension *computable*: by [[Commutative Algebra VII — Noether Normalization and the Nullstellensatz|Noether normalization]], every finitely generated $k$-domain $A$ is module-finite over a polynomial subring $k[X_1, \dots, X_d]$, and dimension preservation then gives $\dim A = \dim k[X_1,\dots,X_d] = d = \operatorname{trdeg}_k A$. The finer dimension formula $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} + \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ — codimension of a subvariety plus its dimension equals the dimension of the ambient space — runs on [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]] and incomparability to show all maximal chains of primes have the same length (the *catenary* property). This is the bridge from the present chapter into [[Commutative Algebra XII — Dimension Theory|the dimension theory of Noetherian rings]], where height meets the Krull height theorem.

4. **Topology and the theory of proper maps — going up is closedness, and the failure mode is escape to infinity.** In topology a *proper* map (preimages of compacts are compact) is closed, and a finite map of varieties is the algebraic-geometric proper map. A precise statement makes this concrete: $f^* : \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is a closed map *if and only if* $f$ satisfies going up. The contrast that illuminates the bridge is the *non-integral* map $k[T_1] \to k[T_1, T_2]/(T_1 T_2 - 1)$, whose induced map on spectra is the projection of the hyperbola $\{ab = 1\}$ to the $a$-axis; its image is $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$, which is *not closed* — a point has "escaped to infinity" as $a \to 0$ and $b \to \infty$. Integrality is exactly the condition that forbids this escape, which is why an integral extension gives a closed, surjective, finite map, and a general extension gives none of those guarantees.

# Insights

**The unifying frame: this chapter is the dictionary "integral extension $\leftrightarrow$ finite map", and each theorem is one adjective of "finite".** Read the four theorems as the four words in "surjective closed finite-fibred map": [[Thm - Lying Over|lying over]] is *surjective*, [[Thm - Going Up|going up]] is *closed*, [[Thm - Incomparability|incomparability]] is *finite-fibred* (zero-dimensional fibres), and [[Thm - Going Down for Integrally Closed Domains|going down]] is *equidimensional / no fibre-jumping* — the one adjective that costs an extra hypothesis. Once this frame is in place, you never have to memorise which theorem says what: ask "which feature of a finite map is this?" and the theorem names itself. The deep reason the dictionary works is that integrality is *exactly* the algebraic condition making the induced map of spectra behave like a finite covering — it forbids points from disappearing (surjective), from escaping to non-closed limits (closed), and from accumulating into positive-dimensional fibres (finite-fibred). A finite map is a "constant-size" map of spaces, and integrality is its coordinate-free signature.

**The true name of the fibre: it is the spectrum of a single ring, the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$.** Students think of the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ as "the set of primes $\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{q}\cap A = \mathfrak{p}$" — correct, but inert. The operational true name is: **the fibre over $\mathfrak{p}$ is $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the fibre ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \cong (B/\mathfrak{p}B)_{\mathfrak{p}}$, an algebra over the field $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$.** This single reformulation converts every fibre question into the study of one explicit ring over a field, where the full machinery — factoring polynomials, counting maximal ideals, testing Artinian-ness — is available. Lying over becomes "this ring is non-zero"; incomparability becomes "this ring has dimension zero"; finiteness of the fibre becomes "this ring is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, hence Artinian". The reason $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ over $(p)$ is so transparent is precisely that the fibre ring $\mathbb{F}_p[X]/(X^2+1)$ is computed by one factorisation.

**A trigger-reaction pattern worth burning in: "lift a chain" means "lying over at the bottom, going up at each step, incomparability for strictness".** When a problem asks you to follow a chain of primes from one ring to the other, the three theorems fire in a fixed sequence. Lying over places a prime over the bottom of the chain; going up extends the lift one link at a time as the base primes grow; and at every stage incomparability guarantees the lifted primes are *distinct* (because their contractions are distinct), so the strict chain downstairs becomes a strict chain upstairs of the same length. This single reflex proves $\dim A \leq \dim B$, proves that chains lift, and — run in reverse, contracting instead of lifting — proves $\dim B \leq \dim A$. The complementary reflex is the *normality gate*: the moment a problem wants to lift a chain *downward*, stop and check whether $A$ is integrally closed, because going down is the one theorem here that can fail, and the "two lines glued at a point" counterexample is the picture of how it fails — a non-normal base lets two branches sit over one point with no common specialisation below.
