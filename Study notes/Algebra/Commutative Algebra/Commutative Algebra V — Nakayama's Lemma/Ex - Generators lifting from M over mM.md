# Problem Statement

Let $(R, \mathfrak m)$ be a [[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field|local ring]] with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak m$, and let $M$ be a [[Def - Finitely Generated Module|finitely generated]] $R$-module. Take $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$ whose images in $M/\mathfrak m M$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ as a $k$-vector space. Prove that $x_1, \dots, x_n$ generate $M$ as an $R$-module.

(This is Becker Example Sheet 3, Q1(a).)

**Recall:**

The objects in play are a local ring, a finitely generated module, the submodule $\mathfrak m M$, and the submodule form of Nakayama's lemma.

![[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field#Local ring]]

A [[Def - Finitely Generated Module|finitely generated]] $R$-module $M$ has a finite list $m_1,\dots,m_r$ with $M = Rm_1 + \dots + Rm_r$.

The submodule $\mathfrak m M = \{\sum_i a_i m_i : a_i \in \mathfrak m,\ m_i \in M\}$ is generated by the products $a m$ with $a \in \mathfrak m$, $m \in M$; the quotient $M/\mathfrak m M$ is a module over $k = R/\mathfrak m$, i.e. a $k$-vector space, because $\mathfrak m$ annihilates it.

The key tool — the submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]]:

![[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma#Statement]]

For a local ring the Jacobson radical is $J(R) = \mathfrak m$, so the hypothesis "$\mathfrak a \subseteq J(R)$" is met by $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m$.

# Convergent Strategy

**Problem class.** This is a *generation* problem of the canonical local-ring type: prove that a candidate list generates a module by checking it modulo $\mathfrak m$. As the [[Commutative Algebra V — Nakayama's Lemma#Problem-Solving Strategy|topic page strategy]] records, every "does this generate?" question over a local ring routes through the residue-field reduction $M \rightsquigarrow M/\mathfrak m M$, where generation becomes spanning.

**Assumption pattern.** Two hypotheses are present and each is load-bearing. *Locality* gives $J(R) = \mathfrak m$, so the maximal ideal is the Jacobson radical and Nakayama accepts $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m$. *Finite generation* of $M$ is the non-negotiable Nakayama hypothesis. The third input, "images span $M/\mathfrak m M$", is the recognisable trigger: a spanning condition modulo $\mathfrak m$ is the disguised form of "$\mathfrak m M + N = M$".

**Theorem routing.** The route is: let $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$ be the submodule generated by the candidates; translate "images span $M/\mathfrak m M$" into "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$"; apply the submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]] with $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m$ to conclude $N = M$. This is exactly [[Thm - Generators Modulo the Maximal Ideal]], and the exercise is its proof.

**Key decision point.** The single non-obvious move is recognising that *spanning the quotient $M/\mathfrak m M$* is the same data as *filling $M$ modulo $\mathfrak m M$*. Lifting a $k$-linear combination $\bar m = \sum \bar c_i \bar x_i$ to $R$ introduces an error term in $\mathfrak m M$ — and that error term is precisely the "$\mathfrak m M$" in the Nakayama hypothesis. Once you see the spanning condition as "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$", the proof is one invocation of the lemma; the temptation to instead chase generators directly leads nowhere.

# Legal Operations Used

This solution deploys the following legal operations from [[Commutative Algebra V — Nakayama's Lemma#Legal Operations|the topic page's Legal Operations]]:

1. **Reduce modulo $\mathfrak m$ to land in a vector space** (operation 5). Pass from $M$ to the $k$-vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$, where "spanning" is a precise linear-algebra condition.

2. **Lift a spanning set from the residue field** (operation 6). Recognise that a list spanning $M/\mathfrak m M$ is the input to the lifting theorem.

3. **Pass to the quotient $M/N$ to use the submodule form** (operation 7). Form $N = \sum R x_i$ and rewrite the spanning condition as $\mathfrak m M + N = M$, the hypothesis of submodule-Nakayama.

4. **Invert $1 + (\text{Jacobson element})$** (operation 4), used inside Nakayama: $\mathfrak m = J(R)$, so the annihilator $1 - a$ produced by the determinant trick is a unit.

> [!note]- Hint 1
> The conclusion "$x_1,\dots,x_n$ generate $M$" is a statement about a submodule. Give that submodule a name: $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$. You want $N = M$. What does the hypothesis "images span $M/\mathfrak m M$" say about $N$ relative to $M$?

> [!note]- Hint 2
> Translate the spanning condition. If $\bar x_1,\dots,\bar x_n$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$, then every $m \in M$ has $\bar m = \sum \bar c_i \bar x_i$, which lifts to $m - \sum c_i x_i \in \mathfrak m M$. So $m \in N + \mathfrak m M$. Conclude $M = N + \mathfrak m M$.

> [!note]- Hint 3
> You now have $\mathfrak m M + N = M$ with $M$ finitely generated and (since $R$ is local) $\mathfrak m = J(R)$. This is exactly the hypothesis of the submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]]. Apply it.

# Solution

The proof is a single application of Nakayama's lemma, with all the work in the translation. We name the submodule $N$ generated by the candidates, show the spanning hypothesis is exactly "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$", and let the submodule form of Nakayama collapse this to $N = M$.

**Step 1: Name the submodule and translate the spanning hypothesis into $M = N + \mathfrak m M$.**

Set $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$. The hypothesis that $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ is equivalent to $M = N + \mathfrak m M$.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Let $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n \subseteq M$. Take any $m \in M$ and consider its image $\bar m \in M/\mathfrak m M$. By hypothesis the $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ over $k = R/\mathfrak m$, so there are scalars $\bar c_i \in k$ with
> $$\bar m = \bar c_1 \bar x_1 + \dots + \bar c_n \bar x_n.$$
> Lift each $\bar c_i$ to an element $c_i \in R$. Then in $M$,
> $$\overline{m - (c_1 x_1 + \dots + c_n x_n)} = 0 \quad\text{in } M/\mathfrak m M,$$
> which means $m - \sum_i c_i x_i \in \mathfrak m M$. Since $\sum_i c_i x_i \in N$, we get $m \in N + \mathfrak m M$. As $m$ was arbitrary, $M \subseteq N + \mathfrak m M$; the reverse inclusion holds because $N, \mathfrak m M \subseteq M$. Hence
> $$M = N + \mathfrak m M, \quad\text{equivalently}\quad \mathfrak m M + N = M.$$

**Step 2: Apply the submodule form of Nakayama to conclude $N = M$.**

Since $M$ is finitely generated and $\mathfrak m = J(R)$, Nakayama gives $N = M$, so the $x_i$ generate.

> [!note]- Derivation
> Because $R$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, the [[Def - The Jacobson Radical|Jacobson radical]] is $J(R) = \mathfrak m$, so $\mathfrak a := \mathfrak m$ satisfies $\mathfrak a \subseteq J(R)$. We have $M$ finitely generated, $N \subseteq M$ a submodule, and from Step 1, $\mathfrak a M + N = \mathfrak m M + N = M$. The submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]] then gives
> $$N = M.$$
> By definition of $N$, every element of $M$ is an $R$-linear combination of $x_1,\dots,x_n$, so these elements generate $M$ as an $R$-module. $\blacksquare$
>
> (For completeness, the submodule form is itself proved by passing to $M/N$: $\mathfrak m(M/N) = (\mathfrak m M + N)/N = M/N$, and the bare Nakayama lemma on the finitely generated module $M/N$ gives $M/N = 0$.)

> [!note]- Complete formal solution
> **Claim.** Over a local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$, if $x_1,\dots,x_n$ have images spanning $M/\mathfrak m M$ (with $M$ finitely generated), then they generate $M$.
>
> Let $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$. For any $m \in M$, write $\bar m = \sum_i \bar c_i \bar x_i$ in $M/\mathfrak m M$ ($\bar c_i \in k$), lift to $c_i \in R$, and observe $m - \sum_i c_i x_i \in \mathfrak m M$, so $m \in N + \mathfrak m M$. Hence $M = N + \mathfrak m M$.
>
> Since $R$ is local, $J(R) = \mathfrak m$, so $\mathfrak m \subseteq J(R)$. As $M$ is finitely generated and $\mathfrak m M + N = M$, the submodule form of Nakayama's lemma gives $N = M$. Therefore $x_1,\dots,x_n$ generate $M$. $\blacksquare$

# Key Takeaways

**Spanning the residue-field reduction is the same data as generating the module, over a local ring.** The entire force of this exercise is the equivalence "$\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$" $\iff$ "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$" $\iff$ (by Nakayama) "$x_i$ generate $M$". This is the trigger to internalise: whenever you must prove a list generates a module over a local ring, *stop working in the module and pass to the vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$*, where you have bases, dimension, and rank. The lift back is free, supplied by Nakayama. This converts an intractable module question into a finite linear-algebra computation, and it is the single most-used consequence of Nakayama in practice — it is how minimal free resolutions are built, how coherent sheaves are shown to be locally generated, and how one checks a presentation map is surjective.

**The error term lives in $\mathfrak m M$, and that is exactly what Nakayama absorbs.** The mechanical heart of the translation is that lifting a $k$-linear relation $\bar m = \sum \bar c_i \bar x_i$ to $R$ does not give an exact equality $m = \sum c_i x_i$ — it gives one *up to an element of $\mathfrak m M$*. Recognising that this unavoidable error term is precisely the "$\mathfrak m M$" in the hypothesis "$\mathfrak m M + N = M$" is the key insight. The general pattern for spaced practice: whenever you reduce modulo an ideal and lift, expect an error term valued in that ideal times the module, and check whether Nakayama (or a localization argument) can absorb it. The error term is not a nuisance to be eliminated; it is the input to the lemma.

**Both local hypotheses earn their place, and dropping either breaks the result.** Locality is used to make $\mathfrak m = J(R)$, without which Nakayama would not accept $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m$; over a ring with $J(R)$ smaller than $\mathfrak m$, the conclusion fails. Finite generation is the standing Nakayama hypothesis; without it the Prüfer module gives $\mathfrak m M = M \neq 0$ and the whole machine breaks. When returning to this proof after months, the reconstruction hinges on remembering the two-line route — name $N$, translate to $\mathfrak m M + N = M$, invoke submodule-Nakayama — and on recalling *why* the ring must be local: there must be a single residue field to reduce against. See [[Ex - Minimal generators of the maximal ideal and the cotangent space]] for the sharper version that also counts the minimal number of generators.
