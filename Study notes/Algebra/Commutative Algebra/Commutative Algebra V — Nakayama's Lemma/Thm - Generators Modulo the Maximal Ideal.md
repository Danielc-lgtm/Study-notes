# Notation

All rings are commutative with $1$. Throughout, $(R, \mathfrak m)$ is a [[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field|local ring]] with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak m$, and $M$ is a [[Def - Finitely Generated Module|finitely generated]] $R$-module. We write $\mathfrak m M$ for the [[Def - Submodule|submodule]] generated by products, $M/\mathfrak m M$ for the quotient (a $k$-vector space), $\bar x = x + \mathfrak m M$ for the image of $x \in M$, and $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$ for the submodule generated by elements $x_i$. The full registry is on [[Commutative Algebra V — Nakayama's Lemma]].

---

# Statement

> **Theorem (generators modulo the maximal ideal).** Let $(R, \mathfrak m)$ be a local ring with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak m$, let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module, and let $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$ be elements whose images $\bar x_1, \dots, \bar x_n$ **span** the $k$-vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$. Then $x_1, \dots, x_n$ **generate** $M$ as an $R$-module.

> **Corollary (minimal generators).** The minimal number of generators of $M$ equals $\dim_k(M/\mathfrak m M)$, and a generating set is minimal if and only if its images form a $k$-basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$. In particular, every minimal generating set has the same cardinality.

This is the theorem that turns module-generation (hard, no bases) into vector-space spanning (easy, full linear algebra) over a local ring. It is the everyday form in which Nakayama is used.

---

# Motivation

This theorem is the reason vector spaces are useful for studying modules. Over a [[Def - Local Ring and Residue Field|local ring]], the only invariant you can compute by linear algebra is the dimension of the residue-field reduction $M/\mathfrak m M$ — and this theorem says that single number, together with the lifting it licenses, controls the generation of $M$ completely. It converts the intractable question "do these elements generate $M$?" into the elementary question "do their images span $M/\mathfrak m M$?", which is a rank computation over a field.

The gap it closes is exactly the one identified in the [[Commutative Algebra IV — Localization|localization]] and [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama]] chapters: reducing modulo $\mathfrak m$ is easy and loses information, and the theorem is the guarantee that the lost information does not affect generation. The cleanest way to see the stakes is the minimal-generators corollary. Over a general ring, "minimal generating set" is a broken notion — different minimal sets can have different sizes (over $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Z/6$ is minimally generated by $\{1\}$ and irredundantly by $\{2,3\}$). Over a local ring this pathology vanishes: a generating set is minimal exactly when it lifts a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$, so all minimal sets have the *same* size $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, and that size is the honest invariant $\mu(M)$. The number of generators a module needs becomes computable.

Geometrically, this is the statement that **sections of a sheaf that fill the fibre at a point generate the stalk near the point** — finitely many germs, enough to span the fibre, suffice to generate. It is the local-generation principle that makes coherent sheaves manageable. And applied to $M = \mathfrak m$, it computes the embedding dimension $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, the cotangent-space invariant that detects smoothness. So a single theorem ranges from "how many generators?" to "is this point smooth?".

# Sources and Targets

**Sources (Input Broadening)**

The precondition $A$ is *$(R,\mathfrak m)$ local, $M$ finitely generated, and the $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$*. Recognising "spanning the fibre" in disguise is the skill.

The first disguised source is **a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$**. The property $B$ is "I have a $k$-basis of the fibre", which a fortiori spans, so $A$ holds and the lifts generate. The bonus is minimality. The non-obvious part is that you may choose the basis *first*, in the vector space, then lift arbitrarily. *Example problem:* constructing a minimal generating set of a module over a local ring.

The second disguised source is **a single element generating the fibre of a cyclic-looking module**. The property $B$ is "$\bar x$ spans $M/\mathfrak m M$" (so $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M \leq 1$); then $x$ generates $M$, i.e. $M = Rx$ is cyclic. *Example problem:* recognising a module as cyclic by checking its fibre is at most one-dimensional.

The third disguised source is **a map $\varphi : R^n \to M$ that is surjective on fibres**. The property $B$ is "$\bar\varphi : k^n \to M/\mathfrak m M$ is onto", i.e. the images of the standard basis span $M/\mathfrak m M$; then $\varphi$ is surjective. The bridge is "fibrewise surjective $\Rightarrow$ surjective" over a local ring. *Example problem:* proving a presentation map is a surjection by checking it modulo $\mathfrak m$.

**Targets (Output Amplification)**

The conclusion $C$ is "$x_1,\dots,x_n$ generate $M$".

Combine $C$ with **counting the spanning set against $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$**. If $n = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, the spanning images are a basis, so the generating set is *minimal*; if $n > \dim$, you can drop generators down to a basis-lift. The further result $E$ is the exact minimal generator count $\mu(M) = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$. Nonobvious because it pins down "minimal" via a field computation.

Combine $C$ with **a second module and a fibrewise isomorphism**. If $\varphi : M \to M'$ induces an isomorphism $M/\mathfrak m M \cong M'/\mathfrak m M'$ and $M'$ is finitely generated, then $\varphi$ is surjective (its image spans the fibre of $M'$, hence generates); if both are free of the same rank, $\varphi$ is an isomorphism. The further result $E$ is "fibrewise iso lifts to surjection/iso", the basis of rank semicontinuity. Nonobvious because fibre data looks far weaker than module data.

Combine $C$ with **$M = \mathfrak m$**. The generators of $\mathfrak m$ are the lifts of a basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, so the embedding dimension is $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. The further result $E$ is the cotangent-space computation of smoothness. Nonobvious because it identifies "number of equations to cut out the point" with a linear-algebra dimension.

# Why Is It True

The theorem is almost a restatement of the submodule form of Nakayama; the only idea is to recognise "spanning the fibre" as the Nakayama hypothesis "fills modulo $\mathfrak m M$". Let $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$ be the submodule generated by the candidate generators. To say their images $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ is to say every $\bar m \in M/\mathfrak m M$ is a $k$-combination of the $\bar x_i$ — which lifts to: every $m \in M$ is an $R$-combination of the $x_i$ *plus an element of $\mathfrak m M$*. In symbols, $M = N + \mathfrak m M$, i.e. $\mathfrak m M + N = M$. That is precisely the hypothesis of the submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]], with $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m \subseteq J(R) = \mathfrak m$ (this is where locality enters: $\mathfrak m$ *is* the Jacobson radical). Nakayama concludes $N = M$ — the $x_i$ generate.

**The one-line mechanism: "images span $M/\mathfrak m M$" is the same statement as "$N + \mathfrak m M = M$", and over a local ring $\mathfrak m = J(R)$, so Nakayama upgrades it to $N = M$.** Everything else is unwinding the quotient.

The corollary follows by counting. Any generating set's images must span $M/\mathfrak m M$ (reduction is surjective), so any generating set has at least $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ elements — that is the lower bound. Conversely, lifting a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$ gives a generating set of exactly that size by the theorem — that is the upper bound. So the minimum is exactly $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, achieved precisely by basis-lifts, which are therefore the minimal generating sets.

---

# What Makes This Hard

The difficulty is entirely in the *recognition* that "spanning the fibre" equals "$N + \mathfrak m M = M$" — once seen, the theorem is one application of Nakayama. The non-obvious step is the translation from the vector-space statement (images span $M/\mathfrak m M$) to the module statement ($M = N + \mathfrak m M$), which requires understanding that lifting a $k$-combination introduces an error term in $\mathfrak m M$. The most common error is to forget that the theorem needs *locality* — over a non-local ring there is no single residue field, and the conclusion is false (the $\mathbb Z/6$ example: images of $\{2\}$ span $\mathbb Z/6 \otimes \mathbb F_3 = \mathbb F_3$ but $\{2\}$ does not generate $\mathbb Z/6$ as a $\mathbb Z$-module... the point being there is no single $\mathfrak m$ to reduce against).

# Rederivation Scaffold

**This section is self-sufficient: reading only what follows should let you reconstruct the entire proof.**

**High-level strategy:**
Set $N = \sum R x_i$. Translate "images span $M/\mathfrak m M$" into "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$". Apply the submodule form of Nakayama (with $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m = J(R)$, since $R$ is local) to conclude $N = M$. For the corollary, bound any generating set below by $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ and realise the bound by lifting a basis.

**Subgoal decomposition:**

1. **Translate spanning into a module equation.** Show that "$\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$" is equivalent to "$M = N + \mathfrak m M$" where $N = \sum R x_i$.
   - *Hint:* Reduction mod $\mathfrak m M$ is surjective; a $k$-combination of $\bar x_i$ equal to $\bar m$ lifts to $m \in N + \mathfrak m M$.
   - *Why needed:* It produces the exact hypothesis of submodule-Nakayama.

2. **Apply Nakayama.** Conclude $N = M$, i.e. the $x_i$ generate.
   - *Hint:* $\mathfrak m = J(R)$ for a local ring; submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma]] gives $\mathfrak m M + N = M \Rightarrow N = M$.
   - *Why needed:* It is the upgrade from "fills modulo $\mathfrak m M$" to "fills".

3. **Corollary by counting.** Show $\mu(M) = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ and that minimal sets are basis-lifts.
   - *Hint:* Any generating set's images span the fibre (lower bound); a basis-lift generates (upper bound, by the theorem).
   - *Why needed:* It makes "minimal number of generators" a well-defined invariant.

# Lemma Decomposition

> [!note]- Lemma 1: Spanning the fibre means filling modulo $\mathfrak m M$
> **Statement:** With $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$, the images $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ over $k$ if and only if $M = N + \mathfrak m M$.
>
> **Hint:** The quotient map $M \to M/\mathfrak m M$ is surjective and sends $N$ onto $\sum k \bar x_i$; "the $\bar x_i$ span" says this image is everything.
>
> **Why needed:** It converts the vector-space hypothesis of the theorem into the module hypothesis of submodule-Nakayama.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > ($\Rightarrow$) Suppose the $\bar x_i$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$. Given $m \in M$, write $\bar m = \sum_i \bar c_i \bar x_i$ in $M/\mathfrak m M$ with $c_i \in R$ (lifting the field scalars $\bar c_i \in k$ to $R$). Then $m - \sum_i c_i x_i \in \mathfrak m M$, so $m \in N + \mathfrak m M$. Hence $M \subseteq N + \mathfrak m M$; the reverse inclusion is clear. ($\Leftarrow$) If $M = N + \mathfrak m M$, reducing mod $\mathfrak m M$ gives $M/\mathfrak m M = (N + \mathfrak m M)/\mathfrak m M$, which is the image of $N$, namely $\sum_i k \bar x_i$, so the $\bar x_i$ span.

> [!note]- Lemma 2: Any generating set has at least $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ elements
> **Statement:** If $y_1,\dots,y_r$ generate $M$, then $r \geq \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, with equality iff $\bar y_1,\dots,\bar y_r$ is a $k$-basis.
>
> **Hint:** The images $\bar y_j$ span $M/\mathfrak m M$ (reduction of a generating set spans), and a spanning set of a vector space has at least $\dim$ elements.
>
> **Why needed:** It is the lower bound that makes $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ the true minimum.
>
> > [!note]- Full proof
> > If $y_1,\dots,y_r$ generate $M$, their images generate (hence span) $M/\mathfrak m M$ over $k$, since the reduction map $M \to M/\mathfrak m M$ is a surjective $R$-module map factoring through $k$. A spanning set of a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space has at least $\dim_k$ elements, so $r \geq \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, with equality precisely when the spanning images are a basis (a spanning set of size equal to the dimension is a basis).

# Formal Proof

> [!note]- Complete formal proof
> Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be local, $k = R/\mathfrak m$, $M$ finitely generated, and $x_1,\dots,x_n \in M$ with images spanning $M/\mathfrak m M$.
>
> **Step 0 — locality supplies $\mathfrak m = J(R)$.** Because $R$ has a unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, the Jacobson radical is $J(R) = \mathfrak m$. So the ideal $\mathfrak a = \mathfrak m$ satisfies $\mathfrak a \subseteq J(R)$, the hypothesis of Nakayama.
>
> **Theorem.** Set $N = Rx_1 + \dots + Rx_n$. By Lemma 1, the spanning hypothesis gives $M = N + \mathfrak m M$, i.e. $\mathfrak m M + N = M$. Since $M$ is finitely generated and $\mathfrak m \subseteq J(R)$, the submodule form of [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]] yields $N = M$. Thus $x_1,\dots,x_n$ generate $M$.
>
> **Corollary.** Let $d = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$ (finite, as $M$ is finitely generated). By Lemma 2 any generating set has $\geq d$ elements. Choosing $\bar x_1,\dots,\bar x_d$ a $k$-basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$ and lifting to $x_1,\dots,x_d \in M$, the theorem makes $x_1,\dots,x_d$ generate $M$; this generating set has exactly $d$ elements, so the minimum is $d$. A generating set is minimal iff it has $d$ elements iff (by Lemma 2's equality case) its images form a basis. Hence all minimal generating sets have cardinality $d = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$. $\blacksquare$

# Cross-Field Exercise Suggestions

**Local generation of coherent sheaves (algebraic geometry).** For a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ with stalk $M = \mathcal F_p$ over the local ring $\mathcal O_p$, sections whose values span the fibre $\mathcal F_p/\mathfrak m_p \mathcal F_p$ generate $\mathcal F$ near $p$. The nonobvious recognition is that "values span the fibre" is the theorem's spanning hypothesis; this is why the rank of $\mathcal F$ is upper semicontinuous and why generating a sheaf is a fibrewise check.

**Smoothness via the Jacobian criterion (differential and algebraic geometry).** The embedding dimension $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ — computed by this theorem applied to $M = \mathfrak m$ — equals the rank of the Jacobian's cokernel at the point, and the point is smooth when this matches the dimension. The nonobvious link is that counting minimal generators of $\mathfrak m$ *is* measuring the tangent directions, connecting algebra to the implicit function theorem.

**Lifting bases of $G/pG$ for $p$-groups (group theory / number theory).** For a finitely generated abelian $p$-group $G$ over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ (or $\mathbb Z_p$), a set lifting a basis of $G/pG$ over $\mathbb F_p$ generates $G$ minimally — the **Burnside basis theorem** analogue. The nonobvious application is that the Frattini quotient $G/\Phi(G)$ plays the role of $M/\mathfrak m M$, and "lift a basis of the Frattini quotient" is exactly this theorem in the group setting.

# Bridges

- **[[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma|Nakayama's lemma]]** — this theorem is the submodule form of Nakayama, re-expressed for the residue field. The translation "spanning $M/\mathfrak m M$" $=$ "$\mathfrak m M + N = M$" is the only new content; Nakayama does the rest. It is the form in which Nakayama is actually deployed in computations.

- **[[Def - Minimal Generating Set and the Cotangent Space|Minimal generating sets and the cotangent space]]** — the corollary *is* the well-definedness of $\mu(M) = \dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$, which makes that definition meaningful. Applied to $M = \mathfrak m$, it computes the embedding dimension $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, the cotangent-space invariant.

- **[[Thm - Finitely Generated Modules and Surjections from a Free Module|Surjections from free modules]]** — combined with this theorem, a fibrewise-surjective map $R^n \to M$ is surjective, so a presentation can be checked modulo $\mathfrak m$. This is how one builds minimal free resolutions: at each stage, lift a fibre basis.

- **[[Thm - The Local-Global Principle|The local–global principle]]** — this theorem is the local half of "generate globally by generating locally". Over a general ring, a module is generated by elements whose localizations generate at every maximal ideal, and this theorem reduces each local check to a residue-field computation.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] Minimal free resolutions and the Frattini-type quotient *(from Homological Algebra)*
> Iterating this theorem — lift a basis of the fibre, take the kernel, repeat — produces the **minimal free resolution** of a module over a Noetherian local ring, with ranks the **Betti numbers**. The theorem is what guarantees minimality at each step: the generators chosen are exactly a basis of the fibre, so the next differential has entries in $\mathfrak m$.

> [!tip] Upper semicontinuity of fibre dimension *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> Because minimal generators of a stalk are counted by the fibre dimension $\dim_k \mathcal F_p/\mathfrak m_p \mathcal F_p$, this dimension is **upper semicontinuous** in $p$ — it jumps up only on closed sets. For a finitely generated module over a domain this is how one defines the **generic rank** and locates the locus where a sheaf fails to be locally free. The dimension theory lives in **Commutative Algebra XII — Dimension Theory**.
