## §2.1 Noetherian and Artinian Modules — Exercises

The exercises of §2.1 drill the two chain conditions and the single most important fact about them — that they are *independent*. The central skill is the dichotomy between proving a chain condition (exhibit the object as a quotient/extension of something known, or bound chains by a strictly-monotone invariant) and refuting one (produce a single non-stabilising chain). The two headline counterexamples — the dyadic Prüfer group (Artinian, not Noetherian) and an infinite-dimensional vector space (neither) — anchor the independence, while the vector-space collapse shows that over a field all the conditions coincide with dimension. Throughout, the working technique is to classify the submodule lattice and read the chain conditions off its order type.

- [[Ex - The dyadic rationals mod Z are Artinian but not Noetherian]] (⭐⭐) — classify all submodules of the Prüfer group $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]/\mathbb{Z}$ as the cyclic pieces $\tfrac{1}{2^n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}$, then read off that they form a single ascending chain with no top (no Noetherian) but admit no infinite descending chain (Artinian); the crux is that an element of order $2^n$ generates its whole level because odd numerators are units mod $2^n$ ([[Def - Noetherian and Artinian Module]], [[Def - Submodule]], [[Def - Module]]).

- [[Ex - A vector space is Noetherian iff Artinian iff finite-dimensional]] (⭐⭐) — prove the four-way equivalence $\dim_k V < \infty \iff \ell(V) < \infty \iff$ Noetherian $\iff$ Artinian, by bounding chain length with dimension in the finite case and exhibiting both an ascending chain $\langle v_1, \dots, v_n\rangle$ and a descending chain $\langle v_n, v_{n+1}, \dots\rangle$ in the infinite case, then citing the finite-length criterion ([[Def - Noetherian and Artinian Module]], [[Def - Composition Series and Length]], [[Thm - Length is Additive and Finite iff Noetherian and Artinian]]).

- [[Ex - Every finitely generated ideal has a finite generating subset]] (⭐) — show that if a finitely generated ideal $I$ is generated by an arbitrary set $S$, some finite subset $S_0 \subseteq S$ already generates $I$, by expanding each of the finitely many generators of $I$ as a finite combination of elements of $S$ and collecting the finitely many used; the algebraic content of "an infinite system of equations is equivalent to a finite subsystem" ([[Def - Ideal]], [[Def - Ring]]).
