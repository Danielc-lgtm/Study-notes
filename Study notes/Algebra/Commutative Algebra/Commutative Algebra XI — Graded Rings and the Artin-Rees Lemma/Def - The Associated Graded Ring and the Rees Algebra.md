# Notation

All rings are commutative with $1$. Let $R$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{a} \trianglelefteq R$ an [[Def - Ideal|ideal]], $M$ an [[Def - Module|$R$-module]], and $(M_n)_{n \geq 0}$ a [[Def - Filtration and Stable Filtration|filtration]] of $M$. We write $\mathfrak{a}^n$ for the $n$-th power of $\mathfrak{a}$ (with $\mathfrak{a}^0 = R$). The **associated graded ring** is $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ (Becker writes $G_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$), the **associated graded module** is $\operatorname{gr}(M) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} M_n/M_{n+1}$, and the **Rees algebra** is $R^* = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{a}^n$ with associated graded $R^*$-module $M^* = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} M_n$. An overline $\bar{x}$ denotes the image of $x$ in the relevant quotient. The full registry is on [[Commutative Algebra XI — Graded Rings and the Artin-Rees Lemma]].

This is a compound page: it defines four interlocking constructions — the **associated graded ring** $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$, the **associated graded module** $\operatorname{gr}(M)$, the **Rees algebra** $R^*$, and the **Rees module** $M^*$ — because they are two views of the same idea (turning a filtration into a graded object) and the proofs in the chapter pass freely between them. The associated graded keeps only the *quotients* of successive levels; the Rees algebra keeps the *levels themselves*.

# Axiom Motivation

You have a filtered ring or module — a tower $M = M_0 \supseteq M_1 \supseteq \cdots$ — and you want to convert this analytic object (a notion of "smallness", a topology) into an algebraic object you can run Noetherian machinery on. There are exactly two honest ways to do it, and they are the two constructions on this page. Either you remember the levels and *stack* them into one big graded ring (the Rees algebra), or you remember only how each level differs from the next and *collapse* the tower into its layer-by-layer quotients (the associated graded). Both turn "filtered" into "graded", and the art is knowing which to reach for.

**The Rees algebra: stack the powers into one ring so that "stable" becomes "finitely generated".** Start from the cleanest motivation. A stable $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration is one that is eventually generated by powers of $\mathfrak{a}$ from a single level — a *finiteness* statement that is awkward to express level by level. The way to make finiteness visible is to assemble all the levels into one object and ask it to be a finitely generated module. Define
$$R^* = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{a}^n = R \oplus \mathfrak{a} \oplus \mathfrak{a}^2 \oplus \cdots,$$
with the multiplication that sends $x \in \mathfrak{a}^m$ (in the $m$-th slot) and $y \in \mathfrak{a}^n$ (in the $n$-th slot) to $xy \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+n}$ (in the $(m+n)$-th slot). This is a [[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module|graded ring]] — degrees add by construction — and crucially, when $R$ is [[Def - Noetherian Ring|Noetherian]] with $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$, the elements $x_1, \dots, x_r$ placed in the *degree-one slot* generate $R^*$ as an $R$-algebra, so $R^* = R[x_1, \dots, x_r]$ (a quotient of a polynomial ring) is itself Noetherian by [[Thm - Hilbert's Basis Theorem|Hilbert's basis theorem]]. Now the magic: a filtration $(M_n)$ gives the module $M^* = \bigoplus M_n$ over $R^*$, and **$M^*$ is finitely generated over $R^*$ if and only if the filtration is stable** (this is the lemma at the heart of [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma|Artin–Rees]]). The Rees algebra is the device that translates the analytic word "stable" into the algebraic word "finitely generated", where the Noetherian property can act. Why grade it this way and not another? Because the degree-one slot must be $\mathfrak{a}$ itself — that is what makes $R^*$ generated in degree one by generators of $\mathfrak{a}$, which is what makes it Noetherian.

**The associated graded ring: collapse to the layers, because that is where the tangent geometry lives.** The second construction throws away more. Instead of the levels $\mathfrak{a}^n$, keep only the successive quotients $\mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ — the "$n$-th layer", what is new at depth $n$ — and assemble
$$\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}.$$
Why is this the right thing to collapse to? Because each layer is a module over $R/\mathfrak{a}$ (the action of $\mathfrak{a}$ on $\mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ is zero, since $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathfrak{a}^n = \mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ which is what we quotient by), so $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is a graded algebra over the much simpler ring $R/\mathfrak{a}$ — and when $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}$ is maximal, over a *field*. We have linearized: the associated graded ring of a complicated local ring is a graded algebra over its residue field, and the degree-one piece $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ is the cotangent space. The whole geometric payoff — that $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ is the coordinate ring of the tangent cone — comes from this collapse to layers over a field. The cost is that the multiplication is now subtle: you cannot just multiply representatives, because the product of two representatives is only well-defined *modulo the next layer*.

**Why the multiplication on $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is well-defined, and why this is the only delicate point.** Take $\bar{x} \in \mathfrak{a}^m/\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$ and $\bar{y} \in \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$, represented by $x \in \mathfrak{a}^m$, $y \in \mathfrak{a}^n$. We *want* $\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y}$ to be the image of $xy \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+n}$ in $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n}/\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$. The danger is dependence on the representatives. Change $x$ to $x + x'$ with $x' \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$; then $(x+x')y = xy + x'y$, and $x'y \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+1}\mathfrak{a}^n = \mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$ — which is exactly the subgroup we quotient by in the target. So the change vanishes in $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n}/\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$, and the product is well-defined. This computation is the *reason* the construction works, and it is forced by the choice of grading: the next-layer ambiguity $\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$ lands in the next-layer ambiguity $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$ of the target precisely because degrees add. The same one-line check makes $\operatorname{gr}(M) = \bigoplus M_n/M_{n+1}$ a graded $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$-module, using $\mathfrak{a} M_n \subseteq M_{n+1}$.

**Why $\operatorname{gr}$ preserves Noetherian-ness and finite generation, and what it is good for.** The two constructions are linked: $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = R^*/\mathfrak{a} R^*$ is a quotient of the Rees algebra (collapse each level mod the next), so when $R$ is Noetherian, $R^*$ is Noetherian, hence $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is Noetherian. And for a finitely generated $M$ with a stable $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration, $\operatorname{gr}(M)$ is finitely generated over $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$. The point of all this finiteness is that $\operatorname{gr}(M)$ is a *graded* module over a graded ring, so its [[Def - The Hilbert Function and Hilbert Polynomial|Hilbert function]] $n \mapsto \operatorname{length}(M_n/M_{n+1})$ is computable and eventually polynomial — and that Hilbert polynomial is the source of [[Def - Krull Dimension and Height|Krull dimension]]. The associated graded ring is the linearization that makes dimension theory work; the Rees algebra is the bookkeeping that proves the linearization is finite.

# The Definition

Let $R$ be a ring and $\mathfrak{a} \trianglelefteq R$ an [[Def - Ideal|ideal]].

## The associated graded ring

The **associated graded ring of $R$ with respect to $\mathfrak{a}$** is
$$\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1} \qquad (\mathfrak{a}^0 = R, \text{ so the degree-}0\text{ piece is } R/\mathfrak{a}),$$
a [[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module|graded ring]] with multiplication defined as follows. For $x \in \mathfrak{a}^m$ and $y \in \mathfrak{a}^n$ with images $\bar{x} \in \mathfrak{a}^m/\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$, $\bar{y} \in \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$, set $\bar{x} \cdot \bar{y} = \overline{xy}$, the image of $xy \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+n}$ in $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n}/\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$. (This is well-defined: replacing $x$ by $x + \mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$ changes $xy$ by an element of $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$.) The degree-$0$ piece $R/\mathfrak{a}$ is a subring, and $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is an $R/\mathfrak{a}$-algebra.

## The associated graded module

Given a [[Def - Filtration and Stable Filtration|filtration]] $(M_n)_{n \geq 0}$ of an $R$-module $M$ that is an $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration, the **associated graded module** is
$$\operatorname{gr}(M) = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} M_n/M_{n+1},$$
a graded $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$-module via: for $x \in \mathfrak{a}^m$ with image $\bar{x} \in \mathfrak{a}^m/\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$ and $u \in M_n$ with image $\bar{u} \in M_n/M_{n+1}$, set $\bar{x} \cdot \bar{u} = \overline{xu}$, the image of $xu \in M_{m+n}$ in $M_{m+n}/M_{m+n+1}$. (Well-defined because $\mathfrak{a}^{m+1} M_n \subseteq \mathfrak{a} M_{m+n} \subseteq M_{m+n+1}$ using the $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration property.) Write $\operatorname{gr}_n(M) = M_n/M_{n+1}$. For the $\mathfrak{a}$-adic filtration $M_n = \mathfrak{a}^n M$ one writes $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)$.

## The Rees algebra and Rees module

The **Rees algebra** of $R$ with respect to $\mathfrak{a}$ is
$$R^* = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} \mathfrak{a}^n = R \oplus \mathfrak{a} \oplus \mathfrak{a}^2 \oplus \cdots \qquad (\mathfrak{a}^0 = R),$$
a graded ring with multiplication: for $x \in \mathfrak{a}^m$ (in the degree-$m$ slot) and $y \in \mathfrak{a}^n$ (in the degree-$n$ slot), the product is $xy \in \mathfrak{a}^{m+n}$ (in the degree-$(m+n)$ slot). Given an $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration $(M_n)$ of $M$, the **Rees module** is
$$M^* = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} M_n,$$
a graded $R^*$-module via $\mathfrak{a}^m \cdot M_n \subseteq M_{m+n}$ (well-defined because $(M_n)$ is an $\mathfrak{a}$-filtration). When $\mathfrak{a} = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$, the Rees algebra is generated as an $R$-algebra by $x_1, \dots, x_r$ placed in degree one, so $R^*$ is a quotient of $R[t_1, \dots, t_r]$ ($t_i \mapsto x_i$). The associated graded ring is the quotient $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = R^*/\mathfrak{a} R^* = R^*/ \big(\bigoplus_{n \geq 1} \mathfrak{a}^{n+1}\big)$.

# Relate to Other Fields / Compression

The cleanest compression: **the Rees algebra stacks the levels of a filtration into a graded ring; the associated graded ring keeps only the differences between successive levels.** One remembers $\mathfrak{a}^n$; the other remembers $\mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$. The Rees algebra is for proving finiteness; the associated graded is for extracting geometry.

**True name:** the operational name of $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is *"the linearization of $R$ along $\mathfrak{a}$"* — it is the simplest graded ring that records, layer by layer, how $R$ sits relative to $\mathfrak{a}$, and over $R/\mathfrak{a}$ (a field, if $\mathfrak{a}$ is maximal) it is computable. The operational name of $R^*$ is *"the algebra whose finitely-generated modules are exactly the stable filtrations"*. When a problem is about stability or finiteness, reach for $R^*$; when it is about dimension, Hilbert functions, or tangent geometry, reach for $\operatorname{gr}$.

These are the two fundamental blow-up algebras of geometry. The **Rees algebra** $R^* = \bigoplus \mathfrak{a}^n$ is the coordinate algebra of the **blowup** of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ along the subscheme $V(\mathfrak{a})$: the blowup is $\operatorname{Proj} R^*$, the universal way to make $\mathfrak{a}$ into a principal (locally invertible) ideal, replacing the subvariety $V(\mathfrak{a})$ by its projectivized normal directions. The **associated graded ring** $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is the coordinate algebra of the **normal cone** of $V(\mathfrak{a})$ in $\operatorname{Spec} R$ — and when $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of a point, it is the **tangent cone** at that point, with $\operatorname{Proj} \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ the exceptional divisor of the blowup of the point. The polynomial identity $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = R^*/\mathfrak{a} R^*$ is the algebraic shadow of "the exceptional divisor is the fibre of the blowup over the centre".

# Examples / Corollaries

**Is an instance — the associated graded of a polynomial ring at the origin.** Let $R = k[x_1, \dots, x_d]$ and $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_d)$. Then $\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ is the space of degree-$n$ homogeneous polynomials, and $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R) \cong k[x_1, \dots, x_d]$ again — the associated graded ring of a polynomial ring at the origin is the *same* polynomial ring, now read as a graded ring. Geometrically: the tangent cone of affine space at the origin is affine space itself, as it should be — affine space is its own tangent cone everywhere. This computation is worked in [[Ex - The associated graded ring of a polynomial ring]].

**Is an instance — the Rees algebra of a polynomial ring.** With $R = k[x]$ and $\mathfrak{a} = (x)$, the Rees algebra is $R^* = k[x] \oplus (x) \oplus (x^2) \oplus \cdots$. Naming a degree-one variable $t$ for the copy of $x$ in slot $1$, one finds $R^* \cong k[x, t]$ with $t$ tracking the slot — concretely the subring $k[x, xt']$ of $k[x, t']$, isomorphic to a polynomial ring in two variables. Its Proj is the blowup of the affine line at the origin, which (the line being smooth) is the line itself. The general statement that $R^*$ is Noetherian when $R$ is, is [[Ex - The Rees algebra is Noetherian]].

**Is an instance — the tangent cone of a node.** Let $R = k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^2(x+1))$, the nodal cubic, localized at the origin $\mathfrak{m} = (x,y)$. The lowest-degree part of $y^2 - x^2(x+1) = y^2 - x^2 - x^3$ is $y^2 - x^2 = (y-x)(y+x)$, so $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R) \cong k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^2) = k[x,y]/((y-x)(y+x))$. The tangent cone is the *union of two lines* $y = x$ and $y = -x$ — exactly the two branches of the node crossing at the origin. The associated graded ring sees the singularity that the smooth case hid. This is the example that shows $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ is genuinely the tangent cone, not the tangent space.

**Is NOT an instance — confusing $\operatorname{gr}$ with the quotient $R/\mathfrak{a}$.** It is tempting to think $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ "is" $R/\mathfrak{a}$ because the degree-zero piece is $R/\mathfrak{a}$. It is not: $R/\mathfrak{a}$ is only the bottom layer. For $R = k[x]$, $\mathfrak{a} = (x)$: $R/\mathfrak{a} = k$ is a field, while $\operatorname{gr}_{(x)}(R) = k[x]$ is infinite-dimensional. The associated graded ring remembers *all* the layers $\mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$, not just the first; collapsing it to its bottom piece throws away everything the construction was built to keep.

**Corollary — $\operatorname{gr}$ of a DVR is a polynomial ring in one variable.** If $(R, \mathfrak{m} = (\pi))$ is a discrete valuation ring, then $\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1} = (\pi^n)/(\pi^{n+1})$ is one-dimensional over the residue field $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$ for every $n$, generated by $\bar{\pi}^n$, with no relations. So $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R) \cong k[t]$, the polynomial ring in one variable — the tangent cone of a smooth curve at a point is a line. This is the calibration check that $\operatorname{gr}$ detects smoothness: a regular local ring of dimension $d$ has $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R) \cong k[t_1, \dots, t_d]$.

**Calibration check.** Verify the multiplication on $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ is well-defined by checking that changing a representative by $\mathfrak{a}^{m+1}$ changes the product by $\mathfrak{a}^{m+n+1}$. Confirm $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[x_1,\dots,x_d]) \cong k[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ for $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_d)$. Show $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = R^*/\mathfrak{a}R^*$, and that the degree-one piece $\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}^2$ generates $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$ as an $R/\mathfrak{a}$-algebra when $\mathfrak{a}$ is finitely generated. Finally, compute $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ for the cuspidal cubic $R = k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^3)$ at the origin and observe the tangent cone is the doubled line $y^2 = 0$.

# Unlocked by This

> [!tip] The blowup of a variety along a subscheme *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> The Rees algebra $R^* = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{a}^n$ is the algebra whose **Proj** is the **blowup** $\operatorname{Bl}_{V(\mathfrak{a})} \operatorname{Spec} R$ of the variety $\operatorname{Spec} R$ along the subscheme cut out by $\mathfrak{a}$. The blowup is the universal modification that turns $\mathfrak{a}$ into a locally principal (invertible) ideal sheaf: it replaces the locus $V(\mathfrak{a})$ by the projectivization of its normal directions, leaving the rest of the variety untouched. Blowing up a *point* on a surface replaces it by a $\mathbb{P}^1$ of tangent directions — the exceptional curve. Blowups are the basic surgery of birational geometry and the engine of Hironaka's resolution of singularities: every singularity can, in characteristic zero, be made smooth by a finite sequence of blowups along smooth centres. That this Proj is well-behaved rests entirely on $R^*$ being a finitely generated graded $R$-algebra — the Noetherian fact proved in this chapter.

> [!tip] The tangent cone and the normal cone *(from Algebraic Geometry)*
> The associated graded ring $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = \bigoplus_n \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ is the coordinate ring of the **normal cone** $C_{V(\mathfrak{a})} \operatorname{Spec} R$, and when $\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of a point, the **tangent cone** $C_p X$ — the cone of limiting secant directions of $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$ at $p$. Its Proj, $\operatorname{Proj} \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$, is the **exceptional divisor** of the blowup of $X$ at $p$, the $\mathbb{P}^{?}$ of directions glued in to replace the point. The dimension of the tangent cone equals the dimension of $X$ at $p$, and its multiplicity (the leading coefficient of its Hilbert polynomial) is the **multiplicity** of $X$ at $p$ — the number of sheets passing through. The normal cone is also the geometric basis of intersection theory: the Fulton–MacPherson intersection product is defined by deforming to the normal cone, making $\operatorname{gr}$ the foundation of the Chow ring.

> [!tip] Deformation to the normal cone *(from Intersection Theory)*
> The Rees algebra and associated graded ring fit into one family: the **extended Rees algebra** $\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathfrak{a}^n t^{-n} \subseteq R[t, t^{-1}]$ (with $\mathfrak{a}^n = R$ for $n \leq 0$) gives a flat family over the affine line whose general fibre is $\operatorname{Spec} R$ and whose special fibre at $t = 0$ is the normal cone $\operatorname{Spec} \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)$. This **deformation to the normal cone** is how Fulton and MacPherson make intersection products rigorous: to intersect two cycles, deform one into the normal cone of the other, where the intersection becomes linear-algebraic. The single algebraic input is that $\mathfrak{a}^n$ filters $R$ multiplicatively — which is the content of this page.
