## §12.1 Graded Rings and Filtrations — Exercises

The exercises of §12.1 drill the two constructions that turn a filtration into a graded object: the **associated graded ring** $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = \bigoplus \mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ (collapse to layers) and the **Rees algebra** $R^* = \bigoplus \mathfrak{a}^n$ (stack the levels). Across them you practise the central computational reflexes: identify each graded piece $\mathfrak{a}^n/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}$ as a vector space and pin down the $\operatorname{gr}$-multiplication via leading forms; count a graded piece combinatorially and package the counts as a rational Poincaré series; and certify a graded ring Noetherian by exhibiting it as finitely generated in degree one over a Noetherian base. The unifying geometric thread is that $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ is the **tangent cone** at a point and detects singularity, while degree-one generation is the existence of a projective embedding.

- [[Ex - The associated graded ring of a polynomial ring]] (⭐⭐) — compute $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[x_1,\dots,x_d]) \cong k[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ by exploiting the pre-existing total-degree grading (so the $\mathfrak{m}$-adic filtration splits and each layer lifts canonically to the degree-$n$ forms), then contrast with the cuspidal cubic to exhibit a nonreduced "doubled-line" tangent cone via the *initial form* of the relation — the reusable principle being "$\operatorname{gr}$ is computed from lowest-degree forms, and detects singularity" ([[Def - The Associated Graded Ring and the Rees Algebra]], [[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal]], [[Def - Ideal]]).

- [[Ex - A nonstandard grading and its Hilbert function]] (⭐⭐) — for the weighted grading $\deg'(T_1^{e_1}T_2^{e_2}) = e_1 + 2e_2$ on $k[T_1,T_2]$, verify the grading axioms, count $\dim_k A_n = \lfloor n/2\rfloor + 1$ and show it is *quasi-polynomial* (no Hilbert polynomial exists), trace the failure to the violated "generated in degree one" hypothesis of Hilbert–Serre, and compute the Poincaré series $\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-t^2)}$ by factoring over the weighted generators — drilling the diagnostic habit of locating the exact hypothesis a theorem needs (Example Sheet 4 Q10) ([[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module]], [[Def - Polynomial Ring]], [[Def - The Hilbert Function and Hilbert Polynomial]]).

- [[Ex - The Rees algebra is Noetherian]] (⭐⭐) — prove $R^* = \bigoplus \mathfrak{a}^n$ is Noetherian by presenting it as a homogeneous quotient of $R[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ (generators of $\mathfrak{a}$ in degree one) and invoking Hilbert's basis theorem, then deduce $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R) = R^*/\mathfrak{a}R^*$ is Noetherian — the load-bearing finiteness fact under the entire Artin–Rees machine, drilling "Noetherian graded ring = finitely generated in degree one over a Noetherian base" ([[Def - The Associated Graded Ring and the Rees Algebra]], [[Def - Graded Ring and Graded Module]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Thm - Hilbert's Basis Theorem]], [[Thm - A Graded Ring is Noetherian iff Finitely Generated in Degree One]], [[Def - Ideal]]).

- [[Ex - Stable filtrations of a finitely generated module]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove the master dictionary "$(M_n)$ stable $\iff$ Rees module $M^* = \bigoplus M_n$ finitely generated over $R^*$" by inventing the freeze-then-continue-adically ascending chain $M_n^*$, then harvest Artin–Rees and the equivalence of all stable filtrations as corollaries — the deepest technique of the chapter, a change of category from filtered $R$-modules to graded $R^*$-modules where the Noetherian property does the work ([[Def - Filtration and Stable Filtration]], [[Def - The Associated Graded Ring and the Rees Algebra]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Finitely Generated Module]], [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma]], [[Thm - A Graded Ring is Noetherian iff Finitely Generated in Degree One]]).
