## §12.2 Artin-Rees and Krull Intersection — Exercises

The exercises of §12.2 drill the chapter's central engine and its two payoffs. The engine is the [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma|Artin–Rees Lemma]]: over a Noetherian ring, a stable filtration cuts a stable filtration on every submodule, proved by packaging filtrations into Rees modules where "stable" becomes "finitely generated". Its first payoff is *separatedness* — the [[Thm - The Krull Intersection Theorem|Krull intersection theorem]] $\bigcap_n \mathfrak{m}^n M = 0$, obtained by the two-stroke "Artin–Rees gives $N = \mathfrak{m}N$, Nakayama gives $N = 0$". Its second is *topological good behaviour* — the subspace topology on a submodule equals its intrinsic $\mathfrak{a}$-adic topology, obtained by feeding Artin–Rees into the comparison theorem. Across the section you practise the master reflex: replace an infinite filtration condition by a single finiteness or fixed-point equation, then let Noetherian-ness or Nakayama close it.

- [[Ex - The Artin-Rees lemma and the subspace topology]] (⭐⭐) — prove the $\mathfrak{a}$-adic subspace topology on a submodule $N \subseteq M$ equals its intrinsic $\mathfrak{a}$-adic topology, by recognising the subspace topology as the induced filtration $(N \cap \mathfrak{a}^n M)$, using Artin–Rees to make it stable, and the comparison theorem to identify its topology with $(\mathfrak{a}^n N)$ — drilling "topologies coincide $\iff$ filtrations equivalent, never equal" and the warning that naive equality $N \cap \mathfrak{a}^n M = \mathfrak{a}^n N$ is false (Example Sheet 4 Q14) ([[Def - Filtration and Stable Filtration]], [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma]], [[Thm - Stable Filtrations Induce the Same Topology]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Finitely Generated Module]], [[Def - Submodule]]).

- [[Ex - Krull intersection for a Noetherian local ring]] (⭐⭐) — prove $\bigcap_n \mathfrak{m}^n M = 0$ in a Noetherian local ring via the Artin–Rees / Nakayama two-stroke (collapse $N \cap \mathfrak{m}^n M = N$ to $N = \mathfrak{m}N$, then Nakayama), deduce the completion map $M \to \hat{M}$ is injective, and construct an idempotent-ideal counterexample showing the local hypothesis is essential (its job is to make $1 + a$ a unit) — drilling "show a deep submodule is zero" and hypothesis-necessity reasoning ([[Thm - The Krull Intersection Theorem]], [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma]], [[Thm - Nakayama's Lemma]], [[Def - The Jacobson Radical]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Prime and Maximal Ideal]], [[Def - Finitely Generated Module]]).

- [[Ex - Stable filtrations of a finitely generated module]] (⭐⭐⭐) — prove the stability criterion "$(M_n)$ stable $\iff$ Rees module finitely generated" via the freeze-then-continue-adically ascending chain, then derive Artin–Rees and the equivalence of all stable filtrations as corollaries — the structural heart of the section, showing that the engine and one of its consequences are a single dictionary viewed twice (also indexed under §12.1; central to both sections) ([[Def - Filtration and Stable Filtration]], [[Def - The Associated Graded Ring and the Rees Algebra]], [[Thm - The Artin-Rees Lemma]], [[Thm - A Graded Ring is Noetherian iff Finitely Generated in Degree One]], [[Def - Noetherian Ring]], [[Def - Finitely Generated Module]]).
