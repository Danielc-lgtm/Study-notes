type: theorem (two parts); source items: A-D6.2.1 (→ inside, Notation: proper map), A-I6.2.4, (the local-properness half of A-T6.1.3's Step 1, placed here because §10.2's `Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem` lists it as a prerequisite); prereqs: [Def - Banach Manifold and Smooth Maps between Banach Spaces, Thm - Kuranishi Model for a Fredholm Map, Thm - Compactness in Metric Spaces (Three Equivalents), Def - Fredholm Map and Its Index]; statement: (i) A continuous map $F:X\to Y$ between topological spaces with $Y$ locally compact and Hausdorff and $X$ Hausdorff, which is *proper* (preimages of compact sets are compact), is closed (maps closed sets to closed sets); the same holds when $Y$ is any metrisable space. (ii) A smooth Fredholm map $F:X\to Y$ between Banach manifolds is *locally proper*: every $p\in X$ has an open neighbourhood $U_p$ with compact closure in a chart such that $F|_{\overline{U_p}}$ is proper; proof: (i) let $C\subset X$ be closed and $y\in\overline{F(C)}$; take a compact neighbourhood $K$ of $y$ (or, in the metrisable case, a sequence $y_n = F(c_n)\to y$ and $K = \{y_n\}\cup\{y\}$, compact); $F^{-1}(K)$ is compact, $C\cap F^{-1}(K)$ is compact, its image is compact hence closed and contains points of $F(C)$ arbitrarily close to $y$, so $y\in F(C)$ (the writer gives the sequential argument in the metrisable case and the neighbourhood argument in the locally compact case); (ii) in a Kuranishi chart $F\phi(x_0,x_1) = Tx_1 + f(x_0,x_1)$ ($T:X_1\to Y_0$ an isomorphism, $\dim X_0<\infty$, $\dim Y_1<\infty$); shrink to a closed ball $\overline{U_p}$ on which the model holds; the preimage of a compact $K$ is closed in $\overline{U_p}$ and its $x_1$-component lies in the compact $T^{-1}\pi_{Y_0}(K)$, its $x_0$-component in a closed bounded subset of the finite-dimensional $X_0$, hence compact; spec: **used by `Thm - Sard-Smale Theorem` (§10.2), which lists this page as a prerequisite; logically it precedes that use, and the orchestrator should sequence this page ahead of Sard–Smale**; mechanism: **properness turns the topology of the target into control over the source, and a Fredholm map is finite-dimensional in the kernel–cokernel directions after the Kuranishi split**.
