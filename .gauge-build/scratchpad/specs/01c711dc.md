(i) A singular simplex $\sigma : \Delta^k \to M$ is smooth if it extends to a smooth map on a neighbourhood of $\Delta^k$ in its affine hull (equivalently: smooth in the interior with all derivatives extending continuously, Bär's fn. 2); the smooth singular chains $C_k^\infty(M;\mathbb{Z}) \subset C_k(M;\mathbb{Z})$ form a subcomplex and the inclusion induces isomorphisms $H_k^\infty(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong H_k(M;\mathbb{Z})$ for all $k$; the same with any coefficients. (ii) Integration $I : \Omega^k(M) \to C^k_\infty(M;\mathbb{R})$, $I(\omega)(\sigma) := \int_{\Delta^k} \sigma^*\omega$, is a cochain map (Stokes), and the induced map $H^k_{dR}(M) \to H^k_\infty(M;\mathbb{R}) \cong H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$ is an isomorphism for every $k$. (iii) Consequently, for a compact $M$, the pairing $H^k_{dR}(M) \times H_k(M;\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathbb{R}$, $([\omega], [c]) \mapsto \int_c \omega$, identifies $H^k_{dR}(M) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_k(M;\mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{R})$, and $H^k_{dR}(M;\mathbb{Z}) := \{[\omega] \mid \int_c \omega \in \mathbb{Z} \ \forall [c] \in H_k(M;\mathbb{Z})\}$ corresponds to $\operatorname{Hom}(H_k(M;\mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^k(M;\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion}$ (via the universal coefficient theorem); proof: source: none ("one can show"); the page follows Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds* (2nd ed.), Thm 18.7 (smooth singular homology equals singular homology: the smoothing operator via Whitney approximation on simplices — the page proves the needed relative Whitney approximation for maps from $\Delta^k$ inductively over faces, which is a special case of `[NEEDED FROM chapter XIII §13.1: Thm - Continuous Maps into a Manifold are Homotopic to Smooth Maps, relative version]` — the orchestrator should place that theorem in chapter XII §12.7 or earlier, OR the writer proves on this page the simplex version directly following Lee Thm 18.7's proof) and Thm 18.14 (the de Rham theorem: verify the isomorphism for open balls (Poincaré lemma on both sides), for disjoint unions, and inductively for a finite union of opens via the two Mayer–Vietoris sequences and the five lemma, then for arbitrary manifolds via a "good cover"-free induction on the sets in a countable cover using Lee's Lemma 18.13 ("de Rham cover" induction); Bredon V.9 as alternative).
