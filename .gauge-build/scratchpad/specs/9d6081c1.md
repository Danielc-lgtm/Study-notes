statement: For $n\ge2$: (i) $Spin(n)$ is a compact connected Lie subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}^0(\mathbb R^n)^\times$; (ii) $\xi:Spin(n)\to SO(n)$ is a surjective Lie group homomorphism with kernel $\{\pm1\}$, and $\{\pm1\}$ is central; (iii) $\xi$ is a two-sheeted covering map and a local diffeomorphism, so $d\xi:\mathfrak{spin}(n)\to\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is a Lie algebra isomorphism, with inverse $A\mapsto\tfrac14\sum_{i,j}\langle Ae_i,e_j\rangle e_ie_j$; (iv) the curve $t\mapsto\cos t + \sin t\,e_1e_2$, $t\in[0,\pi]$, joins $1$ to $-1$ in $Spin(n)$, so $\xi$ is a non-trivial double cover ($Spin(n)$ is not $SO(n)\times\{\pm1\}$); proof: Lawson–Michelsohn, *Spin Geometry*, Thm I.2.9, Prop I.2.10 (kernel) and the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem (Thm I.2.7: every element of $O(n)$ is a product of at most $n$ reflections — prove by induction on $n$ as a lemma on the page; mechanism: **conjugation by a unit vector is minus a reflection, so even products of unit vectors act by rotations, and the only scalars among them are $\pm1$**.
