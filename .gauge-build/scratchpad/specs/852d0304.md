type: theorem; source items: I5.1.3 (Step 3, "due to work of R. Thom"), R5.1.1 (Step 3); prereqs: [Def - Transverse Immersion with Double Points, Thm - Continuous Maps into a Manifold are Homotopic to Smooth Maps, Thm - Whitney Embedding Theorem, Thm - Tubular Neighbourhood Theorem, Thm - Parametric Transversality, Def - Regular Value and Transversality for Fredholm Maps, Thm - Regular Value Theorem on Manifolds]; statement: Let $S$ be a closed surface and $X$ a smooth four-manifold. Every smooth $f : S \to X$ is homotopic (through smooth maps, by an arbitrarily $C^0$-small homotopy) to an immersion with finitely many transverse double points; proof: source: attributed to Thom, no proof; the page proves it with a finite-dimensional family of perturbations: embed $X \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ with tubular retraction $r : U \to X$ and $S \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ embedded by Whitney; for $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^m, \mathbb{R}^N) =: W$ set $f_A(s) := r(f(s) + A s)$ (defined for $\lVert A \rVert$ small); (Lemma 1, rank stratum) the set $\Sigma \subset \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^2, \mathbb{R}^4)$ of linear maps of rank $\le 1$ is a closed subset which is a finite union of submanifolds of codimension $\ge 3$ (rank $r$ matrices in $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^2, \mathbb{R}^4)$ form a submanifold of codimension $(2 - r)(4 - r)$: Lee Example 5.30 / Guillemin–Pollack Ch. 1, proved on the page by the standard block-matrix chart); (Lemma 2, immersions are generic) the map $\Psi(s, A) := d(f_A)_s$, regarded in the bundle $\operatorname{Hom}(TS, f_A^* TX)$ trivialised locally, is a submersion in the $A$-direction at every point (the derivative in $A$ hits every linear map $T_s S \to T_{f_A(s)} X$ because $dr$ is surjective onto $TX$ and $s \mapsto As$ realises every linear map on $T_sS \subset \mathbb{R}^m$), so by `Thm - Parametric Transversality` (finite-dimensional case) for almost every $A$ the map $s \mapsto d(f_A)_s$ is transverse to each stratum of $\Sigma$, and since $\dim S = 2 < 3 \le \operatorname{codim}$, transverse means disjoint: $f_A$ is an immersion; (Lemma 3, double points are transverse) apply parametric transversality to $G : (S \times S \setminus \Delta_S) \times W \to X \times X$, $G(s, s', A) := (f_A(s), f_A(s'))$, which is a submersion in the $A$-direction onto a neighbourhood of the diagonal $\Delta_X$ (two distinct points $s \ne s'$ of $S \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ can be moved independently by a linear map $A$ — proved: choose $A$ with $As = v$, $As' = v'$ for prescribed $v, v'$, possible since $s, s'$ are linearly independent or, if $s' = \lambda s$, after replacing the embedding $S \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ by $S \subset \mathbb{R}^{m+1}$, $s \mapsto (s, 1)$, so that no two points of $S$ are proportional), so for almost every $A$, $G_A \pitchfork \Delta_X$, which at a double point $(s, s')$ says exactly $df_A(T_sS) + df_A(T_{s'}S) = T_x X$; (Lemma 4, finiteness) the double-point set $\{(s,s') \mid s \ne s', f_A(s) = f_A(s')\} = G_A^{-1}(\Delta_X)$ is a $0$-dimensional submanifold of $S \times S \setminus \Delta_S$, and it is closed in $S \times S$ because an immersion is injective near the diagonal (local immersion theorem), hence compact, hence finite; (Lemma 5) $f_A \simeq f$ by the straight-line homotopy $t \mapsto f_{tA}$, smooth and $C^0$-small; the exceptional set of $A$ has measure zero (union of two measure-zero sets), so a good $A$ exists arbitrarily close to $0$.
