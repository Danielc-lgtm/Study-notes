## Spec (from the manifest)

type: definition (compound: covering as a fibre bundle with discrete fibre; lift of a map through a point; universal covering of a connected manifold); source items: D4.1.9, D4.1.10, D4.1.11, E4.1.7, T4.1.16, T4.1.18; prereqs: [Def - Fibre Bundle, Def - Principal G-Bundle, Def - Covering Space, Def - Lift of a Map, Def - Universal Cover, Def - Simply Connected Space]; spec: Bär's definition (a fibre bundle in the sense of `Def - Fibre Bundle` whose fibre is a discrete space) is the primary form; the page proves on the spot that this is equivalent to the AT II definition `Def - Covering Space` (evenly covered neighbourhoods) — a short two-direction argument: local triviality with discrete fibre $F$ gives $p^{-1}(U) \cong U \times F = \bigsqcup_{f \in F} U \times \{f\}$, and conversely an evenly covered neighbourhood gives a local trivialisation with the discrete fibre $p^{-1}(y)$ (the fibres are all homeomorphic over a connected base — prove or restrict to connected bases as Bär does). Lift through $\tilde y$ as in D4.1.11. Universal covering of a connected manifold $X$: a covering $\bar X \to X$ with $\bar X$ simply connected; the page states existence and uniqueness up to isomorphism as forward links to `Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Universal Covering` and links `Def - Universal Cover` (AT II) noting the definitions agree. Examples verified: T4.1.16 ($\exp : \mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, is a covering and a principal $\mathbb{Z}$-bundle with action $(t,k) \mapsto t + k$: exhibit the local trivialisations over the two arcs $S^1 \setminus \{1\}$ and $S^1 \setminus \{-1\}$ with explicit branch formulas and check the cocycle condition and the freeness/transitivity of the $\mathbb{Z}$-action on fibres, invoking `Def - Principal G-Bundle`); $\mathbb{R}^n \to T^n$ is a principal $\mathbb{Z}^n$-bundle (product of the previous); T4.1.18 ($z \mapsto z^k$ is a $k$-fold covering and a principal $\mathbb{Z}/k$-bundle: trivialisations over the same two arcs with the $k$ branches of the $k$-th root, verified). Non-example: $[0,1) \to S^1$, $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, is a continuous bijection which is not a covering (no evenly covered neighbourhood of $1$; verified). Calibration check: identify the fibre of $z \mapsto z^k$ over $1$, write the transition function of $\exp$ between the two arcs, explain why the universal covering of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{R}$ once `Thm - Higher Homotopy Groups of the Circle Vanish` and simple connectivity of $\mathbb{R}$ are known.
