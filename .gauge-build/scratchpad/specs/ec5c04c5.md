## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: (foundation for A-T5.1.1 (ii), A-T5.2.5, A-T7.1.20); prereqs: [Def - Compact Operator, Def - Cauchy Sequence and Complete Metric Space]; statement: (i) Compact operators form a closed subspace of the bounded operators $X\to Y$ (operator norm); (ii) if $K$ is compact and $A,B$ bounded then $BKA$ is compact (ideal property); (iii) the sum of two compact operators is compact; (iv) in a Hilbert space, an operator is compact if and only if it is an operator-norm limit of finite-rank operators; (v) the Hilbert adjoint of a compact operator is compact; proof: (i) $\epsilon/3$-argument with total boundedness; (ii)–(iii) direct; (iv) "$\Leftarrow$" from (i); "$\Rightarrow$": for compact $K$ and $\epsilon>0$ pick a finite $\epsilon$-net $y_1,\dots,y_N$ of $K(B_1)$ and let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto their span; then $\|K - PK\|\le\epsilon$ (`Thm - Best Approximation by Orthogonal Projection`, Linear Algebra VI, valid in any inner product space for finite-dimensional subspaces); (v) from (iv) and $\|(PK)^*-K^*\| = \|PK-K\|$; spec: reference Brezis *Functional Analysis* §6.1; mechanism: compactness is stable under everything that does not enlarge the image of the ball.
