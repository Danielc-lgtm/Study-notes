**[anchor]** — type: theorem; source items: A-T3.1.3, A-R3.1.5 (integrality and degree), A-E2.2.4 ($\int_{S^2}F_a=2\pi i$, re-derived by Stokes here; the metric route is the exercise), B-T2.5.8, B-E2.5.2, A-T3.1.2(iv) (normalisation), A-I3.1.2 (link III), A-I3.1.3 (link III), the injectivity clause for simply connected bases (new; needed downstream); prereqs: [Def - Chern Classes, Def - First Chern Class via the Classifying Map, Thm - Classification of Principal U(1)-Bundles by the First Chern Class, Thm - The Standard Connection on the Hopf Bundle, Ex - Curvature of the Standard Hopf Connection, Thm - The de Rham Cohomology of Complex Projective Space, Thm - Winding Number of a Map from the Circle to U(1), Thm - Stokes' Theorem on Manifolds, Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group, Thm - The Top de Rham Cohomology of a Closed Connected Oriented Manifold is R]; statement: Let $L\to M$ be a complex line bundle over a compact manifold with Hermitian structure and unitary connection $A$ (curvature $F_A\in\Omega^2(M;i\mathbb R)$). (a) $c_1(L)=[\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F_A]$ equals the topological first Chern class $c_1^{top}(L)$ of chapter III; in particular for the tautological bundle $\mathcal O(-1)\to\mathbb{CP}^N$ with the connection induced by the Hopf connection, $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F=-\omega_N$ as forms, and $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F=-1$. (b) For every smooth $h\colon\Sigma\to M$ from a closed oriented surface, $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma h^*F_A\in\mathbb Z$, and equals $\deg(h^*L)$, the clutching degree of chapter III; in particular for $M=\Sigma$ itself $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma F_A=\deg L$, so $\deg\colon\operatorname{Pic}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb Z$ is $L\mapsto\int_\Sigma c_1(L)$ and $c_1$ is a complete invariant of line bundles over closed oriented surfaces. (c) $c_1(L)\ne0$ implies $L$ nontrivial; if $\int_\Sigma F_A\ne0$ then $L|_\Sigma$ is nontrivial. (d) If $M$ is connected and simply connected, then $c_1(L)=0$ implies $L$ trivial, so $c_1\colon\operatorname{Pic}(M)\to H^2_{dR}(M)$ is injective; proof: (a) naturality of both classes (VI theorem and III's page) reduces to $\mathcal O(-1)$; there IV's Hopf curvature exercise gives $\pi^*F=2i\sum dx_j\wedge dy_j$ and III's $\omega_N$ has $\pi^*\omega_N=\tfrac1\pi\sum dx_j\wedge dy_j$, so $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\pi^*F=-\pi^*\omega_N$ and injectivity of $\pi^*$ gives the form identity; the integral over $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is III's normalisation; (b) trivialise $L$ over $\Sigma\setminus D^\circ$ and $D$ as in III's classification proof, write $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F=\tfrac{i}{2\pi}dA_j$ on each piece, apply Stokes to both pieces, and identify $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\oint(A_2-A_1)=\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\oint g^{-1}dg=-w(g)$ or $+w(g)$ according to orientation — the writer fixes the sign so that (a) holds for $\mathcal O(-1)$ ($\deg=-1$); (c) from the trivial-bundle theorem and Stokes; (d) $c_1(L)=0$ means $\tfrac{i}{2\pi}F_A=d\eta$ for a real 1-form (by definition of exactness in $H^2_{dR}$), so $A':=A-2\pi i\eta$ (check: $F_{A'}=F_A-2\pi i\,d\eta=0$) is a flat unitary connection; its monodromy $\pi_1(M)\to U(1)$ is trivial since $\pi_1=1$, so by V's monodromy theorem $L$ with $A'$ is isomorphic to the trivial flat bundle, hence trivial; reference: Bott–Tu §6 (Ex.) for (b), Griffiths–Harris Ch. 1 §1 for (a), Donaldson–Kronheimer §2.1.4; spec: Convention callout: A's Remark 92 integrates over $\Sigma$, not $M$ (Appendix B item 5). Mechanism summary: "the curvature of a line bundle is $d$ of the potential on each trivialising piece, and Stokes turns the failure of the potentials to match into the winding number of the transition function."
