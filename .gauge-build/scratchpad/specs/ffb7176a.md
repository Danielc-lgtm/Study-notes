type: theorem (two parts); source items: A-D7.1.16 (Proposition 217), A-T7.1.22 (Proposition 217), A-T7.1.23 (Corollary 218); prereqs: [Def - The Perturbed Seiberg-Witten Equations and the Deformation Operator, Thm - The de Rham Complex is Elliptic and Harmonic Forms Represent de Rham Cohomology, Thm - Hodge Theorem for Elliptic Complexes, Def - Residual Set and Generic Property]; statement: Assume $b_2^+(M)\ge1$. (i) Reducible solutions $(0,A)$ of the perturbed equations satisfy $F_A^+ = \eta$; writing $A = A_0 + a$, $F_A^+ = F_{A_0}^+ + d^+a\in Q := F_{A_0}^+ + \operatorname{Im}d^+$, an affine subspace of $W^{4,2}(\Lambda^2_+T^*M\otimes i\mathbb R)$ of codimension $b_2^+$ (since $\Omega^2_+/\operatorname{Im}d^+\cong\mathcal H^2_+$, the harmonic self-dual forms, of dimension $b_2^+$, by the Hodge theorem for the Atiyah complex); hence if $\eta\notin Q$ there are no reducible solutions. (ii) For a generic (residual) $\eta$ — indeed for $\eta$ outside the proper closed affine subspace $Q$ and inside the transversality set $\mathcal H$ — the perturbed moduli space $\mathcal M_\eta$ contains no reducibles, so $\mathcal M_\eta = \mathcal M^{\mathrm{irr}}_\eta$; proof: (i) $F_A^+ = \mu(0) + \eta = \eta$; the codimension and closedness of $\operatorname{Im}d^+$ from the Hodge decomposition (`Thm - The de Rham Complex is Elliptic ...`, `Thm - Hodge Theorem for Elliptic Complexes`) — **Haydys leaves these asserted; supplied**; (ii) the complement of $Q$ is open and dense (proper affine subspace of positive codimension), intersect with the residual $\mathcal H$; spec: mechanism: **reducibles force the perturbation to lie on a codimension-$b_2^+$ wall, which a generic perturbation avoids when $b_2^+\ge1$**; targets: the invariant needs $b_2^+\ge2$ for wall-independence (§11.5).
