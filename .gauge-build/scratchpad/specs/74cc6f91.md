## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem (anchor); source items: A-T5.2.5 (Theorem 153), A-I5.2.5, A-I7.1.6 (the Fredholm property of $\slashed D^\pm_A$ and $d^++d^*$ as instances, Corollary 1); prereqs: [Thm - Elliptic Regularity and the Elliptic Estimate, Thm - Rellich Compactness Theorem, Thm - Differential Operators are Bounded between Sobolev Spaces, Thm - Existence and Ellipticity of the Formal Adjoint, Thm - Sobolev Spaces of Sections on a Compact Manifold via Charts, Def - Fredholm Operator and Index, Thm - Sobolev Embedding Theorem]; statement: Let $M$ be a closed manifold and $L:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(F)$ an elliptic differential operator of order $\ell\ge1$ (with fibre metrics and a Riemannian metric fixed). For every $k\in\mathbb Z$ the extension $L:H_{k+\ell}(M;E)\to H_k(M;F)$ is Fredholm. Moreover: (i) $\ker L\subset C^\infty(M;E)$ and is independent of $k$; (ii) $\operatorname{Im}L = \{f\in H_k(M;F) : \langle f,v\rangle_{L^2} = 0\ \forall v\in\ker L^*\}$ where $\ker L^*\subset C^\infty(M;F)$ is the (finite-dimensional) kernel of the formal adjoint; hence $\operatorname{coker}L\cong\ker L^*$ canonically; (iii) $\operatorname{index}L = \dim\ker L - \dim\ker L^*$ is independent of $k$; (iv) for $k\ge0$, $H_k(M;F) = \operatorname{Im}L\oplus\ker L^*$, $L^2$-orthogonally, and $\Gamma(F) = L(\Gamma(E))\oplus\ker L^*$; proof: Haydys's sketch made complete: (1) kernel smooth: $Lu = 0\in H_k$ for all $k$ ⇒ $u\in\bigcap H_k = C^\infty$ (regularity + embedding); (2) kernel finite-dimensional: the unit ball of $\ker L$ (in $H_{k+\ell}$) is bounded in $H_{k+\ell+1}$ by the elliptic estimate (with $Lu = 0$), hence relatively compact in $H_{k+\ell}$ by Rellich; a Hilbert space with compact unit ball is finite-dimensional (Riesz); (3) closed range: on $(\ker L)^\perp\subset H_{k+\ell}$ the estimate improves to $\|u\|_{k+\ell}\le C\|Lu\|_k$ (Lemma 1: otherwise $u_j\perp\ker L$ with $\|u_j\|_{k+\ell} = 1$, $Lu_j\to0$; by the estimate $\|u_j\|_{k+\ell}\le C(\|Lu_j\|_k + \|u_j\|_{k+\ell-1})$ and Rellich a subsequence converges in $H_{k+\ell-1}$, hence is Cauchy in $H_{k+\ell}$, converging to $u\perp\ker L$, $\|u\| = 1$, $Lu = 0$ — contradiction); a bounded-below operator has closed range (Lemma 2); (4) cokernel: by the duality (iv) of the charts page, $(H_k(F))^*\cong H_{-k}(F)$ via the $L^2$ pairing, and the annihilator of $\operatorname{Im}L$ is $\{v\in H_{-k} : \langle Lu,v\rangle = 0\ \forall u\} = \{v : L^*v = 0 \text{ weakly}\}$ (boundedness page); by regularity for the elliptic operator $L^*$, such $v$ are smooth, so the annihilator is $\ker L^*$ (independent of $k$), finite-dimensional by (2) applied to $L^*$; since $\operatorname{Im}L$ is closed, $\operatorname{coker}L\cong(\operatorname{Im}L)^{\text{ann}}{}^* \cong\ker L^*$ (Lemma 3: for a closed subspace $W$ of a Hilbert space $H$, $H/W\cong W^\perp$ and $W = (W^\perp)^\perp$; here $W^\perp$ is computed with the $L^2$ pairing into $H_{-k}$ — the writer keeps the two inner products apart); (ii)–(iv) follow, the smooth decomposition by regularity; spec: the gaps listed in the content map (closedness of range, regularity of the complement, complementation in $W^{k}$ rather than $L^2$) are exactly Lemmas 1–3; mechanism: **the elliptic estimate plus Rellich makes the kernel finite and the range closed; duality plus regularity for the adjoint makes the cokernel the smooth kernel of $L^*$**.
