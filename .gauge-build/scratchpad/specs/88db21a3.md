## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: A-I4.4.1; prereqs: [Def - Metric-Compatible Connection, Def - Connection Matrix and Local Form of a Connection, Def - Local Frame]; statement: Let $E$ be a Euclidean vector bundle with a metric connection $\nabla$ over $M$ and $m\in M$. There is a local orthonormal frame $(e_i)$ near $m$ with $(\nabla e_i)(m) = 0$ for all $i$. In particular on a Riemannian manifold there is an orthonormal frame of $TM$ near $m$ with $\nabla^{LC}e_i = 0$ at $m$, hence $[e_i,e_j](m) = 0$; proof: take any orthonormal frame $\tilde e$ near $m$ with connection matrix $\tilde A = \sum_k\tilde A_kdx^k$ in coordinates $x$ centred at $m$ ($\tilde A_k(x)$ skew-symmetric by metric compatibility); set $g(x) := \exp(-\sum_kx^k\tilde A_k(m))\in SO(r)$ and $e := \tilde e\,g$; then $A(e) = g^{-1}\tilde Ag + g^{-1}dg$ (anchor `Thm - Gauge Action on Connection Matrices` / the change-of-frame formula) equals $\tilde A(m) - \tilde A(m) = 0$ at $m$; torsion-freeness gives $[e_i,e_j] = \nabla_{e_i}e_j - \nabla_{e_j}e_i = 0$ at $m$; spec: technical lemma reused by Weitzenböck, Lichnerowicz, the connection Laplacian formula and chapter XI's Bochner identity; alternative (recorded, not used): parallel transport along radial geodesics in normal coordinates (`Def - Normal Coordinates and Geodesic Coordinates`).
