## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem (three parts); source items: A-X5.2.1 (Exercise 152: the small-perturbation statement is its content), A-I7.1.10, A-R7.1.11; prereqs: [Thm - Stabilisation of Fredholm Operators and Continuity of the Kernel, Thm - Riesz-Schauder Theory for Compact Perturbations of the Identity, Thm - Closed Range is Automatic for Finite-Dimensional Cokernel, Def - Compact Operator]; statement: Let $X,Y$ be Hilbert spaces. (i) $\operatorname{Fred}(X,Y)$ is open in the space of bounded operators, and $\operatorname{index}:\operatorname{Fred}(X,Y)\to\mathbb Z$ is locally constant; in particular for a continuous path $T_t$ of Fredholm operators, $\operatorname{index}T_0 = \operatorname{index}T_1$. (ii) If $T$ is Fredholm and $K$ is compact then $T+K$ is Fredholm and $\operatorname{index}(T+K) = \operatorname{index}T$. (iii) (Atkinson) $T$ is Fredholm if and only if there is a bounded $S:Y\to X$ with $ST - 1$ and $TS - 1$ compact; proof: (i) choose $V$ with $\operatorname{Im}T + V = Y$; for $T'$ near $T$, $T'_V$ is surjective with $\dim\ker T'_V = \dim\ker T_V$ (stabilisation (ii)); then $\ker T'\subset\ker T'_V$ is finite-dimensional, $\operatorname{coker}T'$ is a quotient of $V$ (since $\operatorname{Im}T' + V = Y$), so $T'$ is Fredholm (closed range automatic) with $\operatorname{index}T' = \dim\ker T'_V - \dim V = \operatorname{index}T$; (iii) "$\Rightarrow$": $S := $ (inverse of $T$ on $(\ker T)^\perp\to\operatorname{Im}T$) composed with the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{Im}T$; then $ST = 1 - P_{\ker T}$, $TS = 1 - P_{(\operatorname{Im}T)^\perp}$, finite rank; "$\Leftarrow$": $\ker T\subset\ker(ST) = \ker(1+K_1)$ finite-dimensional by Riesz–Schauder; $\operatorname{Im}T\supset\operatorname{Im}(TS) = \operatorname{Im}(1+K_2)$ closed of finite codimension, so $\operatorname{coker}T$ is finite-dimensional; (ii) $S(T+K) = 1 + (ST-1) + SK$ and $(T+K)S = 1 + (TS-1) + KS$ are compact perturbations of the identity, so $T+K$ is Fredholm by (iii); the path $T+tK$, $t\in[0,1]$, consists of Fredholm operators, so the index is constant by (i); spec: Haydys leaves (i) as Exercise 152 and uses (ii) silently in the dimension formula (R7.1.11: "a one-parameter family $T_t$ of Fredholm operators has $\operatorname{index}T_0 = \operatorname{index}T_1$"); mechanism: **the Fredholm property and the index are determined by finitely many dimensions that cannot jump under small or compact changes**; targets: the index computation for the Seiberg–Witten operator (XI), determinant line bundles (X).
