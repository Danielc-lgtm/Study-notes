## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem (anchor; four parts); source items: A-T5.3.1 (Theorem 156), A-T5.3.2 (the decomposition (158)), A-I5.3.1, A-T5.3.3 (gauge fixing, §5.3.1), A-R5.3.3; prereqs: [Def - Elliptic Complex and Its Laplacians, Thm - The Laplacians of an Elliptic Complex are Elliptic, Thm - Elliptic Operators on Closed Manifolds are Fredholm, Thm - Fredholm Alternative for Elliptic Operators, Thm - Existence and Ellipticity of the Formal Adjoint]; statement: Let $0\to\Gamma(E_1)\xrightarrow{L}\cdots\xrightarrow{L}\Gamma(E_k)\to0$ be an elliptic complex of first-order operators on a closed manifold (all bundles Euclidean). (i) Each $\mathcal H^j(E) = \ker\Delta_j$ is finite-dimensional and consists of smooth sections. (ii) $s\in\mathcal H^j(E)$ if and only if $Ls = 0$ and $L^*s = 0$. (iii) (Hodge decomposition) $\Gamma(E_j) = \mathcal H^j(E)\oplus L(\Gamma(E_{j-1}))\oplus L^*(\Gamma(E_{j+1}))$, the three summands being mutually $L^2$-orthogonal; and $\ker L = \mathcal H^j\oplus\operatorname{Im}L$. (iv) The natural map $\mathcal H^j(E)\to H^j(E)$, $s\mapsto[s]$, is an isomorphism; each cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative, and $L^{-1}(0)/\mathcal G\to\mathcal H^j(E)$, $s\mapsto h(s)$, is a linear isomorphism (gauge fixing); proof: (i) from the Fredholm page applied to the elliptic $\Delta_j$ (kernel smooth and finite-dimensional); (ii) $\langle\Delta s,s\rangle_{L^2} = \|Ls\|^2 + \|L^*s\|^2$ (formal adjoints; smooth $s$ on a closed manifold); (iii) $\Delta_j$ is formally self-adjoint, so by the Fredholm alternative $\Gamma(E_j) = \ker\Delta_j\oplus\Delta_j(\Gamma(E_j))$ orthogonally; $\Delta_jt = L(L^*t) + L^*(Lt)$, giving the three-term decomposition; orthogonality of $\operatorname{Im}L$ and $\operatorname{Im}L^*$: $\langle Lt,L^*r\rangle = \langle LLt,r\rangle = 0$ (this is the "moment's thought" of the source); orthogonality of $\mathcal H^j$ to both from (ii); $\ker L\perp\operatorname{Im}L^*$ likewise, so $\ker L = \mathcal H^j\oplus\operatorname{Im}L$; (iv) surjectivity: for $Ls_0 = 0$, decompose $s_0 = h + Lt$ by (iii), so $[s_0] = [h]$; injectivity: if $h = Lt$ is harmonic then $\|Lt\|^2 = \langle L^*Lt,t\rangle = \langle L^*h,t\rangle = 0$ — Haydys's argument with the "unique solution" step replaced by the decomposition (the content-map gap); the gauge-fixing map $h(s) := $ the harmonic component; spec: Haydys cites [Wel80] for (158) — Wells Thm IV.5.2; mechanism: **self-adjoint elliptic ⇒ finite-dimensional kernel and closed range orthogonal to it; $L^2 = 0$ splits the range of the Laplacian into the two images**; Bridges: `Thm - Hodge Decomposition Theorem` (Hodge I) is the de Rham case stated in the vault.
