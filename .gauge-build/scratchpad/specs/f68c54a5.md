## Spec (from the manifest)

**[anchor]** — type: definition; source items: A-D2.2.13, B-D2.3.1, B-R2.3.1, B-T2.3.1, B-D2.3.4, A-R2.2.6, A-D2.2.12 (link `Def - Adjoint Bundles ad P and Ad P`); prereqs: [Def - Principal G-Bundle, Def - Fundamental Vector Field of a Group Action, Thm - The Fundamental Vector Field Map is a Lie Algebra Homomorphism for Right Actions, Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket, Def - Equivariant and Basic Forms on a Principal Bundle]; spec: A connection form is $\omega\in\Omega^1(P;\mathfrak g)$ with (1) $R_g^*\omega=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega$ and (2) $\omega(\xi_P)=\xi$; the space $\mathcal A(P)$ ($=\mathcal C(P)$ of B) is not a vector space ($0$ fails (2)); corollary proved on the page (B-T2.3.1): (1) and (2) are compatible because $dR_g(\xi_P(p))=(\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\xi)_P(pg)$ (chapter I theorem, restated), and replacing $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}$ by $\operatorname{Ad}_g$ would be inconsistent (verified). Axiom Motivation from A-R2.2.6 (relate to vector-bundle connections) and B's figure (vertical directions are already coordinatised by $\mathfrak g$; a connection is a compatible choice of "horizontal"). Per-clause failure: drop (2) — $\omega=0$; drop (1) — a non-equivariant complement. Verified examples: the product connection $\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta$ on $M\times G$ (both clauses checked, using $R_h^*\theta=\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}\theta$); the Hopf connection (next theorem page); non-example: $\operatorname{pr}_2^*\theta+\pi^*\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak g)$ is a connection iff $\alpha$ is $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant pointwise (e.g. always for abelian $G$; for $SU(2)$ only $\alpha=0$) — verified. Convention callout: B misprints "$R_g^*=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\circ\omega$"; A writes $a$ for the connection form and $\operatorname{ad}$ for $\operatorname{Ad}$. Categorical: a $G$-equivariant splitting of $0\to VP\to TP\to\pi^*TM\to0$. Calibration: $\omega$ restricted to a fibre is the Maurer–Cartan form under $\ell_p$; the difference of two connections vanishes on vertical vectors.
