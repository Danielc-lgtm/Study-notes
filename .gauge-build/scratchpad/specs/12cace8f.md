## Spec (from the manifest)

type: definition (compound: symmetric bilinear form on a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank, unimodularity, rank, signature, definiteness, parity, isomorphism of forms, direct sum); source items: D5.1.4, D5.1.5, T5.1.9, T5.1.11, E5.1.4, R5.1.3, I5.1.10; prereqs: [Def - Bilinear Form, Def - Symmetric and Alternating Bilinear Form, Def - Quadratic Form, Thm - Diagonalization of a Symmetric Bilinear Form, Thm - Sylvester's Law of Inertia, Def - Determinant, Thm - Determinant is Multiplicative, Def - Free Module]; spec: $Q : H \times H \to \mathbb{Z}$ symmetric bilinear on $H \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$; *unimodular* iff $\det(Q(e_i, e_j)) = \pm 1$ for some basis (5.1); *rank* $\operatorname{rk} Q := r$; *signature* $\operatorname{sign}(Q) := $ (number of positive eigenvalues) $-$ (number of negative eigenvalues) of the real symmetric matrix, equivalently the difference of the dimensions of maximal positive and negative definite subspaces of $H \otimes \mathbb{R}$ (well defined by `Thm - Sylvester's Law of Inertia`, which carries I5.1.10 — diagonalisability over $\mathbb{R}$/$\mathbb{Q}$ via `Thm - Diagonalization of a Symmetric Bilinear Form`); $\operatorname{sign}(X) := \operatorname{sign}(Q_X)$; positive/negative definite, indefinite; *even* iff $Q(a,a) \in 2\mathbb{Z}$ for all $a$, else *odd*; isomorphism of forms = a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear bijection intertwining them; $Q_1 \oplus Q_2$. Corollaries proved on the page: T5.1.9 (unimodularity is basis independent: $(f_j) = (e_i) A$, $A \in GL_r(\mathbb{Z})$, $\det A = \pm 1$, $(Q(f_k, f_l)) = A^t (Q(e_i, e_j)) A$; also: unimodular iff $a \mapsto Q(a, \cdot)$ is an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{Hom}(H, \mathbb{Z})$, since an integer matrix is invertible over $\mathbb{Z}$ iff its determinant is $\pm 1$ — adjugate formula); T5.1.11 (even iff all diagonal entries of some, equivalently every, Gram matrix are even: $Q(a,a) = 2\sum_{i<j} Q_{ij} a_i a_j + \sum_i Q_{ii} a_i^2$); signature, rank, parity and definiteness are isomorphism invariants (proved); over $\mathbb{R}$ a form is determined by rank and signature (Sylvester). Examples verified (E5.1.4): $H = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ even, indefinite, signature $0$, unimodular; $(1)$ odd, positive definite, signature $1$; $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ odd, indefinite, signature $0$; R5.1.3: $H$ and $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ are isomorphic over $\mathbb{Q}$ (explicit change of basis $e_1 \pm e_2$ divided by... over $\mathbb{Q}$: $(e_1 + e_2)/1$ and $(e_1 - e_2)$ give $\operatorname{diag}(2,-2)$, then rescale over $\mathbb{Q}$) but not over $\mathbb{Z}$ (parity); non-example: $\operatorname{diag}(2)$ is not unimodular.
