**[anchor]** — type: definition (compound); source items: A-D3.1.2, A-R3.1.1, B-D2.5.2, B-T2.5.1, B-D2.5.3 (→ link `Def - de Rham Cohomology`, `Def - Betti Numbers`), B-D2.5.4 (the class $c_\lambda(P)$; its independence of $\omega$ is the theorem), B §2.5 conventions (the antisymmetrised formula and the basis formula); prereqs: [Def - Ad-Invariant Polynomial, Def - Curvature of a Principal Connection, Thm - Bundle-Valued Forms on the Base are Basic Equivariant Forms on the Total Space, Def - Lie-Algebra-Valued Differential Forms and Their Bracket, Def - Adjoint Bundles ad P and Ad P]; spec: for a principal $G$-bundle with connection $\omega$ and $p\in I(G)$ of degree $d$, the form $p(\Omega)\in\Omega^{2d}(P)$ (A: evaluate the polynomial on the matrix of 2-forms; B: $\lambda(\Omega,\dots,\Omega)$ with the antisymmetrisation normalisation $\tfrac1{d!}\sum_\sigma\operatorname{sign}\sigma\,\lambda(\Omega(X_{\sigma1},X_{\sigma2}),\dots)$ — the page proves the two agree and equal the basis formula $\sum\Omega^{j_1}\wedge\dots\wedge\Omega^{j_d}\lambda(Y_{j_1},\dots,Y_{j_d})$, using that even forms commute (Lemma)); $p(\Omega)$ is basic and invariant (entries of $\Omega$ basic; $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariance of $p$ and equivariance of $\Omega$), so it descends to $p(F_\omega)\in\Omega^{2d}(M)$ — B's $\lambda\circ\bar\Omega$, defined through the fibrewise descent of $\lambda$ to $\operatorname{ad}P$ (B-T2.5.1 proved); the vector-bundle version $p(F_\nabla)$ for a connection on $E$ via $\operatorname{Fr}(E)$ (identical by IV's correspondence); the class $c_p(P):=[p(F_\omega)]\in H^{2d}_{dR}(M)$ (well defined by the Chern–Weil theorem; Convention callout on the coefficient field $\mathbb R$ vs $\mathbb C$ and A's "for definiteness $\mathbb C$"). Verified examples: $p=\operatorname{tr}$ on a $U(1)$-bundle gives $F$ itself; $p=\det$ on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; non-example: $p(\xi)=\operatorname{tr}(\xi)^2$ vs $\operatorname{tr}(\xi^2)$ give different forms (computed on a rank-2 example). Calibration: $p(F)$ for a flat connection is $0$ in positive degree; $p(F)$ is gauge invariant (from V's curvature transformation).
