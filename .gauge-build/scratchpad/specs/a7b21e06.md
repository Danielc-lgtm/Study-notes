## Spec (from the manifest)

type: definition (anchor; compound: orientation of a Fredholm map via a trivialisation of the determinant bundle of $d F$; the sign of a regular point; the integer degree); source items: A-D6.5.1, A-D6.5.2 (equation (175)), A-R6.5.1, A-I6.5.2 (→ inside, Notation: the boundary orientation convention); prereqs: [Def - Determinant Line Bundle of a Family of Fredholm Operators, Thm - Homotopy Invariance of the Determinant Line Bundle, Def - Mod-2 Degree of a Proper Fredholm Map, Def - Orientation of a Vector Space, Thm - Regular Value and Transversality Theorems for Fredholm Maps]; spec: an *orientation* of a Fredholm map $F:X\to Y$ of index $d$ is a trivialisation of the determinant line bundle $\det dF\to X$ of the family $x\mapsto d_xF$ (i.e. a nowhere-zero continuous section, equivalently a continuous choice of orientation of $\det d_xF$); at a regular point $x$ ($\operatorname{coker}d_xF = 0$) $\det d_xF = \Lambda^{\mathrm{top}}\ker d_xF = \Lambda^{\mathrm{top}}T_x\mathcal M$ where $\mathcal M = F^{-1}(y)$, so an orientation of $F$ orients the $d$-manifold $\mathcal M$; for $d = 0$, $\det d_xF = \mathbb R$ canonically and the sign $\varepsilon(x)\in\{\pm1\}$ compares the trivialisation with the canonical orientation; the *integer degree* of an oriented proper Fredholm map of index $0$ is $\deg F := \sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y)}\varepsilon(x)\in\mathbb Z$ (equation (175); **source typo: Haydys p. 58 (175) prints $\sum_i\varepsilon_k$ for $\sum_i\varepsilon_i$**), reducing mod $2$ to $\deg_2 F$; well-definedness on `Thm - Well-Definedness and Homotopy Invariance of the Degree`; boundary orientation convention for the cobordism (A-I6.5.2) recorded here and used on the cobordism page; examples: a finite-dimensional oriented proper map (Brouwer degree, the vault's `Def - Brouwer Degree of a Map` as the finite-dimensional model); an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism ($\deg = -1$); non-example: a Fredholm map whose determinant bundle is non-trivial admits no orientation and no integer degree (only $\deg_2$).
