type: theorem; source items: A-X5.3.2 (Exercise 159 — its content), A-R6.6.2 (→ inside, Motivation: folding the deformation complex into $D_x = (R_x^*,d_xF_w)$); prereqs: [Def - Elliptic Complex and Its Laplacians, Thm - Existence and Ellipticity of the Formal Adjoint]; statement: The complex $0\to\Gamma(E_1)\xrightarrow{L_1}\Gamma(E_2)\xrightarrow{L_2}\Gamma(E_3)\to0$ (first-order operators, $L_2L_1 = 0$) is elliptic if and only if $(L_2,L_1^*):\Gamma(E_2)\to\Gamma(E_3\oplus E_1)$ is elliptic; proof: for $\xi\ne0$: the symbol of the folded operator is $v\mapsto(\sigma_2v,-\sigma_1^*v)$ (sign from the adjoint); it is injective iff $\ker\sigma_2\cap\ker\sigma_1^* = 0$ iff $\ker\sigma_2\cap(\operatorname{Im}\sigma_1)^\perp = 0$; exactness at $E_2$ ($\ker\sigma_2 = \operatorname{Im}\sigma_1$) gives this; the writer proves both directions: ($\Rightarrow$) exactness at $E_1$ (injective $\sigma_1$), at $E_2$, at $E_3$ (surjective $\sigma_2$) gives $\dim E_2 = \dim E_1 + \dim E_3$ and $\ker\sigma_2\cap\ker\sigma_1^* = 0$, so the folded symbol is an injective map between spaces of equal dimension, hence invertible; ($\Leftarrow$) if the folded symbol is invertible then $\sigma_1^*$ is surjective (so $\sigma_1$ injective), $\sigma_2$ is surjective, and $\ker\sigma_2\cap(\operatorname{Im}\sigma_1)^\perp = 0$ with the dimension count gives $\ker\sigma_2 = \operatorname{Im}\sigma_1$; spec: mechanism: **a three-term complex is elliptic exactly when its "gauge-fixed" operator is elliptic**; targets: the Seiberg–Witten operator $D_{(\psi,A)}$ (XI).
