## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: (implicit in every use of Betti numbers and signatures in Bär §5; not stated in the source); prereqs: [Thm - Mayer-Vietoris Sequence for Singular Homology (Gauge Theory), Thm - Homotopy Invariance of Singular Homology (Gauge Theory), Def - The Riemannian Exponential Map, Thm - The Gauss Lemma, Thm - Existence of Riemannian Metrics via Partitions of Unity]; statement: (i) If a space $X$ admits a finite open cover $\{U_1, \dots, U_m\}$ such that every non-empty finite intersection $U_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap U_{i_k}$ is contractible (a finite good cover), then $H_n(X;R)$ is a finitely generated $R$-module for every $n$ and vanishes for $n \ge m$; for $R = \mathbb{Z}$ it is a finitely generated abelian group, so Betti numbers $b_n := \operatorname{rank} H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})$ and the Euler characteristic $\chi(X) := \sum (-1)^n b_n$ are defined. (ii) Every compact smooth manifold, with or without boundary, admits a finite good cover; hence its homology is finitely generated in every degree and vanishes above the dimension; proof: source: none; (i) by induction on $m$ using MV and the fact that $\{U_1 \cap U_m, \dots, U_{m-1} \cap U_m\}$ is a good cover of $U_m \cap (U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_{m-1})$ (Bott–Tu Prop. 5.3.1 argument transposed to singular homology; the vanishing above degree $m - 1$ needs $H_n(\text{contractible}) = 0$ for $n \ge 1$); (ii) via geodesically convex balls: (Lemma A) for a Riemannian metric, every point has a neighbourhood $W$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ such that every geodesic ball $B(y, \varepsilon)$, $y \in W$, is geodesically convex — any two of its points are joined by a unique minimising geodesic lying in the ball (do Carmo, *Riemannian Geometry*, Ch. 3 Prop. 4.2 (Whitehead); Lee, *Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds*, Thm 6.17), proved on the page from the exponential map and the Gauss lemma (linked RG II pages) via the standard second-variation-free argument on the distance function's Hessian along geodesics inside a normal ball; (Lemma B) a finite intersection of geodesically convex balls is geodesically convex, and a non-empty geodesically convex open set is contractible (geodesic contraction to any of its points, with continuity from the smooth dependence of geodesics on endpoints inside a convex normal ball); (Lemma C) for a compact manifold with boundary, choose a metric that is a product $g_{\partial W} + dt^2$ on a collar $\partial W \times [0, \delta)$ (collar existence for smooth manifolds with boundary: flow of an inward-pointing vector field, proved on the page, Lee Thm 9.25) and note that the intersection of a small geodesic ball of the double $DW$ centred on $W$ with $W$ is star-shaped along geodesics from its centre, hence contractible, and intersections of such half-balls are again geodesically convex in $DW$ intersected with $W$. Reference: Bott–Tu Thm 5.1 and Prop. 5.3.1; do Carmo III.4.2; Lee (Riemannian) Thm 6.17. spec: Why-true: "finitely many contractible pieces glued along contractible pieces can only produce finitely much homology"; Sources: any compact smooth manifold; Targets: Betti numbers, Euler characteristic, signature (chapter XIII), Prop. 3.38-type duality statements.
