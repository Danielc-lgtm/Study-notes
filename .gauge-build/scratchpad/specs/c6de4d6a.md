## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem (two parts); source items: A-T7.1.10 (Corollary 198), A-T7.1.11 (Corollary 199, equation (200)), A-T7.1.12 (Remark 201), A-R7.1.7; prereqs: [Thm - The C0 Bound on the Spinor, Def - The Quadratic Map on Positive Spinors, Thm - First Chern Class of a Line Bundle from Curvature, Ex - Energy Identity for U(1) Connections on a Four-Manifold, Thm - Self-Dual Decomposition of 2-Forms in Four Dimensions]; statement: There are constants depending only on the metric such that for every solution $(\psi,A)$: (i) $\|\psi\|_{L^p}\le\kappa_p$ for every $p$ (from the $C^0$ bound and finite volume); (ii) $\|F_A^+\|_{L^2}\le C$ (since $F_A^+ = \mu(\psi)$ is quadratic in $\psi$, $\|F_A^+\|_{L^2}^2 = \tfrac14\|\,|\psi|^2\|_{L^2}^2\le C\|\psi\|_{L^4}^4$); and (iii) $\|F_A^-\|_{L^2}^2\le C - 4\pi^2 c_1(L_{\det})^2$, hence $\|F_A\|_{L^2}^2 = \|F_A^+\|_{L^2}^2 + \|F_A^-\|_{L^2}^2$ is bounded by a constant depending only on the metric and the spin$^c$ structure; **source typo (Haydys B18, p. 65, (200)): the printed second inequality bounds $\|F_A^-\|_{L^2}$ but the proof bounds the square $\|F_A^-\|_{L^2}^2$; the series states the squared bound**; proof: (i) trivial; (ii) from the $C^0$/$L^4$ bound; (iii) the energy identity for the abelian curvature: $c_1(L_{\det})^2 = \tfrac{1}{4\pi^2}(\|F_A^+\|_{L^2}^2 - \|F_A^-\|_{L^2}^2)$ with the signs fixed on `Ex - Energy Identity for U(1) Connections on a Four-Manifold` (VII), so $\|F_A^-\|_{L^2}^2 = \|F_A^+\|_{L^2}^2 - 4\pi^2 c_1(L_{\det})^2\le C - 4\pi^2 c_1(L_{\det})^2$; **the sign convention Haydys leaves ambiguous (B17, the $\int F^\pm\wedge F^\pm = \pm\|F^\pm\|^2$ question) is settled by `Ex - Energy Identity for U(1) Connections on a Four-Manifold`, which chapter XI cites**; (iii)/Remark 201 (T7.1.12): the right-hand side is a constant independent of the solution, used in the finiteness theorem (§11.5); spec: mechanism: **the curvature's self-dual part is controlled by the spinor's $C^0$ bound, and its anti-self-dual part by the topological energy identity**; targets: uniform Sobolev bounds and compactness.
