## Spec (from the manifest)

**[anchor]** — type: definition; source items: A-D2.1.10, A-R2.1.6, conventions ($F_\nabla(X,Y)s=\nabla_X\nabla_Ys-\nabla_Y\nabla_Xs-\nabla_{[X,Y]}s$); prereqs: [Def - Exterior Covariant Derivative on a Vector Bundle, Thm - Existence of the Curvature Form]; spec: $F_\nabla\in\Omega^2(M;\operatorname{End}E)$ with $d^\nabla\circ d^\nabla=F_\nabla\wedge\cdot$; the three equivalent descriptions proved on the page as corollaries (using the existence theorem): (i) $d^\nabla d^\nabla s=F_\nabla s$; (ii) $F_\nabla(\partial_i,\partial_j)=\nabla_i\nabla_j-\nabla_j\nabla_i$ (R2.1.6 written out); (iii) the commutator formula with $[X,Y]$ for arbitrary vector fields (from (ii) by tensoriality). Convention callout: RG I's curvature 2-forms $\Omega^a{}_b$ are the matrix entries of $F_\nabla$; RG III's Riemann tensor $R(X,Y)Z$ equals $F_{\nabla^{LC}}(X,Y)Z$ (link both; proved in §2.5). Verified examples: $F=0$ for the product connection; $F=dx\wedge dy\otimes\operatorname{id}$ for the example above; non-example: $\nabla_X\nabla_Y-\nabla_Y\nabla_X$ alone is not tensorial. True name: "the obstruction to $d^\nabla$ being a differential" / "the commutator of covariant derivatives". Calibration: $F_\nabla$ is antisymmetric; $F_{\nabla}(X,Y)$ is $C^\infty$-linear in $s$; on a line bundle $F$ is a scalar 2-form.
