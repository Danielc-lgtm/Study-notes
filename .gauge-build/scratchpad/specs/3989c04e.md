type: theorem; source items: (needed by A-D7.1.8 — the Banach Lie group structure of $\mathcal G^{k,p}$ — and by the slice theorem; no numbered item); prereqs: [Thm - Sobolev Multiplication Theorem, Thm - Sobolev Embedding Theorem]; statement: Let $M$ be compact, $2k>n$, and $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ entire (in particular $f = \exp$). Then $u\mapsto f\circ u$ is a well-defined smooth map $H_k(M;\mathbb C)\to H_k(M;\mathbb C)$ (smooth in the Fréchet sense between Banach spaces), with derivative $v\mapsto(f'\circ u)v$; in particular $\xi\mapsto e^{i\xi}$ is smooth $H_k(M;\mathbb R)\to H_k(M;\mathbb C)$ with image in $\{g : |g| = 1\}$; proof: the power series $\sum a_ju^j$ converges absolutely in the Banach algebra $H_k$ ($\|u^j\|_k\le C^{j-1}\|u\|_k^j$); a convergent power series in a Banach algebra is smooth with the termwise derivative (Lemma 1: differentiability of power series in Banach algebras — Dieudonné *Foundations* (9.3)); spec: also record: for $2k>n$ and $g\in H_k(M;S^1)$, $g^{-1} = \bar g\in H_k$ and $\mathcal G^{k} := H_k(M;S^1)$ is a group under pointwise multiplication (from (a)); mechanism: the Sobolev algebra is a Banach algebra, and analytic functional calculus works in any Banach algebra.
