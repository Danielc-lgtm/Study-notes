## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: I5.1.2 (Step 2, "standard mollifiers"), R5.1.1 (Step 2); prereqs: [Thm - Tubular Neighbourhood Theorem, Thm - Whitney Embedding Theorem, Def - Smooth Map between Manifolds, Def - Partition of Unity on a Manifold, Def - Homotopy and Homotopy Equivalence]; statement: Let $S$ be a compact smooth manifold (possibly with boundary) and $X$ a smooth manifold. (i) Every continuous $f : S \to X$ is homotopic to a smooth map; more precisely there is a homotopy $F : S \times I \to X$ with $F(\cdot, 0) = f$ and $F(\cdot, t)$ smooth for every $t > 0$ (Bär's formulation). (ii) (Relative form) If $f$ is already smooth on a neighbourhood of a closed subset $A \subset S$, the homotopy can be taken stationary on $A$. (iii) Two smooth maps that are continuously homotopic are smoothly homotopic; proof: source: "using standard mollifiers"; the page follows Lee Thm 6.26 (Whitney approximation theorem: embed $X \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ by `Thm - Whitney Embedding Theorem`, take a tubular neighbourhood $U$ of $X$ with retraction $r : U \to X$ (from the previous page: $r = \pi \circ \Phi^{-1}$), approximate $f$ uniformly by a smooth $g : S \to \mathbb{R}^N$ within the distance $\delta(x)$ that keeps the straight-line segment inside $U$ — the approximation by mollification in charts with a partition of unity, Lee Thm 6.21, proved on the page — and set $F(s,t) := r((1-t) f(s) + t g(s))$; for Bär's "smooth for $t > 0$" clause, replace $t$ by a smoothing family $g_t$ with $g_t \to f$ as $t \to 0$ (mollifier parameter $t$) and set $F(s,t) := r(g_t(s))$, verifying continuity at $t = 0$). Reference: Lee (2nd ed.)
