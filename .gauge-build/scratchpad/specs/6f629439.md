## Spec (from the manifest)

**[anchor]** — type: definition (compound); source items: A-D3.2.2 (primary definition), A-D3.2.1 (4-dimensional formula; stated as the theorem below), A-R3.2.1, A-R3.2.3, A-I3.2.1 (scope remark), A-I3.2.2 (not needed), A-I3.2.3 (→ the previous theorem), A §3.2 conventions; prereqs: [Thm - Classification of Principal SU(2)-Bundles over Four-Manifolds, Thm - Transgression Formula and the Chern-Simons Form, Thm - Gauge Variation of the Chern-Simons Functional]; spec: Let $M$ be a closed oriented 3-manifold and $P\to M$ a principal $SU(2)$-bundle — trivial by III(A). Choose a trivialisation, so connections are $A\in\Omega^1(M;\mathfrak{su}(2))$, and set $\vartheta(A):=\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}\big(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23A\wedge A\wedge A\big)\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$; well-definedness modulo $\mathbb Z$ under change of trivialisation is the corollary of the gauge-variation theorem (two trivialisations differ by a gauge transformation $g\colon M\to SU(2)$, and $\vartheta$ changes by $\deg g\in\mathbb Z$); $\vartheta$ is gauge invariant modulo $\mathbb Z$ and descends to $\mathcal A(P)/\mathcal G(P)\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$; A's 4-dimensional formula (95) is recorded as the characterisation proved on `Thm - The Four-Dimensional Formula for the Chern-Simons Functional`. Convention callout: A defines $\vartheta$ four-dimensionally and needs (I3.2.1) that every closed oriented 3-manifold bounds a compact oriented 4-manifold (Thom, Rokhlin; Lickorish–Wallace) — the series does not prove or use this fact; it is recorded as a scope remark in bold, and the 3-dimensional definition is primary; A's Appendix B item 6 (missing $\tfrac1{8\pi^2}$) noted. Verified examples: $\vartheta(0)=0$; $\vartheta$ of a pure-gauge connection $g^{-1}dg$ is $\deg g\equiv0$; for a connection with $A\wedge A\wedge A=0$ (e.g. abelian, $A$ valued in a line) $\vartheta=\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA)$; non-example: for $U(1)$-bundles over a 3-manifold the same formula is not well defined modulo $\mathbb Z$ without a further normalisation (record as a remark, not a claim). Calibration: $\vartheta$ is a cubic polynomial on the affine space of connections; its differential is computed in the critical-point theorem.
