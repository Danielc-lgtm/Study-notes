## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem (four parts); source items: (the analytic foundation; no numbered Haydys item — Haydys's Remark 138 (R5.1.4) is the one-dimensional instance and is recorded in the Motivation); prereqs: [Def - Fourier Series on the Torus and Sobolev Spaces of Integer Order, Thm - Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality]; statement: For $u\in C^\infty(T^n;\mathbb C)$: (i) $\widehat{\partial_ju}(\xi) = i\xi_j\hat u(\xi)$, hence $|\hat u(\xi)|\le C_N(1+|\xi|)^{-N}$ for every $N$ (rapid decrease); (ii) the Fourier series $\sum_\xi\hat u(\xi)e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}$ converges absolutely and uniformly together with all its derivatives, and its sum is $u$; (iii) (Parseval) $\int_{T^n}u\bar v\,dx = (2\pi)^n\sum_\xi\hat u(\xi)\overline{\hat v(\xi)}$, in particular $\|u\|_{L^2}^2 = (2\pi)^n\|u\|_0^2$; (iv) the map $u\mapsto(\hat u(\xi))_\xi$ is a linear bijection from $C^\infty(T^n)$ onto the rapidly decreasing sequences, with inverse the Fourier series; proof: (i) integration by parts on the torus (no boundary terms — periodicity); (ii) Weierstrass M-test for uniform convergence of the series and each derived series (from (i)); the sum $v$ is continuous with the same Fourier coefficients as $u$ (termwise integration of a uniformly convergent series — justified); Lemma 1 (uniqueness): a continuous function on $T^n$ with all Fourier coefficients zero is zero — proved with the Fejér kernel $K_N(x) := \prod_{j=1}^n F_N(x_j)$, $F_N(t) := \frac1{N+1}\big(\frac{\sin((N+1)t/2)}{\sin(t/2)}\big)^2$: $K_N\ge0$, $(2\pi)^{-n}\int K_N = 1$, $K_N\to0$ uniformly off any neighbourhood of $0$, so $K_N*w\to w$ uniformly for continuous $w$ (approximate identity, Lemma 2, written out), while $K_N*w$ is a trigonometric polynomial whose coefficients are multiples of $\hat w$; hence $w = 0$; (iii) from (ii) by termwise integration against $\bar v$; (iv) from (i)–(iii) and the M-test in the other direction; spec: reference Stein–Shakarchi *Fourier Analysis* Ch. 2–3 (one variable) and Folland *Real Analysis* §8.3; mechanism: **differentiation is multiplication by $i\xi$ on the Fourier side, so smoothness is decay of coefficients, and the Fejér kernel shows nothing is lost**; targets: every page of this chapter.
