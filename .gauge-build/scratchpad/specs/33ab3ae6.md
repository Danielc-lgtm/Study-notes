**[anchor]** — type: theorem; source items: A-T2.4.3, A-D2.4.6 ($Sp(1)$ part), A-D2.4.7 (replacement), A-R3.1.6 (the integer $k(P)$; its Chern–Weil identification is VI), A-I2.4.4 (replaced); prereqs: [Ex - SU(2) is the Group of Unit Quaternions, Thm - Generic Sections are Transverse to the Zero Section, Thm - Homogeneity Lemma for Connected Manifolds, Thm - Homotopic Maps Pull Back Isomorphic Principal Bundles, Thm - Clutching Construction for Bundles over a Closed Manifold, Thm - The Brouwer Degree is an Integer and a Homotopy Invariant, Thm - Sections of a Principal Bundle and Triviality, Thm - Vector Bundles are Associated to Their Frame Bundles]; statement: (A) Every principal $SU(2)$-bundle over a compact manifold of dimension $\le3$ is trivial. (B) Let $X$ be a closed connected oriented 4-manifold and $P\to X$ a principal $SU(2)$-bundle. Then $P$ is trivial over $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ for every $x_0$, hence $P\cong P_g$ for a clutching map $g\colon S^3\to SU(2)$ on the boundary of a coordinate disc $D\ni x_0$; the integer $k(P):=\deg g$ (the Chern number of $P$) is independent of all choices and is an isomorphism invariant; and $W(g):=\tfrac1{24\pi^2}\int_{S^3}\operatorname{tr}\big((g^{-1}dg)^{\wedge3}\big)=\deg g$ (sign to be fixed by the writer's orientation computation) for every smooth $g\colon S^3\to SU(2)$. (C) For every $k\in\mathbb Z$ there is a principal $SU(2)$-bundle over $X$ with $k(P)=k$, e.g. the clutching bundle of $q\mapsto q^k$ (unit quaternions), and $k(P_{g_1g_2})=k(P_{g_1})+k(P_{g_2})$. Scope remark (bold): that $k$ is a complete invariant (bundles with equal Chern number are isomorphic) requires $\pi_3(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z$ (the Hopf degree theorem in dimension 3) and is not proved in this series; nothing in I–VI or the later chapters' stated uses needs it beyond existence and invariance; proof: (A) $E:=P\times_{SU(2)}\mathbb C^2$ is a real rank-4 bundle; a nowhere-vanishing section exists by the generic-sections theorem (rank $4>\dim M$); normalise it by the Hermitian structure to a unit section $u$; since $SU(2)$ acts simply transitively on the unit sphere of $\mathbb C^2$, $P\cong$ the unit sphere bundle $S(E)$ as $SU(2)$-spaces (Lemma: the map $P\to S(E)$, $p\mapsto[p,e_1]$, is an equivariant diffeomorphism), so $u$ is a section of $P$ and $P$ is trivial; (B) a generic section of $E$ over $X^4$ has finitely many zeros, gathered into $D$ by the homogeneity lemma, so $P$ is trivial over $X\setminus D^\circ$ and over $D$ (contractible); the clutching theorem gives $P\cong P_g$; invariance of $\deg g$: the ambiguity $g\mapsto(a|_S)g(b|_S)$ changes the degree by $\deg(a|_S)+\deg(b|_S)=0$ (degree theorem (f) and additivity — Lemma A below); Lemma A (additivity of $W$ under pointwise products): with $\theta_i=g_i^{-1}dg_i$ and $\tilde\theta_1=g_2^{-1}\theta_1g_2$, $(g_1g_2)^{-1}d(g_1g_2)=\tilde\theta_1+\theta_2$ and $\operatorname{tr}((\tilde\theta_1+\theta_2)^3)=\operatorname{tr}(\theta_1^3)+\operatorname{tr}(\theta_2^3)-3\,d\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\theta_1\wedge\theta_2)$ (a direct computation using $d\theta=-\theta\wedge\theta$ and cyclicity of the trace; every sign displayed), so $W(g_1g_2)=W(g_1)+W(g_2)$ by Stokes; Lemma B ($W(g)=\deg g$): $\operatorname{tr}(\theta^3)$ is a bi-invariant 3-form on $SU(2)$, hence a constant multiple of the volume form of $S^3$; the constant is computed at the identity ($\operatorname{tr}(\theta^3)(X,Y,Z)=3\operatorname{tr}(X[Y,Z])$ on the basis $-i\sigma_a$) giving $\int_{SU(2)}\operatorname{tr}(\theta^3)=\pm24\pi^2$ (with $\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)=2\pi^2$ from DG IX's sphere-volume exercise); then $W(g)=\deg g$ by the degree theorem; (C) $\deg(q\mapsto q^k)=k$ (by Lemma A and $W(\operatorname{id})=1$, or by the §3.5 exercise) and the tensor/product rule for clutching functions; reference: Naber, *Topology, Geometry and Gauge Fields: Foundations* §6.? (clutching of $SU(2)$-bundles over $S^4$), Nakahara §10.5 (the $24\pi^2$ computation), Hatcher VBKT §1.2; spec: Convention callout: A proves (A) via the classifying space and cellular approximation (I2.4.4); the series' proof is the generic-section argument and needs compactness (A states the result for all manifolds of dimension $\le3$; the non-compact case is a scope remark).
