## Spec (from the manifest)

**[backlink]** — type: definition (compound); source items: A-D2.1.12, A-D2.1.13, A-D2.1.14; prereqs: [Def - Operations on Vector Bundles and Pull-Back Bundles, Def - Connection on a Vector Bundle]; spec: $\mathcal G(E)=\{g\in\Gamma(\operatorname{End}E):g(m)\in GL(E_m)\}$ with the $C^\infty$ topology (defined: uniform convergence of all derivatives on compacta in local frames; a topological group — proved on the page: composition and inversion are continuous in this topology, using Leibniz for derivatives of products and the inverse-series argument), respecting extra structure (orientation: $\det g>0$; Euclidean: $g$ orthogonal; Hermitian: unitary — each a subgroup, verified); the right action $\nabla^gs:=g^{-1}\nabla(gs)$ (its properties are `Thm - Gauge Action on Connection Matrices`); gauge equivalence. Verified examples: for the trivial line bundle, $\mathcal G=C^\infty(M,\mathbb R^*)$ (or $C^\infty(M,U(1))$ with Hermitian structure); non-example: a bundle map covering a non-identity diffeomorphism of $M$ is not a gauge transformation (that is V's $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$). Convention callout: A's gauge group of $E$ is a subgroup of $\Gamma(\operatorname{End}E)$; B's gauge group (V) is $\operatorname{Aut}$ of the principal bundle; the two are identified in V `Thm - The Gauge Group is the Space of Sections of the Adjoint Group Bundle`. Calibration: $\mathcal G$ is not a vector space; the constant $-1$ acts trivially on connections; $\nabla^g=\nabla$ iff $g$ is parallel.
