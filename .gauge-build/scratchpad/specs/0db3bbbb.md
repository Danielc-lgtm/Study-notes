## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: T4.3.8, I4.3.4 (second part); prereqs: [Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Fundamental Class, Def - Local Homology and Local Orientation]; statement: Let $M$ be a closed connected $R$-oriented $n$-manifold and $T$ a triangulation, i.e. a homeomorphism $|K| \to M$ from a finite simplicial complex, with the $n$-simplices $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_N$ (as singular simplices via the vertex orderings) oriented compatibly with the orientation of $M$ (each $\sigma_i$ restricted to its interior carries $\mu_{\text{standard}}$ to $\mu_x$). Then $c := \sum_i \sigma_i$ is a cycle and $[c] = [M]$; proof: source omitted; the page proves (Lemma 1) $\partial c = 0$: each $(n-1)$-simplex is a face of exactly two $n$-simplices (assume, as part of the hypothesis "triangulation of a manifold", that each $(n-1)$-simplex is a face of exactly two $n$-simplices with opposite induced orientations (this is how Bär's "suitably parametrised, oriented simplices" is read), so that $\partial c = 0$ is a direct cancellation; (Lemma 2) for $x$ in the interior of $\sigma_i$, the image of $[c]$ in $H_n(M, M \setminus \{x\})$ equals the image of $\sigma_i$ alone (the other simplices are chains in $M \setminus \{x\}$), which is $\mu_x$ by the orientation hypothesis; by uniqueness in `Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Fundamental Class`, $[c] = [M]$. spec: the page states clearly that existence of triangulations of manifolds is NOT proved or used anywhere in the series; this theorem is only an interpretation of $[M]$ (Bär Rem. 4.3.11), used in chapter XIII to see that an embedded oriented surface $S \subset X$, triangulated, represents $\iota_*[S]$ as a sum of singular simplices (used in `Thm - Intersection Form via de Rham Cohomology` to compute $\int_S \omega$ as a sum over simplices — there the surface is given with an explicit smooth triangulation, e.g. from a smooth structure on $S$ via the standard triangulation of the sphere/torus).
