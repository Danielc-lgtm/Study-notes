type: theorem; source items: T5.1.1, I5.1.1, I5.1.2, I5.1.3, R5.1.1 (assembly), Rem. 5.1.1; prereqs: [Thm - Hurewicz Theorem, Thm - Continuous Maps into a Manifold are Homotopic to Smooth Maps, Thm - Generic Maps from a Surface to a Four-Manifold are Immersions with Transverse Double Points, Thm - Removal of Double Points by Handle Attachment, Thm - Homotopy Invariance of Singular Homology (Gauge Theory), Def - R-Orientation and the Fundamental Class]; statement: Let $X$ be a compact, oriented, simply connected smooth four-manifold. Every $\alpha \in H_2(X;\mathbb{Z})$ is represented by an embedding $f : S \to X$ of a compact connected oriented surface $S$, i.e. $\alpha = f_*[S]$; moreover $S$ can be taken to be a sphere with finitely many handles attached, and the embedding smooth; proof: source: four-step sketch; the page assembles: Step 1 (Hurewicz: $X$ simply connected, so $h : \pi_2(X,x) \to H_2(X;\mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism by `Thm - Hurewicz Theorem` with $m = 2$; hence $\alpha = f_*[S^2]$ for a continuous $f : S^2 \to X$, using that $[c_2] = [S^2]$ by `Ex - The Fundamental Class of the Sphere and the Identity Map`), Step 2 (smoothing, homotopy invariance keeps $\alpha$), Step 3 (generic immersion, again homotopic), Step 4 (handle attachment, class preserved by the previous page); connectedness of $S$ is automatic. Also proved as a corollary (needed in §13.2): any two classes can be represented by embedded surfaces meeting transversally in finitely many points (perturb the second embedding by the parametric-transversality argument of Lemma 3 of the generic-maps page applied to $G(s, s', A) = (f_1(s), f_{2,A}(s'))$ on $S_1 \times S_2 \times W$). spec: Why-true: "simple connectivity turns homology into homotopy, homotopy classes are represented by smooth generic maps, and the only failure of generic maps to embed is a finite set of transverse double points, each of which is a handle in disguise"; Sources: $H_2$ of any simply connected closed smooth four-manifold; Targets: definition of the intersection form; Whitney's trick is NOT available and not needed here.
