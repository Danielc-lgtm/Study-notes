type: theorem; source items: T5.2.6, I5.2.5, (D5.2.x normal forms $A^{odd}_{k,l}$, $A^{even}_{\pm k, l}$); prereqs: [Def - Unimodular Symmetric Bilinear Form, Parity, and Signature, Thm - Direct Sums of Unimodular Forms, Thm - The E8 Form is Even Positive Definite and Unimodular, Thm - Even Unimodular Forms Have Signature Divisible by Eight, Thm - Existence of Characteristic Elements, Thm - Smith Normal Form]; statement: (i) (Odd case) Every odd indefinite unimodular symmetric bilinear form on a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank is isomorphic to $A^{odd}_{k,l} := \langle 1 \rangle^{\oplus k} \oplus \langle -1 \rangle^{\oplus l}$ with $k, l \ge 1$, $k + l = \operatorname{rk}$, $k - l = \operatorname{sign}$. (ii) (Even case) Every even indefinite unimodular form is isomorphic to $A^{even}_{\pm k, l} := (\pm E_8)^{\oplus k} \oplus H^{\oplus l}$ with $l \ge 1$, $8k + 2l = \operatorname{rk}$, $\pm 8k = \operatorname{sign}$. (iii) Hence two indefinite unimodular forms are isomorphic iff they have the same rank, signature and parity; proof: source: cited (Serre); the page follows Milnor–Husemoller, *Symmetric Bilinear Forms*, Ch. II §4 (indefinite odd forms) and §5 (indefinite even forms), and carries the pre-approved import callout (Import 4) `Imported without proof: the Hasse–Minkowski theorem and Meyer's theorem`, stated as: "a non-degenerate quadratic form over $\mathbb{Q}$ represents zero over $\mathbb{Q}$ iff it represents zero over $\mathbb{R}$ and over every $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Hasse–Minkowski); in particular an indefinite non-degenerate rational form in five or more variables represents zero (Meyer)" — source: Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Ch. IV Thm 8 and Cor. 2; Cassels, *Rational Quadratic Forms*, Ch. 6; architecture: the local–global principle via Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions and the product formula for Hilbert symbols; imported because the analytic number theory it rests on is beyond the series. Everything else on the page is proved. (Lemma 1, isotropy) an indefinite unimodular integral form $Q$ of rank $\ge 2$ has a primitive isotropic vector $x \ne 0$ with $Q(x,x) = 0$: for rank $\ge 5$ this is Meyer applied to $Q \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ (clear denominators and divide by the gcd of the coordinates to make the rational isotropic vector primitive integral); for ranks $2, 3, 4$ the page proves it in full following Milnor–Husemoller Ch. II §4, computing the real and $p$-adic local invariants of a unimodular indefinite form of rank $\le 4$ and invoking only the rational Hasse–Minkowski statement named in the callout. (Lemma 2, splitting off a plane) if $Q$ is unimodular and $x$ is primitive isotropic then $Q(x,\cdot)$ is a primitive functional, so unimodularity yields $y$ with $Q(x,y) = 1$; the sublattice $\langle x, y \rangle$ has Gram matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & a \end{pmatrix}$ (determinant $-1$, hence unimodular), a unimodular sublattice splits off orthogonally ($Q \cong \langle x, y \rangle \oplus \langle x, y \rangle^\perp$, proved), and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & a \end{pmatrix} \cong H$ if $a$ is even, $\cong \langle 1 \rangle \oplus \langle -1 \rangle$ if $a$ is odd (explicit bases). (Lemma 3, odd case) by induction on the rank, using Lemma 2 together with the identity $H \oplus \langle 1 \rangle \cong \langle 1 \rangle \oplus \langle 1 \rangle \oplus \langle -1 \rangle$ (explicit) to turn any split-off $H$ into diagonal blocks once an odd $\langle \pm 1 \rangle$ is present: every odd indefinite unimodular form is isomorphic to $\langle 1 \rangle^{\oplus k} \oplus \langle -1 \rangle^{\oplus l}$ with $k, l \ge 1$, $k + l = \operatorname{rk}$, $k - l = \operatorname{sign}$; the page reproduces Milnor–Husemoller Ch. II §4 Thm 4.3 step by step. (Lemma 4, even case) split hyperbolic planes off the even indefinite $Q$ by Lemma 2 until the orthogonal complement $Q_0$ is definite and even; such a $Q_0$ has rank in $8\mathbb{Z}$ equal to $\pm \operatorname{sign}$ by `Thm - Even Unimodular Forms Have Signature Divisible by Eight` (proved from the ODD case of Serre through `Thm - Van der Blij Lemma`, so there is no circularity), and existence of the normal forms $(\pm E_8)^{\oplus k} \oplus H^{\oplus l}$ with the stated invariants comes from `Thm - The E8 Form is Even Positive Definite and Unimodular` and `Thm - Direct Sums of Unimodular Forms`; uniqueness is the cancellation lemma "$Q_1 \oplus \langle 1 \rangle \cong Q_2 \oplus \langle 1 \rangle$ with $Q_1, Q_2$ even implies $Q_1 \cong Q_2$", proved via characteristic vectors (a characteristic vector of a form $R$ is a $v$ with $R(v,w) \equiv R(w,w) \pmod 2$ for all $w$): for even $Q_i$ the characteristic vectors of $Q_i \oplus \langle 1 \rangle$ are exactly the $(2z, m)$ with $m$ odd, of norm $Q_i(2z,2z) + m^2$, so the only characteristic vectors of norm $1$ are $(0, \pm 1)$; any isomorphism $Q_1 \oplus \langle 1 \rangle \cong Q_2 \oplus \langle 1 \rangle$ preserves the form and hence the set of norm-one characteristic vectors, carries $(0, 1)$ to $(0, \pm 1)$, and therefore restricts to an isomorphism of the orthogonal complements $\langle 1 \rangle^\perp$, i.e. $Q_1 \cong Q_2$; two even indefinite unimodular forms of equal rank and signature become isomorphic after adding a $\langle 1 \rangle$ (they turn odd indefinite, so Lemma 3 applies), and the cancellation strips the $\langle 1 \rangle$ off, giving (ii) and (iii).
