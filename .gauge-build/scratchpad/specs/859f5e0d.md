type: theorem; source items: A-T3.3.2, A-R3.3.7, A-I3.3.4 (replaced by countability), A-E3.3.1 (example), B-? none; prereqs: [Thm - Flat Connections and Monodromy Representations of the Fundamental Group, Thm - Existence of the Universal Cover and the Deck Action of the Fundamental Group]; statement: Let $M$ be a connected manifold and $G$ a compact Lie group. Give $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M,m),G)$ the topology of pointwise convergence (as a subspace of $G^{\pi_1(M,m)}$ with the product topology) and $\mathcal R(M;G)$ the quotient topology. Then $\mathcal R(M;G)$ is compact; if moreover $\pi_1(M,m)$ is generated by $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_N$ then $\rho\mapsto(\rho(\gamma_i))_i$ embeds $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1,G)$ as a closed subset of $G^N$ and $\mathcal R(M;G)$ as a closed subset of $G^N/G$ (diagonal conjugation), a compact Hausdorff space (A-R3.3.7 as a conditional clause; finite generation of $\pi_1$ of a compact manifold is a scope remark, not claimed); proof: (i) $\pi_1(M,m)$ is countable (universal-cover theorem (d)); (ii) a countable product of compact metrisable spaces is sequentially compact, hence compact (diagonal argument, written; this avoids the flagged Tychonoff page); (iii) $\operatorname{Hom}$ is closed in the product (the conditions $\rho(ab)=\rho(a)\rho(b)$ are closed); (iv) the quotient by the continuous action of the compact group $G$ is compact and Hausdorff (compact group acting on a Hausdorff space: the orbit relation is closed — proved); (v) the finitely generated clause; A's Example 107 (torus) as worked corollary: $\mathcal R(T^n;U(1))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z^n,U(1))=U(1)^n$, using $\pi_1(T^n)=\mathbb Z^n$ (AT II's torus exercise and $\pi_1(S^1)$, with the product formula $\pi_1(X\times Y)=\pi_1(X)\times\pi_1(Y)$ proved as a lemma on the page); reference: Goldman, *The symplectic nature of fundamental groups of surfaces* §1 (for the topology on representation varieties), Bourbaki GT for the quotient; spec: Convention callout: A topologises $\mathcal R$ via a finite presentation; the series uses pointwise convergence, which agrees with A's when $\pi_1$ is finitely generated (proved in clause (v)). Targets: compactness of moduli of flat connections as the first "compactness theorem" of the series (contrast with XI).
