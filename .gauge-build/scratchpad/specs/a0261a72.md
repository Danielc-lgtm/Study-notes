## Spec (from the manifest)

type: definition (anchor); source items: B-D3.1.4, B-D3.1.6, B-R3.1.1, B-R3.1.2; prereqs: [Def - Generalized Orthonormal Basis, Index, and the Induced Inner Product on Forms, Thm - The Induced Inner Product and Volume Form are Well-Defined, Thm - Existence and Uniqueness of the Hodge Star]; spec: on an oriented $n$-dimensional $(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ of index $p$, $\star:\Lambda^kV^*\to\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ is the unique linear map with $\omega\wedge\eta = \langle\star\omega,\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}$ for all $\omega\in\Lambda^kV^*$, $\eta\in\Lambda^{n-k}V^*$ (existence/uniqueness wikilinked to the next page); pointwise extension to forms on an oriented semi-Riemannian manifold; $W$-valued forms: $\star(\omega\otimes w) := (\star\omega)\otimes w$ with $\langle v\otimes w,v'\otimes w'\rangle := \langle v,v'\rangle\langle w,w'\rangle$ (D3.1.6, R3.1.2 — needed for $\mathfrak g$-valued curvature in §7.4); R3.1.1: $\star$ depends on the inner product (relevant when the metric is varied in §7.3). **Convention callout (mandatory, see group decision 1):** the three conventions and the exact signs $\star_B = (-1)^p\star_V$, $\star' = (-1)^{k(n-k)}\star_V$; the vault's `Def - The Hodge Star Operator` is $\star_V$; note also that conventions.md's phrase "$(-1)^{k(n-k)}$" describes $\star'$ versus $\star_V$, not Bär versus the vault. Examples: Euclidean $\mathbb R^3$ ($\star dx = dy\wedge dz$, etc., recovering the vault's `Ex - Hodge Star on R^3 Recovers Cross Product and Scalar Triple Product`); Minkowski 2-forms (Bär's six-line star table: $\star(dt\wedge dx) = dy\wedge dz$, $\star(dy\wedge dz) = -dt\wedge dx$, $\star(dt\wedge dy) = dz\wedge dx$, $\star(dz\wedge dx) = -dt\wedge dy$, $\star(dt\wedge dz) = dx\wedge dy$, $\star(dx\wedge dy) = -dt\wedge dz$, each verified from the defining relation on the page); non-example: no such map exists for a degenerate form. Calibration check: verify $\star\star = -1$ on Minkowski 2-forms from the table; verify $\star_B(dt\wedge dx) = -\star_V(dt\wedge dx)$.
