## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: A-I5.2.2, A-R5.2.3; prereqs: [Def - Formal Adjoint of a Differential Operator, Thm - The Principal Symbol is Well-Defined and Multiplicative, Thm - Existence of Smooth Partitions of Unity, Thm - Stokes' Theorem on Manifolds]; statement: Every differential operator $L:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(F)$ of order $\ell$ has a unique formal adjoint $L^*$; $L^*$ is a differential operator of order $\ell$ with $\sigma_{L^*}(x,\xi) = (-1)^\ell\sigma_L(x,\xi)^*$ (fibrewise adjoint); $L$ is elliptic if and only if $L^*$ is; $(L^*)^* = L$ and $(L_2L_1)^* = L_1^*L_2^*$; proof: in a chart with orthonormal trivialisations, $\int\langle\sum A_\alpha\partial^\alpha s,t\rangle\rho\,dx = \int\langle s,\sum(-1)^{|\alpha|}\rho^{-1}\partial^\alpha(\rho A_\alpha^*t)\rangle\rho\,dx$ ($\rho = |g|^{1/2}$) by repeated integration by parts (compact supports); glue with a partition of unity ($L^* = \sum_iL_i^*\circ\phi_i$-type formula, uniqueness makes the pieces agree on overlaps); the symbol statement from the top-order term; ellipticity: $\sigma_L(\xi)$ invertible iff its adjoint is; spec: mechanism: **integration by parts moves each derivative across at the cost of a sign, and the top-order coefficients are just transposed**.
