## Spec (from the manifest)

type: theorem; source items: (Hatcher Prop. 2.21; the engine behind I4.2.3 and excision); prereqs: [Def - Singular Homology with Coefficients, Def - Chain Complex and Its Homology, Thm - Lebesgue Number Lemma for Compact Metric Spaces]; statement: Let $\mathcal{U} = \{U_j\}$ be a family of subsets of $X$ whose interiors cover $X$, and $C_n^{\mathcal{U}}(X;R) \subset C_n(X;R)$ the submodule spanned by simplices with image in some $U_j$. Then the inclusion $\iota : C_\bullet^{\mathcal{U}}(X) \to C_\bullet(X)$ is a chain homotopy equivalence: there is a chain map $\rho : C_\bullet(X) \to C_\bullet^{\mathcal{U}}(X)$ with $\rho \iota = \mathrm{id}$ and $\mathrm{id} - \iota \rho = \partial D + D \partial$ for a chain homotopy $D$; moreover $\rho$ and $D$ are natural and take chains in $A$ to chains in $A$ for any $A \subset X$; proof: source: none; the page follows Hatcher Prop. 2.21 in its four steps as four lemmas: (Lemma 1) barycentric subdivision of linear chains $S : LC_n(Y) \to LC_n(Y)$ with $\partial S = S \partial$ and the cone operator $b : LC_n \to LC_{n+1}$ with $\partial b + b \partial = \mathrm{id} - [b]$ (explicit formulas, induction on $n$); (Lemma 2) the chain homotopy $T$ with $\partial T + T \partial = \mathrm{id} - S$ on linear chains; (Lemma 3) the diameter estimate: each simplex of $S(\Delta^n)$ has diameter at most $\tfrac{n}{n+1} \operatorname{diam}(\Delta^n)$ (the geometric lemma on barycentres, proved with the explicit inequality); (Lemma 4) transport to singular chains by $S\sigma := \sigma_* S(\Delta^n)$, $T\sigma := \sigma_* T(\Delta^n)$, and, for each $\sigma$, the least $m(\sigma)$ with $S^{m}\sigma \in C^{\mathcal{U}}$ (exists by the Lebesgue number lemma applied to $\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{U})$ on $\Delta^n$), $D\sigma := \sum_{0 \le i < m(\sigma)} T S^i \sigma$, $\rho := \mathrm{id} - \partial D - D \partial$; verification that $\rho$ lands in $C^{\mathcal{U}}$, is a chain map, and $\rho \iota = \mathrm{id}$ (since $m = 0$ on $C^{\mathcal{U}}$). Reference: Hatcher Prop. 2.21, pp. 119–124. spec: Why-true: "subdividing enough times makes every simplex small, and subdivision is chain-homotopic to the identity"; Targets: excision, Mayer–Vietoris, the proof of Poincaré duality via small chains is not needed (Hatcher's PD proof uses MV for $H_c$, which uses excision).
