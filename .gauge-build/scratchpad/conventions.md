## Standing conventions

- **Manifolds** are smooth, Hausdorff, second countable; "smooth" means $C^\infty$. $\Gamma(E)$ denotes smooth sections; $\Omega^p(M; E) = \Gamma(\Lambda^p T^*M \otimes E)$.
- **Lie groups** act on principal bundles on the **right**; $R_g(p) = p \cdot g$. Left actions are used for group actions on manifolds and for representations. The fundamental vector field of $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ on a right $G$-space is $\xi_P(p) = \frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, p \cdot \exp(t\xi)$.
- **Lie algebra** $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$ with the bracket of left-invariant vector fields; for matrix groups this is the commutator. $\operatorname{Ad}_g = d_e(h \mapsto ghg^{-1})$; for matrix groups $\operatorname{Ad}_g X = gXg^{-1}$; $\operatorname{ad}_X Y = [X, Y]$.
- **Connection on a vector bundle**: a covariant derivative $\nabla : \Gamma(E) \to \Omega^1(M; E)$ with $\nabla(fs) = df \otimes s + f \nabla s$. In a local frame $e = (e_1, \dots, e_k)$ (row of sections), $\nabla e = e \cdot A$ with $A \in \Omega^1(U; \mathfrak{gl}_k)$ the **connection matrix**; locally $\nabla = d + A$; frame change $e' = e g$ gives $A' = g^{-1} A g + g^{-1} dg$ (Haydys (14)).
- **Curvature of a vector-bundle connection**: $F_\nabla = d^\nabla \circ d^\nabla \in \Omega^2(M; \operatorname{End} E)$; locally $F = dA + A \wedge A = dA + \tfrac12 [A \wedge A]$ (Haydys (18)/(20)); under $e' = eg$, $F' = g^{-1} F g$. $F_\nabla(X, Y)s = \nabla_X \nabla_Y s - \nabla_Y \nabla_X s - \nabla_{[X,Y]} s$.
- **Gauge group of a vector bundle**: $\mathcal{G}(E) = \{g \in \Gamma(\operatorname{End} E) : g(m) \in GL(E_m)\}$ (respecting any extra structure), acting on connections on the **right** by $\nabla^g s = g^{-1} \nabla (g s)$, so $A(\nabla^g, e) = g^{-1} A g + g^{-1} dg$.
- **Connection on a principal bundle**: a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued 1-form $\omega \in \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g})$ with $\omega(\xi_P) = \xi$ and $R_g^* \omega = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} \omega$; equivalently a $G$-invariant horizontal distribution $H = \ker \omega$. Local connection forms $A_s = s^* \omega$ for a local section $s$; under $s' = s \cdot g$, $A_{s'} = \operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}} A_s + g^* \theta$ where $\theta$ is the left Maurer–Cartan form ($= g^{-1} dg$ for matrix groups).
- **Curvature of a principal connection**: $\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12 [\omega \wedge \omega]$ (structure equation), horizontal and $\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant; local curvature $F_A = dA + \tfrac12 [A \wedge A]$; for matrix groups $\tfrac12[A \wedge A] = A \wedge A$. Bianchi identity $d^\omega \Omega = 0$, locally $dF_A + [A \wedge F_A] = 0$.
- **Bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$-valued forms**: $[\alpha \wedge \beta](X, Y) = [\alpha(X), \beta(Y)] - [\alpha(Y), \beta(X)]$ for 1-forms, so $[\alpha \wedge \alpha](X,Y) = 2[\alpha(X), \alpha(Y)]$; this is why the structure equation carries $\tfrac12$.
- **Characteristic classes**: for an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant polynomial $f$ of degree $k$, the Chern–Weil form is $f(F_A) \in \Omega^{2k}(M)$; total Chern class $c(E) = \det\!\big(1 + \tfrac{i}{2\pi} F_A\big)$ for a complex bundle with a unitary connection, so $c_1(L) = \big[\tfrac{i}{2\pi} F_A\big]$ for a Hermitian line bundle; first Pontryagin class $p_1(E) = -c_2(E \otimes \mathbb{C})$; Euler class of an oriented rank-$2m$ real bundle via the Pfaffian, $e(E) = \big[\operatorname{Pf}\!\big(\tfrac{F}{2\pi}\big)\big]$ with the sign convention that $e(TS^2)[S^2] = 2$. State the normalisation on every page that uses it.
- **Hodge star** (Bär's convention, Lemma 3.1.5, adopted for the whole series): on an oriented $n$-dimensional real inner-product space $(V, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ of signature with $p$ negative directions, $\star : \Lambda^k V^* \to \Lambda^{n-k} V^*$ is the unique linear map with $\omega \wedge \eta = \langle \star\omega, \eta \rangle \, \mathrm{vol}$ for all $\omega \in \Lambda^k V^*$, $\eta \in \Lambda^{n-k} V^*$; then $\star\star = (-1)^{k(n-k)+p}$ on $k$-forms. Every page that uses $\star$ states this defining relation and notes in a `> [!warning] Convention:` callout that the other common convention $\alpha \wedge \star\beta = \langle \alpha, \beta \rangle \mathrm{vol}$ differs from it by the sign $(-1)^{k(n-k)}$ (the two agree in dimension 4 on 2-forms). Lorentzian signature is $(-,+,+,+)$ with $c = 1$ (Bär §3.2). Self-dual / anti-self-dual: $\star F = \pm F$ on 2-forms in dimension 4.
- **Yang–Mills**: $\mathcal{YM}(A) = \tfrac12 \int_M |F_A|^2 \, \mathrm{vol}$ with $|F|^2$ from an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$ (for $\mathfrak{su}(n)$: $-\operatorname{tr}(XY)$); Yang–Mills equation $d^A \star F_A = 0$; instantons are (anti-)self-dual connections. (Reconcile the normalisation constants with B §3.3 and say which is used.)
- **Clifford algebra**: $\operatorname{Cl}(V, q)$ generated by $V$ with $v \cdot v = -q(v) \, 1$ (the sign Haydys uses; state it and note the opposite convention exists).
- **Sobolev spaces**: $W^{k,p}$ norms defined with a fixed metric and connection on a compact base; $L^2_k = W^{k,2}$; embedding $W^{k,p} \hookrightarrow C^0$ for $kp > n$; state constants only as "depending on the data".
- **Orientation and degree**: orientation of a manifold via a nowhere-vanishing top form (smooth case) or via local homology generators (topological case, chapter XII); the fundamental class $[M] \in H_n(M; \mathbb{Z})$; intersection form defined as in Bär §5.1 by transverse intersection of oriented surfaces, $Q_X([S_1],[S_2]) = S_1 \cdot S_2 = \sum_{p \in S_1 \cap S_2} \varepsilon(p)$ with $\varepsilon(p) = +1$ iff the orientation of $T_pS_1 \oplus T_pS_2$ agrees with that of $T_pX$, on $H_2(X;\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion}$; the cup-product form $\langle \alpha \smile \beta, [X] \rangle$ and the de Rham form $\int_X \alpha \wedge \beta$ are proved equal to it, not taken as the definition. Unimodular means $\det(Q(e_i,e_j)) = \pm 1$; $\overline{X}$ is $X$ with reversed orientation and $Q_{\overline{X}} = -Q_X$.
- **Homotopy groups**: $\pi_n(X, x)$ via based maps $(S^n, NP) \to (X, x)$ or $(I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x)$, with concatenation in the first coordinate.

Where the content maps report that a source uses a different convention (e.g. $F = dA + A \wedge A$ without the $\tfrac12$ for matrix groups; left actions; opposite Clifford sign), the page states the source's convention in its `> [!warning] Convention:` callout and gives the one-line recipe for converting.

## Pre-approved imports (the only results that may appear in an `Imported without proof` callout)

Each must still be stated exactly, sourced by section/page, described architecturally, justified, and listed on its chapter's `# Imported Results` and on the Series Map.

1. **Freedman's classification of simply connected closed topological 4-manifolds** (existence for every unimodular form; uniqueness up to homeomorphism in the even case, two manifolds in the odd case). Source: Freedman, *The topology of four-dimensional manifolds*, J. Diff. Geom. 17 (1982); Freedman–Quinn, *Topology of 4-Manifolds*.
2. **Donaldson's diagonalisation theorem — the analytic package**: Uhlenbeck compactness and removable singularities, Freed–Uhlenbeck generic-metric transversality, the Taubes existence theorem for instantons, and the structure of the moduli space near reducible connections (cone on $\mathbb{CP}^2$). The *deduction* of the diagonalisation from these (the cobordism argument counting singular points, $n(Q) \le \operatorname{rk} Q$ with equality iff diagonal) is proved in full in chapter XIII. Source: Donaldson–Kronheimer, *The Geometry of Four-Manifolds*, Ch. 4, 7, 8; Freed–Uhlenbeck, *Instantons and Four-Manifolds*.
3. **The Atiyah–Singer index theorem** (used for the index of the Dirac operator on a spin 4-manifold, $\operatorname{ind} D^+ = -\sigma(X)/8$, in the proof of Rokhlin's theorem). Source: Lawson–Michelsohn, *Spin Geometry*, Thm III.13.10 and IV.1.1.
4. **The Hasse–Minkowski theorem / Meyer's theorem** (an indefinite rational quadratic form in five or more variables represents zero), used in the proof of Serre's classification of indefinite unimodular forms. Source: Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Ch. IV Thm 8 and Cor. 2; Milnor–Husemoller, *Symmetric Bilinear Forms*, Ch. II §4–5.
5. **The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem** (used only to see that the K3 surface is simply connected). Source: Milnor, *Morse Theory*, §7; Griffiths–Harris, *Principles of Algebraic Geometry*, Ch. 1 §2.
6. **Casson's theory** (the smooth embedding facts used for the exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ in B §5.3). Source: Gompf–Stipsicz, *4-Manifolds and Kirby Calculus*, Ch. 9; Freedman–Quinn Ch. 2–3.
7. **The Minkowski–Siegel mass formula** (used only for the lower bound on the number of definite forms). Source: Conway–Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, Ch. 16; Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Ch. VII.
8. **Whitehead's theorem that the homotopy type of a simply connected closed topological 4-manifold is determined by its intersection form** — *only* the topological-manifold input (that a closed topological manifold has the homotopy type of a CW complex, and Poincaré duality for topological manifolds if chapter XII proves it only for the manifolds it needs). Everything reachable from Hurewicz, cellular approximation, and Whitehead's CW theorem is proved in chapter XII. Source: Milnor, *On simply connected 4-manifolds* (1958); Milnor, *On spaces having the homotopy type of a CW-complex* (1959).

Everything else is proved: homotopy invariance of singular homology, excision and Mayer–Vietoris, the long exact sequence of a fibre bundle, the lifting criterion and existence of the universal cover, the Hurewicz theorem, orientation theory and the fundamental class, Poincaré duality, Serre's classification (modulo item 4), van der Blij's lemma, Rokhlin (modulo item 3), the Sobolev embedding and Rellich theorems, elliptic regularity and the Fredholm property, the Hodge theorem for elliptic complexes, Sard's theorem and Sard–Smale, the Kuranishi model, the determinant line bundle and its orientation, Chern–Weil, the Weitzenböck formula, Seiberg–Witten compactness and the structure of the moduli space, the classification of $U(1)$- and $SU(2)$-bundles over low-dimensional manifolds, and every remaining statement in both sources.

## Orientation and sign ledger (decided by the coordinator; binding)

- $\mathbb{CP}^1$ and every complex manifold carry the **complex orientation** (the one in which $(\partial_x, \partial_y)$ is positive for a holomorphic coordinate $z = x + iy$). With it, the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^1$ has $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(\mathcal{O}(-1)) = -1$, and $\deg \mathcal{O}(1) = +1$.
- $SU(2) \cong S^3$ is oriented as the **boundary of the closed unit ball in $\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{R}^4$** (outward normal first: a basis $(v_1, v_2, v_3)$ of $T_pS^3$ is positive iff $(p, v_1, v_2, v_3)$ is positive in $\mathbb{R}^4$ with its standard orientation $(1, i, j, k)$).
- Chern classes are normalised by $c(E) = \det\!\big(1 + \tfrac{i}{2\pi} F\big)$; for an $SU(2)$-bundle $c_2(P) = \big[\tfrac{1}{8\pi^2} \operatorname{tr}(F \wedge F)\big]$ with $\operatorname{tr}$ the matrix trace in the defining representation. The **instanton number** is $k(P) := \int_M c_2(P)$; every page that uses the phrase says whether its $k$ equals $+c_2$ or $-c_2$ under these conventions, and the four sign-sensitive pages below agree.
- **One writer, one sign ledger.** The pages `Def - The Hopf Bundle`, `Thm - Classification of Principal SU(2)-Bundles over Four-Manifolds`, `Thm - Second Chern Number of an SU(2)-Bundle over a Closed Four-Manifold is the Clutching Degree`, and `Thm - Gauge Variation of the Chern-Simons Functional` are written by the same agent in one run. That agent computes, under the two orientation conventions above, the signs of $\int_{SU(2)} \operatorname{tr}(\theta \wedge \theta \wedge \theta)$ (where $\theta = g^{-1} dg$), of the clutching degree versus $c_2$, and of $\vartheta(A \cdot g) - \vartheta(A)$ versus $\deg g$, records all of them in a section `# Sign ledger` on `Def - The Hopf Bundle`, and makes the four pages consistent with that ledger. Every other page that needs one of these signs transcludes or wikilinks the ledger rather than re-deriving it.
